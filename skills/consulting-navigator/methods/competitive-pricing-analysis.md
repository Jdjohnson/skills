# Competitive Pricing Analysis
## Purpose
Assemble a comparable competitive price map to show where the firm sits on list, street, and effective price versus rivals. It resolves whether an apparent price gap is real after packaging, discounts, fees, and units of measure are made comparable.
## Use
- Mapping list, street, and effective prices against a defined competitive set.
- Explaining win/loss or share shifts linked to relative price position.
- Preparing evidence before price changes, packaging redesign, or value-based pricing work.
## Avoid
- You need willingness-to-pay or elasticity, not competitor position—use price-sensitivity-meter or price-elasticity-analysis.
- The question is full value-based price architecture—use value-based-pricing after the competitive map.
- No identifiable competitors or public/observable prices exist—use conjoint-analysis or customer interviews instead.
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Defined product/SKU set and competitive set; competitor list, promotional, and effective prices (and fee structure); packaging, unit-of-measure, and feature comparability notes; own cost floors and current discounting practices.

**Nice-to-have:** win/loss reasons, distributor quotes, historical price moves, currency exposures, and contract discount ranges.

**When actual transaction prices are unavailable:** label the analysis list-price-only; do not use a competitor’s advertised price as its realized price.
## Procedure
1. Select the buying situations and SKUs that customers genuinely compare; include substitute packages when they displace the offer.
2. Capture each competitor’s list price, promotion, discount conditions, mandatory fees, contract term, and date observed in a pricing ledger.
3. Translate offers to a common unit and feature basis, such as dollars per active user per month or dollars per delivered kilogram. State what value differences remain unadjusted.
4. Calculate list, street, and effective-price positions. Effective price includes unavoidable charges and expected discounts; show a range where discounting varies.
5. Overlay win/loss evidence and segment by customer type, channel, or region to find where the gap changes buying outcomes.
6. Test response options against own cost floor, price waterfall, and likely competitor reaction: hold, repackage, change discount guardrails, or move list price.
## Output Contract
Deliver a dated price-position map and comparison table for each buying situation. It must show raw observations, normalization formula, list/street/effective prices, gap versus own offer, comparability caveats, and recommended commercial response. The deliverable describes market position; it does not claim demand elasticity or customer willingness to pay.
## Evidence
Preserve screenshots, quotes, contract terms, or mystery-shopping records for observed prices. Treat typical discount rates, bundle allocations, foreign-exchange rates, and fee incidence as assumptions. Separate a public promotion from a negotiated enterprise concession. If feature parity cannot be established, leave the difference visible instead of forcing a false equivalent.
## Checks
- Price observation dates, currency, geography, and tax treatment are visible.
- Every comparison uses the same quantity, term, and service scope.
- List, discounted, and effective figures are not blended into one unlabeled number.
- Mandatory implementation or delivery charges appear in total cost.
- Proposed action respects margin floor and discount authority.
## Failure Modes
- Calling a rival cheaper because its monthly list price excludes a compulsory platform fee.
- Comparing an annual prepaid discount with a cancellable monthly plan.
- Averaging enterprise deal quotes with self-service prices.
- Mistaking a temporary promotional headline for normal street price.
- Raising list price to close a gap when uncontrolled discounting is the actual issue.
