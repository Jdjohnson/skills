# Total Productive Maintenance
## Purpose
Govern equipment-driven production and quality risk by raising overall equipment effectiveness through autonomous care, planned maintenance, and loss elimination. It creates an operating system for preventing recurring losses, not a one-time maintenance backlog review.
## Use
- Raising equipment effectiveness by cutting breakdowns, setup, and quality losses on assets.
- Building operator ownership of basic care alongside planned and predictive maintenance.
- Governing production risk where machine availability drives cost and delivery.
## Avoid
- The constraint is pure process method variation with healthy equipment—use six-sigma or standard-work.
- The process is administrative or service with negligible capital equipment—use process-mapping or service-blueprinting.
- You only need a one-time capacity snapshot, not a maintenance operating system—use capacity-analysis.
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Asset list and criticality ranking for production equipment; downtime, failure, and maintenance history (or structured loss data for OEE); current maintenance roles between operators and technicians; spare parts, lubrication, and planned maintenance practices in use; safety and lockout constraints for autonomous maintenance tasks.

**Nice-to-have:** condition-monitoring readings, changeover observations, spare-parts lead times, and skills matrices.

**When data is thin:** begin with structured shift loss codes on a pilot asset; do not manufacture an OEE trend from anecdotal downtime.
## Procedure
1. **Select the pilot and loss baseline.** Choose a critical asset family and calculate availability, performance, and quality from defined planned production time. Classify stops, speed loss, setup, and defects by shift and product.
2. **Restore basic conditions.** Run a safe initial clean, inspect, lubricate, tighten, and tag defects. Eliminate obvious contamination and access barriers before asking operators to own checks.
3. **Assign autonomous and planned work.** Create operator care standards for inspection, cleaning, lubrication, and abnormality escalation. Build technician preventive tasks from failure modes, intervals, spares, and shutdown windows.
4. **Attack chronic losses.** Use focused improvement teams to investigate the largest repeated loss with evidence. Verify countermeasures by changed loss minutes, not by the number of meetings or tags closed.
5. **Scale the pillars.** Train roles, audit standards, refine maintenance plans from condition and failure data, and extend only after the pilot demonstrates stable gains.
## Output Contract
The plan includes an asset-criticality view, OEE loss tree, pilot charter, operator and technician task split, maintenance calendar, focused-improvement backlog, skills plan, safety controls, and benefits trajectory. Each loss has an owner and review cadence. The output must make it possible to sustain equipment care across shifts, not simply report asset utilization.
## Evidence
Machine counters, work orders, quality records, and verified downtime are observed. A coded cause that has not been physically confirmed is provisional. Record assumptions behind ideal cycle time and planned downtime because they determine performance and availability. Never classify operator care as acceptable without lockout, guarding, and competency approval. Report unknown failure modes separately from known causes.
## Checks
- OEE components reconcile to shift logs and use stable definitions.
- Every autonomous task has a safe method, frequency, standard, and escalation condition.
- Preventive tasks address credible failure modes rather than calendar habit.
- The pilot shows repeatable loss reduction across more than one shift.
- Spare-part and maintenance-window constraints are reflected in the schedule.
## Failure Modes
- Renaming cleaning campaigns as maintenance without inspection standards.
- Blaming operators for stoppages caused by inaccessible design or missing spares.
- Improving availability while ignoring speed losses and defects.
- Scheduling preventive work during the only profitable production window.
- Closing defect tags without validating that the loss has stopped recurring.
