# Value Proposition Canvas
## Purpose
Design and test fit between customer jobs/pains/gains and the products, pain relievers, and gain creators offered. It makes a single segment's desired progress and the proposed offer explicitly comparable before the team scales messaging, features, or channels.
## Use
- Designing or stress-testing how products and services map to customer jobs, pains, and gains
- Aligning product, marketing, and sales on a shared value-prop hypothesis before scaling
- Checking problem–solution fit as an input to business model design
## Avoid
- You need the full business model (channels, revenue, costs)—use business-model-canvas after or around this
- You need deep behavioral research method design first—use jobs-to-be-done or voice-of-the-customer to feed the canvas
- You need enterprise strategy objective linkage—use strategy-map
## Inputs
**Must-have:**
- Defined customer segment or persona
- Evidence or hypotheses on customer jobs, pains, and gains
- Candidate products/services, pain relievers, and gain creators
- Criteria for assessing fit (willingness to pay, usage, preference)

**Nice-to-have:** interview transcripts, lost-deal notes, prototype recordings, and buyer-versus-user roles.

**When data is thin:** keep separate hypothesis cards, state confidence, and test the riskiest job or pain first.
## Procedure
1. **Name one customer and situation.** Define a segment with a coherent context, trigger, and constraints. Separate buyer from end user when priorities differ. Output: customer-situation statement.
2. **Populate the customer profile.** Capture functional, social, and emotional jobs, obstructing pains, and desired gains. Use customer wording and rank by frequency, intensity, and consequence. Output: ranked profile.
3. **Inventory the value map.** List only actual product or service elements, then connect each to a pain reliever or gain creator. Mark elements that merely sound attractive but have no stated customer counterpart. Output: traceable value map.
4. **Test fit and tension.** Compare importance on the profile with strength of the offer's response. Locate overbuilt features, severe unaddressed pains, and claims that require evidence. Output: fit assessment and hypotheses.
5. **Choose experiments and message.** Select the smallest test for the riskiest assumption, such as a concept test, prototype task, or pricing conversation. Draft a value proposition that makes a prioritized job-to-outcome promise. Output: experiment backlog and proposition.
## Output Contract
A two-sided canvas for one customer situation with ranked jobs, pains, gains, offer elements, explicit links, fit gaps, confidence tags, and the next experiment. It names the primary message and evidence needed before a market claim. It guides an offer decision, not a complete revenue, channel, and cost model.
## Evidence
Mark interviews, behavioral data, and workarounds as observed. Treat internal personas and untested benefit claims as assumptions. Do not merge dissimilar segments. Preserve negative evidence. A fit link is inferred until a user demonstrates preference, adoption, or willingness to pay; record its disconfirming test.
## Checks
- The profile describes a real context rather than a demographic label alone.
- Each reliever or creator maps to a specific ranked pain or gain.
- Important jobs without an offer response remain visible.
- The proposition avoids promises beyond the product's demonstrated capability.
- The selected experiment attacks the highest-consequence uncertainty first.
## Failure Modes
- Filling every box with broad marketing adjectives instead of customer evidence.
- Combining buyer, administrator, and frontline user needs on one undifferentiated canvas.
- Claiming a feature relieves a pain without explaining the mechanism.
- Treating all gains as equally important and producing an unfocused offer.
- Declaring fit from an internal workshop without showing it to customers.
