# Balanced Scorecard
## Purpose
Measure and manage strategy execution across financial, customer, internal-process, and learning-and-growth perspectives. The scorecard makes leading operational change and lagging financial results visible in the same management rhythm.
## Use
- Translating strategy into a balanced set of financial and non-financial measures.
- Building a management system that links objectives, metrics, targets, and initiatives.
- Rebalancing performance reviews away from pure P&L lag measures.
## Avoid
- Strategy is still undefined and there is nothing coherent to measure—frame strategy first with 3cs-model or generic-strategies.
- You need a one-time diagnostic score, not an ongoing management system—use gap-analysis or digital-maturity-assessment.
- You only need financial project evaluation—use npv-and-irr-analysis or cost-benefit-analysis.
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Agreed strategy themes or strategic objectives; current financial, customer, process, and learning metrics (or data sources); initiative inventory and ownership model; target-setting process and review cadence constraints.

**Nice-to-have:** a strategy map, benchmark ranges, unit-level scorecards, and reliable metric lineage.

**When data is thin:** use a provisional measure with a named data-build action; do not fill a perspective with convenient measures unrelated to strategy.
## Procedure
1. **Name the strategic objectives.** Write a limited set of outcome statements under the four perspectives, distinguishing means from ends.
2. **Test the cause chain.** Connect capability and process objectives to customer outcomes and financial results; challenge links with no plausible mechanism.
3. **Choose measures.** For each objective, specify one outcome measure and, where useful, a leading driver; define formula, unit, source, owner, and refresh date.
4. **Set targets and thresholds.** Establish baseline, target, tolerance band, and time horizon, using ambition that matches operating capacity.
5. **Attach initiatives.** Identify the funded work expected to move a measure, its milestone, and its accountable executive.
6. **Run the review cadence.** Review exceptions, causal hypotheses, and corrective decisions monthly or quarterly; cascade only measures that local teams can influence.
## Output Contract
A four-perspective management scorecard with objectives, causal logic, measures, baselines, targets, thresholds, data owners, initiative links, and review cadence. It must allow leaders to diagnose why strategy is or is not moving, not simply display a dashboard of unrelated KPIs.
## Evidence
Use audited finance and operational-system records as observed evidence. Label causal links, target stretch, and expected initiative effects as hypotheses until performance confirms them. Preserve metric definitions across periods. Where a proxy is necessary, show why it is a proxy and the date it will be replaced.
## Checks
- Every measure traces to an objective in one of the four perspectives.
- Leading measures have a credible route to the customer or financial outcome they claim to influence.
- No objective has a crowd of redundant KPIs masking ownership.
- Target dates, baselines, formulas, and owners are all explicit.
- The review agenda records decisions, not only red/amber/green status.
## Failure Modes
- Treating the four perspectives as four mandatory metric buckets instead of a strategy system.
- Cascading a corporate revenue KPI to staff who cannot affect it.
- Calling a target a forecast and never deciding what happens when it is missed.
- Updating figures while leaving broken causal assumptions unquestioned.
- Launching initiatives with no designated measure they are meant to move.
