# RACI Matrix
## Purpose
Decompose work into activities and assign clear Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, and Informed roles so ownership and handoffs are explicit. The matrix exposes overloaded approvals and missing handoffs before they slow a cross-functional effort.
## Use
- Clarifying ownership on cross-functional processes or projects
- Reducing decision lag caused by ambiguous accountability
- Implementing a new structure or process with explicit role assignments
## Avoid
- You need a full org structure redesign—use organizational-design
- You are mapping influence and interest, not decision rights—use stakeholder-mapping
- The process itself is broken and undefined—map it first with process-mapping or sipoc-analysis
## Inputs
**Must-have:**
- Scoped process, project, or decision set to assign
- List of roles or parties involved
- Current understanding of who does the work today
- Decision points that need a single Accountable owner

**Nice-to-have:** service levels, delegation limits, and a swimlane map that shows handoffs.

**When data is thin:** mark provisional assignments in the matrix and validate them in a working session with the people who perform the work.
## Procedure
1. Name the bounded process or project outcome, then break it into decisions, deliverables, and recurring activities at a workable level of detail. Avoid rows such as “manage program” that conceal several choices.
2. List roles rather than individual names across the columns. Capture what happens today and flag activities where two groups believe they approve the same choice.
3. Assign one Responsible role to each executable row and one Accountable role to each decision or deliverable. Deliberately distinguish doing the work from accepting the result.
4. Add Consulted roles only where two-way input changes the work, and Informed roles where one-way notice is sufficient. Test whether consultation happens before the decision, not after it.
5. Review rows for empty cells, duplicate Accountables, excessive consultation, and a role that owns an outcome without authority. Resolve exceptions with the sponsor and record the escalation route.
6. Publish the matrix beside the operating cadence; assign an owner to revise it when scope, vendors, or decision rights change.
## Output Contract
Deliver one readable grid with activities as rows and roles as columns. Each populated cell must contain R, A, C, or I; the grid must define the activity scope, name the single accountable role where applicable, show material consultation points, and annotate unresolved decisions. Include a short decision log for contested assignments and an escalation rule. It should let a new team member determine who acts, who signs off, and who receives notice without inferring authority from job titles.
## Evidence
Treat signed delegations, process observations, and existing approval records as observed evidence. Label reported practice as a claim when teams disagree. State assumptions about temporary roles, vendor authority, and geographic coverage directly in the relevant row. If no one can confirm an assignment, leave the cell marked for decision rather than silently assigning the most senior person.
## Checks
- Every decision requiring approval has exactly one A.
- A role with R has enough authority, capacity, and inputs to execute.
- C is reserved for genuine consultation; a broadcast list belongs in I.
- Rows align with process boundaries and do not duplicate each other.
- The sponsor has accepted exceptions and escalation paths.
## Failure Modes
- Listing people instead of roles, so routine staff changes make the artifact obsolete.
- Giving every leader an A to avoid resolving a conflict.
- Treating C as a veto and creating serial approval queues.
- Omitting an external supplier whose action determines the handoff.
- Building a matrix for activities that were never defined or standardized.
