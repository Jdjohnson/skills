# Discounted Cash Flow Valuation
## Purpose
Estimate intrinsic value by projecting free cash flows and discounting them at an appropriate cost of capital. The model explains value through operating drivers and capital needs rather than through a market multiple alone.
## Use
- Valuing a firm, business unit, or major investment from expected free cash flows
- Deal pricing, fairness, or capital allocation needs an intrinsic-value view
- Multiples alone are unreliable due to unique growth, margin, or capital intensity
## Avoid
- You only need a quick market-relative price check—use valuation-multiples
- The decision is project ranking with standard capital hurdles—use npv-and-irr-analysis or capital-budgeting-framework
- Cash timing for liquidity is the issue, not enterprise value—use cash-flow-forecasting
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Historical financials and driver-based forecast assumptions; Working capital, capex, and free-cash-flow definitions; Discount rate (WACC or appropriate cost of capital); Terminal-value method and explicit forecast horizon.

**Nice-to-have:** management plan, peer operating data, tax-loss schedules, and debt-like items.

**When data is thin:** use wider scenario ranges and identify the one operating driver requiring diligence before a price is set.
## Procedure
1. Normalize historical statements, separating recurring operating performance from acquisitions, one-time charges, and owner-specific items. Select enterprise value using FCFF and WACC, or equity value using FCFE and cost of equity.
2. Forecast revenue from explicit volume, price, retention, capacity, or market-share drivers. Forecast operating margin, taxes, working capital, and capex consistently with those drivers.
3. Calculate annual free cash flow for the explicit period, then establish terminal value using a perpetuity-growth or exit-multiple approach that is economically plausible.
4. Derive the discount rate from capital structure, risk, debt cost, and tax treatment; discount each cash flow and terminal value to the valuation date. Reconcile enterprise value to equity value by adjusting for debt, cash, and other claims.
5. Run sensitivities on growth, margin, reinvestment, and discount rate; reconcile key differences with market multiples and document the value range rather than a single sale price.
## Output Contract
An assessment containing normalized historicals, driver forecasts, free-cash-flow schedule, discount-rate build, terminal-value method, enterprise-to-equity bridge, sensitivity table, and valuation range. It must expose the share of value from the terminal period and state which inputs most change the conclusion.
## Evidence
Audited results, contracts, and debt balances are observed. Forecast conversion, margin, and reinvestment relationships are inferred from operating drivers. Long-run growth, capital structure, and terminal margins are assumptions with external plausibility checks. Missing customer concentration, lease obligations, or tax effects are unknown and should appear as adjustments or valuation limitations.
## Checks
- Cash-flow definition and discount rate match the selected valuation perspective.
- Forecast drivers reconcile to historical trends and physical capacity where relevant.
- Terminal growth is below a sustainable long-run economic growth rate.
- The terminal-value share is disclosed and challenged.
- Enterprise value is bridged to equity with all material non-operating claims.
## Failure Modes
- Discounting FCFF at the cost of equity while describing the result as enterprise value.
- Forecasting fast revenue growth with no working-capital or capex consequence.
- Treating a terminal value that supplies 90 percent of value as a minor assumption.
