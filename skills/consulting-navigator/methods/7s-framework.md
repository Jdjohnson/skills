# 7S Framework
## Purpose
Diagnose organizational effectiveness by testing alignment across Strategy, Structure, Systems, Skills, Staff, Style, and Shared values. It reveals how a sensible strategy is undermined by interactions among hard and soft organizational elements.
## Use
- Diagnosing why strategy and organization are misaligned.
- Stress-testing whether a new strategy can be executed with current S-elements.
- Scoping organization redesign before changing only the org chart.
## Avoid
- You need pure industry attractiveness diagnosis—use five-forces.
- You already know the misalignment and need a change sequence—use kotters-8-step-process or adkar-model.
- The issue is a single process bottleneck, not multi-element fit—use root-cause-analysis or bottleneck-analysis.
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Stated strategy and performance goals; current structure, systems, and formal processes; evidence on skills, staffing, and management style; observed shared values and cultural norms; known friction points between strategy intent and day-to-day behavior.

**Nice-to-have:** Organization charts, decision-rights maps, engagement findings, talent data, meeting observations, and external benchmarks.

**When data is thin:** Use interviews across levels to create provisional hypotheses and identify the two elements requiring validation before recommending redesign.
## Procedure
1. **Set the execution question.** State the strategic outcome at risk and the unit, geography, and time horizon. Avoid diagnosing the whole enterprise when the problem is confined to one division.
2. **Describe the seven elements.** Capture current Strategy, Structure, Systems, Skills, Staff, Style, and Shared values using documents, data, interviews, and observation. Do not assume stated values equal lived values.
3. **Rate element fit to the strategy.** For each element, identify how it enables, constrains, or contradicts the target outcome. Keep evidence beside each judgement.
4. **Trace cross-element tensions.** Look for reinforcing mismatches: a growth strategy with approval-heavy systems, risk-averse style, skills gaps, and incentives for local optimization.
5. **Find leverage points.** Identify changes that resolve several tensions together, then distinguish root alignment moves from visible symptoms such as a new reporting line.
6. **Sequence the interventions.** Recommend an owner, dependency, and leading indicator for each move. Reassess the map after material changes rather than treating the first diagnosis as permanent.
## Output Contract
An alignment diagnosis showing the strategy in scope, current-state evidence for all seven elements, fit and mismatch statements, cross-element causal links, priority interventions, and a sequence for follow-through. It must explain why execution breaks across the system, not merely list seven observations or prescribe a new organization chart.
## Evidence
Policies, systems records, workforce data, and observed routines are evidence. Interview themes are inferences unless corroborated. State assumptions about future strategy and cultural norms explicitly. Preserve disagreements between levels; an executive view and frontline view may be diagnostic evidence, not a discrepancy to average away.
## Checks
- All seven elements are addressed with evidence, including Style and Shared values.
- The analysis links elements to a specific strategic outcome.
- At least two cross-element tensions are demonstrated, not asserted.
- Recommendations alter the pattern of misalignment rather than one isolated box.
- Owners and indicators make the diagnosis actionable.
## Failure Modes
- Redrawing Structure while leaving decision systems and leadership behavior untouched.
- Treating stated values posters as evidence of shared values.
- Scoring all seven elements independently and missing their interactions.
- Diagnosing “culture” as a vague explanation when a policy or incentive is observable.
- Applying one enterprise-wide remedy to a localized execution issue.
