# Performance Management System
## Purpose
Design and operate the recurring system of goals, feedback, evaluation, and consequences that measures and improves employee performance. It specifies how the employee cycle works in practice, from goal setting through calibration and rewards, rather than producing a corporate KPI map or a one-off feedback event.
## Use
- Building or overhauling how goals, feedback, and ratings actually run
- Performance differentiation and coaching are weak or inconsistent
- Linking individual and team objectives to strategy needs a durable system
## Avoid
- You only need a one-time goal cascade for strategy deployment—use hoshin-kanri or balanced-scorecard
- The problem is unclear roles not weak performance cycles—use raci-matrix or organizational-design first
- You need developmental feedback without system redesign—use 360-degree-feedback
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Strategy and priority outcomes to cascade into goals; current review, rating, and reward practices; role families and competency or skill expectations; manager capability and tool constraints; legal and compliance requirements for evaluation and documentation.

**Nice-to-have:** prior rating distributions, employee listening results, promotion and attrition patterns, and union or country-specific requirements.

**When data is thin:** interview managers and employees across two role families, then pilot a minimum viable cycle before changing pay or promotion consequences.
## Procedure
1. **Trace strategy to roles.** Translate a small number of enterprise outcomes into team contributions and role-level goals; distinguish results, behaviors, and development commitments. Output: goal architecture and examples by role family.
2. **Map the current employee journey.** Follow a representative employee from annual planning through check-ins, review, calibration, reward, and appeal. Mark handoffs, systems, delays, and discretionary decisions. Output: current-cycle service blueprint.
3. **Design the recurring cadence.** Set goal timing, check-in frequency, evidence capture, rating scale, review windows, and late-change rules. Specify what a manager must do versus what HR administers. Output: cycle calendar and operating rules.
4. **Build differentiation controls.** Define performance standards, rating anchors, calibration panels, challenge routes, and treatment of high, solid, and under-performance. Output: calibration protocol and decision-rights map.
5. **Enable managers and employees.** Provide conversation guides, goal examples, workflow prompts, training, and a support route for difficult cases. Output: adoption package and capability plan.
6. **Pilot, measure, and refine.** Run one cycle in selected populations; test completion, goal quality, rating dispersion, employee understanding, and adverse-impact signals before broader release. Output: release decision and revision backlog.
## Output Contract
An operating design containing the goal cascade, recurring calendar, rating architecture, calibration and appeal rules, role-specific responsibilities, tool requirements, manager enablement, and rollout measures. It must show how rewards, development, and corrective action connect to evaluated performance, while separating policy choices from tested facts.
## Evidence
Treat completed check-ins, ratings, promotion records, and policy documents as observed. Treat a correlation between ratings and exits as an inference, not proof of cause. Record assumptions about feasible manager time, local labor rules, and acceptable rating distributions. Where demographic or role-level data are unavailable, label fairness checks as incomplete and do not use the pilot to make irreversible compensation decisions.
## Checks
- Every role can state no more than a workable number of measurable goals and behavioral expectations.
- Rating anchors describe observable performance, not personality or effort alone.
- Calibration participants see comparable evidence and a documented reason for overrides.
- Check-in, review, and appeal dates work with payroll, promotion, and legal calendars.
- Pilot results include completion, perceived fairness, goal quality, and manager burden measures.
## Failure Modes
- Cascading so many objectives that employees cannot prioritize daily work.
- Treating calibration as forced ranking without comparable evidence.
- Making ratings consequential before managers can give useful feedback.
- Letting goals remain fixed after a legitimate strategy change.
- Measuring form completion while ignoring the quality of coaching conversations.
