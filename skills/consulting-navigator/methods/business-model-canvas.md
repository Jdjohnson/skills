# Business Model Canvas
## Purpose
Map how an organization creates, delivers, and captures value across nine interdependent business-model building blocks. It makes the commercial logic visible in one shared view and turns vague claims into testable hypotheses.
## Use
- Framing or redesigning how value is created, delivered, and captured
- Aligning a cross-functional team on a single business-model hypothesis
- Comparing alternative models before market entry, pivot, or fundraising
## Avoid
- You only need the customer value side sharpened—use value-proposition-canvas first
- You need industry structure or competitive position, not a firm model—use five-forces or generic-strategies
- The question is detailed financial forecast quality—use cash-flow-forecasting or business-case methods after the canvas
## Inputs
**Must-have:**

- Target customer segments and jobs or needs
- Current or proposed value proposition and offerings
- Rough view of channels, relationships, and revenue streams
- Key activities, resources, partners, and major cost drivers

**Nice-to-have:** segment economics, customer interview evidence, conversion data, competitive alternatives, and a current operating model.

**When data is thin:** use sticky notes or a draft board, color-code hypotheses, and select the few assumptions whose failure would invalidate the model.
## Procedure
1. **Set the model boundary.** Decide whether the canvas describes the current enterprise, one product, a geography, or a proposed venture. State the customer and time horizon; mixing several businesses on one sheet defeats comparison. Output: scoped canvas title.
2. **Fill the customer-facing blocks first.** Specify customer segments, their value proposition, channels, and relationship model. For each segment, identify the job served and the evidence that it will pay or stay. Output: demand-side hypothesis set.
3. **Complete the economic engine.** Define revenue streams, price logic, and major cost structure. Link each revenue stream to a segment and each cost to the activity or resource that creates it. Output: preliminary unit-economics logic.
4. **Complete the delivery system.** Add key activities, key resources, and key partners, then draw the dependencies that make the promise deliverable. Surface where a partner controls a critical customer experience or margin pool. Output: operating-model hypothesis set.
5. **Stress the connections and choose experiments.** Look for orphan blocks, such as a channel with no acquisition cost or a segment with no revenue mechanism. Compare alternatives on the same board and turn the riskiest links into tests. Output: revised canvas and experiment priorities.
## Output Contract
Deliver one readable nine-block canvas, a legend distinguishing facts from hypotheses, and an assumption register with owner, evidence needed, and next test. The artifact must show a coherent connection from a named segment through value proposition and channel to revenue, activities, resources, partners, and costs; it is not a feature inventory.
## Evidence
Customer interviews, signed contracts, usage data, and invoices support observed claims. Treat market estimates, willingness-to-pay statements, and partner availability as inferred until validated. Record the date and segment behind each claim; do not let a founder's intuition appear as market evidence. If economics are rough, use ranges and identify the break-even driver rather than presenting a false complete forecast.
## Checks
- All nine blocks are populated at the same scope and horizon.
- Each revenue stream has a customer, price mechanism, and delivery path.
- Major activities and resources explain how the stated value proposition is delivered.
- Distinct segments are not collapsed when their channel or economics differ.
- The top assumptions have experiments, not just debate topics.
## Failure Modes
- Filling blocks with generic labels such as “customers” or “digital marketing.”
- Treating partners as decoration while ignoring dependency and bargaining power.
- Listing every possible segment rather than choosing a testable priority segment.
- Calling projected revenue a validated stream before any pricing evidence exists.
- Using the canvas as a one-time workshop poster with no experiment follow-through.
