# Economic Value Added
## Purpose
Measure residual wealth created after charging operating profit for the full cost of invested capital. It shows whether a unit's earnings exceed the price of capital consumed.
## Use
- You need a residual-income measure of whether operations cover the cost of capital.
- Performance management or incentives should penalize excess capital use.
- Comparing units or periods on value creation, not just accounting profit.
## Avoid
- You want a multi-perspective strategy scorecard, not a capital-charge profit metric—use balanced-scorecard.
- You need standard ratio diagnostics without economic profit adjustments—use financial-ratio-analysis.
- You are valuing a company for a transaction—use discounted-cash-flow-valuation.
## Inputs
**Must-have:**
- NOPAT or adjusted operating profit by unit/period.
- Invested capital definition and balance-sheet adjustments.
- Cost of capital (often WACC) applied as capital charge.
- Accounting adjustment policy (leases, R&D, goodwill, etc.).

**Nice-to-have:** capital budget approvals, peer return data, incentive-plan rules, and several prior periods.

**When data is thin:** start with reported operating profit and disclosed capital, label the result preliminary, and show how each unresolved adjustment could change the sign.
## Procedure
1. **Set the economic boundary.** Choose the entity, period, currency, and capital providers. Reconcile operating profit to management accounts so allocations and discontinued operations are not silently mixed. Output: scope and reconciliation sheet.
2. **Normalize operating profit.** Begin with EBIT, remove financing effects and unusual gains or losses, apply taxes, and record approved accounting adjustments. Capitalize long-lived development spending only with a consistent amortization policy. Output: adjusted NOPAT bridge.
3. **Build invested capital.** Include operating working capital and productive assets; exclude non-operating cash and financing balances. Apply the matching adjustment policy, such as adding unamortized development costs. Output: invested-capital schedule.
4. **Calculate the capital charge.** Multiply opening or average invested capital by the approved cost of capital for the period. Use a unit-specific rate only where risk genuinely differs and governance has approved it. Output: capital-charge ledger.
5. **Derive and interpret the result.** Calculate adjusted NOPAT minus capital charge, then compare the absolute amount, EVA margin, and change from prior period. Separate profit improvement from capital release so management can see the mechanism. Output: unit scorecard and trend bridge.
6. **Turn findings into choices.** Identify investments to accelerate, assets to redeploy, and businesses whose operating improvements cannot cover their capital burden. Output: action list tied to owners and decision dates.
## Output Contract
An EVA scorecard by business unit and period containing the NOPAT bridge, invested-capital schedule, applied capital rate, capital charge, residual value, prior-period comparison, and actions. It must make every adjustment auditable and distinguish an operating-profit improvement from a change caused by less capital employed.
## Evidence
Treat ledger balances, tax rates, and investment records as observed. Mark capitalization lives, capital-rate choices, and allocations as assumptions. Show both profit and capital effects for every adjustment. Where support is weak, provide reported and adjusted views.
## Checks
- The NOPAT bridge reconciles to reported EBIT and tax treatment is explicit.
- Each capital adjustment has a matching profit treatment where applicable.
- Capital charge uses the same period basis as the capital balance.
- Units use comparable adjustment rules before their scores are ranked.
- A sensitivity shows the effect of a one-point change in the capital rate.
## Failure Modes
- Calling EBIT minus interest EVA, which double-counts financing logic.
- Capitalizing development expense in capital but never amortizing it through profit.
- Rewarding a unit for selling necessary productive assets shortly before measurement.
- Applying one corporate capital rate to a high-risk venture without noting the distortion.
- Comparing a newly acquired business with legacy units before purchase-accounting treatment is aligned.
