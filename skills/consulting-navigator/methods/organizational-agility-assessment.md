# Organizational Agility Assessment
## Purpose
Evaluate the organization's ability to sense change, decide quickly, and reconfigure structure, processes, and talent without losing control. It identifies constraints that make a strategy pivot slow or fragile.
## Use
- Diagnosing why the firm cannot adapt as fast as strategy requires
- Baselining before an agile or operating-model transformation
- Comparing agility across business units or geographies
## Avoid
- You need a full structural redesign of roles and reporting lines—use organizational-design or star-model
- The bottleneck is pure process inefficiency rather than adaptive capacity—use value-stream-mapping or business-process-reengineering
- You are already mid-agile rollout and need execution mechanics—use agile-transformation-framework or scrum-framework
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Current org structure, decision rights, and approval paths; evidence on cycle times for decisions, product, and resource reallocation; samples of recent change or pivot attempts and outcomes; leadership and employee views on barriers to speed; strategy or market context that demands faster adaptation.

**Nice-to-have:** Portfolio-reallocation records, team network data, planning calendars, and an outside comparison group using similar complexity.

**When data is thin:** Sample a few recent pivots from signal to resource movement and score only dimensions supported by evidence; an unscored dimension is more honest than a composite number built from opinions.
## Procedure
1. **Set the adaptive challenge.** Specify the market shift or strategic demand, including required response time. Output: agility question and time horizon.
2. **Define the assessment dimensions.** Establish indicators for sensing, decision flow, resource mobility, team reconfiguration, learning, and guardrails. Output: scored rubric with behavioral anchors.
3. **Trace recent response episodes.** Follow several actual pivots from first signal through approval, funding, staffing, launch, and learning. Output: end-to-end response case files.
4. **Score patterns and locate constraints.** Rate each dimension by evidence, compare units where useful, and distinguish an isolated heroic response from a repeatable capability. Output: agility profile and constraint map.
5. **Prioritize the capability roadmap.** Select interventions that remove the limiting constraint, with an owner, leading measure, and sequencing logic. Output: improvement roadmap.
## Output Contract
Deliver an agility gap profile showing each capability, rating evidence, response-time examples, variation by unit, and the few constraints that most restrict adaptation. The accompanying roadmap states what to change in governance, talent deployment, planning, or operating routines and how improvement will be measured.
## Evidence
Use dated decision records, staffing moves, backlog history, and episode interviews as evidence. Keep perceptions as perceptions; a leadership claim that approvals are fast does not outweigh elapsed-time data. State the response window assumed by the strategy, since speed without an external demand is not a virtue. Do not collapse unlike units into one score without explaining their operating context.
## Checks
- Ratings are anchored in actual response episodes, not an aspirational agile vocabulary survey.
- Sensing, deciding, and reconfiguring are assessed separately; a fast meeting is not proof of adaptability.
- The profile shows distribution across units rather than concealing weak pockets in an average.
- Each roadmap action addresses a named bottleneck and has a leading indicator such as approval days or redeployment time.
- Governance controls that must remain are treated as design constraints, not automatically as bureaucracy.
## Failure Modes
- Calling a business agile because it runs daily stand-ups while capital allocation remains annual.
- Giving a high score to a one-off crisis response driven by executive intervention.
- Recommending teams be “more empowered” without changing the authority or budget boundary that blocks them.
- Treating organizational speed as the goal when the real failure is poor sensing of customer or market change.
