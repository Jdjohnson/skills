# BCG Matrix
## Purpose
Prioritize multi-business portfolio moves by placing units on relative market share and market growth into Stars, Cash Cows, Question Marks, and Dogs. It provides a first-cut view of which businesses consume cash, generate it, or need an explicit exit decision.
## Use
- Prioritizing investment, harvest, and divest actions across a multi-unit portfolio.
- Explaining cash generation vs cash use with relative market share and market growth.
- Creating a first-cut portfolio strategy before multi-factor scoring.
## Avoid
- You need multi-factor industry attractiveness and competitive strength—use ge-mckinsey-matrix.
- A single business needs positioning choice, not portfolio allocation—use generic-strategies.
- Market boundaries and share data are too weak to place units—gather evidence first via market-sizing or competitive-benchmarking.
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Business units or product lines with clear market definitions; market growth rates for each unit's market; relative market share vs largest competitor; cash flow or investment needs by unit (directional).

**Nice-to-have:** share trend, capital intensity, synergies, market maturity forecast, and management capacity.

**When data is thin:** show a placement range or confidence marker; never create false quadrants from incompatible market definitions.
## Procedure
1. **Set the portfolio boundary.** Choose genuinely independent units or product lines and state the market for each; do not compare shares from unrelated geographies.
2. **Calculate the axes.** Divide the unit's share by the largest rival's share and calculate relevant market growth over a consistent period.
3. **Choose the cutoffs.** State the growth threshold and the relative-share midpoint before viewing results; sensitivity-test units near either line.
4. **Plot bubbles.** Place each unit by the two axes and size its bubble by revenue, capital employed, or cash flow, consistently.
5. **Interpret cash role.** Identify Stars needing investment, Cash Cows funding the portfolio, Question Marks requiring a win-or-exit thesis, and Dogs requiring a harvest, sell, or strategic exception decision.
6. **Make resource choices.** Add investment bands, owners, and review dates; do not let the quadrant substitute for a business case.
## Output Contract
A four-cell portfolio chart with market definitions, share and growth calculations, bubble-size basis, boundary assumptions, cash-role interpretation, and a decision for every unit. The artifact must distinguish a Question Mark with a credible path to share leadership from one that should be exited.
## Evidence
Use reported market shares and reputable market-size estimates as observed evidence. Mark market boundaries, forecast growth, and share proxies as assumptions. Record whether share includes captive channels or only addressable demand. Present a near-boundary unit in both plausible cells when measurement error can flip its treatment.
## Checks
- Each unit's market and largest competitor are defined consistently.
- Both axes use the same geography and period for a given unit.
- Bubble size is labeled and does not obscure a loss-making cash role.
- Threshold changes do not silently reverse recommendations.
- Every quadrant label leads to an explicit capital or exit decision.
## Failure Modes
- Using absolute share, which hides whether a unit leads its closest rival.
- Calling a large low-growth business a Star because it has high revenue.
- Treating all Dogs as automatic disposals despite strategic linkage or cash generation.
- Funding every Question Mark without a time-bound share-gain thesis.
- Mixing mature-market growth with an emerging-market share denominator.
