# ADKAR Model
## Purpose
Diagnose and close individual adoption gaps across Awareness, Desire, Knowledge, Ability, and Reinforcement so a defined change actually sticks. It locates the first missing condition for each audience rather than prescribing training to everyone.
## Use
- Diagnosing why a defined change is not sticking at the individual and team level
- Building a person-level adoption plan after the change case and design are already set
- Sequencing enablement so awareness and desire are secured before skill-building
## Avoid
- You need an enterprise-wide mobilization sequence rather than individual adoption gaps—use kotters-8-step-process
- The issue is structural org design, not personal transition readiness—use organizational-design
- You only need a psychological stages view of letting go and starting anew—use bridges-transition-model
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Clear statement of the change and desired post-change behaviors; target audience segments (roles, sites, or teams) affected by the change; evidence of current awareness, desire, knowledge, ability, and reinforcement gaps; list of sponsors, people managers, and enablement owners per segment.

**Nice-to-have:** Adoption telemetry, help-desk themes, manager observations, and baseline proficiency measures.

**When data is thin:** Interview a deliberately varied sample of affected roles, score their confidence separately from their behavior, and treat the segment diagnosis as preliminary.
## Procedure
1. **Name the required behavior.** Express the change in observable terms: for example, a supervisor approves work in the new system rather than sending a spreadsheet.
2. **Segment the affected population.** Group people by role, location, exposure, manager, or workflow; do not average executives and frontline users into one score.
3. **Assess the five conditions in order.** Gather evidence for awareness of why change is needed, desire to participate, knowledge of how to act, ability to perform under real conditions, and reinforcement after launch.
4. **Find the first broken link.** A team unable to perform may lack practice, but it may also lack desire. Target the earliest weak condition because downstream interventions rarely repair an upstream gap.
5. **Design targeted interventions.** Match sponsorship messages to awareness, local incentives and listening to desire, training and job aids to knowledge, coached practice to ability, and manager routines or measures to reinforcement.
6. **Measure behavior and revisit.** Track adoption by segment, reassess the weak condition after intervention, and escalate manager or system barriers that people cannot solve themselves.
## Output Contract
Provide a segment-level adoption diagnosis showing the desired behavior, evidence for each of the five conditions, the first material gap, the intervention, accountable sponsor or manager, timing, and adoption measure. The output should make a manager able to act on a specific barrier, not describe organization-wide change activity in general.
## Evidence
Observed evidence includes system use, completion records, direct observation, and attendance. Survey confidence is reported as perception, not proof of ability. Mark causal claims—such as “a bonus will create desire”—as assumptions and test them. Protect anonymity where interview comments could be attributed to a manager; report patterns by segment instead of exposing individuals.
## Checks
- Each segment has one observable post-change behavior.
- Awareness, desire, knowledge, ability, and reinforcement are assessed separately.
- Interventions address the earliest significant gap, not the most convenient program.
- Ability is evidenced in work conditions, not only course completion.
- Reinforcement names a continuing manager, system cue, or consequence.
## Failure Modes
- Launching training before people understand why their current behavior must change.
- Treating a favorable pulse-survey score as proof that staff can execute a new workflow.
- Diagnosing resistance as a communications issue when tools, time, or incentives make the behavior impossible.
- Measuring launch attendance once and never checking whether managers reinforce the change.
