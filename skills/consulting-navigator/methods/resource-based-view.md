# Resource-Based View
## Purpose
Diagnose which firm resources and capabilities meet VRIO tests and can sustain competitive advantage. The assessment separates strengths that are merely useful today from resource bundles competitors cannot readily reproduce.
## Use
- Auditing which resources and capabilities can sustain competitive advantage (valuable, rare, inimitable, organized)
- Explaining persistent performance differences not explained by industry structure alone
- Prioritizing capability investments, protection, or divestment
## Avoid
- The question is industry attractiveness or power structure—use five-forces
- You need activity-level cost and linkage economics inside the firm—use value-chain-analysis
- You need a quick external-plus-internal workshop summary—use swot-analysis only as a starting frame, then deepen with RBV
## Inputs
**Must-have:**
- Inventory of candidate resources and capabilities (tangible, intangible, organizational)
- Evidence on value creation, scarcity, imitability/substitutability, and organizational support (VRIO tests)
- Performance outcomes linked to claimed advantages
- Competitive context for what rivals can access or copy

**Nice-to-have:** competitor hiring patterns, patents and contracts, customer switching data, and investment history.

**When data is thin:** score evidence confidence separately from the VRIO judgment and prioritize research on the claims most likely to drive investment.
## Procedure
1. Inventory resource bundles, not just assets: combine people, routines, data, relationships, governance, and complementary technology where they create an outcome together.
2. State the customer or economic value each bundle creates and link it to an observable performance result. Remove items that are inputs but have no plausible mechanism for value.
3. Test rarity against actual competitors and substitutes, including what rivals can buy, hire, license, or access through partners.
4. Examine imitation barriers: causal ambiguity, accumulated learning, social complexity, legal protection, path dependence, and switching costs. Distinguish expensive copying from impossible copying.
5. Test organization: incentives, decision rights, processes, capital, and leadership attention must enable the firm to capture the resource’s value.
6. Classify each bundle as competitive parity, temporary advantage, unused advantage, or potentially sustained advantage. Recommend protect, scale, repair organization, partner, or exit actions with evidence confidence.
## Output Contract
Provide a VRIO assessment table for each material resource bundle. It must describe the bundle, performance mechanism, evidence for value/rarity/imitation/organization, confidence, competitive classification, and recommended action. Add a capability investment agenda that identifies what should be protected, built, organized differently, or stopped. The deliverable is an internal advantage assessment, not a list of corporate values or an activity-cost map.
## Evidence
Treat verified performance outcomes, contracts, patents, retention data, and competitor facts as observed. Mark claims about rival capability, future substitutes, and causal mechanisms as inferred. State the market boundary used for scarcity and any assumption that a resource can be transferred across businesses. Do not let confidential self-descriptions substitute for evidence that the organization actually captures value.
## Checks
- Each row names a coherent bundle and a measurable value mechanism.
- Rarity is tested against relevant competitors and substitutes, not internal pride.
- Inimitability has a stated barrier rather than a vague claim of uniqueness.
- Organization is assessed independently from resource quality.
- Classifications lead to differentiated allocation choices.
## Failure Modes
- Calling every capable employee group “rare” without a competitor comparison.
- Treating a patent as advantage when customers do not value the protected feature.
- Assessing a brand, data set, and operating model separately when their combination creates the advantage.
- Confusing temporary lead time with a durable imitation barrier.
- Recommending investment in a valuable capability while ignoring missing incentives or ownership.
