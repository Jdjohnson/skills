# McKinsey Influence Model
## Purpose
Design a behavior-change influence plan by deliberately covering understanding, reinforcement, skill building, and role modeling. It diagnoses why a specific behavior is not taking hold, then coordinates four levers rather than treating communication as the whole intervention.
## Use
- Designing an influence plan that balances narrative, incentives, skills, and role modeling
- Auditing a lagging transformation for missing building blocks rather than more messaging alone
- Aligning HR, ops, and leaders on what must change in systems and examples—not only in slides
## Avoid
- You need structural alignment of strategy, systems, and skills first—use 7s-framework
- The need is a person-level awareness-to-reinforcement path—use adkar-model
- You only need a simple three-stage sponsor narrative—use lewins-change-model
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Target behaviors and decisions the change requires day to day; current formal incentives, KPIs, and informal recognition patterns; skill and confidence gaps relative to the new way of working; map of visible leaders and peer influencers by cohort; existing change narrative and evidence of whether it creates conviction.

**Nice-to-have:** adoption data by cohort, examples of workarounds, and upcoming policy or system changes that could reinforce the new behavior.

**When data is thin:** interview a deliberately mixed set of adopters and holdouts. Treat the lever diagnosis as provisional until a short behavior observation or pulse check confirms it.
## Procedure
1. **Specify the observable behavior.** Write who must do what, in which moment, and what old action must stop. Avoid abstractions such as “embrace digital.”
2. **Segment the adoption problem.** Compare cohorts that have adopted with those that have not. Identify whether the barrier is belief, consequence, ability, or visible example; several barriers may coexist.
3. **Build the four-lever matrix.** For conviction, create a locally credible case for change. For reinforcement, alter measures, consequences, and recognition. For skills, define practice, coaching, and tools. For role modeling, name leaders and peer exemplars who will demonstrate the behavior in real routines.
4. **Check interactions and contradictions.** Remove messages that promise autonomy while a KPI rewards the old output, or training that asks for a behavior a supervisor does not display. Assign one accountable owner and a date to each intervention.
5. **Pilot and adapt.** Test the complete lever combination with one cohort, observe behavior rather than attendance, and revise the matrix before scaling.
## Output Contract
A behavior influence plan with a behavior definition, cohort-by-cohort barrier diagnosis, four-lever intervention matrix, named owners, dates, and adoption measures. It must identify which mechanism changes daily choices and which evidence will show that change, not merely list communications or training events.
## Evidence
Tag behavioral evidence as observed when it comes from system use, meeting observation, or documented choices. Label interviews and survey patterns as inferred. State assumptions about incentives, manager discretion, and cohort differences. Do not infer conviction from message reach: an opened email is not a changed belief. Where the old behavior has no measurable trace, define a sampling protocol before claiming improvement.
## Checks
- The desired behavior is observable and tied to a recurring decision point.
- Every diagnosed barrier has at least one corresponding lever.
- The incentive system does not reward the displaced behavior.
- Role models have specific routines to demonstrate, not only sponsor titles.
- Pilot measures include behavior frequency or quality, not training completion alone.
## Failure Modes
- Launching an inspiring narrative while sales targets still reward the prior behavior.
- Treating a skills deficit as resistance and escalating communications.
- Naming executives as role models who never encounter the affected workflow.
- Funding training without protected time to practice the new routine.
- Measuring event attendance instead of adoption at the decision point.
