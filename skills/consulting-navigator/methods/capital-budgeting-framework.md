# Capital Budgeting Framework
## Purpose
Structure how the enterprise selects, funds, sequences, and governs long-term capital investments as a portfolio. It makes scarce capital allocation and post-approval accountability explicit, rather than evaluating a project in isolation.
## Use
- You need a repeatable process to propose, rank, approve, and track multi-year capital investments
- Capex demand exceeds available capital and trade-offs must be explicit
- Board or finance requires stage gates, hurdles, and post-investment review
## Avoid
- You only need to value one already-scoped project—use npv-and-irr-analysis or discounted-cash-flow-valuation
- The decision is a binary public-policy style cost vs benefit without a capital portfolio—use cost-benefit-analysis
- You are measuring enterprise intrinsic value, not allocating capex—use discounted-cash-flow-valuation
## Inputs
**Must-have:**

- Investment proposals with cash-flow estimates and strategic rationale
- Capital budget envelope and financing constraints
- Hurdle rates, risk categories, and approval authorities
- Prior-year project outcomes for calibration

**Nice-to-have:** capacity plans, asset-condition data, regulatory deadlines, benefit-owner commitments, and alternative phasing options.

**When data is thin:** reserve funds for mandatory work, subject discretionary proposals to a discovery gate, and avoid pretending an early estimate is approval-grade.
## Procedure
1. **Set portfolio rules.** Define capital categories, minimum business-case content, hurdle rates, approval thresholds, and which proposals are mandatory versus discretionary. Output: investment-policy calendar.
2. **Normalize proposals.** Require each sponsor to state timing of cash outflows, benefits, risks, strategic link, operating consequences, and benefit owner in a common format. Output: comparable investment register.
3. **Screen and appraise.** Check feasibility and compliance, then use NPV, IRR, payback, risk, and strategic fit appropriate to the category. Challenge double-counted benefits and proposal optimism with prior outcomes. Output: scored candidate portfolio.
4. **Allocate and sequence.** Rank alternatives within the budget envelope while honoring dependencies, execution capacity, and funding windows. Consider deferral, staged release, or substitution; a positive-NPV project can still lose when capital is constrained. Output: funded portfolio and reserve list.
5. **Govern through gates and realization.** Release funds by evidence-based stages, reforecast cost and benefits, stop or reshape failing projects, and conduct post-investment review. Output: approvals, gate criteria, and benefits scorecard.
## Output Contract
The capital plan identifies each proposed investment's category, cash profile, appraisal result, strategic rationale, rank, funding decision, gate, owner, and expected benefit. It also presents the total envelope, unfunded alternatives, sequencing dependencies, and review cadence. The result is a portfolio allocation decision, not a spreadsheet containing a single IRR.
## Evidence
Use approved budgets, vendor quotes, asset records, historical delivery results, and contractual obligations as observed inputs. Mark volume growth, cost savings, residual value, and implementation duration as assumptions with accountable owners. Apply a consistent nominal or real discount-rate basis. Do not offset a project's risk by simply raising its hurdle rate without explaining why the risk is not modeled in cash flows.
## Checks
- Mandatory, replacement, compliance, and growth investments are comparable but visibly categorized.
- Cash outlays and benefits have dates, not only totals.
- Ranking reflects capital and execution constraints across the entire portfolio.
- Gate criteria can stop spending before the next material commitment.
- Post-investment review compares realized benefits with the approved case.
## Failure Modes
- Funding every positive-NPV proposal while ignoring the capital envelope.
- Ranking strategic initiatives solely by short payback.
- Allowing sponsors to change benefit definitions after approval.
- Treating sunk spend as a reason to pass a failed gate.
- Omitting maintenance projects until asset failure turns them into emergencies.
