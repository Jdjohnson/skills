# Pilot and Scale Methodology
## Purpose
Plan and govern a bounded pilot then controlled scale-up so transformation is proven before enterprise roll-out. It tests an already defined operating change for transferability and creates scale gates, rather than discovering product-market fit or approving a development portfolio.
## Use
- Proving a change concept in a bounded cohort before enterprise commitment
- Designing exit criteria, learning loops, and scale gates for a transformation
- Reducing big-bang risk when operating models, tech, or behaviors are unproven
## Avoid
- You are still searching for product-market fit rather than operationalizing known change—use lean-startup or minimum-viable-product
- The change is a one-time project with no multi-site replication need—use standard project delivery such as pmbok-framework
- You only need a stage-gate for product investment decisions—use stage-gate-process
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Defined pilot scope, sites/cohorts, and non-goals; success metrics and kill/scale thresholds agreed with sponsors; baseline performance and constraint profile of pilot units; resource and change capacity available for scale waves; mechanism to capture playbook, defects, and adaptation rules from the pilot.

**Nice-to-have:** representative site archetypes, union or regulatory constraints, implementation partner capacity, and a benefits baseline across the eventual population.

**When data is thin:** choose a deliberately representative pilot, make provisional thresholds visible, and use the pilot to close the most decision-critical evidence gaps.
## Procedure
1. **Define the replicable change package.** Freeze the core process, technology configuration, roles, training, and non-negotiables to be tested; identify what sites may adapt. Output: pilot package and adaptation rules.
2. **Select pilot units and comparators.** Choose units that expose meaningful constraints, not only friendly volunteers, and retain a baseline or comparison group where practical. Output: selection rationale and baseline plan.
3. **Set success, kill, and readiness criteria.** Establish outcome, adoption, cost, safety, and implementation measures with thresholds that distinguish local success from scale readiness. Output: signed gate scorecard.
4. **Run the bounded pilot.** Train users, deploy the package, track defects and workarounds, and hold short learning reviews without changing measures midstream. Output: pilot evidence log.
5. **Codify the playbook.** Separate core elements that caused results from local adaptations, estimate per-wave resource needs, and close repeatable defects. Output: scale-ready playbook.
6. **Expand by governed waves.** Decide stop, redesign, extend, or scale at each gate; sequence sites by readiness and preserve feedback channels between waves. Output: wave plan and governance cadence.
## Output Contract
A pilot-and-scale plan containing scope, target cohorts, baseline, hypothesis, success and kill thresholds, measurement calendar, governance gates, learning log, reusable playbook, site-readiness criteria, and wave sequence. It must make clear what evidence permits expansion and what result requires redesign or termination.
## Evidence
Capture observed baseline and in-pilot results separately. Label transferability claims as inferences until a second site validates them. State assumptions about trainer capacity, local process variation, and technology readiness. Do not average away a safety, adoption, or equity failure because the aggregate KPI improved. When outcomes are seasonal, compare against an appropriate period rather than declaring victory after a convenient week.
## Checks
- Pilot participants reflect the operating conditions expected at scale.
- Thresholds are agreed before results are visible and include leading adoption measures.
- Core design, local adaptation, defect, and workaround are recorded in different fields.
- Every scale wave has capacity, readiness, rollback, and escalation plans.
- Gate decisions cite evidence and a named accountable sponsor.
## Failure Modes
- Choosing an enthusiastic site whose conditions cannot be reproduced elsewhere.
- Moving the success threshold after an unfavorable result.
- Scaling a charismatic local workaround instead of the actual operating package.
- Treating a pilot launch as proof while adoption has not stabilized.
- Sending waves faster than training, support, or defect repair capacity.
