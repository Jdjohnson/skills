# AI Strategy & Roadmap
## Purpose
Define where and how the enterprise will create value with AI and sequence the capability, use-case, and investment roadmap. It converts scattered experiments into a portfolio with explicit economic logic, delivery dependencies, and funding choices.
## Use
- Setting enterprise AI ambition, priority use cases, and capability build sequence
- Turning fragmented pilots into a funded multi-horizon roadmap
- Aligning data, talent, platform, and governance investments to business outcomes
## Avoid
- You only need ethical policy and model oversight design—use ai-ethics-and-governance
- Current-state digital capability is unknown—run digital-maturity-assessment first
- The need is pure tech delivery process, not portfolio strategy—use sdlc-methodology or mlops-lifecycle
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Business priorities and value hypotheses for AI; current data, platform, and talent baseline; inventory of existing AI pilots and production models; constraints on risk appetite, regulation, and budget.

**Nice-to-have:** Unit economics, customer research, process volumes, vendor options, architecture roadmaps, and change capacity.

**When data is thin:** Use discovery funding and time-boxed value tests; keep portfolio claims as ranges and avoid a platform build justified only by vague innovation ambition.
## Procedure
1. **Anchor to strategic outcomes.** Translate enterprise priorities into measurable opportunity areas such as lower service cost, improved conversion, reduced loss, or faster expert work. Define the horizon and investment envelope.
2. **Inventory and classify use cases.** Capture existing and proposed cases with process owner, user, decision, data need, value mechanism, maturity, and risk. Consolidate duplicate pilots pursuing the same outcome.
3. **Estimate value and feasibility.** Assess each case’s benefit range, adoption dependency, data readiness, integration effort, model fit, and regulatory exposure. Separate a compelling business problem from an available technology.
4. **Choose strategic plays.** Decide which capabilities are shared platforms, which use cases are differentiating, and where to build, buy, partner, or stop. Establish principles for reusable data, model operations, and responsible deployment.
5. **Sequence the roadmap.** Place experiments, foundational data work, production releases, talent development, and governance milestones across near, medium, and longer horizons according to dependency and learning value.
6. **Fund and govern the portfolio.** Assign executive sponsors, product owners, budgets, stage gates, benefit measures, and a cadence for reallocating funds as evidence changes.
## Output Contract
Provide an AI investment roadmap with strategic outcomes, a use-case portfolio, value and feasibility assessments, build-buy-partner choices, shared capability workstreams, funding by horizon, owners, dependencies, governance gates, and benefit measures. It must identify which bets to start, scale, defer, or stop; it is not merely a list of model ideas.
## Evidence
Observed evidence includes process volumes, cost baselines, customer behavior, existing pilot results, data inventories, and delivery capacity. Benefits, adoption rates, and model performance before a test are assumptions expressed as ranges. Do not sum overlapping benefits from several use cases that improve the same process. Maintain a trace from each strategic outcome to its underlying economic driver.
## Checks
- Every priority use case has a named outcome owner and a measurable value mechanism.
- Portfolio value is adjusted for feasibility, risk, adoption, and dependency, not ranked by enthusiasm.
- Shared data and platform investments have more than one credible consuming case or a stated strategic reason.
- Roadmap order reflects prerequisites rather than only executive preference.
- Stop or pivot criteria are funded alongside scale criteria.
## Failure Modes
- Treating a vendor demonstration as evidence of a high-value enterprise use case.
- Funding a central platform before identifying near-term cases that will use it.
- Counting labor savings while assuming unchanged work volumes and no adoption effort.
- Allowing every pilot to continue without a portfolio decision to scale, redesign, or stop.
