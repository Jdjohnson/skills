# CRM Strategy
## Purpose
Design the CRM operating and technology model that unifies customer data and interactions across sales, service, and marketing. It connects customer value choices to processes, information, channels, and performance management rather than starting with software selection.
## Use
- Designing how technology will support acquire–serve–retain processes across channels
- Selecting or re-platforming CRM capabilities against journey and revenue outcomes
- Fixing fragmented customer data and interaction ownership across front-office teams
## Avoid
- You need channel experience design without system strategy—use omnichannel-strategy or customer-journey-mapping
- The problem is pure sales process effectiveness—use sales-force-effectiveness
- You are selecting ERP finance/ops systems—use erp-selection-framework
## Inputs
**Must-have:**
- Priority customer journeys and interaction moments
- Current CRM/MarTech stack and data model gaps
- Sales, service, and marketing process owners and KPIs
- Integration constraints with product, billing, and support systems

**Nice-to-have:** customer segmentation, consent rules, vendor contracts, adoption data, and a target architecture principle set.

**When data is thin:** map one priority journey end to end and use it to expose data and handoff gaps; do not let a vendor demonstration substitute for process evidence.
## Procedure
1. **Set the customer-value and business scope.** Choose segments, journeys, growth or service outcomes, and strategic partners that the CRM model must support. Output: CRM scope and value hypothesis.
2. **Map value creation and journey moments.** Identify offers, interactions, handoffs, service promises, and pain points across sales, marketing, and service. Output: prioritized capability needs.
3. **Design multichannel orchestration.** Decide which channel owns each moment, how context follows the customer, and when cases or opportunities move between teams. Output: interaction and process design.
4. **Define information management.** Establish customer identity, master-data ownership, consent, data quality rules, integrations, analytics, and access permissions. Output: data and integration blueprint.
5. **Set performance and delivery roadmap.** Specify metrics, operating governance, platform increments, migration waves, adoption support, and benefits checkpoints. Output: CRM target operating model and roadmap.
## Output Contract
A CRM target model that states priority journeys and segments, capabilities, cross-functional processes, channel orchestration, customer-data ownership, integration decisions, platform roadmap, KPIs, and adoption governance. It must identify what changes for a seller, marketer, service agent, and customer. A feature list or vendor scorecard alone is insufficient.
## Evidence
Use journey observations, system logs, data-quality profiles, conversion and service metrics, and consent requirements as evidence. Mark assumed customer identity matches, integration feasibility, and adoption capacity. Keep customer preference evidence distinct from internal channel convenience. Reconcile metric definitions before comparing sales, service, and marketing performance.
## Checks
- Each priority journey has an accountable business owner and clear channel handoffs.
- Customer identity, consent, and access rules work across connected systems.
- Capability requirements trace to a measurable commercial or service outcome.
- The roadmap sequences data, process, platform, migration, and adoption dependencies.
- Metrics discourage local optimization such as lead volume without conversion quality.
## Failure Modes
- Selecting a platform before agreeing who owns customer data and process decisions.
- Creating a “360-degree view” that ignores consent, duplicate identities, or data freshness.
- Automating broken handoffs between sales and service.
- Measuring adoption by logins while customer outcomes and data quality decline.
