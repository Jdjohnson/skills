# Kraljic Matrix
## Purpose
Segment spend into strategic, leverage, bottleneck, and non-critical quadrants and assign matching sourcing strategies. It ensures procurement attention reflects both business impact and difficulty of supply, rather than treating every category as a negotiation.
## Use
- Segmenting purchased categories by supply risk and profit impact
- Choosing differentiated sourcing strategies (exploit, balance, diversify, ensure supply)
- Focusing procurement and executive attention on the few categories that can stop the business
## Avoid
- You already have segments and need the full commercial sourcing process—use strategic-sourcing
- The decision is logistics provider selection specifically—use 3pl-4pl-selection-framework
- You need total lifecycle cost of a single buy without portfolio segmentation—use total-cost-of-ownership
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Spend by category or supplier with business criticality; supply-market risk indicators (concentration, switching cost, scarcity); current supplier base and single-source exposure; profit or value-at-risk impact if supply fails or cost spikes.

**Nice-to-have:** contract expiry dates, capacity outlook, inventory cover, and an engineering-approved substitute list.

**When data is thin:** score uncertainty separately from risk and validate the highest-exposure categories with technical and operations leaders before assigning tactics.
## Procedure
1. **Set the portfolio boundary.** Define categories at a level where the supply market and substitution options are meaningfully different. Clean spend, consolidate duplicate supplier names, and identify business units sharing a dependency.
2. **Score profit impact.** Rate the effect of supply interruption or price movement on margin, revenue, safety, and customer commitments. Use a transparent scale rather than spend alone.
3. **Score supply risk.** Assess supplier concentration, entry barriers, scarcity, technical specification, switching time, geographic exposure, and available alternatives. Record evidence and uncertainty for each score.
4. **Place and challenge categories.** Position each category on the two axes, then challenge boundary cases with procurement, engineering, and operations. A low-spend component can still be bottleneck if it stops production.
5. **Assign quadrant tactics.** Set a strategy for each category: partnership and joint planning for strategic items; competitive leverage for leverage items; assurance, redesign, or inventory protection for bottlenecks; simplified ordering for non-critical buys.
## Output Contract
Produce a category portfolio with axis definitions, scored evidence, quadrant placement, confidence flag, current supplier exposure, and a named sourcing action for every material category. The recommendation must distinguish immediate risk treatment from longer-term commercial strategy. It is a portfolio segmentation artifact, not a completed tender or contract negotiation plan.
## Evidence
Use spend ledgers, supplier data, incident records, and technical specifications as evidence. Document market judgments such as capacity scarcity or switching difficulty as assumptions with a review date. Do not convert a supplier’s sales importance into business risk without examining alternatives. Refresh placements after a merger, specification change, or material supply disruption.
## Checks
- The two axes are scored independently; high spend does not automatically mean strategic.
- Category definitions prevent one supplier from appearing in multiple incompatible quadrants.
- Operations and engineering validate interruption consequences for critical materials.
- Each quadrant has a distinct action, owner, and timing.
## Failure Modes
- Labeling every high-spend category strategic and exhausting executive attention.
- Ranking a low-cost proprietary fastener as non-critical despite a 26-week replacement test.
- Applying price competition to a bottleneck part and worsening security of supply.
- Leaving the matrix unchanged when a single source is qualified or lost.
