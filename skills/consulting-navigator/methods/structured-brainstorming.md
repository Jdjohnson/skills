# Structured Brainstorming
## Purpose
Run a facilitated group process that generates and ranks ideas with equal contribution before debate or hierarchy distorts the set. It creates a first-cut option set that preserves dissent and makes the group’s selection logic visible.
## Use
- Generating a broad option set with equal airtime via silent ideation then structured share-out.
- Facilitating a group toward ranked ideas without letting status hierarchy dominate.
- Producing a first-cut list of solutions, risks, or opportunities before prioritization.
## Avoid
- You need multi-round anonymous expert convergence over time—use delphic-method.
- You need deep customer problem discovery rather than group idea listing—use jobs-to-be-done, design-thinking, or focus-groups.
- Options already exist and the task is scoring them—use feature-prioritization or decision-tree-analysis.
## Inputs
**Must-have:** A focused prompt or problem statement for ideation; a defined participant set with relevant expertise; facilitation rules for silent generation, round-robin share, and voting or ranking; timebox and decision criteria for shortlisting.

**Useful additions:** factual pre-read, an independent facilitator, digital capture board, criteria definitions, and an agreed sponsor decision after the session.
## Procedure
1. Write a single generative prompt with scope, constraints, and decision owner. Explain what an idea must solve, but do not reveal a preferred solution in the prompt.
2. Give participants silent individual generation time. Each person writes one idea per card or entry, including a short mechanism and the benefit it might create; no discussion or evaluation occurs yet.
3. Conduct round-robin capture. Invite one unshared idea from each person per turn, record it verbatim, and continue until everyone passes. Combine literal duplicates only with participants’ agreement.
4. Clarify meaning without advocating. Group related ideas after the full set is visible, preserve outliers, and state the shortlist criteria before voting.
5. Run an individual vote or rank using the stated criteria. Reveal results, note tied or polarized items, and decide whether the top entries advance to experiment design, analysis, or a separate selection forum.
6. Publish the complete list, ranked results, dissent notes, and next owner so the group can see what happened to its input.
## Output Contract
Deliver the prompt, participant roster, raw idea inventory, deduplicated or clustered list, criteria, individual or aggregate rankings, selected shortlist, dissent notes, and next-step owner. Preserve raw entries alongside clusters so a facilitator cannot quietly erase minority ideas. This artifact is a ranked option set, not a claim that the workshop proved feasibility.
## Evidence
Label factual inputs supplied before the session and distinguish them from participant hypotheses. Record who contributed an idea only when attribution is wanted and psychologically safe; anonymous voting is usually preferable. Do not convert vote totals into customer evidence or financial forecasts. If a participant has an undisclosed conflict or missing domain knowledge, log it as a limitation rather than suppressing their entry.
## Checks
- The prompt asks for alternatives rather than inviting defense of a chosen answer.
- Every participant receives the same silent generation and share opportunity.
- Criteria are visible before voting begins.
- The record shows whether rankings are individual votes, consensus, or sponsor choice.
- A dissenting high-value idea can proceed even if it is not the numerical winner.
## Failure Modes
- Opening discussion before silent writing, allowing the first executive’s framing to narrow the pool.
- Letting the facilitator merge ideas with different mechanisms because their labels sound alike.
- Voting on popularity when the decision requires technical feasibility or customer evidence.
- Announcing a winner without assigning the experiment or analysis needed to test it.
