# Competency Mapping
## Purpose
Decompose role success into a prioritized competency framework of skills, knowledge, and behaviors that hiring and development can actually use. It makes excellent performance observable and discriminates essentials from generic aspirations.
## Use
- Defining the skills, knowledge, and behaviors that distinguish strong performance in a role or role family.
- Building a shared language for hiring, development, and succession decisions.
- Identifying priority capability gaps against a future operating model or strategy.
## Avoid
- You need to price or grade jobs by relative organizational value—use job-analysis-and-evaluation.
- You only need aggregate climate or engagement scores—use employee-engagement-surveying.
- The real issue is team process and conflict dynamics, not role capability definitions—use high-performance-teams or conflict-resolution-methods.
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Role or role-family scope and performance outcomes that matter; evidence from high and average performers (interviews, critical incidents, work samples); strategy or operating-model implications for future capabilities; stakeholder input from managers and job holders; existing job descriptions or prior competency drafts if any.

**Nice-to-have:** performance distributions, assessment results, career paths, and external professional standards.

**When evidence conflicts:** retain rival behavior descriptions until a review panel resolves whether the difference is context, level, or manager preference.
## Procedure
1. Specify the role boundary, level, decisions, and results that constitute success. Separate a role family where work outputs truly differ.
2. Collect critical incidents from strong and typical performers: situation, action, reasoning, and result. Sample managers, job holders, and internal customers to avoid one manager’s ideal profile.
3. Code incidents into candidate competencies, distinguishing knowledge, transferable skill, and observable behavior. Remove traits that cannot be assessed reliably.
4. Write each competency as a definition plus behavioral indicators at proficiency levels. Anchor indicators in work products or decisions, not adjectives such as “strategic.”
5. Prioritize by impact on outcomes, frequency, future relevance, and difficulty to develop. Mark threshold competencies separately from differentiators.
6. Validate with subject-matter experts using sample hiring and promotion cases, then translate the approved framework into interview rubrics and development actions.
## Output Contract
Deliver a role or role-family framework containing competency definitions, behavioral indicators by proficiency, priority tier, evidence source, and intended uses. Include a mapping to hiring, performance, or learning instruments and an explicit distinction between table stakes and differentiating capability. A duty list or an undifferentiated skills catalogue is not sufficient.
## Evidence
Treat observed work samples and documented incidents as evidence; label manager ratings and future-state predictions as judgments. Do not infer competence from tenure, credential, or job title alone. Preserve examples that challenge the dominant view. When a future operating model changes the role, show which competencies are proposed rather than pretending they are current performance facts.
## Checks
- Indicators describe behavior a trained assessor could see or hear.
- Proficiency levels change the complexity, autonomy, or impact of behavior.
- Each competency maps to a material role outcome.
- The final set is short enough to use in a real interview or review.
- SMEs can classify anonymized incidents consistently against the framework.
## Failure Modes
- Copying a corporate dictionary so every role receives the same 18 vague competencies.
- Writing “leadership” as a competency without decisions or behaviors that distinguish levels.
- Treating a manager’s preferred style as a universal success criterion.
- Mixing required certifications with demonstrated capability.
- Publishing a model that cannot be scored in interviews, calibration, or development planning.
