# Kotter's 8-Step Process
## Purpose
Sequence large-scale organizational change as an eight-step leadership process—from urgency through cultural anchoring—so a transformation becomes a lasting movement, not a project that fades after kickoff.
## Use
- Leading enterprise change that needs a staged leadership sequence, not only a workplan.
- A transformation launched but urgency decayed after kickoff.
- A program office exists without a coalition powerful enough to drive the change.
- The effort is seen as an IT or project-office initiative, not a leadership-led movement.
- Early wins were never harvested, communication is under-scaled, or new behaviors failed to stick.
## Avoid
- The need is person-level awareness–desire–knowledge–ability–reinforcement tracking—use adkar-model.
- Change is a simple team unfreeze–move–refreeze without enterprise movement design—use lewins-change-model.
- You only need readiness scoring before any roadmap—use change-readiness-assessment first.
- The change is a routine process tweak with clear authority and no coalition needed.
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Strategic case for urgency and vision direction; map of sponsors, coalition candidates, and resistance pockets; communication and win-tracking mechanisms; the behaviors and systems to anchor.

**Nice-to-have:** Prior transformation postmortems; employee sentiment baselines; middle-manager capacity; competing initiative inventory.

**When data is missing:** Interview sponsors and frontline leads to reconstruct urgency evidence and informal power; an executive announcement is not a coalition.
## Procedure
1. **Create urgency.** Surface credible evidence the status quo is costly; produce a shared urgency narrative and visible leadership commitment.
2. **Build a guiding coalition.** Assemble a cross-level group with power, expertise, and credibility; produce a named coalition with an operating rhythm.
3. **Form the strategic vision and initiatives.** Clarify how the future state differs from today; produce a concise vision and initiative set the coalition can champion.
4. **Enlist a broad volunteer base.** Communicate repeatedly and bidirectionally; produce an engagement design scaled to the organization, not one town hall.
5. **Enable action by removing barriers.** Clear structural, skill, system, and manager blockers; produce a barrier-removal backlog with owners.
6. **Generate short-term wins.** Plan visible, early performance improvements; produce a win pipeline with metrics, owners, and a publicity plan.
7. **Sustain acceleration.** Use credibility from wins to tackle harder systems; produce the next wave of initiatives rather than declaring victory.
8. **Institute change.** Anchor new approaches in culture, hiring, promotion, processes, and leadership habits; produce anchoring mechanisms and stickiness measures.
## Output Contract
A staged change-movement roadmap: urgency case; coalition design; vision and initiative map; mass engagement plan; barrier-removal actions; short-term win slate; acceleration agenda; institutionalization checklist—plus a diagnosis of where the effort is stalled across the eight steps.
## Evidence
- **Observed:** sponsorship behavior, message reach, win metrics, policy and process changes in force.
- **Inferred:** cultural stickiness only after systems and promotion criteria change, not posters.
- **Assumptions:** names on a slide without meeting power are not a real coalition.
- **Unknowns:** hidden resistance networks—probe with stakeholder mapping rather than ignore.

Thin urgency or win evidence: rebuild those steps before scaling structural change.
## Checks
- Urgency rests on facts leaders and employees both recognize, not slogans.
- Coalition has enough power and credibility to unblock major barriers.
- Vision is specific enough to guide tradeoffs.
- Communication volume and channels match the change's scale.
- Wins are real, visible, and early enough to build momentum.
- Anchoring names concrete HR, process, and leadership mechanism changes.
## Failure Modes
- Skipping urgency and launching a project plan no one prioritizes.
- A weak coalition of willing staff without authority.
- Under-communicating the vision by a large margin.
- Declaring victory after first wins; new behaviors die when the program office disbands.
- Treating the steps as a checklist workshop without leadership behavior change.
