# PRINCE2
## Purpose
Control projects through defined principles, themes, and stage-based processes so justification, roles, and tolerances stay explicit end to end. It gives a project board usable information at planned decision points and escalates only forecasts outside delegated limits.
## Use
- Running projects with explicit business justification, stage boundaries, and defined management roles
- Environments that expect PRINCE2 themes, processes, and management products
- Installing controlled tolerances so exception management replaces silent overrun
## Avoid
- You want a flexible knowledge guide rather than a process method—use pmbok-framework
- Delivery must be continuous product iteration without stage boards—use scrum-framework
- You only need schedule network math—use critical-path-method
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Project mandate and outline business case; organization's project board roles (executive, senior user, senior supplier); agreed tolerances for time, cost, quality, scope, risk, benefits; product descriptions or expected outputs; corporate or program standards the project must inherit.

**Nice-to-have:** delivery history, benefits measures, supplier contracts, configuration-management approach, and a project-support office.

**When data is thin:** start with a short initiation stage, record provisional tolerances, and ask the board to authorize discovery rather than pretending the full plan is known.
## Procedure
1. **Start up the project.** Confirm the mandate, appoint executive, senior user, senior supplier, and project manager, then test whether the idea merits initiation. Create the project brief and an initiation-stage plan.
2. **Direct and initiate.** Have the board authorize initiation; develop the business case, strategies for risk, quality, change, and communication, plus product descriptions and the project plan. Baseline what the board will control.
3. **Divide work into management stages.** Plan each stage with products, activities, resources, controls, and tolerances. The board authorizes a stage only when the business case and forecast remain acceptable.
4. **Control the current stage.** The project manager assigns work packages, receives completed products, logs issues and risks, and reports progress against tolerances. Suppliers accept work-package constraints rather than informal requests.
5. **Manage exceptions and boundaries.** When a forecast exceeds a tolerance, prepare an exception plan for board decision. At stage end, report performance, refresh the business case and risk outlook, then seek authority for the next stage.
6. **Close deliberately.** Verify acceptance, hand over products, confirm follow-on actions and benefits ownership, evaluate performance, and obtain board authorization to close.
## Output Contract
A controlled project baseline containing the business case, role map, management strategies, product descriptions, stage plans, tolerances, reporting cadence, issue and risk controls, and recorded board decisions. It must let a board decide whether to initiate, continue, redirect, or close the project without confusing a routine status report for authorization.
## Evidence
Treat signed mandates, product acceptance criteria, actual cost, and approved decisions as observed. Forecasts of remaining cost, benefit, or risk are inferred and must show their basis. Tolerances are governance choices, not estimates; record who approved them. Maintain version control for baselines so a revised plan does not erase the forecast that triggered an exception.
## Checks
- Each board role has a named person and distinct decision responsibility.
- The business case is refreshed at stage boundaries, not filed once at initiation.
- Every stage has measurable products, acceptance methods, and tolerances.
- Exception reports state the forecast breach and requested board decision.
- Work packages connect supplier effort to a product description and quality method.
## Failure Modes
- Calling a weekly steering meeting a board while nobody can authorize scope or funds.
- Setting tolerances so wide that exceptions are discovered after the stage is unrecoverable.
- Treating a stage boundary as a calendar milestone instead of a reauthorization decision.
- Allowing change requests to alter product baselines without impact assessment.
- Closing after deployment without transferring benefits ownership to operations.
