# Omnichannel Strategy
## Purpose
Create a joined customer experience and channel operating model so journeys, offers, inventory, and service handoffs function as one system. It sets channel roles and prevents a customer’s context, promise, or order from breaking during a switch.
## Use
- You must design seamless journeys spanning digital and physical channels
- Channel conflict, inconsistent offers, or broken handoffs are eroding conversion or loyalty
- Investment decisions require a prioritized omnichannel capability and experience roadmap
## Avoid
- You only need a single-channel funnel fix—use sales-funnel-optimization or funnel-analysis
- The issue is pure brand messaging, not channel integration—use 4ps-of-marketing or brand-equity-model
- You lack basic journey visibility—map with customer-journey-mapping first
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Current channel footprint and role definitions; priority customer journeys and pain points across channels; capability gaps in identity, inventory, pricing, and service integration; economics by channel and cross-channel conversion data.

**Nice-to-have:** Basket-level path data, fulfillment exceptions, channel incentive plans, and transcripts from customers who abandoned a handoff.

**When data is thin:** Trace a small number of high-value journeys end to end, treating each missing system handoff as an unresolved design constraint rather than estimating seamlessness from channel totals.
## Procedure
1. **Choose journeys and moments that matter.** Select the few journeys where a channel switch affects conversion, fulfillment, or trust; define the customer promise at each transition. Output: journey-and-handoff scope.
2. **Map the current orchestration.** For every handoff, show customer identity, offer eligibility, inventory visibility, order status, service history, owner, and system of record. Output: cross-channel handoff map.
3. **Quantify channel conflicts.** Compare price, promotion, stock, service entitlement, and sales-credit rules across routes. Separate deliberate roles from accidental inconsistency. Output: conflict and leakage register.
4. **Set target experience and operating rules.** Specify which channel acquires, advises, transacts, fulfills, or recovers service; then define shared customer ID, inventory promise, offer precedence, and exception ownership. Output: channel-role and integration-rule design.
5. **Prioritize the roadmap.** Size each capability by journey value, dependency, cost, and time to relieve a broken handoff. Sequence policy changes before technology where a rule, not a platform, is the constraint. Output: staged roadmap with accountable owners.
## Output Contract
Deliver a journey-led omnichannel blueprint: channel roles, the target handoff experience, common rules for identity, offers, inventory, and service, plus a capability roadmap. Each roadmap item identifies the journey metric it changes, the operating owner, systems affected, and a transition rule. It is not a collection of digital features or a generic experience wish list.
## Evidence
Mark observed handoffs with transaction paths, screen captures, or service records. Treat a claimed integration as unproven until a customer can complete it under normal exceptions, such as a return without a receipt. State business-rule assumptions separately from technical feasibility. If cross-channel attribution is incomplete, report directional leakage and identify the instrument needed to measure it.
## Checks
- A customer can move between the specified channels without re-entering identity, order, or entitlement information.
- The design resolves a concrete conflict in price, inventory, sales credit, or service ownership.
- Channel economics include transferred demand, not just booked revenue.
- Exception journeys, especially return, pickup, and service recovery, have an owner and rule.
- Roadmap dependencies distinguish policy, process, data, and platform work.
## Failure Modes
- Declaring every channel “equally important” and never assigning a primary role for a journey step.
- Building a unified front end while inventory availability and promotion eligibility remain separate.
- Rewarding stores and digital teams on isolated conversion, which recreates the conflict the design is meant to remove.
- Designing the happy path but leaving pickup substitutions and cross-channel returns to frontline improvisation.
