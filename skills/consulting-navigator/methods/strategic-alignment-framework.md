# Strategic Alignment Framework
## Purpose
Test structure, culture, capabilities, and metrics against the stated strategy to locate and prioritize the misalignments blocking execution. It ranks execution blockers rather than describing internal conditions in the abstract.
## Use
- Strategy is not translating into day-to-day choices and resource allocation.
- Testing whether organization form and culture support a new strategy.
- Prioritizing alignment fixes before major transformation spend.
## Avoid
- You need detailed design-policy diagnosis across rewards and processes—use star-model.
- You need enterprise soft/hard factor fit including style and shared values—use 7s-framework.
- You already know misalignment and need deployment mechanics—use hoshin-kanri or strategy-map.
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Documented strategy and priority outcomes; current structure and key decision rights; culture and behavior signals relevant to strategy; capability and talent placement against strategic needs; metrics and incentive systems in force.

**Useful additions:** capital allocations, portfolio review minutes, frontline examples, technology constraints, and customer evidence showing execution consequences.
## Procedure
1. Convert the strategy into a concise set of strategic imperatives and observable choices. Define what must increase, decrease, or stop for the strategy to be real.
2. For each imperative, examine four levers directly against it: structure and decision rights, norms and leader behavior, required capabilities and their location, and metrics or incentives.
3. Gather contradictions through artifacts and cases. Compare budget decisions, approvals, promotions, operating reviews, and resource deployment with the stated choice.
4. Score each gap on its impact on the strategic outcome, breadth of affected work, urgency, and difficulty to reverse. Separate a symptom, such as slow launches, from the alignment gap, such as regional approval rights built for a domestic model.
5. Design a sequenced remedy portfolio: immediate guardrails, medium-term capability or metric changes, and structural changes that require formal governance. State dependencies and a leading indicator for each remedy.
## Output Contract
Deliver an imperative-by-lever alignment assessment, ranked gap register, and corrective roadmap. Every gap records the strategy it obstructs, evidence, mechanism, severity, accountable executive, intervention, dependency, and leading measure. This is not a strategy map or annual deployment plan; it is the evidence-backed case for which execution constraints to remove first.
## Evidence
Use strategy documents only as the declared intent; test them against allocation and behavior. Treat employee sentiment as a signal, not proof of causality, unless supported by decisions or performance patterns. Mark capability gaps as assumptions until role inventories or delivery evidence validate them. Preserve contrary evidence that shows an exception is working; it can identify an existing practice worth scaling. Do not collapse culture into survey averages when incentives or leadership routines show a different pattern.
## Checks
- Every finding ties to one declared strategic imperative.
- All four levers receive evidence, including the possibility that a lever is aligned.
- Rankings make impact and feasibility explicit rather than relying on workshop consensus.
- Roadmap actions identify behavior change and the operating mechanism that creates it.
- A sponsor can distinguish fix-now blockers from longer-term modernization.
## Failure Modes
- Repeating the strategy as a communication problem when capital allocation contradicts it.
- Declaring “culture” the root cause without naming the routine or consequence that reinforces behavior.
- Launching a reorganization before correcting metrics that reward the old strategic logic.
- Listing dozens of gaps without testing which ones actually constrain priority outcomes.
