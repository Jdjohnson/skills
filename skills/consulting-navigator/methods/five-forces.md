# Porter's Five Forces
## Purpose
Diagnose whether an industry is structurally attractive by scoring the five pressures that shape how economic value is created and captured: new entrants, supplier power, buyer power, substitutes, and rivalry among competitors. It serves entry, expansion, and portfolio decisions by separating a tough industry from a poorly run firm.
## Use
- You are weighing entry or expansion and need a structural read before committing capital.
- Margins stay compressed despite operational improvement and leadership asks whether structure is the cause.
- You must compare power balances across adjacent markets or geographies.
- Rivalry, supplier lock-in, buyer concentration, or new entry dominate the brief's problem signals.
## Avoid
- You need a firm-level capability audit—use resource-based-view or core-competencies.
- The question is macro trend scanning without competitive rivalry—use pestle-analysis.
- You already understand industry structure and need positioning options—use generic-strategies or blue-ocean-strategy.
- The industry boundary cannot be defined provisionally; reframe the market first.
## Inputs
**Must-have:** A defined industry boundary and the competing products or services; evidence on buyer concentration, switching costs, and price sensitivity; supplier concentration and input criticality; current rivals and recent competitive moves; indicators of entry barriers, substitutes, and potential entrants.

**Nice-to-have:** Margin or ROIC benchmarks; channel maps; regulatory inventories; entry/exit history.

**When data is thin:** Treat the boundary as an assumption, tag scores with confidence, and flag which conclusions reverse if it shifts.
## Procedure
1. **Fix the industry boundary** — Define in one paragraph who competes for the same customer need with comparable products, and list the in-scope rivals. Output: boundary statement and rival set.
2. **Score each force** — For each force, gather its structural drivers (concentration, switching costs, capacity, differentiation, growth, exit barriers) and rate pressure high/medium/low with evidence notes. Output: five force scorecards.
3. **Assess joint effect on profit potential** — Combine the scores into a diagnosis: who captures value, where margins are structurally constrained, whether improvement is firm- or industry-driven. Output: attractiveness narrative.
4. **Translate to position implications** — State decision implications (enter, avoid, exit, reposition, change the power balance) without selecting a full strategy. Output: implication list tied to the scores.
5. **Sensitivity check** — Re-score any force resting on a contested boundary or thin evidence and note what changes the recommendation. Output: confidence and sensitivity note.
## Output Contract
An industry attractiveness assessment containing: (1) industry boundary statement, (2) score and evidence for each of the five forces, (3) joint diagnosis of long-run profit potential and value capture, (4) decision implications for entry, expansion, or portfolio stance, and (5) explicit unknowns and sensitivity. It must support a go/no-go or prioritization decision, not a full strategy choice.
## Evidence
- **Observed:** documented market shares, contracts, switching costs, capacity additions, price wars, regulation.
- **Inferred:** power ratings derived from concentration and alternatives.
- **Assumptions:** boundary choices, substitute sets, and potential-entrant lists—label them.
- **Unknowns:** keep force scores provisional; prefer directional high/medium/low ratings over false numeric precision.
## Checks
- Boundary is explicit and the rival list matches it.
- Every force cites a structural driver and an evidence tag.
- The conclusion follows from the five scores jointly, not one dramatic force.
- Firm performance is not confused with industry structure.
- Implications are decision-oriented and name a next method.
## Failure Modes
- Redrawing the industry until the story looks favorable.
- Listing macro trends instead of structural power.
- Scoring the client's capabilities instead of industry structure.
- Treating the forces as equal when one dominates value capture.
- Jumping to strategy labels before the diagnosis is complete.
