# Go-to-Market Strategy
## Purpose
Plan how the firm will reach, convert, and deliver value to target customers through a chosen channel and sales motion. The resulting commercial design specifies the repeatable path from first exposure to revenue and early customer success.
## Use
- Planning how a defined value proposition reaches target customers and converts.
- Choosing sales motion, channels, packaging, and launch sequencing.
- Aligning marketing, sales, product, and partners on one revenue path.
## Avoid
- Target segment and positioning are still unknown—use stp-framework or product-market-fit-analysis first.
- You only need the marketing mix design, not the full revenue motion—use 4ps-of-marketing.
- The product is already live and the issue is funnel leakage only—use sales-funnel-optimization.
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Target segment(s) and value proposition; Offer packaging, pricing hypothesis, and competitive position; Channel options (direct sales, self-serve, partners, marketplaces); Capacity constraints (sales headcount, budget, enablement, timeline).

**Useful additions:** buyer roles, buying triggers, current conversion data, partner economics, and a map of product onboarding dependencies.
## Procedure
1. Map the buying journey from trigger through renewal, separating economic buyer, user, champion, and approver. Identify the moment at which the offer becomes credible and the friction that stops a purchase.
2. Define the commercial motion for each priority segment: self-serve, sales-assisted, field-led, partner-led, or a deliberate hybrid. State the handoff rule, such as when product-qualified usage becomes an account-executive opportunity.
3. Design the offer architecture. Specify package boundaries, price metric, proof points, contract shape, and the minimum implementation promise that sales may make.
4. Choose routes to market by comparing reachable accounts, acquisition cost, partner margin, sales-cycle length, control of the customer relationship, and required capability. Do not select every channel merely to increase coverage.
5. Build the operating chain: demand generation, qualification, selling, contracting, onboarding, adoption, and expansion. Assign one accountable owner and a service-level handoff for every transition.
6. Sequence a ramp plan with launch waves, enablement assets, capacity releases, leading indicators, and decision gates for scaling or revising the motion.
## Output Contract
Deliver a GTM blueprint containing segment-specific journey maps, chosen motions, channel roles, offer and pricing rules, owner handoffs, capacity assumptions, and a 90- to 180-day ramp calendar. Include a metric tree that connects reach, qualified demand, conversion, activation, and retention to revenue. The artifact must make it possible to decide where to invest and what commercial behavior will change.
## Evidence
Mark observed evidence such as win/loss interviews, usage data, pipeline history, and partner performance separately from inferred buyer behavior. Treat willingness to pay, channel productivity, and conversion for a new segment as assumptions until tested. State the unit economics behind each route, including partner discount and sales capacity. If the buyer journey is uncertain, run discovery or a small motion test instead of presenting a universal funnel.
## Checks
- Each priority segment has one primary motion and a clear reason it fits the purchase process.
- Packaging, price metric, and onboarding promise agree with the selected channel.
- No handoff lacks an owner, acceptance criterion, or response time.
- Capacity math covers pipeline creation and delivery, not only quota-carrying sellers.
- Ramp metrics reveal failure before lagging revenue results arrive.
## Failure Modes
- Calling a list of marketing tactics a commercial system while leaving sales and onboarding undefined.
- Sending enterprise buyers into a self-serve path with no implementation proof.
- Adding resellers without a margin model, registration rules, or enablement plan.
- Declaring a sales-led motion before calculating whether deal value can support the acquisition cost.
