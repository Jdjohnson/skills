# Brand Equity Model
## Purpose
Diagnose brand equity by measuring how consumer awareness, associations, quality perceptions, and loyalty create differential value. It identifies which brand asset is weakening or strengthening and where investment can improve preference, retention, or price premium.
## Use
- Diagnosing brand health across awareness, associations, perceived quality, and loyalty.
- Prioritizing brand investments by which equity drivers are weak.
- Explaining price premium or share gaps attributable to brand strength.
## Avoid
- You need to invent brand personality rather than measure equity—use brand-archetypes.
- The problem is short-term campaign ROI mix, not brand asset health—use marketing-mix-modeling.
- No brand or customer perception data can be collected yet—gather evidence first via survey-design-methodology.
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Brand funnel or perception survey data (awareness, consideration, preference); association and attribute ratings vs key competitors; loyalty/retention and price-premium indicators; defined competitive set and brand architecture (master/sub-brands).

**Nice-to-have:** social listening, distribution data, segmentation, historical tracking waves, and category purchase incidence.

**When data is thin:** use directional qualitative evidence for hypotheses, but build a repeatable survey baseline before reporting an equity score.
## Procedure
1. **Define the comparison set.** Specify the master or sub-brand, audience segments, category, competitors, and purchase context so the score is interpretable.
2. **Operationalize four assets.** Choose indicators for awareness, associations, perceived quality, and loyalty; include price premium or preference where the category permits it.
3. **Collect and normalize evidence.** Field consistent questions, merge retention or sales evidence, and normalize scales and weights before comparing brands or waves.
4. **Profile strengths and gaps.** Score each asset by segment and competitor; locate whether weak conversion follows low salience, weak associations, quality distrust, or loyalty erosion.
5. **Trace commercial outcomes.** Test relationships to consideration, repeat purchase, share, or price premium, while distinguishing correlation from causation.
6. **Prioritize investment.** Select interventions tied to the weak asset and define the next measurement wave, expected movement, and budget guardrails.
## Output Contract
A brand-health assessment showing definitions, sampling, competitive benchmarks, dimension scores, segment cuts, trend, evidence confidence, commercial links, and prioritized investment actions. It must distinguish low awareness from low perceived quality and distinguish a loyalty problem from a simple availability problem.
## Evidence
Treat survey responses, repeat purchase, retention, and observed price realization as evidence with stated sampling limits. Mark weighting choices, causal interpretation, and competitor list choices as assumptions. Keep brand architecture explicit so a strong master brand does not conceal a failing product brand. Never compare changed questions or unweighted samples as if they were a clean trend.
## Checks
- Measures cover all four equity assets rather than an awareness-only funnel.
- Survey sample represents the intended market and reports base sizes by segment.
- Competitor comparisons use identical wording and field dates.
- Price premium is adjusted for product, channel, and promotion where feasible.
- Recommended spend links to a diagnosed equity gap and measurable leading indicator.
## Failure Modes
- Announcing a composite score without showing the weak component beneath it.
- Treating social mentions as a substitute for representative awareness measurement.
- Mistaking distribution loss for declining loyalty.
- Comparing a premium brand with a value competitor on unadjusted price alone.
- Using a new creative preference result as proof that equity has already improved.
