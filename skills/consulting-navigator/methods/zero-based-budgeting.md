# Zero-Based Budgeting
## Purpose
Rebuild period spend from a zero base by justifying, ranking, and selecting decision packages under a fixed envelope. It forces leaders to choose the work and service levels the organization will fund, rather than protecting last year’s allocation by default.
## Use
- Rebuilding a period's budget from a zero base with justified decision packages
- Structurally resetting overhead or SG&A after years of incremental budgeting
- Forcing rank-order trade-offs among cost packages under a hard spend envelope
## Avoid
- You need product/service cost causality rather than budget packages—use activity-based-costing
- The goal is continuous local waste removal without a full budget rebuild—use kaizen or cost-optimization-framework
- Only a light variance review of an accepted baseline is needed—use standard planning cycles, not ZBB
## Inputs
**Must-have:**
- Current cost baseline by package, cost center, or activity cluster
- Service-level and outcome definitions for each package
- Decision-package templates and ranking criteria
- Hard budget envelope and non-negotiable compliance spends
- Owner map for package preparation and challenge sessions

**Nice-to-have:** benchmarks, forecasts, and contractual commitments.

**When data is thin:** start with the largest discretionary pools, use ranges, and require owners to name missing evidence.
## Procedure
1. **Set the budget perimeter and rules.** Define the period, spending pools, envelope, mandatory obligations, decision rights, and minimum service commitments. State what is out of scope. Output: zero-base charter.
2. **Define decision units and package templates.** Group work into fundable units such as field marketing, application support, or fleet maintenance. Require purpose, activity, service level, resources, cost, benefits, risks, and a lower funding level where feasible. Output: package inventory and template.
3. **Build packages from activity, not prior allocation.** Owners describe the work needed for an outcome, cost people and non-labor inputs, and offer service alternatives. Separate committed contracts from choices that can stop, change, or defer. Output: base, increment, and decrement packages.
4. **Challenge and rank packages.** Test necessity, legal obligations, demand evidence, outcomes, dependencies, and comparability. Rank against shared criteria while keeping compliance items visible. Output: ranked funding slate.
5. **Select within the envelope and lock accountability.** Fund in rank order, record rejected or deferred packages, and reconcile selections to the financial plan. Assign owners and monthly measures. Output: approved package set and execution scorecard.
## Output Contract
An approved package book showing zero-base rules, packages and service levels, cost and benefit logic, challenge results, rank order, selected funding, and deferrals. It reconciles selections to the hard envelope and makes each cut’s consequence visible. It supports a funding decision, not a list of generic efficiency ideas or last year’s cost-center budget.
## Evidence
Treat contracts, payroll rosters, invoices, statutory requirements, and measured service demand as observed. Label benefit estimates, workload forecasts, demand elasticity, and stopping costs as assumptions without comparable evidence. Preserve the difference between obligation and preference. A benchmark does not prove a package can be cut without comparable scope, labor mix, service level, and timing. Record dependencies before ranking.
## Checks
- Every dollar in scope is in a package or explicitly mandatory.
- Packages describe activities and service levels, not only a percentage reduction from last year.
- Ranking criteria are consistent and challenge comments are retained.
- Selected packages total exactly to the envelope after shared costs and dependencies.
- Deferred work has an owner, risk statement, and reconsideration trigger.
## Failure Modes
- Renaming departments as packages while retaining prior amounts.
- Comparing legally required controls with optional advertising as discretionary.
- Ranking packages before defining service level.
- Cutting support without funding the work’s receiving dependency.
- Treating a one-year deferral as structural saving.
