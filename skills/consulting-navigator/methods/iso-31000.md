# ISO 31000
## Purpose
Install internationally recognized principles, framework, and process for how the organization identifies, assesses, treats, and monitors risk. It establishes repeatable risk practice across decisions rather than producing a one-time register.
## Use
- Establishing common principles, framework, and process for managing risk across the enterprise
- Harmonizing how units identify, analyze, evaluate, treat, and monitor risk
- Aligning risk practice to an internationally recognized guideline without mandating one control taxonomy
## Avoid
- You need COSO-style linkage of risk to strategy, culture, and board performance oversight—use coso-erm-framework
- The immediate deliverable is a project risk register and response plan—use risk-management-plan
- You need continuity or crisis operating models rather than risk process standards—use business-continuity-planning or crisis-management-framework
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Scope of the organization or system where risk will be managed; Internal and external context affecting objectives; Existing risk policies, criteria, and decision practices; Stakeholders accountable for risk decisions and oversight.

**Nice-to-have:** Current risk registers, assurance results, incident history, delegated authorities, and reporting cycles.

**When data is thin:** Establish provisional criteria and a pilot business boundary; test shared language and escalation before imposing enterprise scoring.
## Procedure
1. **Set mandate and context.** Define objectives, stakeholders, boundaries, and the internal and external conditions that create uncertainty. Establish what decisions risk management must improve.
2. **Design the framework.** Assign accountability, integration points, resources, communication routes, and review requirements. Embed risk considerations in planning, investment, and operational decisions instead of assigning them only to a central team.
3. **Establish risk criteria.** Agree consequence dimensions, likelihood definitions, time horizon, tolerance, and escalation thresholds. Make criteria proportionate: a local safety decision and an enterprise acquisition should not use the same depth of assessment.
4. **Operate the process.** Identify causes, events, and consequences; analyze existing controls and uncertainty; evaluate against criteria; select treatments; and obtain accountable acceptance of residual exposure.
5. **Communicate and consult.** Involve affected stakeholders while risks are framed and treatments chosen. Record disagreements where risk appetite or evidence differs rather than averaging them away.
6. **Monitor, review, and improve.** Track changing context, treatment effectiveness, incidents, and emerging risks. Periodically revise criteria and the framework based on lessons from real decisions.
## Output Contract
A risk-management system design containing principles, governance roles, context-setting guidance, common criteria, process workflow, treatment-accountability rules, monitoring measures, and an adoption plan. It should include a worked assessment format, but the enduring product is the operating framework rather than a populated risk register.
## Evidence
Use incident data, control tests, legal duties, and decision records as evidence. Mark likelihood estimates, control effectiveness, and emerging-risk claims as judgment where evidence cannot support a rate. Record inherent and residual exposure separately. Do not convert different kinds of harm into a single score without stating the aggregation rule and its limitations.
## Checks
- Context, objectives, and criteria are defined before risks are rated.
- Risk owners can accept, fund, or escalate the treatment decision.
- The workflow connects assessment to actual planning and approval forums.
- Criteria allow material uncertainty and do not imply precision beyond evidence.
- Monitoring tests whether treatment changes exposure, not whether forms were completed.
## Failure Modes
- Publishing a risk matrix without integrating it into decisions.
- Treating the risk team as owner of risks created by business operations.
- Scoring risks before defining the objectives being protected.
- Confusing a control inventory with evidence that risk is adequately treated.
