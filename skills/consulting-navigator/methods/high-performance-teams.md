# High-Performance Teams
## Purpose
Evaluate a team's development stage and operating system, then design the norms, roles, and rituals that move it toward reliable high performance. The focus is collective delivery and learning, not rating individual employees.
## Use
- Assessing where a team sits in its development and what norms or rituals would unlock performance.
- Resetting operating agreements after reorg, new leadership, or mission change.
- Designing a practical team effectiveness roadmap (goals, roles, processes, relationships).
## Avoid
- The blocker is a two-party dispute that needs resolution first—use conflict-resolution-methods.
- The root cause is org structure or unclear decision rights across many teams—use organizational-design or raci-matrix.
- You need individual multi-rater development data, not team system design—use 360-degree-feedback.
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Team purpose, goals, and success metrics; Membership, roles, and interfaces to other teams; Evidence of current collaboration patterns (meetings, decisions, conflicts, delivery); Leadership expectations and constraints; Optional stage or effectiveness diagnostic results.

**Useful additions:** meeting observations, pulse-survey results, decision logs, delivery data, and interface feedback.
## Procedure
1. Establish the team boundary: membership, mandate, dependencies, and the work that requires real interdependence. A co-located group with separate work is not automatically a team.
2. Observe the operating system in action. Sample planning, decision, and review meetings; examine how commitments, dissent, escalation, and handoffs actually work.
3. Diagnose the development pattern. Look for forming uncertainty, storming conflict, norming routines, or performing autonomy, while recognizing that a new mission can move a mature team backward.
4. Surface the few constraints on performance: an unclear shared outcome, unsafe challenge, unresolved role overlap, weak feedback, or unreliable rituals. Validate findings with the team rather than imposing a leader-only narrative.
5. Co-create operating agreements covering goal cadence, decision method, meeting roles, response norms, conflict handling, and cross-functional interfaces.
6. Run short improvement cycles, review delivery and relationship indicators, and retire rituals that consume time without improving coordination.
## Output Contract
Deliver a team effectiveness roadmap with a boundary statement, development-stage assessment, observed operating patterns, prioritized constraints, agreed norms, named ritual owners, and review measures. Include what the leader must change as well as what members will practice. The document should enable a team reset, not substitute for a mediation agreement or a task-by-task RACI.
## Evidence
Separate directly observed meeting behavior and delivery records from interview perceptions. Preserve minority views when seniority suppresses challenge. Treat a stage label as a working diagnosis, not a personality test. If sensitive topics cannot be discussed safely, use anonymous input and an external facilitator before declaring consensus on norms.
## Checks
- The shared outcome is measurable and cannot be met by individuals acting independently.
- Recommendations connect to a witnessed pattern, not a generic teamwork ideal.
- Decision, feedback, and conflict norms specify observable behavior.
- Interfaces to other teams have explicit contact points and escalation routes.
- Progress measures include both delivery reliability and team health.
## Failure Modes
- Holding an offsite that creates enthusiasm but leaves weekly decisions unchanged.
- Labeling healthy task conflict as dysfunction and silencing challenge.
- Asking for accountability without clarifying the collective result or interface commitments.
- Treating one difficult relationship as proof that the entire operating system is broken.
