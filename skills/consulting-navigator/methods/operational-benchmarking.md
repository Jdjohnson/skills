# Operational Benchmarking
## Purpose
Compare selected operational processes and practices to best-in-class peers to quantify performance gaps and transferrable practices. The comparison normalizes context so external targets are useful rather than a misleading league table.
## Use
- You need fact-based comparison of process performance and practices against best-in-class peers
- Internal baselines alone cannot set credible targets or gap priorities
- You are preparing a performance transformation case that needs external anchors
## Avoid
- You only need internal before/after measurement of your own process—use statistical-process-control or balanced-scorecard metrics
- The question is pure market position or share, not process performance—use competitive-benchmarking or five-forces
- Root cause of a specific failure is unknown—use root-cause-analysis before benchmarking solutions
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Defined processes and KPIs to compare (definitions and units must match); internal baseline performance data for those KPIs; peer or best-in-class reference sources (site visits, consortia, published data, or confidential exchanges); process context factors that affect comparability (volume, mix, automation, geography); access for interviews or observation of practices behind the metrics.

**Nice-to-have:** A trusted benchmark consortium, peer operating-model diagrams, multi-year trends, and a legal protocol for sharing sensitive information.

**When data is thin:** Use a comparable peer range and disclose the normalization basis; do not label a published headline metric “best practice” when the process boundary is unknown.
## Procedure
1. **Define the comparison unit.** Select a process, customer promise, and KPI dictionary, including numerator, denominator, time window, and inclusions. Output: benchmark specification.
2. **Select peers and normalize context.** Choose relevant leaders and comparators, then record differences in volume, product mix, automation, labor market, and network design. Output: peer panel and comparability notes.
3. **Collect outcomes and practices.** Gather performance data alongside site observations or interviews about how the result is achieved. Output: evidence pack separated by metric and practice.
4. **Calculate gaps with ranges.** Compare internal performance to normalized peer quartiles or a proven leader; show the adjustment and confidence rather than a single unqualified gap. Output: gap table.
5. **Test transferability.** Identify the enabling conditions, investment, and constraints behind each promising practice before recommending an adoption target. Output: practice-transfer assessment and priority list.
## Output Contract
Issue an operational gap report containing the KPI dictionary, peer selection logic, normalization factors, external performance range, internal position, observed operating practices, and transfer recommendations. It must distinguish an achievable target from a peer result that depends on unavailable assets, scale, or regulation.
## Evidence
Retain source, date, unit definition, and collection method for every peer metric. Treat confidential peer statements as directional unless independently corroborated. Document every normalization assumption, especially volume and labor-cost adjustments. Do not reverse-engineer a target from an aspirational business case; the benchmark is evidence, not a mandate.
## Checks
- Internal and peer metrics use the same process boundary and denominator.
- Peer selection reflects operational similarity, not only brand prestige.
- Each cited practice is linked to an observed outcome or clearly marked as a hypothesis.
- The report explains material context differences before ranking performance.
- Proposed targets state the capability, investment, and lead time required to reach them.
## Failure Modes
- Comparing warehouse cost per order when peer order profiles differ by tenfold in line count.
- Copying visible automation while ignoring the scheduling, data, or supplier discipline that makes it work.
- Selecting only heroic companies and treating their result as a near-term commitment.
- Reporting a percentile gap without interviewing anyone about the underlying process.
