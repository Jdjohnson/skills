# Survey Design Methodology
## Purpose
Design sampling, wording, scales, and field protocols so primary survey data are valid, reliable, and decision-ready before collection starts. The deliverable is a field-ready instrument and measurement plan, not an ongoing customer-listening program.
## Use
- You need quantitative primary data from a defined population on attitudes, behaviors, or outcomes
- Constructs, scales, sampling, and analysis must be designed before fielding
- Prior research is insufficient and a defensible instrument is required
## Avoid
- Published sources already answer the question at needed precision—use secondary-research
- You need rich observed behavior in context, not self-report—use ethnographic-research or mystery-shopping
- You only need preference trade-offs among designed features—use conjoint-analysis
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Decision questions and target constructs to measure; population definition, sample frame, and mode (online, phone, in-person); questionnaire draft requirements including scales, logic, and length targets; analysis plan (descriptives, segments, tracking) and bias risks to control.
## Procedure
1. **Turn decisions into constructs.** Write the decision each result will inform, the population, recall period, and measurable construct. Distinguish satisfaction, likelihood to recommend, usage frequency, and purchase intent; they cannot share an unexamined question.
2. **Build the sample plan.** Define the sampling frame, inclusion and exclusion rules, mode, quotas, expected response rate, and minimum completes for the planned cuts. Identify who is missing from the frame and how that omission biases inference.
3. **Draft the questionnaire.** Use one idea per question, neutral wording, answer choices that are mutually exclusive and collectively sufficient, labeled scale anchors, and routing only where it changes relevance. Put sensitive or demanding items after rapport-building questions.
4. **Align the analysis.** Map every item to a planned statistic, segment, or decision threshold. Add randomization, attention checks, and a consistent coding scheme only where they serve that analysis.
5. **Cognitively test and pilot.** Observe respondents interpreting the instrument, then field a pilot to inspect break-off, completion time, straight-lining, missingness, and distribution anomalies. Revise wording, ordering, and quotas before launch.
## Output Contract
A field pack containing the decision-to-measurement map, sample design, questionnaire with exact wording and routing, scale dictionary, respondent instructions, pilot findings, data-quality rules, and analysis specifications. It must state what population and precision the results can support. A list of appealing questions without a sampling and testing plan does not meet the contract.
## Evidence
Observed evidence includes frame counts, pilot behavior, prior response rates, and cognitive-test observations. Treat a chosen quota, expected incidence rate, or assumed nonresponse pattern as an assumption. Preserve the original wording and version number for every fielded item so trends remain interpretable. Report weights, exclusions, and item nonresponse; never imply that a large number of responses repairs a biased frame.
## Checks
- Each decision question has a construct, item, population, and planned analysis.
- No double-barreled, leading, undefined, or overlapping response options remain.
- Scale direction and anchors stay consistent across comparable items.
- The sample plan can achieve the required subgroup reads without post-fielding improvisation.
- Pilot outcomes meet stated thresholds for completion time, break-off, and item comprehension.
## Failure Modes
- Asking respondents to estimate behavior outside a credible recall period.
- Combining price, service, and product quality in one satisfaction item.
- Allowing mobile respondents to see a grid whose rows or anchors are truncated.
- Changing a tracker question while continuing to compare it directly to the old series.
- Applying quota weights to a convenience panel and calling the result representative.
