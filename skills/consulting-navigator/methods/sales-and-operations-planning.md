# Sales and Operations Planning
## Purpose
Govern a repeating cross-functional cycle that reconciles demand, supply, and financial goals into one executable operating plan. The monthly plan commits volume, inventory, capacity, and trade-offs; it is neither forecast nor annual financial plan.
## Use
- Installing a recurring cross-functional process to balance demand, supply, and financial targets
- Reducing plan volatility, expedites, and blame cycles across commercial and operations teams
- Creating one executable volume plan before elevating to full integrated business planning
## Avoid
- You need full P&L, scenario, and strategic integration already—use integrated-business-planning
- The core issue is statistical forecast quality only—use demand-planning-and-forecasting
- Facility footprint is wrong; network redesign first—use logistics-network-design
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Demand forecasts and unconstrained demand signals by product family; supply capacity, inventory, and lead-time constraints; financial targets, margin views, and working-capital limits; clear decision rights for demand-supply trade-offs; KPI baselines for service, inventory, and plan adherence.

**Nice-to-have:** approved commercial launches, supplier commitments, promotion calendars, and prior-cycle decisions with their measured outcome.

**When data is thin:** balance only the families driving most revenue or constraint load, and carry a named assumption for every unavailable supply or demand number.
## Procedure
1. **Set the planning horizon and fences.** Freeze a near-term window where changes require escalation, then choose monthly family-level buckets for the longer horizon. Name the executive who can trade service against inventory or margin.
2. **Build one demand view.** Reconcile statistical history, sales intelligence, promotions, launches, and lost-business signals. Record the unconstrained demand, the consensus volume, and disagreements that require a decision rather than averaging them away.
3. **Translate demand into supply feasibility.** Net inventory, confirmed receipts, labor, equipment, supplier limits, and lead times against each family. Identify the first period and resource where the plan cannot be met.
4. **Price the alternatives.** Compare actions such as allocation, overtime, postponement, substitution, or demand shaping using service, margin, cash, and risk consequences. Bring only material exceptions to the pre-meeting.
5. **Run the executive reconciliation.** Decide the approved volume plan, inventory intent, capacity actions, and customer commitments. Put owner, due date, and trigger in a decision log; publish the plan as the single operating version.
6. **Review adherence next cycle.** Compare actual demand, supply execution, inventory, and expedites with the prior plan. Classify misses as forecast error, execution failure, or changed assumption before adjusting the next plan.
## Output Contract
Produce a monthly decision memo with the approved family-level demand and supply plan, constrained-versus-unconstrained gaps, financial and working-capital implications, exception choices, and a dated decision log. It must identify which commitments are executable, who authorized each trade-off, and which indicators reopen the decision.
## Evidence
Treat booked orders, inventory, capacity calendars, and actual service as observed. Sales intelligence, supplier recovery dates, and promotion uplift require an owner and expiry date. Keep an exception register for unreconciled quantities. Do not silently force supply to equal demand; state whether the gap becomes backlog, lost sales, substitution, or inventory drawdown.
## Checks
- The family totals reconcile to the commercial and operations views.
- Every capacity-constrained period has a chosen action, not a red cell with no owner.
- Finance validates the margin and cash impact of material actions.
- Near-term customer promise dates respect the frozen fence.
- Last cycle's decisions are measured for service, inventory, and expedite effects.
## Failure Modes
- Treating the meeting as a forecast debate instead of resolving volume trade-offs.
- Letting sales maintain a shadow promise plan after the executive sign-off.
- Aggregating a constrained component with unconstrained families until the shortage disappears.
- Approving overtime repeatedly without exposing its margin and labor consequence.
- Changing the plan after the meeting without recording the authority or reason.
