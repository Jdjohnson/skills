# Onboarding Framework
## Purpose
Plan a systematic new-employee integration journey that accelerates productivity, belonging, and delivery of the promised employee experience. The design turns a hire’s first weeks and months into owned milestones rather than a collection of HR events.
## Use
- Designing a systematic 30-60-90 (or longer) integration journey across admin, role, social, and cultural dimensions
- Reducing early attrition and time-to-productivity for critical hires or cohorts
- Standardizing manager and HR responsibilities for new-employee ramp
## Avoid
- You still lack a coherent employee offer to deliver—define employee-value-proposition first
- The need is ongoing multi-year mentoring, not new-hire integration—use mentorship-program-design
- You need culture diagnosis before designing cultural immersion—use culture-assessment first if culture is opaque or contested
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Hire personas/segments (new grad, experienced, leader, remote, acquired); role success milestones and time-to-productivity targets; current onboarding touchpoints and failure points (IT, HR, manager, team); culture and EVP elements that must be experienced, not just told; owner map (HR, manager, buddy, IT) and measurement plan.

**Nice-to-have:** Exit-interview themes from early leavers, manager capacity by cohort, system provisioning lead times, and examples of high-performing new hires’ ramp paths.

**When data is thin:** Interview recent hires at days 7, 30, and 90 and build a provisional journey around observed friction; do not assume a policy exists because it appears in a welcome deck.
## Procedure
1. **Segment the new-hire population.** Separate journeys where needs materially differ, such as remote engineers, frontline hires, leaders, and acquired employees. Output: persona matrix and priority cohort.
2. **Define the destination for each segment.** Translate role success into time-bound milestones for access, task competence, network, cultural fluency, and manager check-ins. Output: milestone scorecard.
3. **Inventory the lived journey.** Plot preboarding through the first review, recording who triggers each touchpoint, what the hire receives, and where waiting or duplication occurs. Output: current journey map.
4. **Design the ramp sequence.** Place administrative readiness before productive work, schedule role practice with feedback, create purposeful social connections, and convert culture statements into observable rituals. Output: 30-60-90 journey and runbook.
5. **Assign delivery ownership and learn.** Give HR, manager, buddy, IT, and the hire explicit responsibilities; instrument completion, confidence, time-to-first contribution, and early exits. Output: operating calendar and review loop.
## Output Contract
Produce an onboarding roadmap and runbook by employee segment. It contains milestone definitions, touchpoints, owners, triggers, content, service-level expectations, and measures from preboarding through the early performance conversation. The artifact lets a manager run a consistent ramp while preserving the necessary role-specific variation.
## Evidence
Use timestamps from provisioning, learning systems, and role milestones as factual journey evidence. Distinguish a hire’s reported experience from an assertion about attrition causation. Record which milestones are managerial judgment, especially for knowledge-work roles. Protect free-text feedback and cohort results where a small group could identify an individual.
## Checks
- Day-one access and equipment are tested from the new hire’s perspective, not merely marked shipped.
- Every milestone has one accountable owner and a completion signal.
- The journey includes practice and feedback, not only information consumption.
- Remote and acquired hires receive an intentionally adapted social connection plan.
- Time-to-productivity is defined by role output rather than course completion.
## Failure Modes
- Treating orientation as the whole journey and ending support after the first week.
- Giving every cohort the same agenda despite different access, network, and role-learning needs.
- Naming a buddy without protecting time for the relationship or explaining its purpose.
- Measuring attendance at welcome sessions while ignoring whether the hire can perform core work.
