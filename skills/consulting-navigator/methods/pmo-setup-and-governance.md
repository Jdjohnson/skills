# PMO Setup and Governance
## Purpose
Design and stand up a central PMO that sets delivery standards, reporting, and governance so multi-project execution is comparable and controllable. The design concerns a permanent multi-project operating system, not the plan for a single project or a temporary cutover office.
## Use
- Standing up or resetting a Project/Program Management Office with clear mandate, services, and authority
- Standardizing portfolio reporting, methods, and stage gates across many projects
- Defining governance forums, escalation paths, and PMO service catalog
## Avoid
- You only need to run one project well—use pmbok-framework or prince2 on that project
- The real problem is org-wide change adoption, not delivery standards—use organizational-change-management
- You need benefits tracking design more than an office model—use benefits-realization-management
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Portfolio or multi-project landscape and pain points; executive mandate and desired PMO type (supportive, controlling, directive); existing methods, tools, and reporting fragments; resource and budget envelope for the PMO; decision rights expectations from sponsors and business units.

**Nice-to-have:** portfolio performance data, PM capability assessments, tool contracts, peer benchmarks, and candidate staff profiles.

**When data is thin:** start with a small service catalogue and two reporting standards, then earn expanded authority through a defined review after one portfolio cycle.
## Procedure
1. **Diagnose the portfolio control problem.** Compare project health definitions, artifacts, stage gates, escalations, and delivery outcomes across representative programs. Output: pain-point baseline.
2. **Choose mandate and PMO posture.** Agree whether the office will advise, enforce, or directly manage, including what it may stop, challenge, or approve. Output: mandate and authority charter.
3. **Design the service catalogue.** Define standards, coaching, portfolio reporting, assurance, tooling, resource support, and talent development services; distinguish mandatory from optional. Output: service catalogue and service levels.
4. **Build governance mechanics.** Set forum purposes, membership, cadence, entry criteria, decision rights, escalation clocks, and artifact templates. Output: governance map.
5. **Stand up the operating model.** Size roles, budget, technology, data ownership, intake process, and transition steps from existing teams. Output: staffing and implementation roadmap.
6. **Prove value and adjust.** Pilot with a portfolio slice, measure reporting comparability and decision latency, then remove low-value controls. Output: maturity plan.
## Output Contract
A target-state PMO design covering mandate, scope, authority, service catalogue, governance forums, portfolio data model, standards, roles, funding, implementation waves, and success measures. It must show where business-unit autonomy remains and how the office avoids becoming an approval queue with no delivery insight.
## Evidence
Use project artifacts, milestone outcomes, and forum records as observed evidence. Treat claimed reporting effort or anticipated adoption as estimates unless timed. State assumptions about executive sponsorship, data availability, and the authority delegated to the office. Preserve dissent from business units where a uniform standard would impose disproportionate cost. Do not label a missing status update as poor delivery performance.
## Checks
- Each PMO service has a customer, owner, cost, and measurable reason to exist.
- Governance forums have distinct decisions rather than overlapping status meetings.
- Portfolio measures use common definitions and roll up from source data.
- Mandate matches staff capacity and sponsor authority.
- Pilot feedback identifies controls that reduce noise, delay, or duplicate reporting.
## Failure Modes
- Launching a reporting factory that cannot resolve dependencies or escalate decisions.
- Mandating templates without training, tools, or time to use them.
- Giving the PMO accountability for outcomes but no authority over gates or resources.
- Combining investment, delivery, and technical decisions in one overloaded forum.
- Measuring maturity by artifacts produced rather than portfolio decisions improved.
