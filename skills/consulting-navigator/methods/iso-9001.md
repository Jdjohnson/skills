# ISO 9001
## Purpose
Assess and design a certifiable quality management system against ISO 9001 requirements so leadership can decide what must change for conformity, audit readiness, and continual improvement of customer outcomes.
## Use
- Customers, bids, or regulators require a certified quality management system.
- Quality depends on undocumented heroics and inconsistent process follow-through.
- Internal audit findings repeat because corrective action is not closed-loop.
- Process ownership, risk-based thinking, or management review cannot be evidenced.
- Sites or suppliers run incompatible quality practices without a shared standard.
- The goal is certification readiness, not full total-quality culture change.
## Avoid
- The need is statistical defect reduction on a single process—use six-sigma or statistical-process-control.
- The constraint is flow or throughput, not QMS compliance—use theory-of-constraints or value-stream-mapping.
- Only a lightweight improvement habit is required—use pdca-cycle or kaizen.
- The organization holds a current certificate and needs a narrow fix, not clause-level system redesign.
## Inputs
**Must-have:** QMS scope (products, services, sites, processes, justified exclusions); current process documentation, roles, and quality records; customer and regulatory quality requirements; gap evidence from internal audits or management reviews.

**Nice-to-have:** Prior certification or surveillance reports; supplier quality agreements; complaint and delivery-quality metrics; risk registers and competence matrices.

**When data is missing:** Treat undocumented processes as uncontrolled and assumed conformity as unverified until evidenced.
## Procedure
1. **Frame scope and context.** Confirm products, sites, interested parties, and exclusions; produce a QMS boundary statement for the audit trail.
2. **Map clause-aligned processes and ownership.** Inventory core and support processes against requirement areas (context, leadership, planning, support, operation, performance evaluation, improvement); produce a process–owner–record matrix.
3. **Assess conformity with evidence.** Rate each requirement conforms / partial / gap using records, interviews, and observation; produce a clause-level gap register with severity and risk impact.
4. **Design controls and risk-based treatments.** For each gap, specify procedures, competence, monitoring, and risk controls that close it without adding bureaucracy; produce a prioritized remediation backlog.
5. **Stand up PDCA governance.** Define internal audit plan, nonconformity handling, corrective action, and management review cadence; produce an operating calendar and evidence checklist for audit readiness.
6. **Validate readiness.** Sample records end-to-end from order through delivery and feedback; produce a readiness scorecard of residual risks and open actions before external audit.
## Output Contract
A clause-aligned gap assessment and certification-ready system design package: scope statement; process–owner–record map; gap register with evidence notes; remediation plan with owners and dates; audit and management-review calendar; and a go/no-go recommendation on certification readiness.
## Evidence
- **Observed:** documented procedures in force, signed records, measured process results, auditor-witnessed practice.
- **Inferred:** systemic failure when the same gap repeats across sites or product lines.
- **Assumptions:** temporary workarounds treated as non-standard until formalized.
- **Unknowns:** unobserved sites, suppliers, or shifts stay open risks; flag as sampling limits.
Thin evidence → expand sampling or label “insufficient evidence,” never green a clause on aspiration.
## Checks
- Every major process has a named owner, defined inputs/outputs, and identifiable records.
- Each gap cites obligation and evidence type, not opinion alone.
- Exclusions are justified and do not hide customer-facing process failures.
- Corrective action links nonconformity → root cause → action → effectiveness check.
- Management review inputs and outputs are defined and schedulable.
- Remediation is prioritized by customer and regulatory risk, not document volume.
## Failure Modes
- A document library that does not match shop-floor reality (paper QMS).
- Claiming risk-based thinking while only listing risks with no process linkage.
- Over-engineering procedures for low-risk activities while core handoffs stay tribal.
- Treating certification as a one-time project instead of a maintained system.
- Closing audit findings with training alone when process design is the defect.
