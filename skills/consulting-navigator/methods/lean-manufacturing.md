# Lean Manufacturing
## Purpose
Redesign the end-to-end production system to eliminate waste, establish pull and flow, and maximize customer value per unit of resource. It joins layout, work methods, scheduling, equipment reliability, and continuous improvement.
## Use
- You need an end-to-end operating design that eliminates waste and shortens flow time while protecting customer value
- Multiple waste types (inventory, defects, waiting, overproduction) must be attacked as one system
- Leadership will sustain standards, pull, and continuous improvement—not a one-off cost cut
## Avoid
- The primary problem is process variation and defect statistics without flow redesign—use six-sigma or statistical-process-control
- You only need a single pull-signal or inventory mechanic—use kanban-system or just-in-time
- The constraint is a single bottleneck and flow is otherwise acceptable—use theory-of-constraints or bottleneck-analysis
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Value-stream map of the target product family (current state); customer demand rate, mix, and delivery requirements; process cycle times, changeovers, scrap/rework, and downtime data; inventory, WIP, and lead-time baselines by stage; cross-functional authority to change layout, standards, and scheduling rules.

**Nice-to-have:** ergonomics, maintenance losses, quality escapes, and capital limits.

**When data is thin:** time a representative product family through the whole plant, label estimates explicitly, and avoid a future-state layout commitment until constraints are observed.
## Procedure
1. **Choose a product family and observe current flow.** Follow material and information from order release to shipment. Quantify value-added time, queue time, defect loops, downtime, changeovers, and inventory at each stage.
2. **Set customer pace and design principles.** Calculate takt from demand and net available time. Define where continuous flow is possible, where supermarkets are required, and which quality or safety conditions cannot be compromised.
3. **Create the target operating system.** Design cells or flow lines, point-of-use materials, work sequence, pull loops, pitch, standard work, visual controls, and equipment-maintenance routines as one connected arrangement.
4. **Plan the transformation.** Prioritize changes that unlock flow, such as quality-at-source, changeover reduction, layout moves, or cross-training. Specify milestones, training, capital needs, and the temporary controls needed during transition.
5. **Deploy and learn.** Implement by area, measure lead time, first-pass yield, WIP, schedule adherence, and safety, then use daily problem solving to correct gaps. Extend the design only after the first area is stable.
## Output Contract
Deliver a target-state design for the selected product family: current and future value-stream views, takt and flow logic, layout or cell concept, pull architecture, standard-work requirements, quality and maintenance controls, deployment roadmap, and performance measures. It must show how the components reinforce one another and which leadership decisions unlock the next phase.
## Evidence
Use observed cycle times, downtime, scrap, and physical inventory for the current state. Treat demand smoothing, supplier responsiveness, and expected changeover gains as assumptions with test dates. Do not label motion or waiting as waste without checking safety, regulatory, or product-integrity purpose. Separate a demonstrated improvement from a target condition that still depends on capital or behavior change.
## Checks
- Takt uses actual customer demand and usable production time.
- The future state identifies a pacemaker process and how upstream work is released.
- Proposed flow does not transfer defects or ergonomic risk downstream.
- Each deployment milestone has a named operational measure and a stability criterion.
## Failure Modes
- Moving machines into a U-cell while leaving batch release and quality hold rules unchanged.
- Calling headcount removal a lean gain when output, safety, and lead time do not improve.
- Installing pull signals without reducing changeover, downtime, or process variation.
- Mapping a future state once and never teaching the standard work needed to sustain it.
