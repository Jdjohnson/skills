# Synergies Valuation Framework
## Purpose
Quantify, time, risk-adjust, and track cost and revenue synergies so deal value and PMI targets rest on a single defensible model. It values incremental combination benefits, rather than the standalone enterprise or the integration program itself.
## Use
- Building a defensible, timed, risk-adjusted synergy case for bid, IC, or lender packages
- Linking diligence evidence to line-item cost and revenue synergy drivers
- Setting baselines and tracking realization against PMI execution
## Avoid
- You need enterprise valuation without synergy decomposition—use discounted-cash-flow-valuation or valuation-multiples
- Integration sequencing is undefined and you only have a wish list—pair with post-merger-integration-roadmap
- The deal has no material combination benefits and you are only screening markets—use commercial-due-diligence alone
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Baseline standalone financials for buyer and target (or carve-out); diligence-backed cost, revenue, and investment drivers by initiative; timing, one-time costs, and probability or risk ratings per synergy; accounting and tax rules for how synergies hit the P&L and cash; ownership map for who will deliver each initiative post-close.
## Procedure
1. **Freeze standalone baselines.** Establish the buyer and target cost, revenue, headcount, volume, and cash baselines at a common scope and period. Remove items already in each plan so the model does not claim business-as-usual improvements.
2. **Build initiative-level drivers.** For procurement, specify spend category, addressable spend, price delta, and ramp. For revenue, identify customers, offer, conversion path, capacity, churn, and margin. Map each initiative to a unique owner and ledger line.
3. **Eliminate overlap and leakage.** Reconcile initiatives across functions, test whether the same site closure or customer cross-sell appears twice, and include dis-synergies such as attrition, lost volume, or stranded cost.
4. **Time and risk-adjust cash flows.** Apply probability, start date, ramp profile, one-time integration cost, tax treatment, and cash-versus-P&L classification. Produce gross, net, and probability-weighted views rather than one undifferentiated headline number.
5. **Create the realization control loop.** Lock baseline definitions at close, define monthly actuals, variance codes, evidence needed to claim realization, and escalation for delayed initiatives.
## Output Contract
A model with a traceable initiative register, driver calculations, gross and net P&L and cash bridges by period, one-time costs, probability-weighted valuation, assumptions, owners, and post-close realization scorecard. It must reconcile to the deal model and make double counting auditable. A single synergy headline or an integration task list does not satisfy the contract.
## Evidence
Use diligence findings, contracts, spend data, customer records, and approved plans as evidence. Label probability, adoption rate, price improvement, ramp date, and retention as assumptions with an owner and review date. Keep a separate field for management aspiration so it cannot silently become forecast. Claims of realized synergy require observable baseline-to-actual evidence, not merely completion of an integration activity.
## Checks
- Baselines exclude savings and growth already committed in standalone plans.
- Each value driver appears once and connects to a P&L or cash-flow line.
- Gross benefits, implementation costs, dis-synergies, taxes, and timing are separately visible.
- Revenue cases include customer, capacity, and margin constraints rather than applying a blanket uplift.
- The probability-weighted case reconciles mathematically to its initiative assumptions.
## Failure Modes
- Counting a purchasing saving in both procurement and operating-margin workstreams.
- Treating headcount removed from an organization chart as cash savings before severance, backfill, and stranded work are resolved.
- Taking a cross-sell forecast as revenue without considering channel conflict or sales capacity.
- Discounting a full-year saving from day one despite a nine-month systems migration.
- Declaring realization when a workstream closes, even though the income statement baseline has moved.
