# SWOT Analysis
## Purpose
Integrate internal strengths and weaknesses with external opportunities and threats into a single strategic position summary. The value comes from matching the four categories into choices, not from producing an unfiltered workshop wall.
## Use
- Synthesizing internal strengths/weaknesses with external opportunities/threats into one position view
- Framing strategy workshops before deeper industry, capability, or option work
- Communicating a concise strategic snapshot to mixed stakeholder groups
## Avoid
- You need rigorous industry structure—use five-forces instead of a shallow threats list
- You need disciplined macro categories—use pestle-analysis rather than freeform opportunities/threats
- You need VRIO-tested resources or activity economics—use resource-based-view or value-chain-analysis
- The four boxes are already filled and you need choices—use generic-strategies or ansoff-matrix
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Defined firm or unit scope and competitive context; evidence or informed judgments on internal strengths and weaknesses; evidence on external opportunities and threats (ideally informed by prior scans); decision or workshop question the SWOT must inform.
## Procedure
1. **Set a precise strategic question.** Name the business unit, market, time horizon, and decision. “How should we defend the mid-market over 18 months?” produces a usable synthesis; “What are our strengths?” does not.
2. **Separate internal from external evidence.** Gather internal capabilities, assets, constraints, and performance for strengths and weaknesses. Gather market changes, customer shifts, regulation, competitor moves, and macro conditions for opportunities and threats. Reject items placed in a box merely because they sound positive or negative.
3. **Make a short, evidenced four-box list.** Consolidate duplicates, state each item as a condition with consequence, and tag its source and confidence. Limit each quadrant to the issues material to the decision.
4. **Match the boxes.** Test strength–opportunity moves to pursue, weakness–opportunity gaps to repair or partner around, strength–threat defenses, and weakness–threat exposures to avoid. Capture the causal rationale for each pairing.
5. **Select implications.** Score the resulting moves for strategic fit, expected value, urgency, and feasibility. Name the few implications that need a follow-on analysis or a decision owner.
## Output Contract
A one-page position summary with four evidence-tagged quadrants, a set of explicit cross-quadrant matches, and a short list of strategic implications. It must distinguish conditions the organization controls from conditions it faces, and show why the priority implications follow from the synthesis. Four disconnected brainstorm lists are not an acceptable output.
## Evidence
Use operating metrics, customer evidence, and verified market observations as facts; describe leadership beliefs and forecasts as assumptions. Time-stamp external claims because opportunities and threats can expire. Do not turn an aspiration such as “innovative culture” into a strength without evidence of a repeatable advantage. Record disagreements instead of forcing consensus where the disputed item could alter a selected match.
## Checks
- Each item belongs unambiguously to an internal or external category.
- Every quadrant contains material conditions, not generic adjectives or project names.
- The number of items is small enough for participants to discuss trade-offs.
- Each chosen implication connects at least two quadrants and has a stated rationale.
- A reader can trace major claims to evidence or an explicit assumption.
## Failure Modes
- Listing “competition” as a threat without identifying the specific change or consequence.
- Calling a market trend a strength because the firm benefits from it.
- Treating “lack of budget” as an external threat when it is an internal allocation choice.
- Letting the loudest workshop participant fill all four boxes with pet initiatives.
- Ending at the matrix and never converting matches into strategic choices.
