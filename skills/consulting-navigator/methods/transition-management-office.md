# Transition Management Office
## Purpose
Design and run a dedicated Transition Management Office that governs people, process, and cutover risk across a transformation. It joins delivery milestones with readiness evidence so that a technically complete change is not mistaken for an adoptable one.
## Use
- Standing up dedicated governance for a large transformation or post-deal transition.
- Coordinating multi-wave change, cutover, and adoption beyond pure project delivery.
- Creating a single accountability hub for readiness, resistance, training, and benefits handoff.
## Avoid
- You only need classic project portfolio controls—use pmo-setup-and-governance.
- The change is small enough for a workstream lead without a standing office.
- Legal entity or deal integration mechanics dominate over people transition—use post-merger-integration-roadmap or m-and-a-integration-framework as the primary construct.
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Transformation or transition scope, waves, and critical dependencies; RACI for sponsors, workstream leads, and business owners; risk, readiness, and benefits reporting requirements; decision rights for cutover, exception handling, and stop/scale calls; resource model for TMO roles (change, comms, training, analytics).

**Nice-to-have:** prior lessons, stakeholder maps, service-continuity plans, baseline adoption metrics, and local change networks.

**When data is thin:** begin with a minimum readiness dashboard and a decision calendar; reserve firm wave commitments until owners validate dependencies and local impacts.
## Procedure
1. **Charter the office.** Define the transition outcome, scope, authority, funding, roles, escalation path, and relationship with the PMO. Name decisions the office owns versus decisions it only informs.
2. **Build an integrated wave map.** Connect technical release, process readiness, communications, training, people changes, data conversion, and service continuity for every wave. Surface cross-workstream dependencies and decision deadlines.
3. **Set readiness measures and thresholds.** Specify evidence for business, people, operational, and technical readiness; set red/amber/green definitions and the minimum conditions for cutover.
4. **Run the operating rhythm.** Hold risk triage, readiness reviews, sponsor decision forums, and local escalation sessions. Maintain one issue log with accountable owners, due dates, dependencies, and consequences of delay.
5. **Manage cutover and handoff.** Coordinate go/no-go decisions, contingency activation, hypercare, benefits tracking, and transfer of residual ownership into the line organization.
## Output Contract
The TMO run-model includes the charter, organization design, integrated transition plan, readiness scorecard, decision calendar, risk and dependency log, wave gate criteria, escalation protocol, and handoff plan. It gives a sponsor a single view of whether a wave can proceed and what decision is required. It is not a generic project status report.
## Evidence
Use signed plans, attendance, completed training demonstrations, system test results, and local owner attestations as observed evidence. Mark a reported readiness status as inferred until corroborated by the agreed test. State assumptions about local capacity, regulatory approvals, and change saturation. Separate a waived gate from a met gate, recording approver, expiry, compensating control, and residual risk.
## Checks
- Each wave has named business and technical accountable owners.
- Cutover criteria include operational adoption, not only system deployment.
- Dependencies have one source of truth and dated decision owners.
- Escalations reach a forum able to resolve them within the required window.
- Hypercare exit criteria transfer responsibility cleanly to operations.
## Failure Modes
- Duplicating the PMO’s milestone report without managing readiness.
- Letting every workstream define its own red/amber/green scale.
- Approving a wave because training was delivered, though proficiency is untested.
- Escalating issues after the decision date has passed.
- Leaving local managers unaware that hypercare ownership has ended.
