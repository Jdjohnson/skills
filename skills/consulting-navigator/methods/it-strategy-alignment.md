# IT Strategy Alignment
## Purpose
Diagnose how well IT capabilities, portfolio, and operating model actually support business strategy and where the critical gaps sit. It measures fit against the organization’s stated strategic outcomes, rather than a generic technology-maturity rubric.
## Use
- Assessing whether the current IT portfolio and operating model support stated business strategy
- Resetting IT investment priorities after a strategy refresh, M&A event, or digital pivot
- Explaining misalignment between business outcomes and technology spend or delivery
## Avoid
- You need a full enterprise architecture target state—use togaf
- You already know the gaps and need a multi-year digital program design—use digital-transformation-framework
- The issue is pure governance process maturity without strategy fit—use cobit or itil
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Documented business strategy, priorities, and critical outcomes; Current IT strategy, portfolio, and major programs; Capability map linking processes to systems and owners; Spend and demand data for major IT investments; Stakeholder views from business and technology leadership.

**Nice-to-have:** Outcome metrics, customer journeys, architecture principles, product roadmaps, and prior investment cases.

**When data is thin:** Establish a small set of signed-off strategic outcomes and sample the highest-spend initiatives; do not infer alignment from project names alone.
## Procedure
1. **Extract strategic outcomes.** Convert strategy statements into a limited set of measurable outcomes, such as reduce order-to-cash days or enter two new markets. Identify the business owner and target horizon for each.
2. **Map capabilities to outcomes.** Identify the business and technology capabilities that materially enable each outcome, including the process, data, system, and operating-model dependencies.
3. **Inventory investments and run services.** Classify portfolio spend, demand, applications, and major programs against those capabilities. Mark initiatives with no outcome link and outcomes with no accountable technology support.
4. **Assess fit and gaps.** Score contribution, health, dependency, time-to-value, and risk using evidence from delivery performance and stakeholder interviews. Compare strategic capability investment with commodity run cost without assuming all run cost is waste.
5. **Prioritize the misalignment.** Rank gaps by strategic consequence, urgency, and effort; identify funding to stop, protect, re-sequence, or investigate. Capture disagreements between business and IT as findings.
6. **Agree the decision agenda.** Present an outcome-to-capability-to-investment map and the few portfolio decisions needed to restore fit. Define how alignment will be revisited when strategy changes.
## Output Contract
An alignment assessment with strategic outcomes, a capability map, investment and spend mapping, evidence-backed fit ratings, prioritized gaps, and a decision agenda. It must identify both orphan investments and unsupported outcomes, with ownership and rationale for recommended portfolio changes.
## Evidence
Board-approved strategy, portfolio actuals, service performance, and funded cases are observed. Stakeholder perceptions of value are informed judgments and should be attributed. State the scoring scale and weights before comparing capabilities. If an outcome lacks a measurable target, label the alignment rating provisional instead of claiming an objective score.
## Checks
- Every assessed initiative maps to a specific business outcome or is explicitly orphaned.
- Capability gaps distinguish missing capability from poor adoption, architecture debt, or funding delay.
- Portfolio totals reconcile to spend data at the reported level.
- Ratings include business and technology viewpoints where they differ.
- Recommended stops and starts include implications for strategic outcomes.
## Failure Modes
- Calling every digital project strategic because it has executive sponsorship.
- Using a generic maturity survey when the business strategy is unclear or ignored.
- Mapping applications to a capability without showing how that capability affects an outcome.
- Reprioritizing projects without examining locked-in spend and delivery dependencies.
