# Agile Transformation Framework
## Purpose
Design the operating-model, governance, and rollout path that moves an organization from traditional delivery to sustained agile ways of working. It changes the conditions around teams—funding, decisions, portfolio flow, and measures—so local ceremonies can produce lasting results.
## Use
- Designing an end-to-end shift from project/waterfall delivery to product-oriented agile ways of working
- Aligning team topology, funding, governance, and metrics with iterative delivery
- Scaling successful agile pilots into a coherent enterprise operating model
## Avoid
- You only need a single-team delivery method—use scrum-framework or kanban-system
- The problem is digital technology ambition rather than delivery-system redesign—use digital-transformation-framework
- You first need a baseline of agility maturity before designing the target state—use organizational-agility-assessment
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Current delivery model (roles, ceremonies, tooling, and governance); portfolio of products/value streams and major constraints; leadership intent on funding model, decision rights, and risk appetite; evidence from any agile pilots including what worked and what stalled.

**Nice-to-have:** Value-stream performance data, employee sentiment, architecture constraints, regulatory controls, and change capacity.

**When data is thin:** Map one representative value stream and run a constrained pilot; do not declare an enterprise target state from training attendance alone.
## Procedure
1. **Establish the transformation case.** Quantify the delivery problem in lead time, predictability, quality, or customer outcomes, and align leaders on the decisions they are willing to change.
2. **Diagnose the delivery system.** Trace demand from portfolio intake through teams, approvals, release, and operations. Identify bottlenecks in funding, dependencies, architecture, governance, and measures—not just team practices.
3. **Design the target operating model.** Define durable product or value-stream boundaries, team topology, product ownership, communities of practice, decision rights, and interfaces with risk and operations.
4. **Redesign portfolio and governance mechanisms.** Replace annual project commitments where possible with outcome-based funding, rolling priorities, lightweight controls, and review forums synchronized to team cadence.
5. **Launch a learning wave.** Select value streams with meaningful demand and manageable dependencies, prepare leaders and coaches, establish baseline measures, and operate the new model long enough to expose constraints.
6. **Scale through evidence.** Compare pilot outcomes with baseline, correct systemic blockers, sequence additional waves, and institutionalize measures, capability development, and leadership routines.
## Output Contract
Provide a multi-wave operating-model roadmap that specifies target team and value-stream structure, portfolio and funding changes, governance controls, rollout waves, leadership actions, capability needs, baseline metrics, and decision checkpoints. It must show how the wider system changes around teams, not merely prescribe a set of sprint ceremonies.
## Evidence
Observed evidence includes flow metrics, approval queues, delivery outcomes, staffing assignments, and pilot results. Treat claims about cultural readiness or expected productivity as hypotheses with tests. Document non-negotiable regulatory controls separately from habits that can be redesigned. Report pilot results by value stream because enterprise averages can hide a blocked dependency.
## Checks
- Target team boundaries follow customer value and dependencies, not only the existing org chart.
- Funding, governance, and metrics are changed alongside delivery rituals.
- Each wave has measurable outcomes, an executive owner, and a dependency plan.
- Risk, architecture, and operations participation is designed into the model.
- Scaling depends on demonstrated learning rather than copying a pilot’s labels.
## Failure Modes
- Renaming departments as squads while retaining project funding and approval gates.
- Training teams without changing the executive decisions that create queues.
- Scaling a pilot that succeeded only because it had unusually few dependencies.
- Measuring velocity as transformation success while customer lead time worsens.
