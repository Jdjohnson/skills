# Change Leadership Development
## Purpose
Design and deliver the capability path that equips sponsors, change agents, and people managers to lead change effectively in live transformations. It builds practiced leadership behavior around a real initiative, rather than issuing a general management course catalogue.
## Use
- Building the sponsor, change-agent, and people-manager skills required to lead a specific transformation
- Designing a change leadership curriculum and practice path tied to live initiatives
- Closing capability gaps that keep well-designed change plans from being led well
## Avoid
- You need a readiness diagnosis before investing in leader development—use change-readiness-assessment
- You need general leadership development unrelated to a change agenda—use mentorship-program-design or competency-mapping alone
- The primary gap is communications content design, not leader skill—use communication-strategy-for-change
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Target change leadership roles (sponsors, agents, people managers); competency gaps observed in prior or current change efforts; live or near-term initiatives where leaders will practice; available development channels (coaching, cohort programs, action learning).

**Nice-to-have:** recordings of sponsor briefings, manager-pulse results, and feedback from people who experienced earlier rollouts.

**When data is thin:** start with observed critical moments and validate the gap through short leader and employee interviews before assigning development time.
## Procedure
1. **Name the change moments that leaders must handle.** Select the live decisions: launch, explaining trade-offs, surfacing resistance, or reinforcing new routines. Define what a sponsor, change agent, and manager must say and do at each moment.
2. **Diagnose capability by role.** Review communications, meeting observations, and manager conversations against a role-specific rubric. Separate a knowledge gap from a confidence, authority, or incentive problem; training cannot repair all four.
3. **Build a practice path.** Give sponsors narrative rehearsals and visible commitment routines; give change agents facilitation and resistance-coaching drills; give managers team-conversation scripts, listening practice, and escalation boundaries. Use cases from the actual transformation.
4. **Put learning into the operating cadence.** Schedule a sponsor clinic before major decisions, manager huddles before local rollout, and coached action-learning cycles after difficult conversations. Assign a coach and an accountable executive to each cohort.
5. **Observe application and adjust.** Sample actual town halls, team meetings, and resistance cases. Feed evidence into individual practice plans and remove systemic blockers such as absent decision rights or conflicting sponsor messages.
## Output Contract
Produce a role-based development roadmap containing the critical change moments, capability rubric, cohort plan, practice exercises, coaches, calendar, and measures of applied behavior. It must distinguish sponsor, change-agent, and manager obligations, identify changes to the transformation plan that leaders cannot solve alone, and set a review point after the first live application.
## Evidence
Treat recorded behavior, attendance, escalation logs, and employee feedback as observed evidence. Mark interpretations of leadership confidence or intent as inferred, and record who made that judgment. Label any assumed proficiency baseline or cohort grouping. If a leader has not yet faced the relevant moment, report readiness as untested rather than competent. Protect coaching notes; aggregate themes before reporting to sponsors.
## Checks
- Every capability is linked to a real upcoming leadership moment.
- Practice uses transformation cases, not generic personality exercises.
- The roadmap names an owner for observation and coaching after training.
- Sponsor visibility is measured by actions and follow-through, not course completion.
- Gaps caused by governance, workload, or incentives are escalated outside the curriculum.
## Failure Modes
- Sending managers to a single workshop while sponsors continue to disappear after launch announcements.
- Teaching a polished change narrative without rehearsing hostile questions or local trade-offs.
- Rating a leader as capable from self-assessment alone.
- Treating repeated escalations as a coaching deficit when decision authority is unclear.
