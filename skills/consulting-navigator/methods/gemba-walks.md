# Gemba Walks
## Purpose
Gather first-hand evidence by observing work at the place value is created and learning from frontline reality. Leaders go to the work with a focused question, see the conditions and handoffs directly, and ask respectful questions before prescribing a solution.
## Use
- Leaders need first-hand evidence of how work actually runs at the place of value creation.
- Validating process issues, safety risks, or standard adherence before prescribing fixes.
- Coaching frontline problem-solving and building a continuous-improvement culture.
## Avoid
- You need structured quantitative capability data—use statistical-process-control or six-sigma.
- The goal is a full waste map with timed lead-time analysis—use value-stream-mapping.
- You need a scoped improvement event that implements change, not just observation—use kaizen.
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Access to the actual workplace during normal operating conditions; a clear observation purpose or question (safety, flow, quality, standard work); willingness to go see with respect and ask rather than prescribe on the spot; optional current standard work or KPI context for the area.

**Conduct rule:** communicate the learning purpose and prohibit performance-rating inspections.
## Procedure
1. Choose one answerable question or route and schedule the visit during normal work. Read standards and safety rules to prepare questions, not a verdict.
2. Begin at the point where the customer request enters and follow one item, case, or worker journey through actual handoffs. Observe motion, waiting, rework, information flow, queues, and deviations from the stated standard.
3. Ask open questions such as “What makes this step difficult?” and “What happens when the signal is late?” Listen to the person doing the work, ask permission before photographs or notes, and avoid correcting work mid-task unless safety requires it.
4. Record facts separately from interpretations: location, time, condition, observable sequence, quotation, and question raised. Mark the frequency of an observation as unknown unless it is measured across a representative sample.
5. Close with a short reflection with the frontline team: check that the observer understood the work and ask which problem they would solve first. Thank participants and explain how notes will be used.
6. Synthesize observation themes, compare them with standards and performance data, and select a small number of questions for PDCA, root-cause analysis, or kaizen. Return to the area with what was learned and the status of any commitments.
## Output Contract
Produce a dated observation record with purpose, route, participants, context, facts, frontline insights, uncertainties, and follow-up questions. Include photographs only where permitted. It is an evidence and coaching artifact, not a worker report card or approved redesign.
## Evidence
What the observer sees or hears at a stated time is observed. Interpretations about causes, frequency, intent, or prevalence are inferences. The chosen route and normal-operation definition are assumptions. Never infer competence from one workaround; verify whether the system made it necessary.
## Checks
- The observation purpose is narrow enough to guide where and what to see.
- Notes distinguish direct facts from the observer’s explanation.
- The walk includes the work and handoffs, not only a conference-room discussion.
- Frontline participants have an opportunity to correct misunderstanding.
- Follow-up owners and dates are communicated back to the area.
## Failure Modes
- A manager announces the solution during the first five minutes, turning the visit into an audit.
- Observing a staged demonstration instead of normal work during the actual demand pattern.
- Recording a worker’s workaround without tracing the upstream condition that forced it.
- Leaving with notes and never returning, which teaches staff that speaking up has no value.
