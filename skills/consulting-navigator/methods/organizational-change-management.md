# Organizational Change Management
## Purpose
Plan and run the people-side workstreams—sponsors, stakeholders, communication, training, and resistance—so a defined change is adopted and sustained. It integrates those workstreams with technical delivery so go-live is not mistaken for behavior change.
## Use
- Any material change in systems, structure, process, or operating model with significant people impact
- Adoption risk is as high as technical delivery risk
- Multiple workstreams need one integrated people-side plan
## Avoid
- The change is purely technical with no role, process, or behavior impact—use change-control-process
- You only need individual adoption stages for a discrete change—use adkar-model
- Leadership sponsorship and vision for change do not yet exist—establish sponsorship before running full OCM
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Defined change scope, timeline, and success metrics; impacted roles, groups, and geographies; sponsor map and current leadership commitment; baseline change readiness or engagement signals; technical or project plan the people workstreams must track.

**Nice-to-have:** Labor relations constraints, prior change lessons, local leader capacity, service disruption windows, and an adoption measurement baseline.

**When data is thin:** Segment impacts through structured manager and employee interviews before publishing broad messages; do not use a global communications plan as evidence that local roles are ready.
## Procedure
1. **Define the change and adoption outcomes.** Link the technical release or structural move to the behaviors, roles, and performance measures that must change. Output: change case and adoption definition.
2. **Assess impact and readiness by segment.** Map who experiences what change, when, and how severe it is; identify sponsor strength, capability gaps, and likely resistance. Output: impact-and-readiness heat map.
3. **Build integrated workstreams.** Design sponsor actions, stakeholder engagement, communication, training, manager enablement, and resistance response against the project milestones. Output: OCM roadmap and dependency plan.
4. **Mobilize sponsors and managers.** Give leaders visible actions, talking points, escalation routes, and coaching moments that fit their affected populations. Output: sponsor and manager action calendar.
5. **Measure adoption and adapt.** Track behavior, proficiency, sentiment, service impact, and resistance signals after each release; redirect support where adoption lags. Output: adoption dashboard and intervention log.
## Output Contract
Produce an integrated people-side delivery plan with impact segments, sponsor commitments, engagement tactics, communication moments, learning paths, resistance responses, owners, and adoption measures. It visibly aligns with project milestones and makes clear which actions happen before, during, and after deployment. A communication calendar alone does not satisfy the contract.
## Evidence
Mark role impacts as validated by workflow observation, manager input, or employee research. Treat readiness scores as a sampling view, not a claim about every individual. State which adoption outcomes are leading indicators, such as training practice completion, and which are lagging business results. Protect anonymity in resistance reporting so employees can identify real obstacles without retaliation risk.
## Checks
- Each impacted group has a specific behavior change, support route, and accountable leader.
- Sponsor commitments are observable actions, not merely names on a governance chart.
- Training is tied to changed work and is available before the changed task is required.
- Project cutover, communications, manager conversations, and hypercare dates are synchronized.
- Adoption measures reveal both use and proficiency, not only attendance or system logins.
## Failure Modes
- Sending a launch email and treating awareness as willingness and ability to use the new process.
- Asking managers to reinforce a change without giving them time, scripts, or answers to local workflow questions.
- Applying the same training to highly affected operators and lightly affected executives.
- Closing the change workstream at go-live while resistance and workarounds emerge during hypercare.
