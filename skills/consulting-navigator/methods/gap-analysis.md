# Gap Analysis
## Purpose
Diagnose the delta between current and desired performance or capability to prioritize what must close. It puts the as-is and to-be state on the same measurement scale so leadership can see which shortfalls matter, how large they are, and what constraints shape closure.
## Use
- Current performance or capability can be scored against an explicit desired state.
- Prioritization of initiatives depends on size and importance of shortfalls.
- A transformation or improvement case needs a transparent baseline-to-target delta.
## Avoid
- You need peer comparison norms rather than internal target deltas—use competitive-benchmarking or operational-benchmarking.
- Root causes of a known shortfall are needed, not the size of the gap—use root-cause-analysis.
- The problem is pure environmental scanning without a target state—use pestle-analysis or swot-analysis.
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Explicit desired-state criteria or target performance levels; current-state measures or capability scores on the same dimensions; evidence sources for both current and target (data, standards, strategy); weighting or priority logic for which gaps matter most; constraints that bound feasible closure of gaps.

**Scale rule:** use a rubric before comparing qualitative current ratings with numeric targets.
## Procedure
1. State the decision the assessment will inform and select dimensions that the target state genuinely requires, such as service level, control maturity, cycle time, skill coverage, or system capability.
2. Define the target threshold, evidence source, date, and scoring rubric for each dimension. Separate mandatory minimums from aspirational ambitions so they do not receive the same urgency by default.
3. Establish the current baseline using the same definition and observation period. Capture dispersion across sites, functions, or customer segments rather than masking it with a single enterprise average.
4. Calculate the gap in the native unit or score each dimension against importance, risk, and feasibility. A two-point shortfall in a regulated control can outrank a larger convenience gap.
5. Investigate the most consequential gaps enough to distinguish a missing capability, insufficient capacity, inconsistent adoption, or an unrealistic target. Do not claim a root cause without further analysis.
6. Create a closure roadmap that names the intervention, owner, dependency, resource estimate, milestone, and leading measure. Reassess at defined intervals as the baseline or target changes.
## Output Contract
Deliver a current-versus-target matrix with definitions, evidence, dates, gap size, priority, risk, feasibility, owner, and closure action. A heat map must link to underlying numbers. The artifact supports what to close first, not a broad complaint list.
## Evidence
System measures, audits, and direct observations are observed evidence. Capability scores derived from interviews or sampled evidence are inferences and must retain the rubric. Strategic targets, weighting choices, and feasible improvement rates are assumptions approved by the accountable leader. When a baseline is unavailable, call the gap unmeasured rather than assigning a convenient red/amber/green status.
## Checks
- Every row compares like with like: same unit, definition, scope, and time basis.
- Target thresholds have an owner and a reason, such as a regulation, strategy, or service promise.
- Priority does not equal gap size alone; significance and closing constraints are visible.
- Averages do not conceal a site whose gap would prevent the overall target.
- Roadmap milestones have an observable leading indicator before the final outcome date.
## Failure Modes
- Setting all targets to “best in class” without considering the operating model or investment case.
- Scoring a capability as mature because a policy exists, although teams do not use it.
- Comparing last quarter’s actuals with a target from a different demand or staffing context.
- Treating a large but low-consequence preference gap as more urgent than a compliance breach.
