# Job Analysis & Evaluation
## Purpose
Decompose job content and evaluate relative organizational worth so roles can be graded, compared, and paid consistently. It evaluates the job as designed, not the incumbent’s performance, tenure, or negotiating power.
## Use
- Documenting job content (duties, responsibilities, requirements) and sizing relative organizational value for grading and pay architecture
- Creating a defensible job hierarchy after growth, reorg, or integration
- Aligning titles, spans, and compensation bands to actual work
## Avoid
- You need behavioral excellence criteria for development, not job worth—use competency-mapping
- You need the employee offer story for attraction—use employee-value-proposition
- You only need headcount supply/demand plans—use workforce-planning
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Job incumbent and manager interviews or questionnaires; Current org charts, titles, and job descriptions; Evaluation factors or grading method (e.g., factor comparison, point factor); Compensation market data if linking grades to pay; Business process context for how work actually flows.

**Nice-to-have:** Comparable evaluated jobs, union or statutory requirements, compensation philosophy, and recent organization-design decisions.

**When data is thin:** Use structured interviews with both incumbent and manager, record disputed scope, and withhold final grade calibration until the role’s decision authority is verified.
## Procedure
1. **Define the job population and evaluation basis.** Confirm whether the exercise covers all jobs, a function, or merged organizations; select the approved point-factor or factor-comparison method and calibration jobs.
2. **Collect job facts.** Gather duties, outputs, reporting relationships, decisions, financial or people impact, working conditions, knowledge, and problem-solving demands through questionnaires, interviews, and process observation.
3. **Write a job profile.** Distill the role into purpose, accountabilities, scope, decision rights, interfaces, required knowledge, and constraints. Resolve differences between title, formal description, and actual work with the manager.
4. **Evaluate factors consistently.** Rate only job requirements against defined factors such as know-how, problem solving, accountability, and working conditions. Cite factual evidence for each level and do not reward an exceptional incumbent.
5. **Calibrate relative worth.** Compare factor profiles against anchor jobs, test internal consistency across similar roles, and challenge grade compression or inversions. Re-evaluate inconsistent evidence rather than forcing a desired grade.
6. **Build grade and communication outputs.** Group evaluated roles into grades, connect grades to pay ranges only after compensation review, and give managers a defensible explanation of the role-based result and appeal route.
## Output Contract
An evaluated job file for each role, containing a validated job profile, factor ratings with rationale, total points or comparative ranking, proposed grade, calibration comparators, unresolved scope questions, and decision record. For the population, provide a grade architecture and a list of title or pay anomalies requiring action.
## Evidence
Interviews describe work but may reflect individual preference or outdated practice; cross-check them with process artifacts and manager confirmation. Keep observed job facts separate from evaluation judgment. State the factor definitions, weighting, anchors, and grading cutoffs in force. Never use salary, gender, incumbent performance, or external market premium to determine relative internal job worth.
## Checks
- Similar roles use the same factors, definitions, and evidence standard.
- Evaluation addresses the role’s ongoing design, not temporary project work.
- Point totals and grade boundaries reconcile to the approved method.
- Calibration compares genuine job content, not titles that happen to match.
- Job profiles identify decision scope, scale, and interfaces clearly enough to support appeals.
## Failure Modes
- Letting a manager’s desired pay outcome determine the factor ratings.
- Copying a generic job description that omits actual decision authority.
- Evaluating high-performing incumbents rather than the job.
- Combining market pricing with job evaluation until internal value and external scarcity are indistinguishable.
