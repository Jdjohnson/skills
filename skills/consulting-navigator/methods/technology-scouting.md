# Technology Scouting
## Purpose
Gather and filter external technology signals into a prioritized scouting view of what is emerging, maturing, and relevant to the firm. It is a repeatable external sensing loop, not a portfolio allocation decision or an implementation design.
## Use
- Systematically identifying and filtering emerging technologies relevant to strategy or operations
- Feeding horizon views into portfolio, R&D, or investment decisions
- Comparing external technology signals against internal capability gaps before major bets
## Avoid
- You already know the technology and need deployment design—use iot-strategy, ai-strategy-and-roadmap, or digital-transformation-framework
- You need full alternative futures under uncertainty—use scenario-planning
- The question is pure macro non-tech context—use pestle-analysis
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Strategic domains or problem spaces to scout against; time horizons of interest (near, mid, far); sources for patents, startups, vendors, academia, and competitors; evaluation criteria (maturity, fit, risk, adoptability); internal capability baseline for comparison.
## Procedure
1. **Write scouting questions.** Translate strategic problems into technology search domains, use cases, constraints, and time horizons. Define exclusions so the team does not collect interesting but irrelevant demonstrations.
2. **Scan diverse signal sources.** Search patents, research publications, startups, standards bodies, vendor releases, deployments, funding, and competitor moves. Capture the original signal, date, source type, and what it suggests rather than copying marketing conclusions.
3. **Normalize and cluster signals.** Group signals into technology themes, distinguish an enabling component from an application, remove duplicates, and separate early research from evidence of deployed performance.
4. **Assess maturity and relevance.** Score technical maturity, adoption evidence, strategic fit, integration burden, regulatory exposure, talent availability, economics, and capability gap. Use confidence bands where evidence is sparse.
5. **Produce an action-oriented watchlist.** Place themes on near, mid, and far horizons; recommend monitor, engage, experiment, partner, or deprioritize. State the next evidence trigger that would move each priority theme.
## Output Contract
A scouting report containing the search questions, signal inventory with source and date, technology-theme map, maturity and relevance assessments, capability-gap comparison, horizon placement, and prioritized action watchlist. It must explain why a technology is relevant to a stated strategic domain and what evidence would change its position. A collage of vendor slides or a generic trend list is not enough.
## Evidence
Treat a patent, standard, published benchmark, customer deployment, or direct technical test as evidence of a specific claim only. A funding round or vendor roadmap may signal interest but does not prove performance or adoption readiness. Record source independence and confidence; three articles repeating one press release count as one signal. State the internal capability baseline and avoid rating a technology “easy to adopt” without architecture, data, and talent evidence.
## Checks
- Every theme maps to a defined strategic domain or is explicitly excluded.
- Maturity ratings distinguish laboratory demonstration, pilot, repeatable deployment, and scaled adoption.
- At least two independent signal types support high-priority claims where available.
- Scoring criteria are applied consistently across competing technologies.
- Each recommended experiment has a learning question, owner, cost range, and stop condition.
## Failure Modes
- Letting the latest vendor roadshow set the search agenda.
- Equating patent volume with a usable product or an attractive business case.
- Ranking technologies without considering data quality, integration constraints, or scarce skills.
- Treating a proof of concept at one company as evidence that the technology scales in this operating context.
- Refreshing a trend deck annually while leaving stale watchlist decisions in place.
