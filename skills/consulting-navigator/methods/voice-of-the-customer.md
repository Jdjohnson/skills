# Voice of the Customer
## Purpose
Systematically gather, structure, and prioritize customer requirements from multiple listening posts into a usable needs baseline for design and quality. It turns scattered statements into a maintained hierarchy of needs with traceability and priority, rather than elevating the loudest anecdote.
## Use
- You must systematically collect and structure customer needs, wants, and pain points
- Multiple channels of customer input need a common requirements backbone
- Downstream design methods (QFD, Kano, roadmap) need customer language as input
## Avoid
- You only need a one-off statistically designed questionnaire—use survey-design-methodology
- You already have multi-source evidence to reconcile—use customer-evidence-triangulation
- The job is to model jobs and outcomes rather than stated requirements—use jobs-to-be-done
## Inputs
**Must-have:**
- Target customer segments and use contexts
- Collection channels (interviews, surveys, support, win-loss, social, observations)
- Coding scheme for needs, importance, and satisfaction or performance gaps
- Governance for who owns, updates, and prioritizes the requirement set

**Nice-to-have:** consent scripts, account weighting, journey stages, a verbatim repository, and product taxonomy.

**When data is thin:** recruit across contexts, retain statements as provisional, and exclude internal requests.
## Procedure
1. **Set the listening frame.** Specify the target segments, decision to inform, contexts to cover, collection period, and sampling gaps. Choose channels that reveal both stated needs and recurring operational complaints. Output: listening plan.
2. **Collect and preserve raw voice.** Gather interviews, support contacts, survey comments, observations, and win-loss notes with segment and context tags. Keep the original words beside any interpretation. Output: tagged statement set.
3. **Code statements into needs.** Remove duplicates without erasing meaningful differences, then cluster statements into primary, secondary, and where useful tertiary need categories. Phrase each requirement in customer language, not as an internal solution. Output: requirement hierarchy.
4. **Assess importance and performance.** Combine direct importance ratings, frequency, severity, satisfaction gaps, and strategic segment relevance. Separate an urgent rare issue from a common mild issue. Output: priority scorecard.
5. **Validate and govern.** Review clusters with customers or frontline teams, resolve disputed coding, publish traceable priorities, and set a refresh cadence and owner. Output: approved needs baseline.
## Output Contract
A prioritized customer-requirements set containing the raw statement record, segment and context tags, need hierarchy, importance and performance evidence, confidence, unresolved contradictions, and ownership for updates. The deliverable shows how each priority was derived and can feed design specifications, quality deployment, or roadmap decisions. It is not a single survey report or an unstructured collection of quotes.
## Evidence
Treat recorded customer statements and observed behavior as evidence, but do not confuse frequency with importance. Label coder interpretation, merged clusters, and segment weights as assumptions. Preserve dissenting statements and sample limitations. A sales representative's summary is a lead until tied to an identifiable conversation or pattern. Use a consistent coding guide; when two coders disagree materially, record the reason and resolve it before assigning a high priority.
## Checks
- Requirements are phrased as needs, not preselected features or internal projects.
- Every high-priority need traces to raw statements and a defined segment.
- Collection covers more than one listening channel or explicitly states the gap.
- Importance, satisfaction gap, and evidence strength are not collapsed into an unexplained score.
- A named owner can refresh priorities as new feedback arrives.
## Failure Modes
- Letting one strategic account's feature request stand in for the market.
- Rewriting customer words into solution language before the need is understood.
- Pooling incompatible segments and losing the context that makes a requirement meaningful.
- Treating NPS or satisfaction averages as a list of actionable requirements.
- Coding a large archive once and never updating it after product or market changes.
