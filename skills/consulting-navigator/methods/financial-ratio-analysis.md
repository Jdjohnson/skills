# Financial Ratio Analysis
## Purpose
Diagnose financial health and performance drivers by decomposing profitability, liquidity, efficiency, and leverage ratios. Comparable measures reveal whether a weak return comes from margin, asset use, financing, or a short-term funding strain.
## Use
- Diagnosing profitability, liquidity, efficiency, and leverage drivers from financial statements.
- Benchmarking a business unit or peer set on a common ratio scorecard.
- Explaining ROE or ROIC changes via DuPont-style decomposition before deeper modeling.
## Avoid
- You need transaction-ready earnings restatement rather than ratio diagnosis—use financial-due-diligence.
- The decision requires project-level investment ranking—use npv-and-irr-analysis.
- You already know the broken lever and need an operating cash fix—use working-capital-management.
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Income statement, balance sheet, and cash flow statements for comparable periods; peer or internal benchmark definitions; segment or business-unit splits where available; accounting notes needed to normalize one-time items.

**Useful additions:** covenant thresholds, management KPIs, revenue concentration, and operating data such as units shipped or average inventory.

**Comparability rule:** align fiscal periods, currencies, accounting choices, and business perimeter before calculating a peer ratio.
## Procedure
1. Define the question and comparison set: trend, plan, peer, lender covenant, or business-unit diagnosis. Select the few ratios that can answer it rather than compiling every available metric.
2. Standardize statements and calculate averages for balance-sheet denominators where appropriate. Flag acquisitions, discontinued operations, lease accounting, and unusual items that distort comparison.
3. Calculate profitability measures such as gross margin, operating margin, return on assets, and return on invested capital; calculate current and quick ratios, days sales outstanding, inventory turns, payable days, and debt service or debt-to-equity measures.
4. Decompose return on equity into net profit margin × asset turnover × financial leverage. Trace the period-to-period movement in each component to identify the actual lever behind the headline return.
5. Compare results against history and the selected benchmark, using absolute dollars alongside percentages. Investigate material variance with statement notes and operating facts, not ratios alone.
6. Translate the diagnosis into ranked hypotheses and operating questions: price realization, input cost, utilization, collection, inventory policy, capital intensity, or financing mix. Assign an owner and a next analysis for each material gap.
## Output Contract
Deliver a dated scorecard with formula definitions, normalized statements, trend and benchmark comparisons, DuPont bridge, material driver commentary, and a short list of actions or investigations. It must show both the sign and economic magnitude of each gap. A ratio dashboard without interpretation, denominator definitions, or comparability notes is incomplete.
## Evidence
Financial-statement balances and disclosed covenant terms are observed. Restatements for a discontinued line or unusual charge are inferred when calculations are transparent. Benchmark percentiles, target liquidity, and peer classification are assumptions that need a stated source and date. If a segment lacks a reliable capital base, do not manufacture ROIC; report the limitation and use a ratio with valid inputs.
## Checks
- Every numerator and denominator has a stated period and unit.
- Balance-sheet ratios use a consistent opening, closing, or average convention.
- The DuPont components multiply back to the reported or normalized ROE.
- Peer comparisons exclude businesses with fundamentally different models or disclose the mismatch.
- A favorable leverage-driven ROE is not presented as operating improvement.
## Failure Modes
- Comparing year-end inventory to twelve months of sales and calling the result a turnover trend.
- Declaring liquidity healthy from the current ratio while receivables are severely overdue.
- Treating a debt-funded share repurchase as an improvement in shareholder return quality.
- Averaging peer ratios when a few much larger firms make the mean misleading.
