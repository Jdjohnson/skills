# Enterprise Risk Management
## Purpose
Integrate identification, assessment, appetite-setting, ownership, and oversight of material risks across the enterprise. It gives executives a comparable view of exposures that cut across functions and connects risk choices to strategy, capital, and performance decisions.
## Use
- You need an integrated approach to identify, assess, own, and govern risks across the enterprise.
- Board and executive risk appetite, tolerance, and reporting must be coherent.
- Strategic, financial, operational, and compliance risks must be compared on one framework.
## Avoid
- You need a detailed project-level risk register and response plan—use risk-management-plan.
- You are implementing a specific control framework standard as the primary deliverable—use coso-erm-framework or iso-31000 as the detailed standard path.
- The immediate need is crisis response playbooks—use crisis-management-framework or business-continuity-planning.
## Inputs
**Must-have:**
- Strategy objectives and material value drivers.
- Existing risk registers, incidents, and audit/compliance findings.
- Risk appetite preferences and decision rights.
- Current control environment and risk-owner map.

**Nice-to-have:** insurance coverage, stress-test results, capital plans, loss-event data, and emerging-risk intelligence.

**When data is thin:** use calibrated ranges and record disagreement among executives; do not collapse a strategic uncertainty into an arbitrary red-amber-green score.
## Procedure
1. **Connect risk to objectives.** Start with strategic goals, value drivers, and decisions that could be impaired. Define a common taxonomy so the same exposure is not entered independently by three functions. Output: enterprise risk universe.
2. **Identify and consolidate exposures.** Combine registers, incidents, audit findings, and leadership workshops. Express each risk as cause, event, and consequence, then identify dependencies and concentrations. Output: consolidated risk inventory.
3. **Set appetite and measures.** Specify qualitative boundaries and quantitative tolerances where possible: for example, maximum liquidity loss, safety events, or service interruption. Assign escalation thresholds and a governing body. Output: appetite statement.
4. **Assess inherent and residual risk.** Estimate likelihood and impact before controls, evaluate control effectiveness, then rate the remaining exposure on the shared scale. Include velocity and correlation for material enterprise risks. Output: residual-risk profile.
5. **Choose treatments and owners.** Decide whether to accept, reduce, transfer, or avoid each priority exposure; assign an accountable executive, treatment date, and key risk indicator. Output: treatment portfolio.
6. **Report and refresh.** Present the board with top exposures, appetite breaches, trend, and decisions needed; update after material strategy, incident, or environment changes. Output: board risk dashboard.
## Output Contract
A prioritized enterprise risk view containing the risk taxonomy, cause-event-consequence statements, inherent and residual assessments, appetite and tolerance thresholds, dependencies, accountable owners, treatments, indicators, and board escalation items. It must permit executives to compare risks across silos and make explicit trade-offs.
## Evidence
Classify incident history, control tests, audit results, and contractual limits as observed. Mark scenario estimates and control-effectiveness judgments as inferred. Risk appetite is a management choice, not a data fact, and should carry its approving authority and date. Preserve dissenting views where uncertainty is material rather than averaging away the disagreement.
## Checks
- Each top risk maps to a strategic objective or material value driver.
- Duplicate risks and causal dependencies have been reconciled.
- Inherent and residual assessments are not confused.
- Appetite thresholds have owners, indicators, and escalation consequences.
- The board report identifies decisions, not just a heat map.
## Failure Modes
- Creating a long catalogue that never identifies the few exposures requiring executive trade-offs.
- Scoring cyber, supply, and liquidity risks with incompatible definitions.
- Naming a risk owner who lacks authority over the treatment.
- Treating controls as effective because they exist rather than because they are tested.
- Reporting residual risk without showing which dependencies can make several risks crystallize together.
