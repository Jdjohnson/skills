# Social Impact Assessment
## Purpose
Evaluate the social effects of activities on people and communities into an evidence-based impact assessment.
## Use
- Evaluating how a project, policy, or operating model affects people and communities.
- Building defensible social outcome evidence for ESG, financing, or license-to-operate decisions.
- Comparing intended social benefits against observed stakeholder outcomes.
## Avoid
- You need an enterprise ESG priority roadmap rather than outcome evaluation—use esg-strategy.
- The question is purely GHG quantification—use carbon-footprint-assessment.
- You only need a stakeholder influence/interest map without impact measurement—use stakeholder-mapping.
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Defined activity, project, or portfolio whose social effects will be assessed; affected stakeholder groups and baseline social conditions; indicators and evidence sources for outcomes (jobs, health, equity, community access, etc.); comparison or counterfactual basis for attributing change where feasible.

**Nice-to-have:** Administrative data, grievance logs, local research partners, demographic disaggregation, and prior impact commitments.

**When data is thin:** State the causal confidence limit, collect a baseline before rollout where possible, and use qualitative evidence to describe experience without inventing population effects.
## Procedure
1. Define the intervention, geography, time horizon, affected groups, and decisions that the assessment will inform. Include groups with unequal exposure, not only program participants.
2. Build an impact pathway from activity to outputs, short-term outcomes, and longer-term effects. Identify plausible harms, distributional effects, and external factors that could produce the same observed change.
3. Select a compact indicator set covering material effects such as income, safety, access, health, voice, or equity. Set baselines and a comparison approach: before/after, matched group, contribution analysis, or documented qualitative comparison.
4. Gather evidence with methods appropriate to the group: records, surveys, interviews, observation, and grievance data. Safeguard consent, anonymity, accessibility, and translation needs.
5. Analyze changes by affected group, not only the overall average. Test whether the evidence supports attribution, contribution, or merely association, then weigh magnitude, duration, reversibility, and likelihood.
6. Recommend mitigation, benefit-sharing, redesign, monitoring, or cessation. Return results to participants in an understandable form and assign owners for commitments.
## Output Contract
Produce an assessment report with the intervention boundary, stakeholder groups, impact pathway, baseline, indicators, evidence methods, findings by group, attribution confidence, material positive and negative effects, and a time-bound management plan. The report must say what changed for whom and how certain that conclusion is. It is not an ESG catalogue, a reputation narrative, or a stakeholder influence grid.
## Evidence
Obtain informed consent and protect identities where disclosure creates risk. Label self-reported benefit, administrative observation, and causal inference separately. Do not treat attendance or money spent as an outcome. Disaggregate where gender, income, disability, location, or tenure plausibly changes exposure. If a comparison group is unavailable, explain why and avoid causal language stronger than the evidence supports.
## Checks
- Material affected groups helped shape indicators or interpretation.
- Baseline dates precede the change being assessed.
- Negative effects and trade-offs receive the same attention as intended benefits.
- Findings distinguish outputs from outcomes and impacts.
- Recommendations include an accountable owner, deadline, and feedback route.
## Failure Modes
- Counting training seats as improved livelihoods without tracking employment or earnings.
- Averaging results so a harmed neighborhood disappears inside a positive overall result.
- Interviewing only project supporters while calling the result community evidence.
- Claiming attribution when a regional policy changed at the same time.
