# Commercial Due Diligence
## Purpose
Independently test a deal target’s market attractiveness, competitive position, and revenue durability for investment decision. It converts a management growth story into evidenced underwriting risks, upside, and bid implications.
## Use
- Validating a target’s market position, demand drivers, and competitive sustainability pre-deal.
- Stress-testing revenue forecasts and win/loss dynamics before final bid or IC.
- Informing synergy and integration planning with external commercial reality.
## Avoid
- You need books-and-records quality of earnings—use financial-due-diligence.
- The question is pure industry structure without a deal target—use five-forces.
- Culture or people risk is the gating concern—use cultural-due-diligence or hr-due-diligence.
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Defined target perimeter, products, and geographies; historical revenue by segment, channel, and customer cohort where available; competitor set, pricing evidence, and recent win/loss or pipeline data; customer and channel interviews or survey access; management growth narrative and key commercial assumptions.

**Nice-to-have:** CRM extracts, contract renewals, market studies, lost-deal records, and a draft investment case.

**When access is constrained:** state which assertions could not be independently tested and reduce confidence rather than treating management interviews as proof.
## Procedure
1. Translate the investment thesis into falsifiable questions: market growth, target share, customer stickiness, price realization, and synergy dependency.
2. Recut target revenue by product, geography, channel, cohort, and concentration; identify the claims that drive the valuation model.
3. Size the relevant market from independent demand and supply evidence, then test whether the target’s historical growth is share gain, market growth, acquisition, or one-off business.
4. Conduct a structured interview program across customers, former customers, channel partners, competitors, and experts. Ask comparable questions on switching, alternatives, spend, and purchase criteria.
5. Benchmark the competitive set on offer, route to market, pricing, share signals, and capacity to respond. Reconcile contradictions with data-room evidence.
6. Build base, downside, and upside commercial cases; quantify the revenue or margin effect of each decisive risk. Present underwriting conclusions, not a catalogue of research.
## Output Contract
Produce an investment-committee report with the thesis questions, market and target fact base, customer evidence, competitor assessment, forecast bridge, risk-ranked cases, and implications for price, protections, and the first 100 days. Every major conclusion must say whether it validates, qualifies, or invalidates a deal assumption.
## Evidence
Separate data-room facts, independently observed market evidence, interview perceptions, and management forecasts. Preserve interview anonymity in reporting and do not treat a small interview sample as market-share measurement. Show the forecast assumptions changed in each case, including volume, churn, price, and sales capacity. Unknown customer access is itself a diligence finding.
## Checks
- Target perimeter matches the financial model and excludes carved-out revenue.
- Interview coverage spans buyers, detractors, and channels rather than management-selected fans.
- Market size uses a consistent geography, customer, and product boundary.
- Forecast adjustments trace to specific evidence and model lines.
- Risks are sized and prioritized by valuation impact, not merely listed.
## Failure Modes
- Accepting a 30% pipeline conversion rate without comparing it to closed-won history.
- Mistaking a temporary sole-source contract for durable competitive advantage.
- Interviewing only references supplied by the seller.
- Adding TAM growth and target share gains without testing the sales capacity required for both.
- Reporting customer concentration as a percentage while ignoring renewal dates and termination rights.
