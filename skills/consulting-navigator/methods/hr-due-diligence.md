# HR Due Diligence
## Purpose
Assess a deal target’s human capital cost, capability, retention risk, and employment liabilities for investment and PMI planning. The work supplies decision-grade people facts for price, deal protections, and day-one preparation.
## Use
- Quantifying human-capital cost, risk, and capability for a buy-side or sell-side deal.
- Identifying retention, leadership, and labor liabilities before price finalization.
- Feeding PMI workforce and org design with diligence-grade people facts.
## Avoid
- The question is cultural fit and norms rather than people economics—use cultural-due-diligence.
- You need ongoing talent system design outside a deal—use talent-management-strategy.
- Operations and capacity are the unknown, not headcount or HR systems—use operational-due-diligence.
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Headcount, org charts, and cost of workforce by function and site; Compensation, benefits, incentive, and pension/liability summaries; Employment contracts, works councils/unions, and change-of-control terms; Turnover, vacancy, and critical-role identification data; HRIS/process maturity and known compliance issues.

**Useful additions:** data-room index, deal perimeter, synergy case, payroll extracts, litigation log, and management interviews.
## Procedure
1. Set the diligence perimeter: legal entities, countries, employee populations, transaction structure, signing date, and the questions that can affect valuation or closing conditions.
2. Reconcile headcount from organization charts, payroll, and finance records. Split permanent, contingent, vacant, and transferred roles; explain any mismatch before calculating cost.
3. Analyze workforce economics by function and location: base pay, variable pay, benefits, pensions, payroll tax, severance, and one-time obligations. Identify normal run-rate versus transaction-triggered cost.
4. Identify critical roles, leadership dependencies, turnover hotspots, and retention exposures. Test whether named talent is actually tied to customer, regulatory, technical, or operating continuity.
5. Review contracts, collective arrangements, policies, HR systems, and compliance matters for consent, change-of-control, classification, wage, immigration, privacy, and labor-relations risk.
6. Convert findings into a red-flag register with quantified downside, confidence, owner, mitigation, and a handoff list for day-one and integration planning.
## Output Contract
Deliver an HR diligence pack containing a reconciled workforce baseline, cost bridge, critical-talent map, contract and liability review, red flags, quantified exposures, and integration implications. Separate findings that affect price, purchase agreement protections, closing, and post-close action. The output must be traceable to the deal perimeter and data room, not a generic talent strategy.
## Evidence
Treat signed contracts, payroll files, benefit plan documents, and legal notices as primary evidence. Label management assertions and incomplete-country data as provisional. Do not extrapolate a local labor practice across jurisdictions. Use ranges when the likelihood or amount of a claim is uncertain, and clearly distinguish a synergy assumption from an existing people cost or liability.
## Checks
- Workforce totals reconcile to finance and the stated transaction perimeter.
- Cost estimates include employer burden, benefits, and contingent labor where material.
- Critical-role risk names a successor or retention action, not only a red rating.
- Legal and employee-relations issues identify jurisdiction, affected population, and timing.
- The red-flag register distinguishes pre-close remedies from post-close work.
## Failure Modes
- Using organization charts as headcount evidence despite payroll discrepancies.
- Counting planned layoffs as synergies without severance, consultation, or local law implications.
- Missing change-of-control clauses for a small group of revenue-critical sales leaders.
- Treating voluntary turnover as a simple percentage while ignoring a single plant or engineering team.
