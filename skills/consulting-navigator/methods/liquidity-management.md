# Liquidity Management
## Purpose
Preserve and extend a stressed company’s cash runway through short-interval forecasting, payment triage, and facility management.
## Use
- Stabilizing a distressed or stressed company by extending cash runway
- Building and governing a short-interval cash forecast with levers and triggers
- Supporting lender, board, or restructuring negotiations with defensible liquidity actions
## Avoid
- The business is healthy and you only need long-range cash modeling—use cash-flow-forecasting or working-capital-management
- You need a full recovery strategy beyond cash survival—use turnaround-strategy after liquidity is controlled
- The issue is pure treasury policy design in a stable firm—use treasury-management-framework
## Inputs
**Must-have:**
- 13-week (or shorter) cash receipts and disbursements by category
- Bank positions, facilities, covenants, and maturity schedule
- AP/AR aging, inventory, and critical vendor/customer terms
- Near-term obligations (payroll, taxes, debt service, capex commitments)
- Governance rights for spend freezes, payment prioritization, and escalations

**Nice-to-have:** daily bank feeds, customer collection promises, a vendor criticality list, borrowing-base detail, and legal advice on restricted payments.

**When data is thin:** forecast receipts from named invoices and disbursements from approved obligations. Show an uncommitted estimate separately; do not let an optimistic sales forecast fund payroll.
## Procedure
1. **Establish the cash-control perimeter.** Consolidate bank balances, legal entities, restricted cash, facilities, and authority limits as of a cut-off time. Output: opening liquidity position.
2. **Build a rolling receipts-and-payments bridge.** Forecast each week from invoice collection dates, payroll calendar, tax dates, purchase orders, and debt schedules; reconcile the first two weeks daily. Output: 13-week cash forecast.
3. **Classify disbursements.** Rank payments as legally required, business-critical, value-preserving, or deferrable. Identify the consequence, approver, and latest safe payment date for each material item. Output: payment waterfall.
4. **Stress the runway.** Model delayed collections, supplier holds, covenant tests, facility availability, and mandatory outflows. Define trigger levels for a draw, spend freeze, lender update, or contingency action. Output: downside liquidity cases.
5. **Operate the cadence.** Hold a short cash meeting, refresh actual-versus-forecast variance, release approved payments, and escalate breaches immediately. Output: action log and lender-ready dashboard.
## Output Contract
Provide a rolling weekly cash plan that reconciles opening cash to closing cash, identifies usable facilities and covenant headroom, and lists payment priorities, collection actions, owners, dates, triggers, and contingency levers. It must show the earliest breach week under base and downside cases, not merely a year-end cash projection.
## Evidence
Use bank statements and confirmed facility terms for cash and availability. Tag invoice dates, customer promises, and sale proceeds by confidence; distinguish a contractual receipt from a hoped-for collection. Record whether a payment can legally be delayed rather than assuming it can. Reconcile actual cash daily in the near term, explain variance by driver, and revise timing assumptions rather than plugging the forecast.
## Checks
- Opening balances agree to bank evidence and restricted cash is excluded from spendable liquidity.
- Every major weekly receipt has an invoice, counterparty, or explicit probability.
- Payroll, taxes, debt service, and covenant reporting dates appear on the calendar.
- The payment waterfall names decision rights for exceptions.
- Downside cases test facilities and covenant headroom, not only lower revenue.
## Failure Modes
- Showing cash on hand without separating blocked balances and undrawn availability.
- Paying the loudest supplier before evaluating production, legal, and customer impact.
- Forecasting collections at invoice due date despite chronic lateness.
- Treating an emergency revolver draw as permanent operating cash.
- Waiting for the weekly meeting to disclose a same-day breach.
