# Delphic Method
## Purpose
Produce a defensible expert consensus or range estimate through anonymous, iterated judgment rounds with controlled feedback. Anonymity and statistical feedback allow disagreement to narrow without a senior participant anchoring the group.
## Use
- Structured, anonymous multi-round expert consensus is needed on uncertain judgments or forecasts
- Social influence and status would distort a live workshop
- Evidence is sparse and expert judgment is the best available input
## Avoid
- Primary facts can be observed or measured directly—use secondary-research, expert-interviewing, or ethnographic-research
- You need creative idea generation rather than convergence—use structured-brainstorming or design-thinking
- Only one expert is available, so rounds cannot triangulate judgment
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Clear judgment or forecast questions with defined scales or scenarios; A panel of independent domain experts; Facilitator-managed multi-round questionnaire process; Rules for anonymity, feedback of group statistics, and stopping criteria.

**Nice-to-have:** Calibration questions, evidence packets, panel diversity criteria, pre-specified consensus threshold, and secure survey administration.

**When data is thin:** Broaden the answer to an interval, recruit experts with different evidence access, and report disagreement rather than forcing consensus.
## Procedure
1. **Formulate answerable prompts.** Define the forecast horizon, unit, scenario, response scale, and decision use. Test questions for ambiguity so experts are estimating the same event.
2. **Recruit a balanced independent panel.** Select participants for relevant knowledge and differing perspectives; screen conflicts of interest. Keep identities hidden from one another and assign neutral response identifiers.
3. **Run the first round.** Collect individual estimates, confidence, rationale, and conditional assumptions without discussion. Code qualitative reasons into themes while preserving anonymized wording.
4. **Provide controlled feedback.** Send each participant the distribution, median or range, their prior response, and summarized arguments for outlying views. Ask them to revise or defend their estimate in the next round.
5. **Assess convergence and report.** Repeat until the pre-set stability rule, round limit, or irreducible disagreement is reached. Report central tendency, spread, stable minority views, assumptions, and implications.
## Output Contract
An anonymous consensus report that specifies the panel composition, question wording, response scale, round count, response rates, statistical distribution by round, final range or estimate, convergence rule, rationales, and residual disagreement. It must preserve the distinction between a consensus estimate and a unanimous fact.
## Evidence
Expert answers are judgments, not observations, even when experts are credible. Preserve versions of questions and feedback so movement between rounds is auditable. Do not reveal identifiable comments without consent. Flag correlated expertise, shared employer incentives, non-response, and late panel changes because each can distort apparent convergence.
## Checks
- The question has a single time horizon and unambiguous unit of measure.
- Panel expertise covers the relevant technical, commercial, and operating perspectives.
- Feedback gives distributions and reasons without naming or ranking participants.
- Stopping criteria are defined before reviewing the preferred result.
- Final reporting includes dispersion and minority rationales, not only a median.
## Failure Modes
- Replacing anonymous rounds with a workshop in which executives negotiate the number.
- Asking an imprecise question that produces agreement only because interpretations differ.
- Calling a median consensus when half the panel remains far from it.
- Recruiting experts from one vendor ecosystem and mistaking shared incentive for independent judgment.
