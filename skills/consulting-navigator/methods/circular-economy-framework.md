# Circular Economy Framework
## Purpose
Redesign products and material flows to eliminate waste and keep resources circulating through reuse, repair, remanufacture, and recovery. It seeks the highest feasible retained value in each loop, rather than treating recycling as the default answer.
## Use
- Redesigning products, packaging, or supply chains to keep materials in use longer
- Building a transition roadmap from linear to circular value-chain models
- Prioritizing reuse, remanufacture, recycling, and waste-elimination opportunities
## Avoid
- You only need a GHG inventory number without redesigning flows—use carbon-footprint-assessment
- The problem is supplier disruption resilience, not material circularity—use supply-chain-resilience-analysis
- You need green logistics and procurement practices without a circular business-model redesign—use green-supply-chain-management
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Material and product flow map from input through use and end-of-life; waste, scrap, return, and recovery volumes and costs; product design constraints and customer use/return behaviors; supplier and reverse-logistics capability baseline.

**Nice-to-have:** bill of materials, repair records, secondary-material specifications, lifecycle impacts, and contract restrictions.

**When data is thin:** measure one material-intensive product family and state the mass-balance gaps before projecting the result across the portfolio.
## Procedure
1. **Map the current material system.** Trace virgin inputs, components, packaging, use, returns, scrap, and disposal for a defined product family. Quantify mass, value, ownership, and point of loss at each handoff.
2. **Identify value-retention opportunities.** For each material or component, test prevention, life extension, sharing, repair, reuse, refurbishment, remanufacture, recycling, or safe biological return in that order of retained value.
3. **Design the loop.** Specify product changes, collection route, inspection and grading rules, partner responsibilities, customer incentive, and outlet for recovered material. A loop without return volume or a buyer is not viable.
4. **Evaluate trade-offs.** Compare recovered yield, unit economics, quality, logistics distance, regulatory requirements, and environmental consequences against the linear baseline. Keep distinct material streams separate where contamination changes value.
5. **Sequence the transition.** Select pilots, design releases, supplier changes, reverse-logistics milestones, and metrics such as return rate, recovered-content share, and avoided disposal cost.
## Output Contract
Deliver a target circular model showing the current and proposed loops, material quantities and value losses, design changes, operating partners, economics, constraints, pilot scope, and transition roadmap. State which loop is proposed for each major material; a waste-reduction pledge without a return, recovery, or use pathway is incomplete.
## Evidence
Weigh-scale records, bills of material, invoices, and return inspection data are observed. Yield, customer return behavior, and secondary-market price forecasts are assumptions or inferences and need ranges. Record system boundaries: transport, contamination, and energy inputs can alter the preferred loop. Do not claim circularity from a recyclable component unless collection, sorting, and end-market evidence exist.
## Checks
- Material flows balance within a disclosed loss tolerance.
- The proposal favors a higher-value loop only when quality and collection make it feasible.
- Returned-product ownership and data capture are defined.
- Economics include reverse logistics, inspection, and disposal of unusable returns.
- Pilot metrics test both retained material value and customer participation.
## Failure Modes
- Declaring packaging circular because it is technically recyclable where no collection system accepts it.
- Mixing contaminated scrap with clean offcuts and overstating recoverable yield.
- Designing a take-back program without a repair, remanufacture, or resale outlet.
- Choosing recycling when a minor design change would allow component reuse.
