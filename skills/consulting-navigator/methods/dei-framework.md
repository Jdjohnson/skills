# DEI Framework
## Purpose
Design a prioritized DEI roadmap that hard-wires representation, equitable processes, and inclusive experience into talent systems and leadership accountability. It connects workforce outcomes to the rules and decisions that create them, rather than treating inclusion as an events calendar.
## Use
- Designing an integrated DEI strategy linking representation, equity of process, and inclusion experience to business outcomes
- Translating diagnostic findings into prioritized initiatives with owners, metrics, and governance
- Embedding equity into talent systems rather than running standalone awareness programs
## Avoid
- You lack a culture or engagement baseline and are guessing at root causes—run culture-assessment or employee-engagement-surveying first
- The issue is only a single disputed interpersonal incident—use conflict-resolution-methods
- You need pure workforce supply/demand planning without equity design—use workforce-planning
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Workforce representation and flow data (hire, promote, exit) by relevant segments; Policy and process inventory (hiring, performance, pay, promotion); Employee experience evidence on inclusion and belonging; Leadership ambition, risk constraints, and legal/compliance boundaries; Current DEI initiatives and spend.

**Nice-to-have:** Pay-equity analysis, accessibility audit, candidate funnel data, employee resource group feedback, and manager capability assessments.

**When data is thin:** Start with process transparency and voluntary listening, report coverage limits, and avoid inferring causal group differences from small populations.
## Procedure
1. **Set outcome domains and safeguards.** Define the workforce populations, business outcomes, legal constraints, privacy rules, and decision rights. Establish which representation, advancement, pay, access, and belonging outcomes are in scope.
2. **Diagnose workforce flows.** Compare hiring, performance, promotion, development access, pay, and exits across relevant groups and levels. Find the precise transition where a gap appears; do not rely on an overall representation average.
3. **Inspect mechanisms and experience.** Trace how talent processes operate in practice through criteria, calibration, manager discretion, accommodations, sponsorship, and employee testimony. Identify barriers that plausibly produce the observed gaps.
4. **Design interventions at the mechanism.** Specify changes such as structured interview rubrics, transparent promotion criteria, pay-review triggers, accessible tools, or sponsorship pools. Match each intervention to a barrier, owner, resource, and intended outcome.
5. **Govern, measure, and adapt.** Build a roadmap with executive accountability, regular disaggregated measures, employee feedback loops, and escalation for stalled commitments. Sunset activities that cannot show a credible pathway to an outcome.
## Output Contract
A DEI roadmap that links baseline disparities and experience findings to talent-system interventions. It contains outcome measures, population definitions, process changes, accountable leaders, timing, investment, governance cadence, risk controls, and evaluation design. The roadmap distinguishes legal obligations, voluntary aspirations, and unresolved evidence questions.
## Evidence
Protect confidentiality through aggregation and minimum group-size rules. Treat HR records as observed but audit missing demographic data and classification consistency. Employee narratives illuminate experience; they do not establish population prevalence on their own. State causal hypotheses separately from descriptive gaps, and obtain legal review for jurisdiction-specific employment actions.
## Checks
- Measures examine flows by level or function, not only enterprise representation.
- Each initiative addresses a named process mechanism and has an accountable operating owner.
- Data publication rules avoid re-identifying small employee groups.
- Employee feedback can challenge leadership’s interpretation of the numbers.
- Targets, resources, and manager incentives are mutually consistent.
## Failure Modes
- Celebrating entry-level hiring while ignoring promotion and attrition at manager level.
- Mandating training as the sole remedy for biased criteria or opaque pay decisions.
- Publishing subgroup results that expose a small number of employees.
- Asking employee resource groups to own fixes that leaders control.
