# Working Capital Management
## Purpose
Redesign receivables, inventory, and payables policies and processes to free cash and shorten the cash conversion cycle. The work makes operating decisions about cash tied up between purchase, production, sale, collection, and supplier payment.
## Use
- Reducing cash conversion cycle by redesigning AR, inventory, and AP policies
- Freeing cash from operations without cutting growth investment
- Building a sustainable working-capital improvement program with process owners
## Avoid
- The problem is group banking, FX, or funding policy—use treasury-management-framework
- You only need diagnostic ratios without an operating redesign—start with financial-ratio-analysis
- Demand and supply planning, not payment terms, is the root cause—use demand-planning-and-forecasting or inventory-optimization
## Inputs
**Must-have:**
- AR, inventory, and AP aging and policy data
- Cash conversion cycle history and segment splits
- Customer and supplier term cards and dispute root causes
- Process maps for order-to-cash, forecast-to-stock, and procure-to-pay

**Nice-to-have:** credit overrides, SKU margin and demand variability, payment-channel cost, collection queues, and supplier criticality.

**When data is thin:** start with the largest cash pools and reconcile samples to the ledger; mark untested causes rather than guessing.
## Procedure
1. **Reconcile the cash-cycle baseline.** Calculate DSO, DIO, DPO, and cash conversion cycle for comparable months. Tie balances to the general ledger and split material pools by business, customer, SKU, and supplier. Output: trusted baseline and cash sizing.
2. **Trace causes behind balances.** Follow invoices from order to cash, inventory from forecast to receipt, and supplier invoices from receipt to payment. Separate policy choices from delays such as disputed invoices, missing proof of delivery, obsolete stock, late approval, or early payment. Output: cause-and-control map.
3. **Segment and quantify levers.** Identify customers, SKUs, and suppliers where terms, replenishment, scheduling, or collections can change. Translate each lever into cash release, P&L effect, risk, and effort. Output: initiative register with DSO, DIO, or DPO effect.
4. **Design operating rules.** Specify credit holds, billing controls, dispute escalation, safety-stock policies, payment runs, and exception authority. Do not transfer disruption to a key customer or supplier. Output: redesigned process and policy set.
5. **Sequence delivery and track realization.** Assign owners, milestones, and leading indicators. Report released cash only when the balance-sheet movement occurs and does not reverse next month. Output: roadmap and benefits tracker.
## Output Contract
An operating cash-cycle roadmap with a reconciled baseline, root causes, segment initiatives, target DSO/DIO/DPO changes, owners, and benefits tracking. It explains trade-offs among cash release, service, margin, and supplier resilience. It distinguishes collectible cash from structural improvement and includes sustaining controls.
## Evidence
Use ledger balances, invoice dates, goods receipts, payment files, and contract terms as observed evidence. Treat aged-balance reason codes as provisional until sampled. Label assumed collection rates, demand stability, supplier willingness, and inventory-service impacts. Calculate days metrics on consistent denominators and disclose seasonality. A stretched payment is not an improvement if it violates terms or creates supply risk.
## Checks
- DSO, DIO, and DPO reconcile to a stated period and denominator.
- Every major initiative identifies a specific balance population and root cause.
- Cash release is separated from EBITDA, timing shifts, and supplier financing.
- Service, stock-out, and supplier-risk guardrails accompany inventory or payable actions.
- The tracker has a finance owner who validates realized balance-sheet movement.
## Failure Modes
- Celebrating lower DSO after writing off overdue invoices.
- Extending supplier terms and triggering shortages from vulnerable suppliers.
- Cutting safety stock without separating obsolete stock from critical items.
- Blaming collections when invoice errors prevent payment.
- Booking the same cash benefit through inventory and forecast improvement.
