# Scrum Framework
## Purpose
Deliver complex work in short inspected increments using fixed roles, events, and artifacts so the plan adapts to learning each sprint. The framework creates a team operating rhythm, not a portfolio governance process or a continuous-flow queue.
## Use
- Delivering complex products where requirements emerge and short feedback loops reduce risk
- Organizing a cross-functional team around a prioritized backlog, sprints, and inspect-adapt events
- Replacing big-batch handoffs with potentially shippable increments each sprint
## Avoid
- Scope, design, and compliance must be fully fixed before build with heavy stage gates—use waterfall-methodology or stage-gate-process
- Work is continuous flow optimization of tickets without sprint goals—use kanban-system
- You need multi-project governance standards more than a team delivery framework—use pmo-setup-and-governance
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Product vision and at least an initial ordered backlog; stable cross-functional team capacity; named Product Owner and Scrum Master accountabilities; Definition of Done and sprint cadence agreement; access to stakeholders for sprint review feedback.

**Nice-to-have:** release forecast, automated build and test pipeline, user research cadence, and a shared product metric.

**When data is thin:** begin with a small ordered backlog and inspect a usable increment; do not create a detailed annual plan to compensate for uncertain requirements.
## Procedure
1. **Establish accountabilities and product goal.** Confirm that the Product Owner can order work, Developers can decide how to build it, and the Scrum Master can improve the system. Write a product goal that guides backlog ordering.
2. **Refine the Product Backlog.** Break valuable work into understandable items, clarify acceptance criteria, estimate enough to support a near-term forecast, and order items by value, risk, learning, and dependency.
3. **Plan a Sprint.** Select a Sprint Goal, choose work that supports it within observed capacity, and create a Sprint Backlog with a delivery plan. The team forecasts; it does not accept an externally imposed task list as a commitment.
4. **Create and inspect the Increment.** Developers coordinate daily around progress toward the goal, adapt their plan, integrate work, and meet the Definition of Done. An incomplete feature is not an increment merely because a task board says done.
5. **Review and adapt.** At Sprint Review, inspect the working result with stakeholders and adjust backlog order. At Retrospective, choose one or two concrete improvements for roles, practices, tools, or relationships and verify them in the next sprint.
## Output Contract
Produce a functioning sprint delivery system: ordered Product Backlog, Sprint Goal and Sprint Backlog, a potentially releasable Increment meeting the Definition of Done, review feedback, and retrospective improvement actions. Include event cadence, role accountabilities, capacity evidence, and impediments needing escalation.
## Evidence
Completed work is evidenced by the Definition of Done, integrated result, and reviewable behavior, not by percentage-complete estimates. Velocity is an observed team-specific history, not a target to compare across teams. Label release dates and scope as forecasts whose confidence depends on backlog maturity and capacity stability. Keep stakeholder feedback distinct from a Product Owner ordering decision.
## Checks
- One Product Owner owns backlog ordering and one Sprint Goal focuses each sprint.
- The increment is demonstrable and satisfies agreed quality criteria.
- Daily inspection changes the plan when evidence changes.
- Reviews involve relevant stakeholders and affect subsequent backlog order.
- Retrospective actions are small, owned, and checked in the next sprint.
## Failure Modes
- Running daily status meetings while work remains unintegrated until the end of a release.
- Treating story points as an individual productivity score.
- Letting several executives reorder the backlog directly during a sprint.
- Calling unfinished testing “done” to preserve a velocity number.
- Retrospectives producing a wish list with no experiment or follow-through.
