# SCR
## Purpose
Compress an executive message into Situation, Complication, Resolution so the ask lands in three beats. It is an opening format for a settled question, designed to make the action and its reason immediately legible.
## Use
- Writing a tight executive summary or board brief as situation, tension, then resolution
- Compressing a longer SCQA or pyramid storyline into three spoken or written beats
- Standardizing how teams open memos, emails, and steering updates
## Avoid
- The decision question is still ambiguous and must be negotiated—use scqa to make the question explicit
- You need multi-level supporting logic under the answer—use pyramid-principle after the SCR opener
- There is no agreed resolution yet and the work is still diagnostic—use issue-tree or root-cause-analysis
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Audience-accepted baseline context (situation); the forcing tension or change (complication); a clear resolution: recommendation, decision, or next action; optional evidence bullets that fit under the resolution.

**Nice-to-have:** audience decision rights, page or speaking-time constraint, key risks, and the one proof point most likely to change the decision.

**When data is thin:** use a conditional resolution with a named validation step; do not pretend that a missing decision question has already been resolved.
## Procedure
1. **Fix the audience and ask.** Identify the decision maker, the action sought, and the available space. SCR loses force when it tries to serve several audiences with different asks.
2. **Write the situation in one grounding sentence.** State the stable context that makes the listener care, using a fact they recognize. Exclude history that does not bear on the current action.
3. **Introduce one complication.** Name the pressure, break, or opportunity that makes inaction costly. Quantify it where useful, but make the consequence intelligible without a data appendix.
4. **State the resolution first and make it executable.** Specify the decision, owner, timing, resources, and any condition. Add only the two or three evidence bullets needed to support the ask.
5. **Edit for a three-beat reading.** Check that the resolution follows the complication without requiring an unstated question. Move background, options, and detailed proof to the body or appendix.
## Output Contract
Deliver a three-part executive opening with a labeled or clearly ordered Situation, Complication, and Resolution. The resolution must include the decision or next action, responsible owner, timing, and material caveat. It should fit at the top of a one-page memo or in a short spoken update, with supporting logic available separately.
## Evidence
Use agreed facts for the situation and a verifiable metric or event for the complication. A resolution may be a recommendation, but state its basis and condition rather than presenting a forecast as fact. Do not infer stakeholder agreement from silence. If evidence is contested, add a limited validation action or return to SCQA to make the unresolved question explicit.
## Checks
- The reader can identify the requested action within the first three sentences.
- The situation establishes context without consuming the resolution's space.
- The complication explains why timing matters now.
- The resolution is specific enough to approve, delegate, or reject.
- Supporting evidence is subordinate; the opening does not turn into a pyramid argument.
## Failure Modes
- Using a generic situation that could introduce any meeting.
- Naming a complication but offering a resolution with no owner or deadline.
- Burying the ask after a list of analysis findings.
- Adding an implicit question that the audience has not agreed.
- Calling a long option comparison an SCR summary.
