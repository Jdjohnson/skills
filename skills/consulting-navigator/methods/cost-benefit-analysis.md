# Cost-Benefit Analysis
## Purpose
Compare quantified costs and benefits of alternatives on a common basis to support a defensible go/no-go or choice. It makes both the status quo and the distribution of gains and losses visible before resources are committed.
## Use
- Comparing total expected costs and benefits of a project, policy, or initiative on a common basis
- You need a transparent rationale for go/no-go or option choice beyond intuition
- Benefits span financial and quantified non-financial effects that must be made explicit
## Avoid
- You need pure time-value investment metrics for a capital project—use npv-and-irr-analysis
- You are building a multi-year capital portfolio process—use capital-budgeting-framework
- Uncertainty is the core issue and you need option value under flexibility—use real-options-analysis or monte-carlo-simulation
## Inputs
**Must-have:**
- Defined alternatives including status quo
- Itemized costs over the appraisal horizon
- Claimed benefits with quantification method and evidence
- Discount rate, time horizon, and treatment of intangibles/risk

**Nice-to-have:** affected-population volumes, implementation phasing, tax treatment, and independent benchmark evidence.

**When data is thin:** retain a benefit as a non-monetized effect with direction and affected group; do not quietly price it with an unsupported proxy.
## Procedure
1. **Specify alternatives and standing.** Define the decision, baseline, scope, horizon, and whose costs and benefits count. Include a credible do-nothing case. Output: appraisal boundary.
2. **Build the impact chain.** Map each alternative’s inputs, behavioral changes, outputs, and outcomes, then identify who experiences each effect. Output: cost-benefit inventory.
3. **Measure and monetize.** Estimate quantities first, apply unit values second, and time every stream. Keep transfers distinct from net societal benefits when relevant. Output: annualized valuation table.
4. **Discount and compare.** Convert future streams to a common present-value basis; show net benefits, benefit-cost ratio, and break-even conditions where meaningful. Output: comparative results table.
5. **Test uncertainty and distribution.** Vary pivotal assumptions, identify switching values, and show which groups bear costs or receive benefits. Output: recommendation with sensitivity and equity notes.
## Output Contract
A comparative business case containing the baseline and alternatives, quantified and non-quantified effects, valuation sources, timing and discount assumptions, present-value results, sensitivity ranges, distributional impacts, and a decision recommendation. Readers must be able to see whether a large ratio is driven by volume, valuation, timing, or an excluded cost.
## Evidence
Separate measured utilization, prices, and realized outcomes from forecasts. Cite the basis for each unit value and identify transfer payments rather than counting them as new value twice. State the appraisal perspective explicitly. If a benefit cannot be monetized credibly, report its physical quantity and decision relevance; do not convert a preference into cash merely to complete a spreadsheet.
## Checks
- Status quo is a real comparator, not zero cost and zero benefit.
- Every monetary line has quantity, unit value, timing, and owner.
- Costs and benefits are not double-counted across linked effects.
- Discounting and inflation treatment are consistent across alternatives.
- Sensitivity identifies the assumptions that could reverse the recommendation.
## Failure Modes
- Counting a vendor’s revenue and the buyer’s payment as two benefits.
- Omitting operating, transition, enforcement, or opportunity costs.
- Monetizing an intangible with a number that implies more confidence than the evidence supports.
- Comparing alternatives with unequal horizons without a stated residual-value treatment.
