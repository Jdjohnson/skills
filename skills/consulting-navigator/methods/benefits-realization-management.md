# Benefits Realization Management
## Purpose
Define, own, track, and harvest the intended benefits of change initiatives from business case through post-implementation so value is realized, not just delivered. It keeps a benefit alive after project milestones close and shifts accountability to the business owner who can change the outcome.
## Use
- Linking change initiatives to measurable benefits with owners, baselines, and tracking through and after delivery.
- Preventing benefit leakage between business case approval and post-implementation results.
- Creating a benefits map that ties initiatives to strategic outcomes and financials.
## Avoid
- You need project cost/schedule control rather than outcome tracking—use earned-value-management.
- You need a one-time post-project lessons review—use post-implementation-review.
- You only need a strategy-to-KPI scorecard without initiative-level benefit ownership—use balanced-scorecard.
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Approved or draft business case with claimed benefits; baseline metrics and data owners for each benefit; initiative roadmap and dependency map; accountable benefit owners in the business (not only project managers).

**Nice-to-have:** benefit valuation rules, forecast confidence, change-adoption measures, and finance sign-off criteria.

**When data is thin:** register the benefit as unquantified with a date-bound measurement plan; avoid adding its value to the investment case total.
## Procedure
1. **Decompose the claim.** Translate each business-case promise into a benefit statement, metric, beneficiary, mechanism, timing, and disbenefit.
2. **Establish the baseline.** Freeze the pre-change value, measurement window, counterfactual, and data source so later movement is not automatically credited to the initiative.
3. **Assign ownership.** Name the operational leader who controls adoption and the finance partner who validates valuation; project managers supply delivery evidence, not benefit ownership.
4. **Map dependencies.** Link capabilities, policy changes, training, vendors, and parallel initiatives to each benefit; identify the last dependency before cash can be harvested.
5. **Forecast and track.** At each review compare planned, forecast, and realized benefit; explain variance by delivery, adoption, external factors, or baseline error.
6. **Intervene and close.** Trigger corrective actions for leakage, formally transfer sustainable measures to operations, and conduct a post-realization review.
## Output Contract
A living benefits register and realization report containing the benefit map, baselines, calculation rules, benefit owners, timing profile, dependency links, forecast versus realized values, confidence, and corrective actions. It must show cashable value separately from avoided cost, service, risk, and other non-financial benefits.
## Evidence
Classify source-system performance and finance-approved savings as observed. Treat causality, adoption conversion, and counterfactual performance as assumptions or inferences. Do not count the same saving under two initiatives. Retain evidence for baseline revisions and report disbenefits such as training time or service disruption beside the upside.
## Checks
- Each benefit has one operational owner and one measurable baseline.
- Calculation logic distinguishes gross impact from net, cashable impact.
- Dependencies appear before the claimed realization date.
- Forecasts reconcile to the approved business case or explain the change.
- Closed projects remain tracked until their benefits stabilize or are retired.
## Failure Modes
- Declaring a benefit realized when software goes live rather than when behavior and economics change.
- Giving ownership to a project manager who cannot alter the operating result.
- Double-counting procurement and working-capital savings from the same inventory reduction.
- Resetting a weak baseline after implementation to preserve the original claim.
- Ignoring disbenefits that erode the net value of a supposedly successful change.
