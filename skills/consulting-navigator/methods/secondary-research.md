# Secondary Research
## Purpose
Systematically collect, appraise, and synthesize already-published or internal evidence into a citable fact base before or instead of new primary fieldwork. The method makes existing evidence auditable and decision-relevant rather than simply accumulating links.
## Use
- Existing public, licensed, or internal documents can answer or bound the question
- You need a rapid evidence base before committing to primary fieldwork
- Industry, market, regulatory, or competitor facts must be synthesized from multiple published sources
## Avoid
- The answer requires proprietary customer or operational truth that does not exist in published sources—use survey-design-methodology, expert-interviewing, or ethnographic-research
- You already have primary data and only need analysis—use regression-analysis, cluster-analysis, or the relevant model
- The question is pure process observation inside your four walls—use gemba-walks or process-mapping
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Focused research questions and decision use of the evidence; source universe (databases, filings, reports, internal archives) and access rights; inclusion, exclusion, and recency criteria; citation and confidence standards for conflicting sources.

**Nice-to-have:** search synonyms, prior bibliographies, a data extraction form, subject-matter reviewers, and a source-access log.

**When data is thin:** report the evidence gap and design primary research around it; do not convert a small set of convenient articles into a market fact.
## Procedure
1. **Write answerable research questions.** Define the claim, population, geography, period, and decision use. Break broad questions into evidence needs such as market size, buyer behavior, regulation, or competitor action.
2. **Set the protocol.** Choose databases and archives, query terms, date range, inclusion and exclusion rules, source-quality criteria, and a citation format before searching. Record access restrictions and expected bias by source type.
3. **Search and screen systematically.** Run documented searches, capture candidate records, remove duplicates, and screen titles, abstracts, or summaries against the protocol. Maintain a decision log for exclusions that could be challenged later.
4. **Extract and appraise.** Capture the claim, underlying population, date, method, key number, limitation, and exact location in each retained item. Rate authority, recency, directness, and conflict of interest rather than treating all publications equally.
5. **Synthesize by question.** Compare converging and conflicting evidence, normalize definitions, and separate facts from interpretation. Build a claim-evidence matrix that shows confidence, caveats, and the research gap that warrants fieldwork.
## Output Contract
Deliver an assessment containing the protocol, search coverage, screened source set, claim-evidence matrix, synthesized findings, confidence ratings, citations, and unresolved gaps. A decision maker must be able to trace every material claim to the supporting item and see whether evidence is sufficient for action or only for a hypothesis.
## Evidence
Published material is evidence, not automatically fact. Preserve publication date, geography, sampled population, methodology, and sponsor where available. Prefer the original dataset or filing over a summary that repeats it. State when a currency conversion, market-definition mapping, or proxy is an assumption. Do not merge incompatible market estimates into an average merely to produce one number.
## Checks
- Each question has explicit search terms, sources, and inclusion rules.
- Material claims link to a retained item and its limitation.
- Conflicting estimates are explained by scope, date, or method where possible.
- Citation details allow another analyst to locate the item.
- The final brief distinguishes evidence gaps from negative findings.
## Failure Modes
- Searching only the first page of results or only familiar publishers.
- Quoting a market number without its geography, year, and definition.
- Citing a secondary article when the underlying filing is available.
- Treating sponsored thought leadership as independent evidence.
- Mixing forecast growth rates and historical actuals in the same comparison.
