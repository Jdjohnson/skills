# Cost Optimization Framework
## Purpose
Design a prioritized, value-protecting cost-reduction program with levers, targets, and an implementation roadmap. It seeks durable changes in demand, process, sourcing, and operating model rather than indiscriminate cuts.
## Use
- You must reduce structural cost while protecting customer value and critical capabilities
- Prior cost cuts failed because they were tactical or across-the-board
- You need a multi-lever cost roadmap (demand, process, supplier, footprint, overhead)
## Avoid
- You first need accurate product or activity costs—use activity-based-costing
- The mandate is rebuild every budget from zero line-by-line—use zero-based-budgeting
- The problem is IT run-cost only—use it-cost-optimization
## Inputs
**Must-have:**
- Current cost baseline by function, category, and driver
- Strategy and non-negotiable service or quality constraints
- Benchmark or internal variance evidence on cost opportunities
- Implementation capacity and one-time investment tolerance

**Nice-to-have:** supplier contracts, demand forecasts, process measures, site data, and benefits already in flight.

**When data is thin:** target a cost pool only after reconciling its total to finance; represent uncertain savings as a range and assign a data owner.
## Procedure
1. **Set the value guardrails.** Translate strategy into protected customers, capabilities, service levels, safety requirements, and reinvestment priorities. Output: cost-program charter and constraints.
2. **Build a driver-based baseline.** Reconcile spend by function and category, then connect material pools to volume, complexity, utilization, headcount, or footprint drivers. Output: cost-and-driver fact base.
3. **Find structural levers.** Diagnose demand elimination, specification simplification, process redesign, sourcing, automation, organization, and asset-footprint options. Avoid treating a spending freeze as a structural lever. Output: lever inventory with mechanisms.
4. **Size and prioritize initiatives.** Estimate gross savings, one-time cost, timing, value risk, dependencies, and owner; rank the portfolio against the target and guardrails. Output: initiative portfolio and wave plan.
5. **Mobilize benefits control.** Define baselines, benefit recognition rules, milestone gates, and intervention routes for initiatives that damage service or miss savings. Output: governed savings roadmap.
## Output Contract
A value-protecting savings roadmap with reconciled baseline, driver diagnosis, initiative cards, gross-to-net savings, investment, timing, service risks, dependencies, accountable owners, and benefit-tracking rules. The roadmap must expose how each lever changes the operating system, not simply list a percentage reduction by department.
## Evidence
Use finance-approved actuals for the baseline and label benchmark comparisons as directional unless like-for-like scope is demonstrated. Separate cash release, P&L savings, cost avoidance, and revenue effects. State which demand or service assumptions make a lever viable. Savings cannot be counted until the organization has a mechanism to remove or redeploy the underlying resource.
## Checks
- Baseline totals reconcile to the ledger and exclude double-counted initiatives.
- Each lever has a causal driver and a protected-value test.
- Gross savings, leakage, one-time cost, and realization date are distinct.
- Portfolio timing respects dependencies and management capacity.
- Finance and business owners agree how benefits will be recognized.
## Failure Modes
- Cutting training, maintenance, or quality controls that later create higher operating cost.
- Adding benchmark gaps without understanding the volume or service difference behind them.
- Reporting vacancy holds as permanent savings while work and backfill demand remain.
- Launching too many initiatives for the same procurement or technology team to execute.
