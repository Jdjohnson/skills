# 5S System
## Purpose
Organize the workplace through Sort, Set, Shine, Standardize, and Sustain so work is efficient and abnormalities are instantly visible. It changes the physical conditions of a work area, rather than merely displaying status or documenting a task.
## Use
- Stabilizing a chaotic shop floor, warehouse, or office work area before deeper lean work.
- Reducing search time, motion waste, and visual disorder at the point of work.
- Building the physical foundation for visual management and standard work.
## Avoid
- The bottleneck is process design or flow, not workplace organization—use value-stream-mapping or bottleneck-analysis first.
- You need enterprise process redesign rather than workstation hygiene—use business-process-reengineering or lean-manufacturing.
- Problems are purely capacity or demand mismatch—use capacity-analysis.
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Defined work area and ownership boundaries; current layout photos or floor map and inventory of tools/materials in place; safety and quality issues linked to clutter or misplacement; agreement on what belongs at the point of use versus storage.

**Nice-to-have:** Search-time observations, maintenance records, staff photographs of recurring abnormalities, and facilities support.

**When data is thin:** Time a few representative retrievals and use red-tag counts as a baseline; do not claim productivity gains that were not measured.
## Procedure
1. **Set the boundary and observe work.** Mark the station, aisle, desk, or cell being changed. Watch a complete work cycle and record searching, walking, reaching, and unsafe workarounds.
2. **Sort.** Red-tag every item whose use, owner, or frequency is unclear. Move obsolete items to a holding area, decide retain/relocate/dispose, and record decisions so useful tools are not discarded.
3. **Set in Order.** Put retained items at point of use according to frequency and sequence. Create labeled homes, shadow boards, floor marks, min/max locations, and return paths; then test whether a new operator can find each item.
4. **Shine.** Clean the area while inspecting it. Record leaks, worn fixtures, damaged containers, missing guards, and sources of dirt; cleaning is an inspection activity, not cosmetic work.
5. **Standardize.** Photograph the intended condition, define visual rules and cleaning checks, assign area ownership, and add the standard to shift handover or daily work.
6. **Sustain.** Audit against the visual standard at a fixed cadence, solve repeat causes, and revise locations when the work changes. Track whether the area returns to disorder between audits.
## Output Contract
An auditable workplace standard consisting of a before-and-after layout, retained-item list, labeled locations, visual condition standard, ownership and audit cadence, red-tag disposition, and open abnormality list. It must let an unfamiliar operator see what belongs, where it belongs, and what requires attention within seconds.
## Evidence
Photographs, inventory, timed searches, safety observations, and audit results are observed evidence. Item frequency and proposed locations are hypotheses until operators test them. Record why an item is retained at point of use, especially for safety-critical spares. Do not use a clean appearance as evidence that the underlying source of contamination or disorder is fixed.
## Checks
- Every retained item has a defined home, label, and owner.
- Red-tag decisions are documented before material leaves the area.
- Visual controls make missing, excess, or misplaced items obvious.
- Cleaning identifies defects and sources, not only surface dirt.
- Audits test the actual shift condition, not a staged tour before inspection.
## Failure Modes
- Throwing away infrequently used changeover parts because no one recognized them.
- Installing shadow boards that do not match the operator’s work sequence.
- Calling a one-day cleanup “Sustain” without an owner or recurring audit.
- Hiding leaking equipment with fresh paint instead of raising maintenance work.
- Standardizing a layout before peak-volume operators have tested it.
