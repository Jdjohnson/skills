# Net Promoter System
## Purpose
Run loyalty as a closed-loop management system that measures NPS, diagnoses drivers, and drives accountable recovery and improvement actions. It combines a consistent loyalty signal with follow-up for individual customers and operational learning for teams.
## Use
- You need an operating system linking loyalty measurement to root-cause action and closed-loop recovery
- Leadership wants a single loyalty KPI tied to operational accountability
- Detractor patterns must drive prioritized experience fixes across touchpoints
## Avoid
- You only need one-off research insights—use voice-of-the-customer or focus-groups
- The problem is product-market fit, not relationship loyalty—use product-market-fit-analysis or jobs-to-be-done
- You lack any customer contact infrastructure to close the loop—build CRM capability first via crm-strategy
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Consistent promoter-score survey instrument and response process; customer identity linkage for closed-loop follow-up; operational owners for major journey moments; baseline NPS trend and driver comments.

**Nice-to-have:** transaction metadata, response-bias analysis, contact-permission rules, service-recovery authority, and a customer journey taxonomy.

**When data is thin:** start with one high-volume journey and report response count and composition beside the score. Do not treat a small convenience sample as a company loyalty trend.
## Procedure
1. **Define the relationship and sampling moment.** Decide whether the program measures a transactional interaction or an overall relationship. Set contact timing, population, sampling rules, survey frequency, and suppression rules so customers are not over-contacted.
2. **Collect the score and reason.** Ask the likelihood-to-recommend question consistently, capture the open-text reason, link the response to the journey and customer where permission allows, and classify respondents as promoters, passives, or detractors.
3. **Calculate and segment the signal.** Compute NPS as the percentage of promoters minus the percentage of detractors. Show counts, response rate, confidence limitations, and cuts by journey, segment, location, or product only when the sample supports them.
4. **Close the inner loop.** Route detractor feedback to a named owner within an agreed window. Record contact attempts, recovery outcome, root issue, and systemic barrier; never promise a callback without capacity to make it.
5. **Run the outer loop.** Aggregate recurring causes, assign operational owners and deadlines, track action completion, and review whether fixes improve experience and loyalty over subsequent cycles.
## Output Contract
A loyalty operating system with survey design and cadence, sampling and linkage rules, NPS calculation dashboard, response and representativeness metrics, closed-loop workflow, journey-driver taxonomy, action register, owner review cadence, and recovery escalation policy. It must connect individual feedback to corrective action rather than stopping at a score.
## Evidence
Survey responses and logged contacts are observed feedback; they represent respondents, not automatically all customers. Label driver coding and causal conclusions as interpretations. State assumptions about non-response and whether a transaction sample represents the relationship. Keep contact permissions, vulnerable-customer handling, and case notes protected. Never improve the score by excluding difficult journeys or by reclassifying neutral responses as promoters.
## Checks
- The survey wording and respondent classification stay stable across trend periods.
- Every displayed score includes promoter, passive, detractor, and response counts.
- Detractors have a named recovery owner and target contact time.
- The action register separates one-off recovery from recurring process defects.
- Leaders review completed actions and subsequent experience, not score movement alone.
## Failure Modes
- Celebrating a higher NPS while detractor callbacks are overdue.
- Comparing scores after changing the sampling moment or survey wording.
- Treating passives as loyal simply because they are not detractors.
- Sending surveys to every transaction until response quality collapses.
- Coding open comments into generic “service” labels that cannot guide an owner.
