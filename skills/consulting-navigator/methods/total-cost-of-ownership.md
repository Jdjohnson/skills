# Total Cost of Ownership
## Purpose
Estimate and compare full lifecycle costs of a product, service, or supplier choice beyond purchase price. It exposes the economic consequences that sit in logistics, operations, quality, and exit rather than in the bid sheet.
## Use
- Comparing sourcing, make-buy, or supplier options on full lifecycle economics.
- Exposing hidden cost drivers beyond purchase price in category decisions.
- Building a defensible cost model for negotiation and award governance.
## Avoid
- You need a general project investment go/no-go with benefits—use cost-benefit-analysis.
- Category strategy and market approach are undefined—use kraljic-matrix then strategic-sourcing.
- Only activity cost inside the factory is in scope—use activity-based-costing.
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Purchase price and contract commercial terms; inbound logistics, duties, inventory holding, and quality cost data; usage, maintenance, switching, and end-of-life cost assumptions; volume forecast and time horizon for the comparison; comparable cost structure across alternative suppliers or options.

**Nice-to-have:** lead-time distributions, service records, working-capital rates, warranty claims, and disposal obligations.

**When data is thin:** use a common cost taxonomy, mark missing components, and test high-impact ranges before treating a supplier as cheaper.
## Procedure
1. **Set the comparison boundary.** Define the alternatives, unit of analysis, demand profile, useful life, cost currency, tax treatment, and discount convention. Ensure every alternative is assessed from the same start point through exit.
2. **Build the cost tree.** Separate acquisition, inbound freight and duties, receiving, inventory, installation, use, maintenance, quality failures, switching, and disposal. Assign each branch a driver and cost owner instead of placing residuals in an unexplained overhead bucket.
3. **Quantify alternative-specific economics.** Convert prices, lead times, defect rates, energy use, labor, and working capital into totals at the chosen volume. Reconcile purchase-price totals to the bid and operational costs to their source data.
4. **Normalize and stress the model.** Align units, payment timing, service levels, foreign exchange, and quality definitions. Run sensitivities on the few inputs capable of reversing the ranking, such as scrap rate or safety stock.
5. **Make the award case.** Present total and per-unit cost by layer, the delta to each alternative, confidence bands, negotiation levers, and costs deliberately excluded from the decision.
## Output Contract
The decision model shows a shared lifecycle cost tree, assumptions by option, annual and cumulative totals, present-value treatment where relevant, sensitivities, and a recommendation. It identifies whether the difference comes from price, inventory, failure, usage, or exit. It supports supplier award or make-buy choice; it does not claim to measure project benefits.
## Evidence
Use invoices, freight quotes, defect records, maintenance work orders, and contractual terms as observed evidence. Derive inventory cost from agreed carrying rates and lead-time exposure, showing the calculation. Label forecast volumes, currency paths, and disposal estimates as assumptions. A cost with no credible driver stays visible as unknown or is excluded with an explanation. Do not offset a weak supplier cost with an unpriced promise of better service.
## Checks
- All alternatives use the same horizon, volume, and cost categories.
- Unit conversions and currency dates are traceable.
- Double counting between product cost, warranty, and quality loss is ruled out.
- The leading ranking driver has an explicit source and sensitivity.
- Savings are separated into cash, P&L, and working-capital effects.
## Failure Modes
- Selecting the lowest quoted price before accounting for expedited freight.
- Charging one supplier for inventory while assuming another requires none.
- Hiding failed incoming-quality costs in a general overhead line.
- Comparing a leased option’s monthly fee with a purchased option’s lifetime cost.
- Calling uncertain disposal liability a zero because no invoice exists yet.
