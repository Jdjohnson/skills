# Scenario Planning
## Purpose
Build a small set of divergent, plausible future narratives from critical uncertainties so leaders can stress-test strategy, surface brittle assumptions, and decide which options stay robust across more than one future—not produce a single “correct” forecast.
## Use
- The team cannot agree on one base case and plans collapse when assumptions shift.
- Strategy is exposed to regulation, technology, or demand shocks that cannot be timed precisely.
- Leadership wants alternative futures rather than one forecast line.
- Investments look acceptable in the plan but catastrophic under a different world.
- Time horizon is long enough that structural change, not only noise, matters.
## Avoid
- You need a single base-case forecast with confidence intervals—use time-series-forecasting or monte-carlo-simulation.
- You only need a structured external-factor inventory without narrative futures—use pestle-analysis.
- Near-term operational planning with known demand patterns—use demand-planning-and-forecasting.
- The decision is reversible and low-stakes; lightweight sensitivity tables may be enough.
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Decision or strategy under uncertainty and explicit time horizon; candidate critical uncertainties and predetermined trends; stakeholder views on forces that could reshape the business; criteria for decision-relevance (what would change a choice).

**Nice-to-have:** Prior driver maps, competitor moves, regulatory calendars, early quantitative sketches of demand or cost under different worlds.

**When data is missing:** Surface uncertainties in structured workshops; label predetermined trends versus true uncertainties; do not invent probabilities the evidence cannot support.
## Procedure
1. **Define scope and decision.** Fix the strategic question, horizon, and stakeholders who must use the results; produce a framing brief.
2. **Surface trends and critical uncertainties.** Separate predetermined forces from high-impact uncertainties; shortlist the two or three axes that most split decision-relevant futures.
3. **Build divergent scenario frames.** Combine uncertainty poles into a small set of coherent worlds; check internal consistency and plausibility; name each scenario.
4. **Develop narrative learning scenarios.** Describe how each world unfolds for customers, competitors, regulation, and capabilities; note research needs where evidence is thin; add light quantification only where it clarifies scale without fake precision.
5. **Derive decision scenarios and implications.** Translate each narrative into strategy implications, early indicators, and options that are robust, contingent, or abandoned, so leaders can use the set in portfolio and capability choices.
## Output Contract
A decision-relevant scenario set: a handful of named narratives; key uncertainties and trends used; implications per scenario; robust vs. contingent moves; monitoring signals—not a single forecast or a PESTLE grid alone. The artifact must support a strategy robustness decision.
## Evidence
- **Observed:** current trends with data trails; committed regulations or demographics treated as predetermined where justified.
- **Inferred:** second-order effects inside a scenario (competitor responses, cascading demand).
- **Assumptions:** deliberately varied across scenarios; never hidden inside a “most likely” path only.
- **Unknowns:** timing of shocks stays open—use indicators, not false dates.
Thin evidence → keep scenarios qualitative and flag a research agenda; do not collapse to one probability-weighted path.
## Checks
- Scenarios are few and divergent, and each uncertainty used actually changes the strategic choice under review.
- Each scenario is internally consistent and plausible, not wishful or purely dystopian.
- Implications specify what the organization would do differently in each world.
- Early-warning indicators are observable, not vague mood statements.
- Participants can restate the logics without relying on a single champion’s narrative.
## Failure Modes
- Writing three versions of the same base case (tunnel vision with labels).
- Assigning precise probabilities to deep uncertainties and treating the mean as truth.
- Building rich worlds that never reconnect to a live investment or capability decision.
- Letting the preferred strategy dictate the only “serious” scenario.
- Stopping at workshop energy without write-up, indicators, or option implications.
