# Stakeholder Communication Plan
## Purpose
Plan audience-specific project messages, channels, and cadences so the right stakeholders get timely, decision-ready information.
## Use
- Turning stakeholder analysis into a concrete who-what-when-how communication cadence for a project.
- Preventing surprise by aligning status, decisions, and risks to each audience.
- Coordinating project messaging with change or PMO reporting rhythms.
## Avoid
- Stakeholders are not yet identified or power/interest is unknown—use stakeholder-mapping first.
- The need is org-wide change narrative and resistance handling—use communication-strategy-for-change or resistance-management-plan.
- You only need decision rights, not messaging—use raci-matrix.
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Stakeholder map with influence, interest, and information needs; key messages, decisions, and reporting metrics available; governance calendar (steering, standups, releases); channels available (email, portal, forums, demos); sensitive topics and blackout or confidentiality constraints.

**Nice-to-have:** Readership data, preferred-language needs, existing message templates, sponsor availability, and feedback-channel performance.

**When data is thin:** Start with the most influential and most affected groups, then use short feedback checks to refine their information needs and preferred channel.
## Procedure
1. Segment the stakeholder map by decision role and information need: sponsor, steering member, delivery team, affected operator, customer, regulator, or partner. Do not use job level as the only segmentation.
2. For each segment, define the communication objective: request a decision, enable action, surface risk, build awareness, or confirm a completed commitment. Write the smallest message that accomplishes that objective.
3. Assign message owner, approver, channel, format, frequency, timing trigger, and response owner. Match urgency and sensitivity to a channel; a confidential decision is not a portal update.
4. Align the schedule with governance events and operational milestones. Put decision pre-reads early enough to allow review, and create an escalation route for missed approvals or emerging risks.
5. Build reusable artifacts: a decision brief, weekly delivery update, risk notice, release note, and FAQ. State the source metric, reporting cut-off, and version owner for each.
6. Launch the calendar, track delivery and response, and review whether messages changed decisions or reduced surprises. Retire redundant reports rather than adding another audience-wide digest.
## Output Contract
Provide a communication matrix and calendar that lists each audience, purpose, message, sender, approver, channel, cadence or trigger, timing, confidentiality rule, feedback route, and success measure. Include the first cycle’s artifacts and escalation logic for late decisions. The plan must make project information actionable for different audiences; it is not a stakeholder map, a RACI, or a broad change narrative.
## Evidence
Use the stakeholder map and governance records as the starting evidence. Mark unvalidated preferences, informal influence, and assumed channel access. Keep sensitive content to the least-privilege audience and record approval where policy requires it. Differentiate sent from received and understood: distribution lists do not prove communication occurred. Capture questions and decisions so future messages correct recurring ambiguity.
## Checks
- Every recurring message has a business purpose and accountable sender.
- Decision-makers receive a clear ask, deadline, and supporting evidence.
- Frontline and external audiences receive language appropriate to their action.
- Calendar dates do not conflict with known blackout periods or release windows.
- Escalations specify who acts when an expected response is absent.
## Failure Modes
- Sending the sponsor a dense operational dashboard with no decision request.
- Announcing a release to customer-facing teams after customers already see it.
- Reusing a steering-deck metric after its data cut-off has passed.
- Treating unanswered emails as approval for a high-impact change.
