# Star Model
## Purpose
Diagnose organization effectiveness by testing alignment across strategy, structure, processes, rewards, and people design policies. The result identifies reinforcing and contradictory choices before a redesign turns into an org-chart exercise.
## Use
- Auditing whether design policies reinforce or fight strategy.
- Scoping what must change before an org redesign.
- Explaining why a prior reorg failed to improve results.
## Avoid
- You already know the design gaps and need to draw the target structure—use organizational-design.
- The issue is strategy-culture fit without design policies—use 7s-framework or strategic-alignment-framework.
- You only need activity-level ownership—use raci-matrix.
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Stated strategy and priority capabilities; current structure and major process maps; reward, metric, and performance practices; people systems (skills, career paths, staffing rules); evidence of friction or underperformance by design area.

**Useful additions:** decision-rights records, employee journey data, spans and layers, and examples of exceptions that leaders regularly escalate.
## Procedure
1. Translate the strategy into three to five capabilities that must work unusually well, such as rapid local pricing or integrated account coverage.
2. Describe the five policies separately. For structure, trace reporting and lateral links; for processes, follow decisions and handoffs; for rewards, inspect what is measured and paid; for people, inspect selection, development, and deployment.
3. For each capability, mark whether each policy enables it, is neutral, or impedes it. Record a concrete proof point rather than a preference. A collaboration capability, for example, cannot be called supported merely because a collaboration value is printed.
4. Trace the most consequential contradictions across policies. Ask what behavior a rational employee would choose under the existing authority, workflow, scorecard, and career consequences.
5. Convert the contradictions into a sequence of design-policy changes, distinguishing changes required now from structure decisions that can wait. Name the intended behavior and an observable test for every change.
## Output Contract
Deliver a five-policy alignment matrix, a capability-to-policy heat map, and a short misalignment register. Each register entry names the strategic capability, conflicting policies, evidence, affected population, consequence, proposed design lever, owner, and validation signal. It is a diagnosis for a redesign charter, not a proposed organization chart.
## Evidence
Treat approved strategy, policies, system extracts, and observed workflow times as evidence. Interview accounts can show patterns but remain reported perceptions until triangulated. Label an alleged causal connection as a hypothesis when a policy has not been tested in practice. Do not infer reward effects from compensation-plan wording alone; examine payouts, promotion decisions, and performance conversations. Preserve minority observations when they expose a location or role-specific design problem.
## Checks
- Every priority capability is tested against all five policies.
- The matrix separates a policy's written intent from its experienced consequence.
- At least one decision or workflow is traced end to end rather than assessed from documents alone.
- Each proposed lever changes a policy, not simply a leader's communication.
- The diagnosis makes clear which changes are prerequisites for organizational-design work.
## Failure Modes
- Treating structure as the answer and ignoring incentives that reward the old silo behavior.
- Calling a slow approval a process problem without checking who holds decision rights and what risk metric encourages escalation.
- Rating every policy red, producing an exhaustive complaint list with no capability-based priority.
- Recommending new values when staffing, promotion, and recognition still select for the contrary behavior.
