# Action Learning
## Purpose
Build team capability by solving a real organizational problem through structured questioning, action, and reflection cycles. The output is dual: progress on a sponsor’s problem and evidence of new learning habits among participants.
## Use
- A real, unresolved work problem can be the vehicle for team capability building.
- You need insight and action from the people who own the work, not an external expert monologue.
- Leadership wants simultaneous problem progress and learning habits.
## Avoid
- The question needs specialist technical judgment from outside the team—use expert-interviewing or delphic-method.
- There is no real problem sponsor or authority to act on findings—secure mandate first.
- You only need a one-off knowledge transfer with no ongoing problem ownership—use training-needs-analysis or mentorship-program-design.
## Inputs
**Must-have:** A live, consequential business problem the set can influence; a small peer set with diverse roles and a facilitator/coach; time-boxed cycles for questioning, action, and reflection; sponsor who will authorize experiments and surface organizational constraints.

**Nice-to-have:** Baseline problem measures, learning objective, stakeholder access, and budget for small experiments.

**When data is thin:** Begin with questions to identify what must be learned, then run a bounded action that produces evidence instead of filling gaps with expert assertions.
## Procedure
1. **Contract with the sponsor.** Define the live problem, decision boundary, progress measures, group authority, cadence, and what cannot change. The problem must create real consequences.
2. **Form the set.** Assemble four to eight people with different knowledge and a shared commitment to attend. The coach establishes questioning, reflective listening, confidentiality, and equal-airtime rules.
3. **Frame through questions.** The presenter speaks briefly; members ask open questions before proposing solutions. Capture assumptions, stakeholders, unknowns, and hypotheses.
4. **Choose an action experiment.** Select a small, authorized intervention with a named owner, deadline, expected signal, and method for gathering feedback. Action must occur between sessions.
5. **Review evidence and learn.** At the next meeting, inspect results, ask what surprised the set, and identify how questioning, power, or collaboration affected the action. Adapt the next experiment.
6. **Close and transfer.** Summarize outcomes, actions tested, learning behaviors, and recommendations for the sponsor. Agree how participants will use the practice in regular work.
## Output Contract
An action-learning record containing the sponsor problem and boundary, participant set, question log or learning themes, action experiments with owners and evidence, results against problem measures, reflection insights, and transferable commitments. It must show both action on the real problem and learning from that action; a workshop notes document alone is insufficient.
## Evidence
Operational results, participant attendance, action completion, and stakeholder feedback are observed. The group’s explanations for results are hypotheses until further tested. Record the sponsor’s authority limits and any blocked actions. Treat the coach’s observations about learning behaviors as qualitative evidence, not as a performance rating.
## Checks
- The sponsor can authorize experiments and receives regular progress updates.
- The set contains diverse perspectives but is small enough for genuine questioning.
- Each cycle produces an action with owner, deadline, and observable signal.
- Reflection examines both the business result and how the group learned.
- Participants, not the coach, own the next action.
## Failure Modes
- Choosing a fictional case because no sponsor will tolerate real experimentation.
- Letting the most senior member answer every question before the set thinks.
- Holding reflective conversations with no action between sessions.
- Treating the coach as a subject-matter expert who supplies the solution.
- Measuring participation smiles instead of progress on the sponsor’s problem.
