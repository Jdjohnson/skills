# COSO ERM Framework
## Purpose
Apply COSO's five-component architecture to design enterprise risk governance that hard-wires risk appetite and oversight into strategy-setting and performance management. The result connects risk decisions to how the organization sets objectives and reviews results.
## Use
- Building or upgrading enterprise risk governance tied to strategy and performance
- Aligning board oversight, risk appetite, and management accountability
- Integrating risk into objective-setting, culture, and review cycles rather than a siloed list
## Avoid
- You need a lightweight process standard for risk process steps across any context—use iso-31000
- The immediate need is a project-level risk register and responses—use risk-management-plan
- You need operational continuity or IT recovery design, not enterprise risk governance—use business-continuity-planning or disaster-recovery-planning
## Inputs
**Must-have:**
- Strategy and performance objectives at enterprise and unit level
- Current risk inventory, appetite statements, and escalation practices
- Governance roles (board, management, risk, audit) and reporting cadence
- Culture and decision-rights evidence on how risk is owned day to day

**Nice-to-have:** board committee charters, incentive plans, internal-audit themes, and prior risk-event reviews.

**When data is thin:** start with the few strategic objectives that could be materially impaired; do not manufacture enterprise-wide risk ratings from workshop opinion.
## Procedure
1. **Anchor the assessment in strategy.** Select strategic objectives, performance measures, and decisions where risk appetite should alter a choice. Output: strategy-risk linkage map.
2. **Examine governance and culture.** Trace board oversight, management accountability, incentives, and escalation behavior to identify where ownership is nominal or conflicted. Output: governance-and-culture gap log.
3. **Test strategy and objective-setting.** Check whether alternatives, appetite, tolerances, and resource commitments are considered before objectives become fixed. Output: objective-setting control design.
4. **Design the performance component.** Define how risks are identified, assessed for severity, prioritized, treated, and aggregated against appetite. Output: performance workflow and risk aggregation view.
5. **Install review and reporting loops.** Set trigger-based review, lessons from changes or failures, management information, and board reporting requirements. Output: five-component target architecture and migration backlog.
## Output Contract
A five-component ERM architecture showing decision rights, appetite and tolerance statements, objective-setting checkpoints, risk-performance workflow, review triggers, and information flows to management and the board. It must show where risk changes a strategic or performance decision. A list of risks without governance design does not meet the contract.
## Evidence
Use charters, meeting packs, delegated authorities, incident records, and performance measures as direct evidence. Record appetite statements as untested when management cannot demonstrate a decision that used them. Label design choices as proposed controls, not existing practice. Escalation thresholds require an accountable forum and decision consequence, not merely a reporting color.
## Checks
- All five components have concrete practices, owners, and interfaces.
- Appetite is expressed against strategic objectives and measurable tolerance where feasible.
- Board, executive, business, risk, and audit roles do not duplicate assurance responsibilities.
- Material risks can be aggregated without hiding concentration across units.
- Reviews can revise objectives or controls after a change in strategy or performance.
## Failure Modes
- Treating the framework as a five-column risk register.
- Publishing appetite statements that never affect planning, capital allocation, or incentives.
- Assigning risk ownership to a central team while business leaders control the decisions.
- Confusing a crisis response protocol with enterprise-level governance.
