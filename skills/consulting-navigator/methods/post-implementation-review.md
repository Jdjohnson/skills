# Post-Implementation Review
## Purpose
Evaluate a completed change against stated outcomes and extract decision-ready lessons for scale, rework, or stop. It examines results after stabilization and recommends action, rather than maintaining a live benefits dashboard or completing administrative project closure.
## Use
- Evaluating whether a change achieved intended outcomes after stabilization
- Capturing transferable lessons before the team disbands
- Feeding go/no-go or rework decisions after a pilot or major release
## Avoid
- Benefits tracking should be continuous ownership, not a one-time review—use benefits-realization-management
- You only need project administrative close-out and archive—use project-closure-and-lessons-learned
- The change has not yet stabilized enough for outcome measurement—wait or use formative checkpoints
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Original objectives, success criteria, and benefit hypotheses; actual outcome and adoption data after a defined stabilization window; cost, timeline, and scope variance against the change plan; stakeholder and user feedback on what worked and what blocked adoption; inventory of open risks, defects, and residual resistance.

**Nice-to-have:** baseline data, counterfactual or comparison cohort, support-ticket trends, audit results, and prior pilot learnings.

**When data is thin:** narrow the conclusion to what can be observed, retain unresolved benefit claims, and schedule a follow-up measurement rather than calling the change successful.
## Procedure
1. **Reconstruct the original case.** Retrieve approved objectives, targets, baseline, scope, risks, and intended users; record any authorized changes made during delivery. Output: review frame.
2. **Set the stabilization window.** Confirm that users have had enough exposure and early defects are separated from steady-state performance. Output: measurement cutoff and cohort definition.
3. **Reconcile outcomes with intent.** Compare actual benefit, adoption, cost, schedule, quality, and risk results to the original case. Explain variances through evidence, not hindsight narratives. Output: outcome scorecard.
4. **Investigate causes and transferability.** Interview users and delivery owners, inspect workarounds, and distinguish design flaws from local conditions. Output: lessons and causal findings.
5. **Make a disposition decision.** Recommend scale, sustain, remediate, pause, or stop; assign owners, dates, and measures for residual actions. Output: signed action register.
6. **Publish reusable learning.** Convert selected lessons into changes to playbooks, controls, training, or future business cases. Output: learning release.
## Output Contract
A review package with original success criteria, actual results, scope and variance reconciliation, evidence-tagged findings, unresolved risks, lessons, and a recommendation on scale, remediation, sustainment, or stop. It must identify accountable owners for actions and avoid retroactively redefining success to match the result.
## Evidence
Treat system measures, approved baselines, cost records, and dated incident logs as observed. Label stakeholder explanations and causal stories as inferred unless corroborated. State assumptions about seasonality, adoption maturity, and comparison groups. Keep missing data visible; an absent measure is not proof of benefit. If a benefit accrues later than the review window, specify the future owner and measurement date.
## Checks
- Original targets and authorized scope changes are traceable.
- Outcome measures use the same definitions and populations as the original case.
- Adoption and business outcomes are reported separately.
- Lessons include a causal condition and a recipient who can reuse them.
- The disposition decision has named owners, deadlines, and a route for escalation.
## Failure Modes
- Holding the review at go-live before users have reached normal operating behavior.
- Calling on-time delivery a benefit despite flat business outcomes.
- Asking only the project team, who may miss frontline workarounds.
- Rewriting targets after the result is known.
- Capturing lessons in a document with no change to future delivery practice.
