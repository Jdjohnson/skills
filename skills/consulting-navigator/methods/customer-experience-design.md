# Customer Experience Design
## Purpose
Design the intended end-to-end customer experience and the roadmap of touchpoint, process, and channel changes to deliver it. The work turns a diagnosed journey into deliberate experience principles, moments, and operating changes rather than a list of isolated fixes.
## Use
- Redesigning end-to-end customer experience across channels and touchpoints
- Turning journey insights into a prioritized experience target state and roadmap
- Aligning product, service, and marketing on one intended experience
## Avoid
- You still need to discover the current journey and pain points—use customer-journey-mapping first
- The problem is a single operational process without multi-touchpoint scope—use process-mapping or service-blueprinting
- You only need messaging/persona clarity—use customer-persona-development or brand-archetypes
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Current-state journey map with pain points and emotion peaks; Customer segments or personas and success metrics (NPS, CSAT, effort); Channel and touchpoint inventory with owners; Operational constraints, tech stack limits, and service levels.

**Nice-to-have:** Brand promise, service transcripts, experience design standards, accessibility requirements, and planned product releases.

**When data is thin:** Design only the highest-consequence moment, label its hypotheses, and test a prototype before committing shared platform work.
## Procedure
1. **Select the experience promise.** Translate the brand and segment need into two or three observable principles, such as “progress is visible without calling support.” Identify the journey moments where breaking each principle is most costly.
2. **Set a target state for each moment.** For the priority moments, specify the customer action, desired feeling, channel handoff, response time, and recovery path. Sketch the future flow from the customer’s viewpoint before debating systems.
3. **Design the service mechanics.** Define content, interface, policy, staffing, data, and escalation changes needed to make the target moment reliable. Expose conflicts, such as a low-effort promise that requires an unavailable account status.
4. **Prioritize experience bets.** Score changes by customer impact, reach, operational feasibility, dependency, and reversibility. Separate quick repairs from enabling capabilities and assign a single accountable experience owner to each bet.
5. **Prototype and sequence.** Test scripts, screens, or service scenarios with relevant customers; revise the target state; then build a roadmap with releases, measures, and handoff decisions.
## Output Contract
A target-experience package containing principles, a future-state journey for priority moments, moment-level design requirements, an ownership map, and a phased roadmap. Each roadmap item states the customer outcome, delivery dependency, accountable leader, release test, and success measure. It must make clear what customers will experience differently, not merely catalogue touchpoints.
## Evidence
Tag current friction and metric baselines as observed; distinguish customer interpretation from team inference. Treat the target emotion, channel preference, and behavior change as hypotheses until tested. Record service, legal, privacy, and technology constraints beside the design decision they limit. A prototype result from one segment does not automatically establish a universal design rule.
## Checks
- Every priority moment has a defined customer outcome and a recovery path when service fails.
- The future state removes or deliberately manages a known current friction.
- Cross-channel handoffs preserve context rather than asking customers to repeat information.
- Owners can authorize the policy or operational changes assigned to them.
- Measures include customer effort or sentiment and an operational reliability measure.
## Failure Modes
- Redesigning attractive screens while leaving the cancellation policy that causes the frustration.
- Declaring an omnichannel promise without a shared customer record at the handoff.
- Treating a journey-map pain point as a solution without testing the proposed moment.
- Giving marketing responsibility for a service promise that operations cannot staff.
