# Hoshin Kanri
## Purpose
Cascade breakthrough objectives through catchball into aligned goals, metrics, and actions at every organizational level. It turns a small number of strategic shifts into negotiated, reviewable work rather than a cascade of disconnected targets.
## Use
- Cascading a few breakthrough objectives into aligned annual plans and metrics.
- Creating catchball negotiation so corporate goals become team-owned targets.
- Linking strategy to PDCA review rhythms across levels of the organization.
## Avoid
- Strategy content itself is unclear or contested—use strategic-alignment-framework or strategy-map first.
- You only need a performance scorecard without deployment mechanics—use balanced-scorecard.
- The need is project portfolio governance, not annual policy deployment—use pmo-setup-and-governance or benefits-realization-management.
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Enterprise vision and a small set of breakthrough objectives for the horizon; Current performance baselines and resource constraints; Leadership willing to run catchball across levels; Review cadence and owners for X-matrix or deployment plans.

**Useful additions:** annual objectives, initiative inventory, capacity constraints, and prior-year learning.
## Procedure
1. Translate the long-range direction into no more than a few breakthrough objectives with a target condition and a reason the current trajectory will not reach it.
2. Establish annual objectives and leading measures that plausibly close the breakthrough gap. Remove projects that do not contribute, even if they have executive sponsors.
3. Draft an X-matrix linking breakthroughs, annual objectives, metrics, priority initiatives, and accountable leaders. Use relationship marks sparingly so the logic remains visible.
4. Conduct catchball: send the draft down and across the organization, request countermeasures, expose conflicts over resources, and revise targets or means until teams accept both the aim and constraints.
5. Create local deployment plans that show each team's contribution, experiments, owners, and monthly milestones. A local metric must connect upward, not simply be a departmental activity count.
6. Run PDCA reviews: inspect gaps, test countermeasures, escalate cross-boundary blockers, and adjust means while preserving the breakthrough intent unless evidence warrants a strategic reset.
## Output Contract
Provide a deployment package with the breakthrough logic, annual objectives, linked X-matrix, catchball decisions, local action plans, owners, target values, and review calendar. It must reveal how a frontline action contributes to a strategic objective and where leadership has accepted trade-offs. A KPI dashboard without negotiated means is incomplete.
## Evidence
Use baseline performance and capacity data for target setting. Record target assumptions, causal links, and resource dependencies separately from measured results. Catchball objections are evidence about feasibility, not resistance to be edited out. When a target lacks a credible mechanism, mark it as an aspiration and require an experiment before cascading it as a commitment.
## Checks
- Breakthroughs are few enough that leaders can review them deeply.
- Every annual objective has both a lagging outcome and a leading measure.
- Relationship links in the X-matrix are explainable in plain language.
- Catchball produces documented changes or explicit leadership decisions on unresolved conflicts.
- Review meetings use gap-to-target countermeasures, not status recitals.
## Failure Modes
- Cascading targets top-down and calling acknowledgment catchball.
- Filling an X-matrix with every initiative so priorities become unreadable.
- Letting departments improve local measures that undermine the breakthrough objective.
- Reviewing red metrics monthly without assigning a countermeasure owner or test date.
