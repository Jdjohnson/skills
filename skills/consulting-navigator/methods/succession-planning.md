# Succession Planning
## Purpose
Identify critical roles, assess internal successor readiness, and develop people so key positions have viable continuity. It creates role-specific successor slates and development commitments rather than a general headcount forecast.
## Use
- Protecting continuity in leadership and other critical roles.
- Building ready-now and ready-later successor pools.
- Reducing key-person risk before growth, M&A, or retirements.
## Avoid
- You need broad workforce supply/demand forecasts—use workforce-planning.
- You need to design the whole attract-develop-retain system—use talent-management-strategy.
- Critical roles and competencies are undefined—use competency-mapping or job-analysis-and-evaluation first.
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Critical role list and role requirements; current incumbents, flight or retirement risk, and tenure; talent assessments or performance and potential data; development options and mobility constraints; time horizon for expected vacancies.

**Useful additions:** emergency delegations, external labor-market availability, diversity representation data, succession history, and confidentiality protocols.
## Procedure
1. Define role criticality using business impact, scarcity of knowledge, time to replace, and exposure to vacancy. Do not equate hierarchy with criticality; pivotal technical and operational roles may qualify.
2. Build a role profile for each priority seat: outcomes, essential experiences, capabilities, relationships, location requirements, and minimum transition conditions.
3. Assess incumbent risk and vacancy horizon. Separate an immediate emergency successor from a candidate who could assume the role after development.
4. Nominate a slate using calibrated evidence. Rate each person ready now, ready within one to two years, ready later, or not currently viable, and explain the rating against the role profile rather than reputation.
5. Create individual development moves: stretch assignment, acting role, mentor, formal learning, exposure, or planned rotation. Name sponsor, timing, and a readiness proof point.
6. Review slates confidentially at a fixed cadence, update for role changes and employee preferences, and trigger contingency action when a critical role has no credible cover.
## Output Contract
Provide a critical-role register, role-risk heat map, confidential successor slate, readiness definitions, individual development actions, and review calendar. For every critical role, show incumbent risk, emergency cover, one or more longer-term successors where available, readiness evidence, gaps, and accountable executive. Empty slates are explicit continuity risks with a mitigation action.
## Evidence
Use performance history, assessed capability, and completed experiences as evidence; do not treat manager sponsorship as readiness proof. Record employee interest, mobility, and eligibility separately from potential. Restrict access to named-person records and avoid publishing comparative labels beyond the decision group. When data are thin, classify readiness as provisional and schedule a specific assessment. Never promise a role to a candidate through the planning artifact.
## Checks
- Critical roles have defined outcomes and replacement horizon, not only titles.
- Readiness criteria are consistent across functions and explicitly distinguish emergency coverage from succession.
- Development actions close a named role gap and have an owner.
- Talent review considers availability, willingness, and diversity of the pipeline.
- The review cadence updates both vacant-risk roles and candidates’ changed circumstances.
## Failure Modes
- Naming a high performer as successor without evidence of the role’s required experience.
- Calling a deputy emergency cover when they lack authority, relationships, or availability to act tomorrow.
- Keeping a confidential list unchanged for years after candidates leave or decline mobility.
- Using succession meetings to promise promotions instead of managing role continuity and development.
