# Workforce Planning
## Purpose
Forecast demand and supply of people and skills against strategic goals and close gaps with hire, build, borrow, or redesign actions. It converts a business plan into role, skill, location, and timing choices rather than a single undifferentiated headcount target.
## Use
- Aligning multi-year headcount and skill supply to strategy
- Stress-testing plans under growth, automation, or restructuring scenarios
- Quantifying hire-build-borrow gaps before budget and recruiting cycles
## Avoid
- You need explanatory drivers of attrition or engagement—use people-analytics
- You need named successors for critical roles—use succession-planning
- You need the full talent system design—use talent-management-strategy
## Inputs
**Must-have:**
- Strategic plans and growth or efficiency assumptions by unit
- Current workforce by role, skill, location, and cost
- Attrition, retirement, and mobility historical rates
- Productivity or staffing ratios where applicable
- Scenarios for demand drivers (volume, automation, mix)

**Nice-to-have:** approved requisitions, time-to-fill data, contingent-labor contracts, and licensing constraints.

**When data is thin:** estimate critical roles only, use broad capacity bands, and name the data that would change the decision.
## Procedure
1. **Set the planning units and horizon.** Break strategy into businesses, locations, role families, and periods. Choose FTE, productive hours, certifications, or a combination. Output: a planning grid with business owners.
2. **Translate business drivers into demand.** Connect volume, projects, service levels, and automation to labor demand using workload ratios or engineered capacity. Calculate base, upside, and downside cases. Output: demand by role and period.
3. **Build the supply roll-forward.** Start with incumbents; add hires, moves, and contractors; subtract exits and retirements. Separate people counts from usable skills where qualification takes time. Output: unconstrained supply forecast.
4. **Expose gaps and constraints.** Compare demand and supply by role, geography, and time. Mark shortages, surpluses, skill risks, recruiting lead times, and cost effects. Output: gap heat map and risks.
5. **Choose gap-closing actions.** Test hiring, redeployment, training, automation, outsourcing, demand deferral, or work redesign. State lead time, cost, and residual risk. Output: sequenced action portfolio.
6. **Govern the refresh.** Assign owners, set review cadence, and define triggers such as a delayed launch or attrition spike. Output: approved plan with refresh rules.
## Output Contract
A decision-ready workforce plan containing the demand model, supply roll-forward, role-level gaps, scenario ranges, and actions by owner. It identifies when capacity becomes constrained, not merely year-end headcount. Each action states affected roles, contribution date, cost range, and its business assumption.
## Evidence
Treat payroll and HRIS rosters, accepted offers, retirement eligibility, and workload as observed. Label productivity ratios, turnover forecasts, automation savings, and mobility rates as assumptions unless measured in comparable conditions. Preserve the source period and definition for every ratio; a staffed position is not necessarily productive capacity. Distinguish confirmed credentials from self-reported skills. Do not offset a regulated-technician shortage with surplus general labor.
## Checks
- Demand drivers reconcile to the operating plan for each material unit.
- Supply begins with a dated roster and accounts separately for exits, joins, and internal moves.
- Gaps are visible by period, role, and location before aggregate totals are shown.
- Every closing action has a feasible lead time and a named owner.
- Scenarios identify the assumptions that move the plan most.
## Failure Modes
- Applying one revenue-per-employee ratio to roles with different workloads.
- Treating vacancies as productive supply before a start date.
- Counting training enrollment as demonstrated proficiency.
- Solving an engineering shortage with unrelated administrative surplus.
- Publishing an annual number after a first-quarter shortfall is unavoidable.
