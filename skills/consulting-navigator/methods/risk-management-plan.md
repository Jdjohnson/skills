# Risk Management Plan
## Purpose
Identify, score, and treat project risks with owners and responses so uncertainty is managed against delivery objectives, not merely listed. The plan makes uncertainty actionable before it becomes an issue, claim, or missed commitment.
## Use
- Building a project-level approach to identify, analyze, treat, and monitor delivery risks
- Setting risk appetite, thresholds, and escalation for a specific project or program
- Linking mitigations to owners, cost, and schedule impact before execution
## Avoid
- You need enterprise or portfolio ERM architecture—use enterprise-risk-management or coso-erm-framework
- The failure mode is detailed design reliability for a product/process—use fmea
- You only need to govern approved baseline changes—use change-control-process
## Inputs
**Must-have:**
- Project objectives, scope, and constraints
- Stakeholder risk appetite and escalation thresholds
- Known assumptions, dependencies, and external interfaces
- Historical issues from similar projects if available
- Cost/schedule baseline for impact scoring

**Nice-to-have:** contingency reserve rules, contract risk allocation, and external early-warning indicators.

**When data is thin:** use qualitative ranges with an explicit confidence flag and schedule the next evidence-gathering action in the register.
## Procedure
1. Agree the project objectives at risk, rating scales, appetite, reporting cadence, and escalation threshold. Define what distinguishes a risk from an issue already occurring.
2. Elicit risks from scope assumptions, interfaces, schedule dependencies, technical reviews, procurement, and lessons learned. Write each as cause–event–effect, not as a vague topic such as “supplier.”
3. Assess likelihood and impact on cost, schedule, scope, quality, safety, or benefits. Record rationale, proximity, and cross-risk dependencies; combine ratings only according to the agreed scale.
4. Select a response: avoid, mitigate, transfer, accept, or exploit where relevant. Specify preventive action, contingent action, trigger, residual rating, owner, due date, and required budget or reserve.
5. Prioritize the register by exposure and urgency, then escalate items beyond delegated tolerance. Link accepted risk to contingency and decision records.
6. Review at the delivery cadence, update evidence and triggers, close retired risks with rationale, and convert realized risks into managed issues without erasing their history.
## Output Contract
Provide a living project risk register and plan. For every material entry include a unique ID, cause–event–effect statement, category, likelihood, multidimensional impact, score rationale, proximity, owner, response, trigger, response due date, residual exposure, status, and escalation decision. Add the project’s appetite and review cadence plus a summary of top exposure and contingency use. The artifact must guide delivery action; it is not a brainstorm list or corporate risk policy.
## Evidence
Document source evidence for each rating: prior project data, contract terms, technical assessment, or owner judgment. Label untested probability and impact estimates as assumptions. Keep external dependencies visible even when their owners sit outside the project. Do not reduce a risk score merely because an action has been promised; reduce it only after evidence shows the control is effective.
## Checks
- Entries describe uncertain events and effects, not completed problems.
- Owners can authorize or coordinate the stated response.
- Ratings use the agreed scales and cite a rationale.
- Every high exposure has a dated response, trigger, and residual view.
- Escalations and contingency decisions match the stated appetite.
## Failure Modes
- Listing “budget” or “schedule” as a risk without an event that could affect it.
- Marking every item high and making prioritization meaningless.
- Closing a risk after a mitigation meeting, before the mitigation has changed exposure.
- Giving a vendor-owned risk to an internal analyst with no contractual leverage.
- Leaving inter-project dependencies off the register because they are inconvenient to control.
