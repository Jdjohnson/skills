# Debt Restructuring Framework
## Purpose
Redesign debt instruments, covenants, and claim priority so the firm can service obligations and restore a viable going-concern capital structure. It creates a creditor-negotiation path grounded in cash capacity and legal claim position.
## Use
- Covenant breach or near-breach requires a negotiated capital-structure fix
- Debt service capacity has structurally declined and market refinance is blocked
- Distressed or pre-distress situations need a creditor-aligned path back to viability
## Avoid
- The firm is healthy and only needs routine refinancing or capital raising—use treasury-management-framework or capital-budgeting-framework
- The bottleneck is operational cash generation with no balance-sheet redesign required—use turnaround-strategy or cost-optimization-framework first
- You need a sale or going-concern valuation rather than a creditor negotiation path—use discounted-cash-flow-valuation
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Full debt schedule: instruments, rates, covenants, maturities, security, and guarantees; Integrated cash-flow forecast and liquidity runway under base and downside cases; Collateral map and priority-of-claims waterfalls by creditor class; Creditor roster with exposure, leverage, and negotiation stance; Business-plan assumptions that support post-restructure debt service capacity.

**Nice-to-have:** Intercreditor agreements, tax analysis, equity-holder support, asset valuations, and draft standstill terms.

**When data is thin:** Freeze discretionary cash uses, build a 13-week cash view, and qualify creditor proposals as preliminary until claims and collateral are verified.
## Procedure
1. **Stabilize liquidity and information.** Create a short-term cash control, confirm reporting cadence, and secure necessary waivers or standstill discussions. Reconcile every instrument, guarantee, collateral right, and covenant calculation.
2. **Test sustainable debt capacity.** Build integrated base and downside cash flows after essential operating investment. Calculate interest coverage, amortization capacity, covenant headroom, and the maturity wall under each case.
3. **Construct the claims waterfall.** Determine legal priority, security value, guarantor support, and recovery range by creditor class. Separate economic negotiating leverage from nominal loan balance.
4. **Develop restructuring alternatives.** Model maturity extension, amortization holiday, rate reset, covenant reset, debt-for-equity exchange, new-money priority, asset sale, and partial haircut. Show cash, dilution, recovery, and execution consequences.
5. **Negotiate and execute the path.** Select a creditor-consistent proposal, define consents and conditions precedent, coordinate legal documentation, and track milestones through closing and post-close covenant reporting.
## Output Contract
A restructuring roadmap containing the reconciled debt schedule, liquidity cases, claims waterfall, viable capital-structure alternatives, creditor engagement plan, selected term-sheet principles, approvals, and closing sequence. It states post-restructure debt-service capacity and covenant headroom. It must not disguise an operating turnaround plan as a debt solution.
## Evidence
Treat executed agreements, lender statements, collateral filings, and closed financial results as observed. Forecast EBITDA, asset-sale proceeds, and recovery values are assumptions with ranges. Do not call a creditor supportive until it has indicated terms or signed documentation. Preserve legal uncertainty as an issue for counsel rather than resolving it through spreadsheet convention.
## Checks
- Every funded and contingent obligation appears in the debt schedule.
- Base and downside cases both preserve enough cash for agreed operations.
- Claim priority has legal review before it informs a recovery proposal.
- New money, security, and guarantees are modeled for their effect on every creditor class.
- The execution plan identifies consent thresholds and a fallback if they are missed.
## Failure Modes
- Negotiating an extension before discovering a springing covenant or cross-default.
- Offering a maturity fix that leaves interest cash-pay above sustainable capacity.
- Treating lenders with different collateral positions as one homogeneous creditor group.
- Promising a recovery value based on an asset sale with no buyer evidence.
