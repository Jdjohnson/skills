# Expert Interviewing
## Purpose
Extract deep, contextual primary insight from subject-matter experts to ground or challenge case hypotheses. One-to-one probing reveals operational mechanisms, exceptions, and uncertainty that a desk review or group discussion can obscure.
## Use
- Deep qualitative insight from practitioners or specialists is required to ground analysis.
- Secondary sources are thin, outdated, or commercially biased.
- You need to test hypotheses or map how a market, process, or technology really operates.
## Avoid
- You need anonymous multi-expert consensus under uncertainty—use delphic-method.
- The evidence needed is behavioral or large-n attitude data—use survey-design-methodology or customer-evidence-triangulation.
- You only need published facts already available—use secondary-research first.
## Inputs
**Must-have:**
- Interview guide tied to explicit learning objectives or hypotheses.
- Target expert profiles and access path (warm intro, panel, client network).
- Note-taking and synthesis protocol (including attribution rules).
- Enough interviews planned to spot convergence and contradiction.

**Nice-to-have:** screening questionnaire, conflict disclosures, interpreters, compensation policy, and a hypothesis tracker.

**When data is thin:** reduce the confidence of the conclusion, seek a countervailing role, and avoid presenting one articulate expert as market consensus.
## Procedure
1. **Specify learning objectives.** Translate the case into questions that experts can answer from direct experience and list hypotheses that could be confirmed, refined, or disproved. Output: interview brief.
2. **Recruit a contrasting panel.** Define role, geography, tenure, and perspective criteria; include operators, buyers, regulators, or former participants when they have different knowledge. Screen for actual experience and conflicts. Output: respondent matrix.
3. **Prepare the guide and safeguards.** Order broad narrative prompts before hypothesis tests, plan probes for examples and exceptions, and agree attribution, confidentiality, and note-taking rules. Output: interview guide and consent record.
4. **Conduct probing conversations.** Establish the expert’s vantage point, ask them to reconstruct real decisions, then challenge inconsistencies with neutral follow-ups. Capture the basis and date of each claim, not just the conclusion. Output: structured notes.
5. **Synthesize convergence and dissent.** Code claims by learning objective, evidence basis, confidence, and respondent type. Seek disconfirming interviews before treating repetition as truth. Output: insight matrix.
6. **Return an attributable view.** Report what experts said, where accounts diverge, and which claims need validation through data or research. Output: interview insight report.
## Output Contract
An assessment with the learning objectives, respondent profile mix, interview protocol, claim-by-claim synthesis, evidence basis, convergence and dissent, attribution treatment, and implications for case hypotheses. It must let a reader distinguish direct expert knowledge from interpretation and identify which claims remain unverified.
## Evidence
An interview is evidence of what a person with a stated vantage point reports; it is not automatically a fact about the market. Record dates, role relevance, incentives, and whether a claim is first-hand. Mark researcher synthesis as inferred. Honor confidentiality agreements by aggregating or anonymizing claims before circulation.
## Checks
- The panel covers relevant and contrasting vantage points, not a client-friendly echo chamber.
- Every major claim has a traceable note, respondent type, and confidence level.
- The guide includes probes for concrete examples and counterexamples.
- Contradictory testimony is displayed rather than averaged into false consensus.
- Any commercially sensitive assertion is reviewed for attribution and disclosure limits.
## Failure Modes
- Recruiting prestigious titles with no direct knowledge of the decision at issue.
- Asking leading questions that invite agreement with the case team’s hypothesis.
- Recording conclusions but not the example, date, or basis that would allow evaluation.
- Treating three similar experts from one vendor as independent corroboration.
- Quoting an expert outside the agreed attribution boundary.
