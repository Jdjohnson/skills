# Valuation Multiples
## Purpose
Estimate relative enterprise or equity value by applying peer trading and transaction multiples to normalized metrics. It anchors value in observed market pricing while making the comparability judgment explicit.
## Use
- Anchoring value with trading or transaction multiples of comparable companies.
- Cross-checking intrinsic DCF outputs against current market pricing.
- Building an initial bid or fairness range when peer data is available.
## Avoid
- No credible peer set exists or the business is unique—use discounted-cash-flow-valuation.
- You still need verified sustainable earnings as the multiple base—complete financial-due-diligence first.
- The asset is best valued as staged options under uncertainty—use real-options-analysis.
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Target financial metric used as the multiple base (e.g., EBITDA, revenue, earnings); peer company or precedent transaction set with stated selection criteria; market prices, enterprise value bridges, and multiple definitions; normalization adjustments for one-time items, capital structure, and accounting differences.

**Nice-to-have:** consensus estimates, segment disclosures, control-premium data, transaction dates, and currency conversion history.

**When data is thin:** narrow the conclusion to a broad range, show excluded peers, and rely on discounted-cash-flow-valuation where comparability is too weak.
## Procedure
1. **Define the valuation question.** Specify enterprise or equity value, stake, valuation date, metric, currency, and whether the evidence is public trading, precedents, or both. State the relevant market and business-model characteristics.
2. **Screen and select comparables.** Build a long list then filter for sector, geography, size, growth, margin, customer mix, capital intensity, and transaction circumstance. Document inclusions and exclusions before looking at the resulting median.
3. **Normalize the metric and bridge value.** Adjust EBITDA, revenue, or earnings for material one-time items and accounting differences. Rebuild market capitalization, debt, leases, cash, and non-controlling interests consistently to derive enterprise value.
4. **Calculate and apply the range.** Compute relevant multiples, inspect mean, median, quartiles, and outliers, then apply selected ranges to the normalized target metric. Convert enterprise value to equity value using a dated net-debt bridge.
5. **Triangulate and explain judgment.** Compare trading and precedent indications, test the effect of peer selection and normalization, and explain any chosen premium or discount with evidence rather than preference.
## Output Contract
The valuation book presents the target definition, peer-screening criteria, comparable table, normalized metrics, enterprise-value bridge, observed multiple distribution, applied range, equity-value bridge, sensitivities, and exclusions. It separates data from judgment and states the valuation date. It supports an initial bid, negotiation, or fairness discussion; it is not an intrinsic cash-flow forecast.
## Evidence
Market prices and filing data are observed only as of the stated date. Adjustments to recurring earnings require a calculation and supporting explanation. Treat selected peer ranges, control premia, and liquidity discounts as judgments, with alternatives shown. Do not mix LTM and forward multiples without clear labels, or enterprise value and equity value without a bridge. Flag missing segment data and stale transaction information as limitations.
## Checks
- Peer selection criteria are applied before the outcome range is chosen.
- All multiples use consistent numerator and denominator definitions.
- Net debt, leases, minorities, and associates are treated consistently.
- Outliers are explained rather than silently removed.
- The applied range is traceable to the observed distribution and target differences.
## Failure Modes
- Choosing peers because they produce a desired price.
- Applying an EV/EBITDA multiple directly to equity value.
- Using reported EBITDA that includes a non-recurring gain.
- Combining a 2022 transaction premium with today’s trading environment.
- Calling a median objective when the peer screen is arbitrary.
