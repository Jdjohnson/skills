# Hypothesis-Driven Problem Solving
## Purpose
Drive diagnosis by stating provisional answers early and collecting only the evidence that can confirm or kill them. It concentrates effort on discriminating tests while keeping credible alternatives visible.
## Use
- The problem is complex enough that exhaustive analysis would be too slow or costly.
- You can state a provisional answer and design efficient tests to confirm or kill it.
- Engagements need a governing story that sequences workstreams and evidence.
## Avoid
- You have no basis for even a weak initial hypothesis—use issue-tree and mece to structure the problem first.
- The task is pure exploration of customer needs without a proposed solution—use jobs-to-be-done or design-thinking.
- You already know the answer and only need implementation planning—use implementation-plan methods such as raci-matrix or critical-path-method.
## Inputs
**Must-have:** A crisply framed decision or problem statement; One or more falsifiable governing hypotheses (and alternatives); An evidence plan mapping each hypothesis to critical data or analyses; Disconfirming tests and decision criteria for accept/revise/kill.

**Useful additions:** prior analyses, access to decision makers, a fact base, and a time-boxed workplan.
## Procedure
1. Frame the decision and success measure precisely enough that competing answers can be tested. Ask what decision will change, by when, and what counts as material impact.
2. Form a governing hypothesis from available facts, experience, and an initial issue decomposition. State its causal claim, predicted observations, and plausible alternatives.
3. Rank tests by discriminating power, speed, cost, and reversibility. Prefer a test that separates two explanations over a broad data collection exercise that merely adds detail.
4. Assign an evidence owner, source, method, and threshold for each critical test. Define in advance what observation will confirm, revise, or kill the hypothesis.
5. Run the tests in short waves; update the hypothesis log after each result. Stop workstreams whose result can no longer change the decision.
6. Synthesize the surviving answer with the evidence chain, residual uncertainty, and implications. Convert the validated diagnosis into a recommendation only after testing alternative explanations.
## Output Contract
Provide a hypothesis log and decision narrative that show the governing answer, alternatives, test design, results, changes of view, and final confidence. Include the evidence needed to reproduce each pivotal conclusion and the few unknowns that could still alter the decision. This is a falsifiable diagnosis, not an exhaustive issue tree or a list of unranked causes.
## Evidence
Label the starting answer as an assumption, even if a senior sponsor proposed it. Treat pre-specified tests and observed results differently from post-hoc rationalization. Seek disconfirming evidence deliberately, including data that would favor an alternative. Do not declare a hypothesis true because no one found contrary evidence when the test had little power or poor data coverage.
## Checks
- Every hypothesis predicts an observable difference from at least one alternative.
- Critical tests have a decision threshold before results are known.
- The plan eliminates low-value analysis rather than relabeling all analysis as testing.
- Changes to the hypothesis are recorded with their evidence trigger.
- The final recommendation states confidence and the most consequential unresolved test.
## Failure Modes
- Starting with an executive preference and treating confirming anecdotes as proof.
- Writing hypotheses so vaguely that any result can be called support.
- Building a detailed issue tree but never choosing the test that will change the answer fastest.
- Continuing a favored workstream after a result has already falsified its causal claim.
