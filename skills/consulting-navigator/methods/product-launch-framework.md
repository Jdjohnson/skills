# Product Launch Framework
## Purpose
Orchestrate cross-functional new product introduction so positioning, demand gen, enablement, supply, and support hit the market ready together. The framework turns a release date into a coordinated readiness decision rather than a handoff from product to marketing.
## Use
- A new product or major release requires coordinated go-live across marketing, sales, ops, and support
- Launch risk is high because multiple functions and partners must execute in parallel
- You need a stage-ready checklist, owners, and success metrics for introduction
## Avoid
- Product-market fit is still unproven—use product-market-fit-analysis, lean-startup, or minimum-viable-product first
- You only need channel strategy without launch execution detail—use go-to-market-strategy
- The work is continuous roadmap sequencing rather than a discrete launch event—use product-roadmap-development
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Defined offer, target segments, and value proposition; launch timing, geographies, and channel plan; cross-functional workstreams and readiness criteria; demand forecast, inventory plan, and success metrics.

**Nice-to-have:** launch retrospectives, partner agreements, beta feedback, media calendar, training completion data, and contingency stock options.

**When data is thin:** stage a limited geography or customer cohort, declare which readiness tests are provisional, and set a decision date before committing public announcements.
## Procedure
1. **Write the launch charter.** Specify the offer, audience, commercial objective, launch tier, date window, and accountable launch leader. Separate announcement, orderability, shipment, and support dates when they differ.
2. **Build workstreams and dependencies.** Establish product, product marketing, sales enablement, demand generation, channel, supply, legal, finance, and support tracks. Convert each deliverable into a dependency with an owner and required-by date.
3. **Define readiness gates.** For each workstream, set observable entrance criteria: approved positioning, trained sellers, priced SKUs, available inventory, support articles, and partner access. Name the executive who can accept a material gap.
4. **Run an integrated critical path.** Review dependency slippage weekly, highlight decisions that threaten first revenue, and maintain a launch risk register. Resolve cross-functional conflicts at the launch level rather than in separate status meetings.
5. **Rehearse the market moment.** Test order entry, fulfillment, sales demonstration, escalation, messaging, and reporting through a mock customer journey. Capture defects and retest the affected gate.
6. **Launch and learn.** Track leading indicators against the charter, conduct an early performance review, and decide whether to expand, correct, or pause the next wave.
## Output Contract
A launch plan with dates and owners for every readiness workstream; gating criteria and decision rights; one integrated dependency path; a risk and contingency register; a rehearsal record; and an early-market scorecard. It must identify what blocks first revenue and customer support, not just list promotional activities.
## Evidence
Treat signed pricing, stock availability, trained-user completion, and tested order flows as observed. Demand forecasts and media response are forecasts, so show the scenario and owner. Label untested partner capability and country-specific compliance as unknowns. Never close a readiness item because a slide exists; require the defined operational proof.
## Checks
- Every critical-path item has one accountable owner and a real due date.
- Launch gates distinguish must-pass readiness from post-launch enhancements.
- Supply, billing, and support are tested through an end-to-end customer scenario.
- Public claims match approved product capability and legal review.
- Success metrics include adoption or revenue plus service health, not impressions alone.
## Failure Modes
- Treating the release date as the launch date while orders cannot be booked.
- Training sales on features without a customer problem, objection, and qualification path.
- Promising channel partners a date before inventory allocation and portal access exist.
- Letting every workstream report green while one unowned integration blocks shipment.
- Measuring campaign clicks while ignoring activation, returns, or support load.
