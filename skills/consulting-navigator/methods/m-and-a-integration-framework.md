# M&A Integration Framework
## Purpose
Design and sequence the workstreams that combine two organizations so deal value is captured without destroying the going concern.
## Use
- Planning how two organizations will combine after a signed or imminent deal
- Translating synergy targets into workstreams, owners, and milestones
- Sequencing Day-1, first 100 days, and steady-state operating model changes
## Avoid
- You are still deciding whether the deal creates value—use synergies-assessment and commercial-due-diligence first
- The need is only a valuation model with no people or process change—use discounted-cash-flow-valuation or financial-due-diligence
- You need a pure culture diagnosis without an integration plan—use cultural-due-diligence or culture-assessment
## Inputs
**Must-have:**
- Deal thesis and approved or draft synergy targets by category
- Org charts, leadership maps, and critical-role inventory for both entities
- Baseline operating model for core functions (sales, ops, finance, IT, HR)
- Customer, contract, and system overlap or conflict list
- Integration constraints (regulatory, labor, systems freeze windows)

**Nice-to-have:** clean-team findings, data-room issues, separation obligations, transitional service agreements, retention-risk signals, and a tested Day-1 communications calendar.

**When data is thin:** protect customer service, payroll, compliance, and decision rights first. Keep synergy estimates provisional until a workstream owner has validated its baseline and dependencies.
## Procedure
1. **Translate the deal thesis into outcomes.** Break revenue, cost, capability, and risk objectives into measurable value cases with timing and named executive sponsors. Output: integration charter.
2. **Set governance and decision rights.** Establish an integration leader, workstream leads, escalation forums, issue thresholds, and clean-team rules where required. Output: governance map.
3. **Plan Day-1 continuity.** Identify decisions, communications, access, payroll, customer coverage, legal-entity actions, and system controls that must work on close. Output: Day-1 readiness checklist.
4. **Design workstreams and dependencies.** Map future operating choices across people, customers, process, technology, finance, and legal; expose handoffs such as org decisions before system access changes. Output: integrated master plan.
5. **Mobilize value capture.** Assign initiatives, baselines, milestones, cost to achieve, leading indicators, and risk mitigations through the first 100 days and into steady state. Output: value-capture dashboard.
## Output Contract
Deliver an integration plan with a deal-thesis traceability table, governance structure, Day-1 readiness view, cross-functional workstream plans, dependency map, milestone calendar, synergy register, costs to achieve, and risk log. Every synergy has an accountable owner, baseline, mechanism, timing, and decision dependency. The plan must preserve business continuity while making value capture auditable.
## Evidence
Tie reported value to approved deal assumptions, then label new estimates as validated, directional, or unverified. Keep legal, labor, antitrust, and data-sharing constraints visible in the master plan. Record the source date for headcount, spend, customer, and system baselines because either company may change before close. Do not count a synergy twice across workstreams or treat an announced organizational change as realized savings.
## Checks
- Day-1 actions have an owner, backup, deadline, and rehearsal where failure would halt operations.
- Workstream milestones show their dependencies rather than separate parallel timelines.
- Customer and critical-talent risks are reviewed with the value plan.
- Synergy baselines exclude one-time costs and double counts.
- Governance has clear authority for conflicts between value, continuity, and speed.
## Failure Modes
- Treating Day 1 as a communications event while access, invoicing, and customer escalation are unresolved.
- Launching functional workstreams without a single dependency owner.
- Announcing headcount savings before identifying roles essential to revenue continuity.
- Measuring “synergy identified” as though it were cash captured.
- Allowing each legacy company to retain its own approvals indefinitely.
