# PMBOK Framework
## Purpose
Structure project management practice across standard knowledge and process domains so planning and control are complete, consistent, and auditable. It tailors a comprehensive plan set to the project context instead of imposing a single lifecycle, stage-gate sequence, or sprint cadence.
## Use
- Establishing or auditing a comprehensive project management system against PMI knowledge/process domains
- Designing project plans that deliberately cover integration, scope, schedule, cost, quality, resource, communications, risk, procurement, and stakeholders
- Aligning a PMO or client methodology with a widely recognized body of knowledge
## Avoid
- You need a single mandatory process method with stage gates and roles already prescribed—use prince2
- The work is pure product agile and a lightweight empirical framework fits better—use scrum-framework
- You only need one technique (e.g., schedule math)—use critical-path-method or earned-value-management directly
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Project charter or mandate and success criteria; organizational process assets and constraints; stakeholder list and governance expectations; scope outline and major deliverables; constraints on time, cost, quality, and compliance.

**Nice-to-have:** lessons from comparable projects, vendor market intelligence, risk appetite statements, and an established enterprise toolset.

**When data is thin:** issue a tailored planning backlog, identify the unknowns blocking baselines, and obtain sponsor agreement on the minimum controls needed before execution.
## Procedure
1. **Assess project context and tailoring needs.** Review delivery uncertainty, regulatory obligations, contract model, team distribution, and organizational maturity. Decide which management domains need full plans and which need lightweight controls. Output: tailoring rationale.
2. **Integrate scope, schedule, cost, and quality.** Define deliverables and acceptance criteria, decompose work, connect dependencies, estimate resources, and establish baselines. Output: integrated baseline pack.
3. **Plan supporting management domains.** Establish resource, communication, risk, procurement, stakeholder, and change-control approaches with named owners and escalation points. Output: subsidiary plans and registers.
4. **Set monitoring architecture.** Specify status data, variance thresholds, review cadence, decision forums, and how approved changes update baselines. Output: control dashboard design.
5. **Operate and adapt.** Direct work, manage issues and risks, verify deliverables, and adjust the tailored plan when conditions change. Output: controlled execution record.
## Output Contract
A tailored project management plan set: charter linkage, deliverable breakdown, integrated baselines, subsidiary plans, governance calendar, registers, reporting definitions, and change-control path. It must state which domains are deliberately light, why they are light, and how the project will demonstrate acceptance—not merely list familiar project terms.
## Evidence
Treat approved charter scope, contracts, estimates, and stakeholder commitments as observed inputs. Label productivity estimates, vendor lead times, and risk probabilities as assumptions or inferences. Preserve the date and owner of each baseline. If a key domain lacks evidence, show the exposure in the risk register rather than implying plan completeness. Never mix approved budget with an unconstrained planning estimate.
## Checks
- Scope, schedule, cost, and acceptance criteria reconcile to the same deliverable set.
- Tailoring decisions are explicit; omitted controls have a risk rationale.
- Decision rights and escalation thresholds are usable by the actual sponsor group.
- Risks link to owners, responses, and contingency where material.
- Approved changes update the affected baselines and stakeholder communication.
## Failure Modes
- Copying every possible management artifact into a small project without tailoring.
- Treating a work breakdown structure as a schedule with dependencies.
- Building risk, procurement, and stakeholder plans after suppliers or opponents are already engaged.
- Reporting percent complete without objective acceptance evidence.
- Letting change requests alter delivery while the baseline remains unchanged.
