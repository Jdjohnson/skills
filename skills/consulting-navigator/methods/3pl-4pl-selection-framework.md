# 3PL/4PL Selection Framework
## Purpose
Structure make-vs-buy and provider options for logistics, then select 3PL/4PL partners on capability, cost, risk, and fit. It produces a defensible provider and operating-model recommendation rather than a generic procurement ranking.
## Use
- Evaluating make-vs-buy for warehousing, transport, or end-to-end logistics.
- Running a structured RFP to shortlist and select 3PL or 4PL partners.
- Redesigning the logistics operating model after network or channel change.
## Avoid
- You only need to redesign facility locations and flows—use logistics-network-design first.
- The issue is pure supplier power segmentation across all categories—use kraljic-matrix.
- You already selected a partner and need day-to-day waste removal—use lean-logistics.
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Current logistics cost, volume, and service-level baseline by lane or node; scope options (transport-only, warehouse, full 3PL, 4PL orchestration); must-have capabilities, systems interfaces, and compliance constraints; candidate provider list with capability and geographic coverage evidence; total cost of ownership model including transition and exit costs.

**Nice-to-have:** Peak forecasts, claims history, reference calls, site-audit findings, and draft commercial terms.

**When data is thin:** Use ranges for transition and exception costs and make a no-award recommendation if service feasibility cannot be verified.
## Procedure
1. **Define the service boundary.** Separate activities to retain—network design, customer promises, control-tower governance—from activities candidates may operate. Decide whether a 4PL orchestration layer is genuinely needed.
2. **Baseline the current state.** Quantify lanes, orders, cube, peak volatility, cost, damage, fill rate, and exception workload. Reconcile the baseline before comparing bids.
3. **Screen for non-negotiables.** Eliminate providers that cannot meet geography, regulated handling, integration, security, or peak-capacity requirements.
4. **Issue a comparable scenario pack.** Give finalists the same volumes, service rules, transition assumptions, and exception cases. Require a response on governance and subcontractor use, not only unit prices.
5. **Score capability and economics.** Weight service design, technology, operations, financial health, ESG or compliance, transition risk, and total cost. Model accessorials, retained-team cost, implementation, and exit exposure.
6. **Validate the leading option.** Conduct site visits, reference calls, integration workshops, and a transition challenge session. Negotiate service levels, remedies, data ownership, and governance before award.
## Output Contract
A decision pack with the retained-versus-outsourced scope, current-state baseline, disqualified candidates and reasons, weighted scorecard, total-cost comparison, risk register, validation findings, preferred provider, and transition conditions. It must make clear whether the recommendation is a 3PL operating contract or a 4PL coordinating role and who remains accountable for customer outcomes.
## Evidence
Use shipment and cost data as observed evidence. Treat supplier claims, benchmark rates, and capacity promises as unverified until tested through references or sites. Expose assumptions about volume growth, fuel, peak demand, and internal retained costs. Keep commercial price, operating capability, and contractual liability as separate evidence streams.
## Checks
- Every finalist priced the identical scenario and service calendar.
- The cost model includes implementation, accessorial, governance, and exit costs.
- Non-negotiables are pass/fail, not diluted by a weighted average.
- Site validation tests the actual proposed operating location and subcontractors.
- A named internal owner remains accountable for provider governance.
## Failure Modes
- Awarding the lowest line-haul bid while ignoring accessorial and exception charges.
- Calling a provider a 4PL when it lacks neutral orchestration and data-control capability.
- Letting a persuasive sales demonstration substitute for warehouse validation.
- Comparing in-house cost without retained management, systems, and transition expense.
- Signing service levels that measure carrier activity instead of customer delivery outcomes.
