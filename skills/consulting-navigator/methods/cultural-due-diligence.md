# Cultural Due Diligence
## Purpose
Evaluate cultural compatibility and integration risk between buyer and target before committing to deal structure and PMI design. It tests the behavioral conditions required for the deal thesis, not simply whether the two organizations use similar values language.
## Use
- Assessing cultural compatibility and integration risk before signing or close
- Scoping PMI change load when operating models or values appear misaligned
- Explaining soft risks that financial and commercial diligence will not surface
## Avoid
- You need a standalone culture health score with no deal context—use culture-assessment
- The gating issues are compensation, headcount, or labor liabilities—use hr-due-diligence
- You are already post-close and need change sequencing—use post-merger-integration-roadmap or organizational-change-management
## Inputs
**Must-have:**
- Stated values, leadership behaviors, and decision norms for both organizations
- Evidence from interviews, surveys, or observation of how work actually gets done
- Prior integration or transformation history and known friction points
- Proposed deal thesis and intended operating model post-close

**Nice-to-have:** retention data for pivotal talent, communications archives, customer-facing behavior evidence, and deal-room access protocols.

**When data is thin:** prioritize the small set of cultural conditions essential to the thesis and rate findings as provisional; confidentiality limits are a finding, not a reason to overstate certainty.
## Procedure
1. **Translate the deal thesis into cultural hypotheses.** Identify the behaviors and integration moves needed to realize revenue, cost, innovation, or operating-model synergies. Output: thesis-to-culture test plan.
2. **Set comparative dimensions and sampling.** Define common lenses such as purpose, strategy, culture, brand, employee experience, decision rights, risk appetite, and leadership behavior; select comparable roles on both sides. Output: comparison protocol.
3. **Collect evidence separately and safely.** Interview, review artifacts, and observe where permitted without exposing deal-sensitive information or letting one party frame the other’s evidence. Output: coded evidence base.
4. **Compare patterns and stress points.** Identify alignment, productive differences, and friction likely to affect governance, talent retention, customer promises, or pace of integration. Output: cultural compatibility heat map.
5. **Price and plan the people risks.** Convert material gaps into deal protections, Day 1 choices, integration sequencing, leadership interventions, and residual risks. Output: cultural-risk report for deal governance.
## Output Contract
A buyer-target cultural fit report with deal-thesis hypotheses, comparable dimension findings, evidence confidence, alignment and collision points, affected synergies, pivotal-talent risks, and recommended transaction or PMI actions. It must distinguish a manageable difference from a risk that changes price, conditions, timing, or governance. It is not a single-company engagement survey.
## Evidence
Identify whose account each interview represents and separate stated values from repeated behavior. Preserve anonymity where promised and avoid attributing quotes to identifiable participants in a thin population. Treat management access restrictions, incomplete samples, and pre-close uncertainty as limits. Do not infer compatibility from shared slogans; look for comparable decisions, rewards, escalation, and conflict handling.
## Checks
- Every cultural dimension is connected to a specific element of the deal thesis or operating model.
- Buyer and target evidence is gathered with comparable questions and role coverage.
- Findings distinguish observable practices from consultant interpretation.
- Material risks have an owner, timing, and proposed mitigating action.
- The report states what could not be tested before close.
## Failure Modes
- Treating friendly executive chemistry as evidence of organization-wide compatibility.
- Comparing value statements while ignoring who makes decisions and how bad news travels.
- Flagging every difference as a red flag instead of judging its impact on the integration model.
- Deferring all cultural work to post-close after signing an inflexible synergy commitment.
