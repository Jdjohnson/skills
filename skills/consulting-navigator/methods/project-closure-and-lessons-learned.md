# Project Closure & Lessons Learned
## Purpose
Close the project administratively and capture evidence-based lessons so acceptance, handover, and organizational learning actually happen. The method converts a finished delivery into a signed transition and reusable improvements rather than letting the team simply disperse.
## Use
- Formally closing a project: acceptance, handover, admin close, and knowledge capture
- Converting delivery experience into actionable lessons for future projects or the PMO
- Confirming residual risks, open issues, and benefits ownership after go-live
## Avoid
- The project is still mid-flight and you need performance control—use earned-value-management
- You need ongoing ops improvement cycles rather than project end—use pdca-cycle or action-learning
- Benefits realization will run for quarters after close—pair with benefits-realization-management rather than treating closure as benefits proof
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Original objectives, scope, and success criteria; final deliverable acceptance evidence; issue, risk, and change history; cost and schedule outcomes versus baseline; stakeholder feedback and team retrospectives.

**Nice-to-have:** vendor performance records, asset registers, support runbooks, benefit-owner commitments, audit requirements, and knowledge-repository tags.

**When data is thin:** complete acceptance and handover first, capture lessons with confidence labels, and assign an owner to recover missing financial or performance evidence after closure.
## Procedure
1. **Check completion against the baseline.** Compare delivered products, acceptance criteria, cost, schedule, scope changes, and promised benefits with the approved plan. Identify what remains open rather than declaring success from deployment alone.
2. **Obtain formal acceptance and transfer.** Secure sponsor or customer sign-off, hand over operational documents, access, warranties, support contacts, and asset records. Confirm that an operations owner accepts each residual obligation.
3. **Close commercial and administrative work.** Reconcile invoices, release contracts and resources, archive controlled records, dispose of temporary access, and complete required compliance or procurement actions.
4. **Collect lessons from evidence.** Facilitate a retrospective around concrete events: what was expected, what occurred, why the difference arose, and what should change. Include sponsor, users, delivery team, suppliers, and receiving operations where relevant.
5. **Turn observations into reusable lessons.** Cluster lessons by practice, validate causal claims against project data, and write a recommendation with trigger, action, owner, and reusable artifact. Reject vague entries such as “communicate better.”
6. **Publish the closure pack.** Record residual risks, benefit ownership, lessons actions, repository location, and closure approval. Schedule follow-up only for post-project benefits, not to leave the project indefinitely open.
## Output Contract
A closure pack containing acceptance evidence, final performance against baseline, handover and residual-risk log, financial and contract close status, a structured lessons register, benefit-owner confirmation, archive location, and closure approval. It must make outstanding obligations visible and make each lesson usable by a future team.
## Evidence
Signed acceptance, invoices, change logs, and operational handover records are observed. Causal explanations from retrospectives are hypotheses until corroborated by events or data. Preserve dissenting views when participants disagree. A projected benefit after go-live belongs to a named business owner and should not be reported as realized project value.
## Checks
- Every deliverable has accepted, rejected, or transferred status.
- Residual risks and open issues have an operational owner and due date.
- Final cost and schedule compare to the latest approved baseline and explain approved changes.
- Lessons include a circumstance, mechanism, and action rather than a slogan.
- Contracts, system access, and records have explicit closure evidence.
## Failure Modes
- Calling a system live while support lacks runbooks, credentials, or a known-issues list.
- Recording lessons after the team has left and relying on polished memory instead of project facts.
- Closing vendor purchase orders before warranty deliverables are accepted.
- Counting future benefits as if the project team had already realized them.
- Filing lessons in a repository with no owner, tags, or link to future planning.
