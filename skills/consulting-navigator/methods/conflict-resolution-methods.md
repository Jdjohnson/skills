# Conflict Resolution Methods
## Purpose
Surface interests beneath positions and structure a durable, jointly owned resolution plan for a live interpersonal or inter-group dispute. It provides a deliberate route from accusation and stalemate to commitments that parties can monitor.
## Use
- Interpersonal or inter-group disputes are blocking decisions, delivery, or trust.
- Parties are locked in positions and need structured interest-based problem solving.
- You must produce a workable resolution agreement with clear commitments and follow-up.
## Avoid
- The issue is structural role ambiguity or missing decision rights—use organizational-design or raci-matrix first.
- You need culture-wide diagnosis rather than a live dispute resolution—use culture-assessment.
- Legal, safety, or misconduct issues require formal HR investigation pathways before facilitated resolution.
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Parties to the conflict and their formal/informal power; each side's stated positions and underlying interests; shared facts and contested claims; constraints (policy, budget, timeline, authority); agreement on facilitation ground rules and desired outcomes.

**Nice-to-have:** prior agreements, decision logs, neutral facilitator availability, and privately gathered concerns.

**When power is unequal:** establish safeguards, separate caucuses, and a legitimate escalation route; voluntary agreement is not credible if one party cannot speak safely.
## Procedure
1. Screen the dispute for harassment, safety, legal, or non-negotiable policy issues and route those through the appropriate formal process.
2. Agree the forum, facilitator role, confidentiality boundaries, speaking rules, and decision authority. Interview parties separately where direct conversation would begin defensively.
3. Separate observable events from interpretations. Create a joint fact list and mark facts still disputed rather than litigating motives.
4. Ask each party to state interests, fears, constraints, and success conditions behind its proposed position. Reflect these back until both sides recognize the summary.
5. Generate options that meet multiple interests, then test them against policy, practicality, fairness, and impact on the wider team.
6. Write a resolution with actions, owners, dates, communication boundaries, and an escalation or review point. Obtain explicit acceptance and revisit adherence.
## Output Contract
Produce a confidential resolution record containing the issue scope, agreed facts, material interests, options considered, chosen commitments, owners, dates, review mechanism, and unresolved items. The agreement must specify changed behavior or decisions, not merely record that participants had a productive conversation.
## Evidence
Distinguish firsthand events, documents, and corroborated facts from perception and inference. Do not promise confidentiality where policy requires disclosure. A facilitator may summarize interests without deciding whose story is morally correct. Record any allegation that requires investigation separately from the negotiated work plan.
## Checks
- Parties consent to the process and understand its limits.
- Both accounts appear in the fact and interest record without false equivalence.
- Commitments are observable, owned, and dated.
- The solution fits the actual decision rights and policy constraints.
- A follow-up review tests behavior, not only sentiment immediately after the meeting.
## Failure Modes
- Forcing a joint meeting before a party is safe enough to speak candidly.
- Bargaining over demands without discovering the workload, recognition, or risk underneath them.
- Treating an allegation of misconduct as a communication misunderstanding.
- Writing “improve collaboration” instead of naming who will decide what by when.
- Declaring closure when the same dependency still gives one side a reason to block.
