# Innovation Maturity Assessment
## Purpose
Diagnose organizational innovation capability across process, culture, talent, and governance dimensions and map the gap to a target maturity level. The assessment identifies the system constraints that prevent repeatable innovation, rather than judging the current list of ideas.
## Use
- Diagnosing how mature innovation processes, culture, and governance are today.
- Building a capability roadmap before scaling an innovation operating system.
- Benchmarking innovation readiness across units or against peers.
## Avoid
- You need to run an active idea pipeline, not score capability—use innovation-funnel or stage-gate-process.
- The question is specifically digital technology maturity—use digital-maturity-assessment.
- You already know gaps and need change execution—use organizational-change-management or agile-transformation-framework.
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Scope of units or functions to assess; Maturity dimensions (e.g., strategy, process, talent, funding, culture, metrics); Evidence from interviews, artifacts, and performance data; Optional peer or target maturity benchmarks.

**Useful additions:** product portfolio history, failure-learning records, budget data, decision rights, and employee survey results.
## Procedure
1. Select the organizational scope and define the maturity rubric for each dimension. Describe observable levels rather than using labels such as "advanced" without behavioral criteria.
2. Gather evidence across levels: interviews with sponsors and practitioners, samples of decisions and portfolios, funding records, talent practices, and outcome measures. Seek contrast between stated process and actual behavior.
3. Score each dimension against the rubric, retaining evidence citations in a working file and noting variation by business unit. Do not average away a critical weak link.
4. Calibrate scores with a cross-functional review. Challenge inflated self-ratings by asking for artifacts and repeatable examples; resolve disagreements by revisiting the rubric.
5. Define the target level required by strategy, not by a generic benchmark. Identify the capability gaps that most constrain the intended innovation ambition.
6. Sequence a roadmap of capability interventions, dependencies, owners, investment, and leading measures. Start with the smallest changes that enable the next desired behavior.
## Output Contract
Deliver an innovation maturity profile showing rubric definitions, dimension scores, evidence confidence, variation by unit, target level, priority gaps, and a capability roadmap. It must distinguish a low score caused by weak governance from one caused by scarce technical talent. The result guides operating-system investment; it does not operate a live idea flow or evaluate digital architecture alone.
## Evidence
Use artifacts and performance data to corroborate interview claims. Mark benchmark comparisons as contextual, because industry peers may have a different innovation ambition. Maintain score rationales so a later assessor can challenge them. If evidence is thin for a dimension, report a range or low confidence rather than assigning a precise maturity level; perceived enthusiasm is not proof of capability.
## Checks
- Every level in the rubric describes observable practices or outcomes.
- Scores are traceable to multiple evidence types where material.
- Unit variation is visible when it changes the recommended intervention.
- Target maturity is tied to strategic needs and time horizon.
- Roadmap actions alter a scored capability and include a leading measure.
## Failure Modes
- Copying a peer benchmark level without asking whether the organization needs that capability.
- Scoring culture from leadership slogans while teams lack time or safety to run experiments.
- Averaging strong product development with weak portfolio governance into a reassuring middle score.
- Funding training first when decision rights and incentives prevent the desired behavior.
