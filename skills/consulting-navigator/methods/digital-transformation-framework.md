# Digital Transformation Framework
## Purpose
Design an integrated digital transformation target state and multi-horizon roadmap that rewires business model, journeys, technology, and operating model together. It turns a maturity baseline and strategic ambitions into linked value bets and implementation choices.
## Use
- Integrating digital technology into business model, customer journeys, operations, and operating model as one program
- Building a multi-horizon DX roadmap from maturity baseline to prioritized value bets
- Aligning tech, process, data, and change workstreams under a single transformation design
## Avoid
- You only need a maturity score before designing the target state—use digital-maturity-assessment first
- The scope is purely agile delivery-system redesign—use agile-transformation-framework
- The question is IT-business alignment of the existing portfolio without enterprise reinvention—use it-strategy-alignment
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Business strategy and priority value pools or customer journeys; Current digital maturity and major technology constraints; Operating model pain points (process, data, talent, governance); Investment envelope and risk constraints for multi-year change.

**Nice-to-have:** customer research, platform roadmaps, workforce capacity, and benefits from prior pilots.

**When data is thin:** select a bounded value domain and formulate a testable first horizon instead of declaring an enterprise end state.
## Procedure
1. Define the transformation thesis: named value pools, customers or operations affected, economic ambition, and non-negotiable risk constraints. Test the thesis against strategy and current maturity.
2. Design future customer journeys and operating flows, specifying where data, automation, platforms, and human decisions change the experience or unit economics.
3. Translate the future state into enabling capabilities: products, data domains, architecture, talent, governance, funding, and change mechanisms. Expose dependencies across workstreams.
4. Prioritize a portfolio of value bets by benefits, feasibility, sequencing, and learning value. Create horizons for foundation work, scalable releases, and longer-term business-model shifts.
5. Establish transformation governance, outcome measures, investment checkpoints, and a roadmap that synchronizes technical releases with process and adoption change.
## Output Contract
A target-state design including the transformation thesis, future journeys and operating model, capability architecture, value-bet portfolio, dependency map, three-horizon roadmap, governance, and benefits metrics. It must show how each initiative contributes to a business result and which foundational decisions permit later releases.
## Evidence
Current economics, journey metrics, platform constraints, and adoption data are observed. Benefits forecasts and causal links between a digital feature and value are inferred. Customer behavior changes, funding continuity, and capacity assumptions need explicit owners and test dates. Unknown regulatory impacts, legacy constraints, or adoption barriers must carry a discovery task before irreversible platform investment.
## Checks
- The roadmap contains business outcomes, not a list of technology purchases.
- Every value bet has an accountable owner, measurable leading signal, and stated dependency.
- Customer, operations, data, and people changes are designed together where the journey crosses them.
- Foundation investments have a traceable path to later value creation.
- Governance can stop, re-sequence, or scale a bet using agreed evidence.
## Failure Modes
- Launching a cloud migration labeled transformation with no redesigned value proposition or workflow.
- Funding attractive pilots independently, then discovering their data models cannot interoperate.
- Publishing a three-year roadmap that assumes adoption without changing incentives, roles, or training.
