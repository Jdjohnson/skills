# Crisis Management Framework
## Purpose
Stand up command, decision, and communication structures so the organization can respond coherently during acute high-severity crises. It prepares leadership to act under time pressure while protecting people, legitimacy, and critical operations.
## Use
- Building command, decision, and communication protocols for high-severity incidents
- Standing up crisis teams, escalation triggers, and stakeholder notification paths
- Preparing leadership for acute events that exceed normal operations and continuity runbooks
## Avoid
- You need steady-state process continuity and recovery sequencing—use business-continuity-planning
- The gap is IT infrastructure restore procedures after outage—use disaster-recovery-planning
- You still need enterprise risk governance and appetite, not incident command—use coso-erm-framework or iso-31000
## Inputs
**Must-have:**
- Crisis severity levels and activation triggers
- Named crisis roles, deputies, and decision rights
- Stakeholder map for employees, customers, regulators, media, and partners
- Communication templates and legal/brand constraints for public statements

**Nice-to-have:** incident chronology templates, emergency contact verification, media monitoring arrangements, and exercise results.

**When data is thin:** build a minimum command roster and escalation tree first; do not rely on untested contact lists during an event.
## Procedure
1. **Define activation thresholds.** Distinguish an operational incident from a crisis using impact, uncertainty, stakeholder exposure, and executive decision criteria. Output: severity model and activation authority.
2. **Establish the command system.** Name incident lead, executive sponsor, functional leads, deputies, decision rights, meeting rhythm, and log keeper. Output: crisis-team charter.
3. **Create the first-hours playbook.** Sequence life safety, fact collection, legal hold, containment, stakeholder notification, and decision escalation. Output: 0–24-hour action checklist.
4. **Design communication discipline.** Match audiences to approved messages, spokespersons, channels, update cadence, and rumor correction rules. Output: stakeholder communication matrix.
5. **Exercise and improve.** Run a realistic scenario, time the handoffs, capture decisions, and revise authority gaps and scripts after the debrief. Output: tested playbook and improvement backlog.
## Output Contract
A crisis capability package containing severity triggers, an activation protocol, command structure, authority matrix, first-hours checklist, decision log format, stakeholder messages, media rules, exercise findings, and improvement actions. It must enable a named leader to convene the right people and make the first consequential decisions before facts are complete.
## Evidence
Treat incident logs, exercises, legal requirements, and verified contact details as evidence. Label initial event facts by confidence and timestamp; never convert an unverified report into a public statement. Keep legal, regulatory, and safety constraints explicit. Pre-approved messages are starting points and require confirmation against the actual event.
## Checks
- Activation can occur outside office hours with identified deputies.
- Command roles separate decision authority, operations coordination, and communications.
- The first-hours sequence protects people before reputation or commercial concerns.
- Each critical audience has an owner, channel, and escalation route.
- Exercises test delayed information, contradictory reports, and unavailable leaders.
## Failure Modes
- Treating a binder of continuity procedures as a crisis command model.
- Letting multiple executives issue uncoordinated messages.
- Waiting for perfect facts and missing a required notification window.
- Rehearsing only a tabletop narrative while never testing calls, deputies, or access.
