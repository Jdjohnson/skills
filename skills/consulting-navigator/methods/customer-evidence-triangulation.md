# Customer Evidence Triangulation
## Purpose
Reconcile already-collected interview, survey, and behavioral evidence into one defensible, confidence-weighted statement of what customers value and actually do. This module is an original synthesis drawing on established concepts: combining independent methods so their agreement or disagreement is itself evidence; structuring raw customer statements into organized needs; and treating say–do gaps between intention and behavior as findings to explain, not noise.
## Use
- Multiple customer evidence streams disagree or resist reconciliation.
- A significant bet rides on what customers actually value versus what they claim.
- Anecdotes are driving large decisions without quantitative or behavioral confirmation.
- Insights need confidence levels before feature prioritization or commitment.
## Avoid
- Only one evidence source exists — nothing to triangulate; gather a second stream first.
- The question is purely operational with no customer judgment involved.
- Evidence has not yet been collected — this method reconciles streams rather than replacing fieldwork.
## Inputs
**Must-have:** qualitative evidence (interview, support, or sales-call notes); quantitative attitude evidence (survey or ratings); behavioral evidence (usage, transaction, or churn data). All three streams must address the same customer question.
**Nice-to-have:** consistent segment labels, collection dates, known sample biases.
If one stream is weak, cap confidence and list it as a research gap.
## Procedure
1. **Lock the decision question.** State the customer claim the business needs to trust (for example, "Do mid-market buyers pay for automated reconciliation?"). Output: scoped question and success criteria.
2. **Structure qualitative voice.** Organize raw statements into a need hierarchy without yet declaring truth. Output: structured need map.
3. **Align survey and behavior to the same themes.** Map attitude metrics and behavioral KPIs onto the same structure so comparisons are like-for-like. Output: themes × streams crosswalk.
4. **Score convergence, divergence, and say–do gaps.** For each theme, mark where streams agree, conflict, or diverge between stated preference and action; propose the best explanation for each gap. Output: reconciliation matrix.
5. **Write confidence-weighted insights.** Convert the matrix into claims, each with confidence (high/medium/low), supporting streams, residual unknowns, and implication for the bet. Output: decision-ready insight brief.
## Output Contract
A confidence-weighted insight brief containing: (1) the decision question, (2) the structured claim set from qualitative evidence, (3) the crosswalk to survey and behavioral measures, (4) convergence / divergence / say–do findings with explanations, (5) final claims with confidence levels and what would raise them, and (6) explicit unknowns. It must support a commit / defer / research-more decision, not a vague "customers want more value" summary.
## Evidence
Facts: system metrics, timestamps, recorded interview content. Inferences: themed needs and gap explanations. Assumptions: samples represent the target segment; survey items measure the same construct as behavior. Unknowns: missing segments, outdated streams, confounded metrics. When evidence is thin, lower confidence. Convergence across independent methods raises confidence; one vivid interview never overrides consistent behavior.
## Checks
- All three stream types appear in the crosswalk, or absences are justified.
- Qualitative content is structured into themes, not quote piles.
- Say–do gaps are explained or flagged, not smoothed away.
- Every final claim has a confidence tag and named supporting streams.
- Segment mismatches (interviewed vs. in the funnel) are called out.
## Failure Modes
- Cherry-picking the stream that supports the preferred roadmap.
- Treating more interviews alone as triangulation (no independent methods).
- Averaging survey scores with usage rates into meaningless composites.
- Ignoring intention–behavior gaps as "noise."
- Overstating confidence when streams never measured the same construct.
