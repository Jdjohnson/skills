# Stage-Gate Process
## Purpose
Divide product initiatives into stages separated by evidence-based go/kill gates that govern portfolio risk and investment.
## Use
- Governing product or innovation portfolios with formal stage deliverables and go/kill decisions.
- Reducing late-stage failure by forcing evidence at discovery, business case, and development gates.
- Aligning cross-functional owners on decision rights before major spend.
## Avoid
- Uncertainty is extreme and you need rapid assumption testing—use lean-startup.
- Pure software product teams need continuous flow—use agile-product-management or scrum-framework.
- You only need a visual feature timeline—use product-roadmap-development.
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Defined stages, gate criteria, and decision rights; project business case, risks, and evidence packages per gate; portfolio capacity and strategic fit criteria; cross-functional gate committee or equivalent governance.

**Nice-to-have:** Standard deliverable templates, portfolio economics, post-launch learning, and a gate calendar aligned with funding cycles.

**When data is thin:** Set an evidence-building stage with a small capped investment. Do not use a polished slide deck to substitute for missing customer, technical, or financial proof.
## Procedure
1. Define stages from idea through launch or scale, with a specific learning or work objective for each. Assign a single accountable project leader and a gate owner with authority to allocate or stop funds.
2. For every gate, publish must-meet criteria, should-meet criteria, required evidence, scoring rules, funding request, and allowable decisions: go, kill, hold, recycle, or conditional go.
3. Teams complete stage work and assemble a concise evidence package: customer problem, technical feasibility, financial case, risk register, resource request, and prior-gate commitments. Pre-read reviewers before the meeting.
4. The gate committee tests evidence against criteria, challenges unanswered risks, considers portfolio fit and capacity, and records the decision with conditions. A conditional go names the evidence due, owner, deadline, and spending cap.
5. Review the portfolio across projects to surface blocked capacity, duplicate bets, and delayed kills. Refresh criteria when learning shows a gate is approving avoidable late-stage failures.
## Output Contract
Deliver a governed product-investment path showing stages, entry and exit criteria, gate authorities, evidence packages, decision dates, funding tranches, decision records, conditions, and portfolio capacity implications. Each decision must be auditable: it states what evidence was reviewed and why funds were released, paused, redirected, or stopped. This is more than a funnel graphic or a chronological delivery plan; it controls investment under uncertainty.
## Evidence
Evidence must be dated and tied to the decision criterion. Mark market-size estimates, technical assumptions, and cost forecasts separately from validated findings. Do not let sunk spend satisfy a gate. Keep decision minutes and dissenting risk views, especially where a senior sponsor sponsors the project. Criteria may vary by project class, but exceptions require a recorded rationale and an expiry date.
## Checks
- Every stage has a distinct purpose and a named exit decision.
- Gate criteria are known before work begins in the preceding stage.
- Go decisions include capacity and funding, not enthusiasm alone.
- Conditional-go commitments have measurable due dates and owners.
- Killed and held projects remain visible in portfolio reporting.
## Failure Modes
- Passing a gate because the team completed activities rather than proving the criterion.
- Releasing full development funding on an untested problem statement.
- Convening the same sponsors as both project advocates and unchallenged gatekeepers.
- Turning a hold decision into a silent backlog item with no revisit date.
