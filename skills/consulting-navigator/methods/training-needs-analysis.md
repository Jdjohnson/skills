# Training Needs Analysis
## Purpose
Decompose change-driven skill gaps by role and prioritize the training and enablement needed for competent performance. It distinguishes a teachable capability shortfall from process, motivation, access, or staffing problems.
## Use
- Decomposing role-level skill gaps created by a process, system, or operating-model change.
- Scoping training, coaching, and job-aid investments before go-live.
- Prioritizing learning interventions for high-risk cohorts and moments of truth.
## Avoid
- The gap is motivation, incentives, or role modeling rather than capability—use mckinsey-influence-model or resistance-management-plan.
- You need enterprise talent architecture beyond this change—use talent-management-strategy or competency-mapping as the primary method.
- Performance problems are process design defects training cannot fix—use process-mapping or root-cause-analysis first.
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Future-state roles, tasks, and performance standards under the change; current competency or proficiency evidence by role/cohort; error, rework, or quality data that signal skill-related risk; delivery constraints: time-to-go-live, trainers, systems, and release windows; existing curricula, SOPs, and job aids that can be reused or retired.

**Nice-to-have:** manager observations, employee self-assessments, language needs, and system practice environments.

**When data is thin:** sample the highest-risk roles through task observation and mark untested cohorts for early assessment rather than assigning the same course to everyone.
## Procedure
1. **Translate the future state into critical tasks.** For each role, describe trigger, action, tool, decision, standard, and consequence of error. Focus on moments that affect customers, controls, safety, or cutover readiness.
2. **Assess current proficiency.** Combine observation, work samples, manager calibration, assessments, and performance data. Distinguish a knowledge gap from a skill gap, missing access, unclear procedure, or capacity constraint.
3. **Score training need.** Rate each role-task gap by criticality, population size, current proficiency, time to proficiency, and go-live timing. Record the evidence behind each score.
4. **Design the intervention mix.** Match gaps to practice, simulation, coaching, job aids, certification, or process repair. Sequence prerequisites and use the actual system where transfer to work matters.
5. **Plan measurement and ownership.** Set readiness thresholds, completion evidence, post-launch performance checks, retraining triggers, and accountable managers.
## Output Contract
The roadmap lists role-task requirements, evidence of current capability, categorized gaps, priority scores, intervention design, cohorts, delivery schedule, resource demand, and success measures. It explicitly names gaps training will not solve and routes those to the relevant owner. It is a decision tool for readiness investment, not a catalogue of available courses.
## Evidence
Treat observed task performance, assessment results, and error rates as evidence; retain sample sizes and date ranges. Manager ratings are useful but should be calibrated across teams. State assumptions about learner availability, trainer capacity, and proficiency thresholds. If a failure could arise from a broken workflow or denied system access, tag it as a competing hypothesis until tested. Do not infer mastery from attendance alone.
## Checks
- Every intervention maps to a named role-task gap and performance standard.
- High-risk tasks have a practical demonstration or work-sample check.
- Priority reflects consequence and readiness, not simply learner count.
- Delivery sequencing fits release dates and operational coverage.
- Managers own reinforcement after the course, with a measure at work.
## Failure Modes
- Sending all employees the same e-learning regardless of task exposure.
- Treating a permissions problem as a skill deficit.
- Measuring completion while customer errors continue rising.
- Training a future process before its screens and procedures are stable.
- Omitting supervisors, who must coach and verify new behavior.
