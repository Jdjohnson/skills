# Client Satisfaction Surveying
## Purpose
Systematically collect and convert client feedback on engagement quality into prioritized recovery and delivery improvements. It asks the buyers and users of a professional-service engagement about the experience while action can still improve the relationship.
## Use
- You need systematic, comparable feedback on consulting delivery quality during or after an engagement
- Relationship health and value-for-money signals must inform recovery actions before close
- The firm wants a repeatable quality review across projects, not ad-hoc sponsor chats
## Avoid
- You are measuring end-customer loyalty for a product business—use net-promoter-system or voice-of-the-customer
- No engagement has started or the client relationship is purely transactional with no service dimension
- You need deep causal diagnosis of delivery failure—use post-implementation-review after the survey flags issues
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Named client stakeholders who experienced the work; defined service dimensions (value, communication, team, outcomes, commercial fairness); survey instrument and timing plan (mid-engagement and/or close); baseline or prior scores if available for comparison.

**Nice-to-have:** engagement phase plan, client contact preferences, prior recovery commitments, and an independent interviewer for sensitive accounts.

**When data is thin:** run short structured conversations with more than one client role and report individual themes separately instead of manufacturing a representative score.
## Procedure
1. **Set the review moment and audience.** Choose a mid-engagement checkpoint, close-out review, or both. Identify sponsor, day-to-day counterpart, and affected users; explain confidentiality and who will see themes.
2. **Tailor the instrument without losing comparability.** Use a stable core covering value, outcomes, communication, team, and commercial fairness, plus two or three engagement-specific questions. Use clear scales with anchored ends and one open question for evidence.
3. **Collect independently and protect candor.** Invite each named stakeholder through a neutral channel, monitor response mix, and follow up without asking the delivery lead to solicit praise. Keep raw comments separate from attributed action records where promised.
4. **Interpret the pattern.** Calculate dimension scores, compare roles and prior checkpoints, code open comments, and distinguish an isolated preference from a material relationship risk. A recommend-intent score may be included, but cannot replace service-dimension diagnosis.
5. **Close the loop.** Hold a recovery conversation with the accountable partner, agree specific corrections, communicate what will change to the client, and recheck at a defined date.
## Output Contract
Provide a delivery-improvement plan containing respondent coverage, timing, dimension scores, themes and verbatim-safe evidence, priority issues, accountable owner, recovery action, client communication, and follow-up date. The plan must show how feedback changed the engagement; a satisfaction number with no response is incomplete.
## Evidence
Responses, completed interviews, and scored items are observed evidence. Theme coding and comparisons to a prior project are inferred judgments. State response rate, role coverage, timing, and whether anonymity limits attribution. Do not report a tiny sample as a firm-wide benchmark. Escalate comments alleging misconduct, confidentiality issues, or commercial misrepresentation through the appropriate channel rather than treating them as routine survey feedback.
## Checks
- Client sponsor, operational counterpart, and material user groups are represented or their absence is disclosed.
- Questions separate outcome value from how the team delivered the work.
- Scores include response counts and distribution, not only an average.
- Each priority issue has a named recovery owner and client-visible next step.
- Follow-up confirms whether the corrective action improved the experience.
## Failure Modes
- Waiting for final invoicing to ask for feedback that could have saved the engagement at midpoint.
- Sending one generic loyalty question and missing a failing workstream or communication problem.
- Letting the project partner collect responses from a dissatisfied sponsor.
- Publishing anonymous comments so specifically that the author becomes identifiable.
