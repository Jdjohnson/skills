# Carbon Footprint Assessment
## Purpose
Quantify organization greenhouse gas emissions by scope and source into a defensible carbon inventory baseline. The inventory makes the emissions boundary, activity data, and conversion factors visible so reduction decisions start from a comparable measure.
## Use
- Establishing a GHG baseline for targets, disclosure, or supplier engagement
- Locating the largest emission sources before abatement investment
- Creating comparable inventory data for ESG strategy or TCFD climate metrics
## Avoid
- You need a multi-year ESG positioning and initiative roadmap rather than an inventory—use esg-strategy
- The ask is climate governance and financial-risk disclosure structure—use tcfd-reporting
- You only need a qualitative maturity score of sustainability capabilities—use sustainability-maturity-assessment
## Inputs
**Must-have:**

- Organizational and operational boundary definition (equity share, control, or other)
- Activity data for energy, fuel, travel, logistics, and material use
- Emission factors and calculation methodology (e.g., GHG Protocol scopes)
- Supplier and value-chain data sufficient for material Scope 3 categories

**Nice-to-have:** meter-level energy data, renewable-energy instruments, freight distances, supplier product footprints, and prior inventory workpapers.

**When data is thin:** prioritize material sources, document proxies by category, and report data-quality limitations alongside the total.
## Procedure
1. **Set inventory boundaries.** Choose the reporting entity, consolidation approach, facilities, period, gases, and operational boundary. Record acquisitions, divestitures, and leased assets so the baseline can later be reproduced. Output: boundary statement.
2. **Build the emissions-source register.** List combustion, purchased energy, refrigerants, transport, waste, travel, purchased goods, and other material value-chain sources. Classify each source into the relevant scope and identify its data owner. Output: scoped source map.
3. **Collect and normalize activity data.** Gather fuel units, kWh, miles, tonnes, spend, or supplier quantities; reconcile them to utility bills, ledgers, and operational totals. Convert units only once and preserve the original value. Output: auditable activity-data file.
4. **Apply factors and calculate.** Multiply activity data by the appropriate factor and global-warming potential, keeping factor version, geography, and calculation method with each line. Aggregate to scope, category, site, and source. Output: emissions workpapers.
5. **Review materiality and baseline use.** Check completeness, investigate unusually large changes, identify the largest sources, and define a refresh cadence. Output: inventory table, data-quality assessment, and reduction opportunity list.
## Output Contract
The assessment provides a boundary statement, scope- and source-level emissions in tonnes CO2e, activity-data lineage, factor register, calculation workpapers, completeness and data-quality assessment, and a ranked hotspot view. It clearly distinguishes measured emissions from estimates and supports a baseline year suitable for targets or supplier engagement.
## Evidence
Invoices, meter readings, fuel purchase records, mileage logs, and supplier-specific data are observed evidence. Spend-based estimates, distance proxies, and missing refrigerant charges are assumptions. Apply one documented factor source per calculation line and retain its period and geography. Do not add scopes together across inconsistent boundaries, claim Scope 3 completeness without a category screen, or call avoided emissions part of the inventory total.
## Checks
- Boundary choice is stated and applied consistently to all entities.
- Every material source maps to a scope and a data owner.
- Activity units, factors, and tonnes CO2e calculations are traceable.
- Scope 2 treatment and renewable instruments are disclosed separately.
- Year-over-year changes are explained by activity, boundary, or factor changes.
## Failure Modes
- Combining utility bills from acquired sites with a pre-acquisition baseline.
- Using a national grid factor for electricity purchased in another geography.
- Counting supplier-reported emissions and spend-based estimates for the same purchase.
- Reporting only Scopes 1 and 2 while calling the result a full footprint.
- Replacing missing data with zero emissions rather than an explicit estimate.
