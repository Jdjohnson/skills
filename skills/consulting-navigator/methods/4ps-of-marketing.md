# 4Ps of Marketing
## Purpose
Design a coherent marketing mix by aligning product, price, place, and promotion into one executable offer system. The work exposes conflicts among commercial decisions that are often made in separate functions.
## Use
- Designing or redesigning a coherent product-market offer.
- Aligning product, pricing, channel, and promotion before a launch or relaunch.
- Diagnosing which mix element is underpowered relative to competitors.
## Avoid
- The offer is primarily a service experience where people, process, and physical evidence dominate—use 7ps-of-service-marketing.
- You still lack a clear segment and positioning—use stp-framework first.
- You need channel-only or pricing-only depth rather than a full mix design—use competitive-pricing-analysis or omnichannel-strategy.
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Defined target segment(s) and value proposition draft; current product features, variants, and packaging; pricing structure, discounts, and cost floors; channel map (direct, retail, digital, partners); promotion history and available media/budget constraints.

**Nice-to-have:** Customer research, competitor shelf or digital audits, channel margin data, and message testing.

**When data is thin:** Build an explicit first-release hypothesis for each lever and identify the smallest market test that can disconfirm it.
## Procedure
1. **Anchor the target customer and promise.** Write the segment, job, desired value, and reason to believe. Reject mix choices that serve a different customer.
2. **Specify Product.** Choose the offer configuration, quality level, assortment, packaging, brand cues, and warranties. Identify what is deliberately excluded.
3. **Design Price.** Set list price, architecture, discount guardrails, payment terms, and channel margin against willingness to pay, competitor reference points, and cost floor.
4. **Design Place.** Select where and how customers buy, receive, and replenish the offer. Test channel coverage, inventory availability, partner incentives, and channel conflict.
5. **Design Promotion.** Match message, proof, media, timing, budget, and call to action to the segment and buying stage.
6. **Run the coherence test.** Ask whether the product claim, price signal, channel experience, and promotion all reinforce the same position. Fix contradictions before launch.
## Output Contract
A marketing-mix blueprint showing the target segment and value proposition, decisions for each of the four levers, the rationale and constraints for those decisions, dependencies between levers, owner, and launch experiments. It should be specific enough to brief product, sales, channel, and communications teams without becoming a complete go-to-market project plan.
## Evidence
Use customer demand, sales, cost, and channel data as observed evidence. Mark competitor interpretation and expected response as inference. Label willingness-to-pay, media response, and partner behavior as assumptions until tested. Do not present an attractive campaign as proof that the product or distribution can deliver the promise.
## Checks
- One segment and one value proposition anchor all four choices.
- The price is consistent with product quality and channel margin requirements.
- The chosen channel can make the offer available where promotion creates demand.
- Promotion includes a concrete proof point, not only a slogan.
- At least one conflict between levers has been resolved explicitly.
## Failure Modes
- Advertising a premium product while discounting it continuously.
- Launching a broad assortment through a channel that cannot stock or explain it.
- Treating price as a finance decision after the offer is already designed.
- Buying awareness media without a distribution path to conversion.
- Copying a rival’s mix although the target segment and economics differ.
