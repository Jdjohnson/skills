# Profit Pools
## Purpose
Map where total industry profits concentrate across value-chain steps and segments to guide where to compete. The analysis distinguishes large revenue flows from the places where the industry earns its economic surplus.
## Use
- Mapping total industry profit by activity, segment, or value-chain step to find where money pools
- Redirecting growth, partnership, or M&A focus toward high-profit pools
- Explaining why high-revenue positions still produce weak returns
## Avoid
- You only need activity cost and differentiation inside one firm—use value-chain-analysis
- You need industry structural power without profit distribution—use five-forces
- You need project-level investment NPV, not industry profit geography—use discounted-cash-flow-valuation
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Defined industry value chain or segment map; estimates of revenue and margin (or profit) by step, channel, or customer segment; boundary assumptions for geography and product scope; at least directional evidence on who captures surplus at each step.

**Nice-to-have:** public-company filings, channel markups, capacity and utilization data, transaction multiples, interview estimates, and growth forecasts.

**When data is thin:** use ranges and indexed profit estimates, document the coverage gap, and test whether the strategic conclusion changes at the low and high ends of each uncertain pool.
## Procedure
1. **Draw the economic boundary.** Specify product category, geography, period, and value-chain stages. Include adjacent services or channels only when they compete for the same profit opportunity.
2. **Define comparable profit measures.** Choose operating profit, EBITDA, gross profit, or an explicit proxy. Normalize currency, fiscal period, ownership model, and transfer-pricing distortions before comparing stages.
3. **Estimate revenue and profit by pool.** Aggregate the revenue base and apply margin estimates for each stage or segment. Preserve a separate “unallocated/unknown” bucket rather than forcing every dollar into a precise node.
4. **Map pool depth and shape.** Display revenue width and profit depth across the chain. Identify pools that are large but thin, small but highly profitable, rapidly shifting, or concentrated in a few owners.
5. **Explain capture mechanisms.** Investigate why the pool exists: switching costs, scarcity, regulation, control of customer access, asset intensity, proprietary data, or risk bearing. Distinguish temporary windfalls from durable economics.
6. **Translate geography into choices.** Evaluate enter, partner, acquire, reposition, or defend options against the firm’s capabilities and cost of access. A lucrative pool is not automatically accessible or attractive to this firm.
## Output Contract
An industry profit map with explicit scope, value-chain definitions, revenue and profit estimates by pool, margin assumptions, confidence ranges, explanations of profit capture, and strategic implications. It must reveal the distribution of total industry profit rather than calculate the company’s internal cost stack.
## Evidence
Reported segment profit and audited filings are observed, while channel economics derived from interviews are inferred. State whether margins include shared corporate cost, working capital, or taxes. Never add percentages from overlapping segments; reconcile pool totals to the defined industry revenue base. Label an estimate as directional when private-company data are unavailable.
## Checks
- Pool boundaries are mutually understandable and avoid double-counting revenue.
- All margin measures use the same profit definition or disclose the adjustment.
- Total revenue and profit reconcile to a stated market-size range.
- At least one explanation supports each material profit concentration.
- Recommended moves account for the firm’s right to win, not margin alone.
## Failure Modes
- Mapping sales volume and labeling the largest bar a profit pool.
- Comparing a manufacturer’s gross margin with a distributor’s EBITDA margin without adjustment.
- Ignoring loss-making stages that subsidize access to a profitable downstream pool.
- Treating a momentary supply shortage as a permanent capture mechanism.
- Recommending acquisition of a pool without modeling the price paid to enter it.
