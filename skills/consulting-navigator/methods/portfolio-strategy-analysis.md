# Portfolio Strategy Analysis
## Purpose
Set invest-hold-harvest-divest stances and capital priorities across a multi-business corporate portfolio. It combines unit economics and market position with parenting advantage and ownership choices, turning matrix signals into a corporate rebalancing decision.
## Use
- Rebalancing capital and management attention across a multi-business set
- Deciding invest, hold, harvest, or divest stances by business unit
- Testing whether the corporate parent adds more value than it costs
## Avoid
- You have a single product line, not a multi-business portfolio—use product-roadmap-development or ansoff-matrix
- You only need a 2x2 growth-share snapshot without capital decisions—use bcg-matrix
- The issue is one underperforming unit's turnaround, not portfolio shape—use turnaround-strategy
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Business unit list with revenue, growth, margin, and capital employed; competitive position and market attractiveness by unit; capital allocation history and planned investment needs; parenting links (shared customers, platforms, brands, capabilities); corporate risk, synergy, and ownership constraints.

**Nice-to-have:** buyer appetite, standalone cost estimates, tax implications, management bench strength, and transaction timing windows.

**When data is thin:** use ranges for unit value and parent contribution, then separate the immediate allocation decision from a diligence-dependent divestiture decision.
## Procedure
1. **Set the portfolio perimeter.** Define what counts as a business unit, shared service, venture, or non-core asset, and reconcile its financial history. Output: portfolio boundary and fact base.
2. **Assess standalone position.** Compare each unit’s market attractiveness, competitive position, growth, cash generation, capital intensity, and risk. Output: unit position profiles.
3. **Test parenting advantage.** Identify where corporate ownership improves access, capabilities, capital, governance, or risk capacity—and where overhead, cross-subsidy, or constraint destroys value. Output: parent-value assessment.
4. **Generate ownership and investment stances.** For each unit, develop invest, hold, harvest, partner, acquire, or divest cases with conditions and timing. Output: strategic stance set.
5. **Allocate scarce capital and attention.** Rank funding needs by expected return, strategic fit, risk concentration, and executable capacity; make trade-offs explicit. Output: capital and management agenda.
6. **Sequence portfolio moves.** Set decision gates, diligence, stakeholder actions, and trigger metrics for changes in stance. Output: rebalancing roadmap.
## Output Contract
A prioritized portfolio agenda listing every unit’s current position, parenting logic, recommended ownership stance, capital request or release, priority rank, decision trigger, and next diligence. It must reconcile requested investment with the available capital envelope and show why a matrix placement alone does not determine the answer.
## Evidence
Use audited unit financials, capital employed, contractual commitments, and market data as observed. Mark synergy estimates, exit multiples, and future share assumptions as forecasts. Keep standalone and allocated corporate costs distinct. State whether a unit’s cash supports another unit by deliberate choice or accounting convention. If a comparison relies on different reporting periods or currencies, normalize before ranking.
## Checks
- The portfolio includes hidden shared-service costs and capital, not only headline revenue.
- Unit comparisons use consistent definitions of margin, growth, and capital employed.
- Every claimed parenting benefit has an owner, mechanism, and alternative if the unit were independent.
- Capital releases and requests reconcile to a single funding envelope.
- Divest or harvest recommendations include transition risk and buyer or customer implications.
## Failure Modes
- Treating a BCG quadrant as an automatic buy or sell instruction.
- Protecting a weak unit because it is historically important to the parent.
- Counting the same synergy in several units’ business cases.
- Ranking by growth while ignoring capital intensity and cash conversion.
- Recommending divestiture without testing separation costs, stranded overhead, or buyer appetite.
