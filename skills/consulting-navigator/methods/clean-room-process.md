# Clean Room Process
## Purpose
Analyze competitively sensitive counterparty data in a legally ring-fenced team so pre-close decisions can use it without antitrust or information-sharing risk. The process turns a narrow question into a controlled finding without exposing raw competitive detail to the wider deal team.
## Use
- Pre-close analysis requires competitively sensitive data from both parties
- Antitrust or regulatory constraints prohibit normal deal-team data sharing
- Synergy, pricing, or customer analysis must proceed under counsel-supervised access rules
## Avoid
- The deal has closed and full data access is lawful—use m-and-a-integration-framework
- Only non-sensitive public or high-level diligence data is needed—use financial-due-diligence or commercial-due-diligence
- No counterparty competitively sensitive data is required for the decision
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Counsel-approved clean-team protocol, membership list, and access rules; inventory of sensitive data categories and what may leave the clean room; specific pre-close analysis questions the clean team must answer; controlled data extracts or VDR access from both parties under clean-room terms; redaction and review process for findings shared with the broader deal team.

**Nice-to-have:** data room index, retention schedule, secure-workspace test, and a pre-approved aggregate reporting format.

**When data is thin:** ask counsel to narrow the question or permit a safe aggregate; never fill a missing competitor datum with an unrestricted team’s estimate.
## Procedure
1. **Translate the deal question into permitted analysis.** Define the decision, data fields needed, permitted granularity, recipients, and deadline. Counsel approves the scope before access is granted.
2. **Establish the ring fence.** Vet members, obtain acknowledgements, provision segregated storage, restrict communications, and train the team on prohibited conduct. Log access and designate an escalation contact.
3. **Classify and ingest data.** Inventory each file, owner, sensitivity level, approved use, retention period, and allowed output. Reject fields outside scope and maintain a chain of custody for extracts.
4. **Perform the analysis inside the room.** Work only on the approved questions, use aggregation or masking where required, and document transformations so results can be reviewed without releasing the underlying records.
5. **Clear and distribute findings.** Submit tables, narratives, and visualizations for counsel review. Release only approved redacted conclusions, retain the review record, and close access when the work ends.
## Output Contract
Produce a counsel-cleared clean-room report that records the question, team and access protocol, data categories examined, analysis method, allowed aggregation level, findings, limitations, and release approvals. The broader deal team receives only sanctioned outputs; raw files and disallowed detail remain inside the ring fence.
## Evidence
Treat data-room extracts and access logs as observed within the clean room. Mark any reconciliation, aggregation, or normalization judgment and retain the calculation trail. Counsel’s permissions govern what may be reported, even when the underlying evidence is persuasive. Do not infer a competitor’s prices, customers, or future conduct beyond the approved analysis. Record deleted, withheld, and unverified fields as limitations.
## Checks
- Every member, file, and outbound item is authorized in the protocol.
- The requested output matches the approved granularity and recipient list.
- Access logs and version history support an audit trail.
- Counsel reviews both analytic output and presentation language before release.
- The room has a documented closure, retention, and incident-escalation step.
## Failure Modes
- Allowing a commercial executive to join a clean-team discussion because they “need context.”
- Releasing a small-cell table that reveals account-level competitor pricing through subtraction.
- Reusing a diligence extract for a new question without renewed scope approval.
- Treating an encrypted folder as sufficient when membership and outbound communication are uncontrolled.
