# Organizational Design
## Purpose
Systematically design organization structure, roles, spans, and interfaces so strategy can be executed through clear accountabilities. It creates a workable form for the enterprise, including the trade-offs between control, customer proximity, expertise, and cost.
## Use
- Strategy, scale, or operating model requires a new structure
- Accountabilities, spans, or interfaces are broken at scale
- Designing post-merger or post-divestiture organization form
## Avoid
- You only need to clarify who decides on a few activities—use raci-matrix
- The issue is cultural alignment not structure—use culture-assessment or 7s-framework
- You need a diagnostic lens before redesign—use star-model or strategic-alignment-framework first
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Strategy and operating priorities the structure must enable; current org chart, headcount, and role descriptions; work process map for critical value streams; pain points on decision rights, interfaces, and accountability; constraints on cost, talent, and governance.

**Nice-to-have:** Manager workload data, salary bands, customer-segment economics, regulatory separation requirements, and merger transition agreements.

**When data is thin:** Design around the few critical value streams and decision bottlenecks first, then label unvalidated span or workload assumptions before finalizing headcount moves.
## Procedure
1. **Translate strategy into design criteria.** State the capabilities, customer segments, speed, control, and cost outcomes the structure must enable; rank the unavoidable trade-offs. Output: design principles and test criteria.
2. **Diagnose the current operating anatomy.** Map units, layers, spans, key roles, decision bottlenecks, and interfaces along priority value streams. Output: current-state design diagnosis.
3. **Generate structural archetypes.** Develop genuinely different choices for grouping work, such as product, customer, geography, function, or hybrid, with the consequences for authority and capability depth. Output: option set.
4. **Detail the preferred form.** Specify unit mandates, executive accountabilities, critical roles, spans, layers, governance forums, and interface mechanisms. Output: target organization blueprint.
5. **Test and transition.** Run scenario and workload tests, resolve pivotal decision rights, then sequence appointments, consultation, communications, and operating-routine changes. Output: transition plan and design decisions.
## Output Contract
Deliver a target organization blueprint containing design principles, structural logic, unit charters, reporting relationships, key roles, span and layer choices, and interface mechanisms for the most important cross-functional work. It also records rejected options, transition dependencies, and decisions still requiring governance. An org chart without mandates and interfaces is incomplete.
## Evidence
Ground pain points in workload, decision-cycle, customer, or financial evidence rather than anecdotes alone. Declare the strategic trade-offs selected by leadership. Treat headcount estimates as scenarios until role scope and operating routines are validated. Where legal entities or labor agreements constrain the design, mark them as fixed boundaries instead of pretending the conceptual ideal is implementable.
## Checks
- Each unit has a mandate that is distinct from adjacent units and aligned to a value-creation purpose.
- Critical decisions have a home, escalation route, and interface with affected units.
- Proposed spans reflect manager workload and work complexity, not a uniform numerical rule.
- The design works through a customer or product handoff without relying on personal relationships.
- Transition risks identify roles that cannot be left vacant during the structural move.
## Failure Modes
- Moving boxes on an org chart while leaving overlapping mandates and decision forums untouched.
- Selecting a functional structure for cost efficiency when customer ownership is the strategic priority.
- Cutting layers without recalculating manager workload, coaching capacity, or control risk.
- Treating RACI assignments as a substitute for resolving a flawed structural boundary.
