# Inorganic Growth Strategy
## Purpose
Design a systematic multi-year acquisition roadmap with themes, screens, capital rules, and sequencing tied to strategy. It defines where external investment can create value before a specific target enters diligence.
## Use
- Building a systematic multi-year acquisition (or major investment) program linked to strategy.
- Defining target screens, themes, capital allocation, and sequencing before active deal pursuit.
- Aligning corporate development, business units, and the board on when and what to buy.
## Avoid
- The decision is still build versus buy for one capability—use buy-vs-build-analysis first.
- A specific target is already in diligence—use commercial-due-diligence and financial-due-diligence.
- Growth should be organic adjacency expansion only—use adjacency-expansion or ansoff-matrix.
- You need post-announcement integration design for a signed deal—use m-and-a-integration-framework or post-merger-integration-roadmap.
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Corporate growth objectives and gaps organic plans cannot close; M&A themes linked to strategy (capability, market, scale, adjacency); Capital envelope, leverage capacity, and risk appetite for deals; Internal integration capacity and prior deal performance lessons; Market maps of potential target clusters and competitive bid dynamics.

**Useful additions:** shareholder-return targets, tax constraints, target relationships, synergy archetypes, and historical deal scorecards.
## Procedure
1. Translate corporate strategy into explicit external-growth gaps: capability, geographic access, customer access, scale, or adjacency. Eliminate themes that organic investment can close within the required horizon.
2. Form a small number of investment theses. For each, state value creation logic, target archetype, acceptable size, geographic boundary, required control, disqualifiers, and integration premise.
3. Set capital-allocation rules across themes, including return threshold, leverage headroom, maximum exposure to one thesis, and reserves for integration. Incorporate what prior deals revealed about execution capacity.
4. Map target universes and rank clusters, not individual companies, by strategic fit, scarcity, likely valuation, bid intensity, regulatory complexity, and achievable synergies.
5. Sequence the program by dependency and absorptive capacity. Decide which capability acquisition must precede market roll-up, where partnerships provide an option, and when to pause for integration learning.
6. Establish governance: thesis owner, sourcing responsibility, screening scorecard, board decision points, and triggers to retire an investment theme when facts change.
## Output Contract
Deliver an inorganic-growth roadmap with prioritized investment theses, target screens, capital rules, target-cluster maps, sequence, governance, and explicit no-go criteria. Show the linkage from each thesis to a strategic gap and the value-creation mechanism expected after closing. This is an acquisition program architecture, not a recommendation to buy one named target or an integration plan.
## Evidence
Separate verified market facts, company information, and past deal performance from valuation assumptions and synergy estimates. Treat rumored target availability as uncertain until confirmed through appropriate channels. Use ranges for valuation and return where bidding conditions are unknown. Do not imply that a strategic fit alone establishes a deal's financial merit or that estimated synergies can be captured without integration capacity.
## Checks
- Every thesis closes a defined strategic gap that organic plans cannot close in time.
- Target screens include disqualifiers as well as attractive features.
- Capital rules prevent several individually attractive deals from exceeding aggregate capacity.
- Sequence reflects integration bandwidth and regulatory lead time.
- Governance distinguishes a thematic screen from the later investment decision on a specific deal.
## Failure Modes
- Creating broad themes such as "technology" that cannot eliminate any target.
- Counting the same revenue synergy in multiple competing acquisition theses.
- Pursuing a roll-up while the integration team is still overloaded by the prior deal.
- Treating a target's availability rumor as evidence that a premium bid will create value.
