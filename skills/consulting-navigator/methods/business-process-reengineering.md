# Business Process Reengineering
## Purpose
Radically redesign end-to-end processes around outcomes to achieve discontinuous gains in cost, cycle time, or quality. It starts from the outcome required by a customer or business, not from preserving the existing departmental sequence.
## Use
- Radical step-change in cost, cycle time, or service quality is required
- Legacy process design no longer fits strategy, technology, or customer expectations
- Cross-functional work must be rebuilt around outcomes rather than departments
## Avoid
- Incremental waste removal on the current process is enough—use lean-manufacturing, kaizen, or value-stream-mapping
- You only need local workplace organization—use 5s-system
- Change readiness or sponsorship is missing—use change-readiness-assessment and organizational-change-management first
## Inputs
**Must-have:**

- Clear performance targets (cost, time, quality, customer outcome)
- Current-state process map with volumes, handoffs, and systems
- Customer and stakeholder requirements for the redesigned process
- Authority and sponsorship to change roles, policies, and technology

**Nice-to-have:** benchmark performance, policy constraints, demand segmentation, technology architecture, and labor or regulatory obligations.

**When data is thin:** validate the current-state map through walkthroughs before proposing a new operating model; do not substitute a workshop consensus for actual handoff evidence.
## Procedure
1. **Name the outcome and discontinuity target.** Define the customer result, end-to-end owner, and non-negotiable measures such as order-to-cash time or first-time-right rate. Set the magnitude of improvement that incremental repair cannot plausibly deliver. Output: redesign charter.
2. **Expose the current design logic.** Map steps, decisions, queues, rework loops, data re-entry, systems, and ownership across functions. Quantify the waits and defects that separate touch time from elapsed time. Output: baseline and structural constraints.
3. **Design from a clean sheet.** Ask what sequence, roles, information, and technology would serve the outcome if the old departments did not exist. Consolidate fragmented work, move decisions to the point of information, and eliminate controls that no longer manage a real risk. Output: alternative end-to-end designs.
4. **Select and detail the target process.** Evaluate alternatives against outcome measures, controls, feasibility, and transition risk. Specify new roles, decision rights, data objects, system interfaces, and exception paths. Output: target operating process.
5. **Pilot the break and transition.** Test the target in a bounded segment, measure the discontinuity, resolve policy and system changes, then sequence migration by volume and readiness. Output: implementation roadmap with benefits tracking.
## Output Contract
The design package contains the current structural constraints, target outcome measures, a target end-to-end process, changed roles and decision rights, technology and policy implications, transition waves, and benefits measures. It explains which legacy handoffs disappear or move, rather than merely digitizing the same route.
## Evidence
Use time stamps, case samples, system logs, and customer requirements as observed evidence. Mark assumed volumes, exception rates, and automation capability. Keep mandated controls separate from habitual approvals. A projected benefit must name the eliminated work, changed rule, or capacity released; never credit a technology purchase with improvement before the process change exists.
## Checks
- The target is anchored to an outcome, not a department's local efficiency.
- Current-state measures prove why incremental improvement is insufficient.
- New decision rights match the location of information and accountability.
- Exceptions, compliance controls, and failure recovery are designed explicitly.
- A pilot measures cycle time, quality, and adoption against the baseline.
## Failure Modes
- Automating every existing handoff and preserving the original delay.
- Redrawing boxes without changing decision rights or incentives.
- Promising dramatic savings while excluding severance, retraining, and transition costs.
- Designing a happy path that abandons complex customer exceptions.
- Launching enterprise-wide before a pilot proves the new role and data model.
