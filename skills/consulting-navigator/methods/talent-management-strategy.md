# Talent Management Strategy
## Purpose
Design the integrated system to attract, develop, deploy, and retain the talent the strategy requires. It joins talent levers into a business-led roadmap, rather than forecasting headcount or naming successors for a few roles.
## Use
- Building or resetting how the firm wins for critical talent
- Aligning fragmented HR programs to strategy
- Prioritizing multi-year talent investments and ownership
## Avoid
- You only need headcount supply/demand numbers—use workforce-planning
- You only need succession for a few critical seats—use succession-planning
- The issue is pay equity or job architecture alone—use job-analysis-and-evaluation first
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Business strategy and critical capability needs; current talent inventory and gap signals; existing HR programs across attract-develop-retain; labor market and competitor talent moves; budget, brand, and cultural constraints.
## Procedure
1. **Translate strategy into talent segments.** Identify the roles, skills, locations, and leadership populations that disproportionately enable the business plan. Define the value proposition each segment needs, instead of treating all employees as one audience.
2. **Diagnose the talent system.** Map the current employee journey from attraction through hiring, onboarding, performance, development, mobility, reward, and retention. Locate handoff failures, ownership gaps, and programs that do not serve the priority segments.
3. **Choose coherent levers.** Design a limited set of mutually reinforcing moves: employee value proposition, sourcing channels, career architecture, manager practices, learning pathways, mobility rules, and retention actions. Test the intended behavior change for each lever.
4. **Sequence investments.** Put foundations such as critical-role definitions, manager capability, and data governance ahead of dependent programs. Set a road map with owners, budget, pilot populations, adoption milestones, and explicit stop conditions.
5. **Measure and adapt.** Track funnel conversion, time to proficiency, internal fill, regretted attrition, engagement, and representation for the targeted segments. Review both talent outcomes and business impact, then reallocate funding from weak levers.
## Output Contract
An implementation roadmap that links strategic capabilities to target talent segments, a designed attract-develop-deploy-retain system, prioritized initiatives, owners, investment, timing, and outcome measures. It must show how the components reinforce each other and what employee experience will change. A collection of HR projects or a headcount plan alone is insufficient.
## Evidence
Use workforce records, candidate funnel data, employee research, labor-market evidence, and program utilization as evidence. Treat an assumed retention lift, employer-brand appeal, or manager adoption rate as an assumption and specify how it will be tested. Break out results for critical talent segments; an enterprise average can conceal the population that matters. Do not infer causes of attrition solely from exit-survey checkboxes.
## Checks
- Each investment traces to a business capability and a defined talent segment.
- Attraction, development, deployment, and retention levers form a coherent employee journey.
- Program owners can make decisions and control the named budget or policy change.
- Measures include leading adoption indicators and lagging talent outcomes.
- The roadmap identifies dependencies on workforce planning, reward, and manager capacity.
## Failure Modes
- Launching a new learning catalog without changing how managers assign developmental work.
- Using a generic employer brand for scarce engineers and frontline operations alike.
- Promising internal mobility while managers are rewarded for hoarding high performers.
- Funding recruiting campaigns without fixing a slow, inconsistent selection process.
- Measuring course completion or event attendance as proof that critical capability improved.
