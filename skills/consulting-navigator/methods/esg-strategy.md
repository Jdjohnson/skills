# ESG Strategy
## Purpose
Define material ESG priorities and a multi-year roadmap that links environmental, social, and governance performance to enterprise strategy. It turns scattered commitments into deliberate choices about where the company will set ambition, invest, measure, and govern.
## Use
- Setting material ESG priorities, ambition, and multi-year initiatives linked to enterprise strategy.
- Turning fragmented sustainability activity into a governed roadmap with owners and metrics.
- Preparing for investor, customer, or board demands for a clear ESG posture.
## Avoid
- You only need a carbon inventory baseline—use carbon-footprint-assessment.
- You need climate financial-risk disclosure under TCFD pillars—use tcfd-reporting.
- You still do not know capability gaps and should diagnose maturity first—use sustainability-maturity-assessment.
## Inputs
**Must-have:**
- Materiality view of environmental, social, and governance topics for the firm.
- Current ESG metrics, initiatives, and stakeholder commitments.
- Business strategy, competitive context, and capital/resource constraints.
- Regulatory and investor disclosure expectations relevant to the firm.

**Nice-to-have:** lifecycle data, supplier emissions, customer tender requirements, peer targets, and baseline investment cases.

**When data is thin:** distinguish a material topic from a target; conduct focused stakeholder validation before committing a numeric ambition.
## Procedure
1. **Set scope and decision criteria.** Define the value chain, jurisdictions, planning horizon, and the decisions the roadmap must influence. Establish how impact, financial exposure, stakeholder relevance, feasibility, and differentiation will be weighed. Output: materiality design.
2. **Create a topic universe.** Assemble environmental, social, and governance topics from operations, value-chain effects, stakeholder demands, regulation, and strategy. Remove duplicates but retain emerging issues that lack historical metrics. Output: long list of topics.
3. **Assess materiality from two directions.** Evaluate the company’s effect on people and environment alongside how each topic can affect enterprise value. Validate ratings with relevant internal and external stakeholders and record conflicts. Output: materiality matrix.
4. **Choose ambitions and initiatives.** For the highest-priority topics, define the intended outcome, target date, initiative options, dependencies, and investment implications. Avoid setting a target where the measurement boundary is not established. Output: strategic initiative portfolio.
5. **Sequence the roadmap.** Place foundational data, policy, operating, and investment work across years; assign executive owners and decision gates. Output: multi-year roadmap.
6. **Embed and govern.** Link metrics to planning, capital allocation, risk review, incentives, and reporting calendars. Specify how new information can change priorities. Output: governance and measurement plan.
## Output Contract
An ESG strategic roadmap with the materiality boundary and results, selected topics, ambition statements, initiative portfolio, milestones, capital or resource implications, accountable owners, metrics, and governance cadence. It must show why lower-priority demands were deferred and how priorities affect real planning choices.
## Evidence
Use emissions records, injury data, workforce metrics, regulations, contracts, and stakeholder submissions as observed evidence. Label impact estimates, future policy assumptions, and peer comparisons as inferred or assumed. Keep external stakeholder views attributable to a group, not falsely universal. A missing value-chain data point should narrow the claim and create a measurement action, not disappear from the matrix.
## Checks
- Materiality considers both external impact and enterprise-value exposure.
- Each selected topic has a boundary, baseline status, owner, and decision use.
- Targets specify unit, scope, baseline year, deadline, and calculation method.
- Roadmap milestones identify dependencies on data, suppliers, or capital approval.
- Governance connects metrics to strategy and investment reviews, not only annual reporting.
## Failure Modes
- Publishing a broad topic list with no prioritization or resource trade-off.
- Setting net-zero or diversity targets without a defined boundary or baseline.
- Letting one investor questionnaire determine company-wide materiality.
- Treating disclosure compliance as the whole strategy.
- Assigning an ESG owner who cannot influence operations, procurement, or capital decisions.
