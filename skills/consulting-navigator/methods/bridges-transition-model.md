# Bridges' Transition Model
## Purpose
Map psychological transition—Ending, Neutral Zone, New Beginning—so operational change does not stall on unprocessed loss and ambiguity. It equips leaders to respond to loss and learning, not just announce a process.
## Use
- Helping people move through the psychological stages of Ending, Neutral Zone, and New Beginning during a major change.
- Designing leader and manager actions for loss, ambiguity, and re-commitment—not only process rollout.
- Explaining why a well-designed change still feels chaotic mid-transition.
## Avoid
- You need a structured individual capability gap model (awareness through reinforcement)—use adkar-model.
- You need an organization-wide eight-step mobilization plan—use kotters-8-step-process.
- The issue is purely technical process redesign with minimal identity or role loss.
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Description of what is ending for affected groups (roles, identity, routines, status); signals of current emotional and behavioral response by segment; timeline and milestones of the operational change; manager and sponsor capacity to support people through ambiguity.

**Nice-to-have:** pulse feedback, manager listening notes, turnover or absence trends, informal-network insight, and communication materials.

**When data is thin:** hold structured listening sessions with affected groups and treat phase assessments as provisional; do not label normal uncertainty as resistance without hearing the loss.
## Procedure
1. **Separate change from transition.** Describe the external implementation event and the internal adjustments each affected group must make.
2. **Inventory endings.** For each segment identify losses of role, status, competence, relationships, routines, and certainty; name what must be acknowledged before new behavior can take hold.
3. **Assess transition signals.** Use listening, pulse data, and manager observations to place groups in Ending, Neutral Zone, or New Beginning, allowing different groups to occupy different phases.
4. **Design ending actions.** Provide clear reasons, respectful recognition of what is ending, accurate information about what remains, and opportunities to let go of obsolete routines.
5. **Lead the neutral zone.** Create temporary structures, short feedback loops, protected learning time, and honest communication about ambiguity while the new way is not yet normal.
6. **Reinforce new beginnings.** Connect the new identity to purpose, show early evidence, clarify new roles, and celebrate credible milestones without pretending transition is finished for everyone.
## Output Contract
A transition support plan that segments affected groups, records their losses and phase signals, specifies leader and manager actions for each phase, aligns actions to change milestones, assigns owners, and defines feedback indicators. It must show how human transition will be managed alongside the technical rollout.
## Evidence
Treat documented role changes, attendance, turnover, and direct feedback as evidence. Mark emotional-stage placement as an interpretation, not a diagnosis. Protect confidentiality when reporting group concerns. Do not collapse a minority group with a distinct loss into an enterprise average merely because the rollout date is common.
## Checks
- The plan names concrete endings, not only generic “change impacts.”
- Phase actions differ between loss acknowledgement, ambiguity support, and new-role reinforcement.
- Managers have scripts, time, and escalation paths appropriate to their span of control.
- Technical milestones and human support moments are sequenced together.
- Feedback can change the plan when a group is stuck in the neutral zone.
## Failure Modes
- Launching an inspiring future-state message before acknowledging jobs, routines, or status people are losing.
- Assuming the neutral zone is a communication failure rather than a period needing temporary structure.
- Forcing a celebration of the new beginning while critical work practices remain unclear.
- Applying one phase label to executives, managers, and frontline staff with different transition timing.
- Using the model as a substitute for fixing an unfair role design or an impossible workload.
