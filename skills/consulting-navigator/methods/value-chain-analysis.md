# Value Chain Analysis
## Purpose
Disaggregate the firm into linked primary and support activities to locate cost drivers and differentiation sources. It asks how activity choices reinforce or weaken competitive advantage across the firm, rather than simply allocating ledger cost to products.
## Use
- Disaggregating the firm into primary and support activities to locate cost and differentiation drivers
- Identifying activity linkages, make-vs-buy candidates, and advantage sources inside the firm
- Connecting operational improvement priorities to competitive positioning
## Avoid
- You need industry-level profit location across players—use profit-pools
- You need VRIO resource tests rather than activity economics—use resource-based-view
- You need process-level waste removal on a single flow—use value-stream-mapping
## Inputs
**Must-have:**
- Map of primary and support activities for the firm or business unit
- Cost, asset, and performance data by activity where available
- Customer value drivers linked to activities (quality, speed, features, service)
- Competitive or best-practice reference points for key activities

**Nice-to-have:** supplier terms, capital by activity, customer complaints, and owner interviews.

**When data is thin:** draw the architecture, mark unverified linkages, and use directional cost ranges.
## Procedure
1. **Set the competitive scope.** Select one business unit, offering, geography, and customer promise. Specify cost, differentiation, or both. Output: scoped advantage question.
2. **Separate primary and support activities.** Lay out logistics, operations, sales, and service alongside procurement, technology, human resources, and infrastructure. Split only where economics or customer value differs. Output: activity architecture.
3. **Attach economics and value drivers.** For each activity, capture major cost behavior, assets, quality, speed, and customer-facing contribution. Identify structural drivers such as scale, location, learning, capacity utilization, and policy choices. Output: activity score sheet.
4. **Trace linkages.** Test how a decision in one activity affects another: supplier specification may change defects, inventory, warranty, and service reputation. Surface cross-activity trade-offs that a functional budget would hide. Output: linkage hypotheses.
5. **Compare and choose interventions.** Benchmark material activities against competitors or internal best practice, identify make-versus-buy candidates, and rank moves by advantage impact, feasibility, and risk. Output: strategic activity agenda.
## Output Contract
An activity map with primary and support activities, score sheet, cost and differentiation drivers, linkage hypotheses, benchmark gaps, and ranked interventions. Each recommendation identifies its owner, competitive mechanism, and measure. It supports an operating-choice decision, not a process-waste map or VRIO score.
## Evidence
Use operational measures and accounting data as facts; label cross-activity causality as inferred until tested. Distinguish full activity cost from an allocation proxy and record comparator, period, and scope for each benchmark. Where support serves several businesses, state the allocation basis and test whether change affects customer value or only shifts cost.
## Checks
- Every material activity has an economic and customer-value role.
- Linkages cross activity boundaries instead of repeating functional observations.
- Recommendations specify whether they improve cost position, differentiation, or both.
- The boundary excludes corporate activities that cannot affect the scoped offering.
- A make-versus-buy proposal includes loss of know-how and coordination consequences.
## Failure Modes
- Treating the diagram as an org chart rather than a chain of activities.
- Cutting service or quality costs that protect the chosen customer promise.
- Comparing total company ratios when the relevant activity mix differs.
- Missing procurement, systems, or people practices that create the visible operating result.
- Recommending outsourcing without evaluating whether the linkage is a source of advantage.
