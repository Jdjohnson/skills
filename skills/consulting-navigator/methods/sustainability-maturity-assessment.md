# Sustainability Maturity Assessment
## Purpose
Score the organization's sustainability capabilities against a maturity model to expose gaps before strategy and investment. It tests repeatable management capability across the enterprise, rather than calculating emissions or selecting an ESG initiative portfolio.
## Use
- Benchmarking sustainability capabilities across governance, data, operations, and culture
- Creating a shared baseline before setting ESG ambition or investment
- Prioritizing capability gaps that block credible sustainability performance
## Avoid
- You already know the gaps and need an ESG initiative roadmap—use esg-strategy
- You need quantified emissions rather than capability maturity—use carbon-footprint-assessment
- You need measured social outcomes of a specific program—use social-impact-assessment
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Current sustainability policies, roles, and governance cadence; evidence of data systems, metrics, and reporting practices; sample of initiatives, targets, and operational practices across units; peer or framework reference points for maturity levels.
## Procedure
1. **Set the assessment boundary and rubric.** Choose the units, geographies, and capability dimensions: governance, materiality, data and controls, targets, operational integration, supplier engagement, and culture. Define behavioral anchors for each maturity level before interviews begin.
2. **Gather evidence by dimension.** Review policies, committee minutes, metric lineage, target approvals, operating procedures, and a deliberately varied sample of sites. Interview process owners to distinguish a stated policy from routine practice.
3. **Rate against observable anchors.** Score each dimension only where the evidence meets the level definition. For example, a spreadsheet reported once is not a controlled, repeatable data process; a target without ownership is not embedded execution.
4. **Calibrate across units.** Compare scoring rationales in a cross-functional session and surface variation hidden by an enterprise average. Record the lowest critical control level where a common claim depends on it.
5. **Prioritize the gap backlog.** Rank gaps by credibility risk, regulatory exposure, strategic value, effort, and dependency. Separate foundational fixes such as data ownership from later ambition choices such as new programs or external commitments.
## Output Contract
A maturity scorecard showing the rubric, evidence references, score and confidence by capability and unit, variation across the organization, and a ranked gap backlog. It must explain why a dimension received its level and which foundational capabilities should be strengthened first. The artifact is a capability diagnosis, not a greenhouse-gas inventory or a polished sustainability narrative.
## Evidence
Mark documents, system records, and observed operating routines as evidence; interview assertions establish hypotheses until corroborated. State the maturity anchors and any peer comparison basis so scoring is reproducible. Do not infer enterprise-wide capability from headquarters practice or one showcase facility. Where a rating rests on a limited sample, display a confidence flag and name the next site or record needed to confirm it.
## Checks
- Every score maps to a published anchor and at least one specific piece of evidence.
- Governance, data, targets, operations, and culture are assessed separately rather than rolled into a single impression.
- Scoring covers low-performing as well as flagship business units.
- The gap backlog distinguishes missing capability from a deliberate strategic choice.
- Priority actions identify dependencies, owner candidates, and the maturity change expected.
## Failure Modes
- Calling a policy launch “managed” when no cadence tests compliance or outcomes.
- Giving every dimension a middle score to avoid a difficult executive conversation.
- Using an external rating as proof that internal data controls are reliable.
- Comparing units with different regulatory obligations without adjusting the assessment boundary.
- Jumping from a scorecard to a long initiative list before repairing data ownership and governance gaps.
