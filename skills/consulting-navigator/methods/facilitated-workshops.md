# Facilitated Workshops
## Purpose
Design and run structured group sessions that convert multi-stakeholder input into owned decisions, agreed requirements, or shared artifacts under a neutral facilitator. This is general facilitation craft—custom purpose, roles, agenda, activities, and decision rules—for integrating multiple viewpoints quickly with buy-in, not for questions analysis can settle offline.
## Use
- Cross-functional alignment is the bottleneck and bilateral meetings will not close it.
- Structured group work must produce a decision, option set, problem frame, or requirements pack in a short window.
- Stakeholders will not own the recommendation unless they co-create it.
- A downstream method (planning, prioritization, design) requires live multi-stakeholder contribution.
## Avoid
- The answer is primarily analytical and does not need group synthesis—do the analysis offline first.
- Power dynamics make open dialogue unsafe—use stakeholder-mapping and private interviews first.
- You need a time-boxed product prototype cycle with fixed design-sprint rules—use design-sprints.
- No Workshop Owner is empowered to act on outputs—the session will be theater.
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Clear workshop purpose and required decision or artifact; participant list with roles and decision rights; agenda, timing, and pre-reads or data packs; facilitation plan (activities, capture method, decision rules).

**Nice-to-have:** Stakeholder map, prior analysis summaries, parking-lot rules, and success criteria.

**When inputs are thin:** Do not run a workshop without a purpose and owner; convert to interviews or a short working session until both exist.
## Procedure
1. **Define and plan** — Agree purpose, deliverables, success criteria, participants, roles (Owner, Facilitator, Participants, Observers), decision rights, and agenda. Output: workshop brief.
2. **Prepare** — Circulate agenda and pre-reading; arrange room or virtual setup, materials, and capture templates; brief the Owner and Facilitator on process. Output: prep pack and confirmed attendance.
3. **Facilitate** — Open with purpose and rules; run the planned activities; manage airtime and conflict; capture decisions, options, and open issues live. Output: real-time capture board.
4. **Close** — Confirm decisions, actions with owners and dates, open issues, and explicit non-decisions; run a short process retrospective. Output: draft outcome list.
5. **Document and follow up** — Issue a Workshop Report (decisions, actions, open issues, outputs—not narrative minutes); track actions and schedule follow-up. Output: report and action log.
## Output Contract
A Workshop Report and supporting artifacts that include: (1) purpose and participants, (2) decisions made and the decision rule used, (3) options or requirements produced, (4) actions with owners and due dates, (5) open issues, and (6) what was explicitly not decided. The report must show owned outcomes ready for the next engagement step—not a status recap or unowned brainstorm dump.
## Evidence
- **Observed:** what participants actually said, voted, or committed to in-session.
- **Inferred:** themes synthesized from discussion—label as synthesis, not fact.
- **Assumptions:** pre-read data counts as shared ground only if participants accept it; otherwise park the challenge.
- **Unknowns:** record open issues; do not force false consensus. Thin evidence outside the room stays thin—workshops do not create facts.
## Checks
- Purpose and deliverable were stated at the start and met at the end (or gaps are explicit).
- Roles and decision rights were clear; the Facilitator stayed neutral on content.
- Report lists decisions and actions with owners, not narrative minutes.
- Dissent and open issues are visible; silence was not treated as agreement.
- Follow-up path exists for every action and unresolved item.
## Failure Modes
- Agenda-free discussion that ends with different private stories.
- Facilitator advocating a preferred answer.
- Status updates rebranded as workshops.
- No Owner, so decisions cannot stick.
- Skipping documentation so the room reopens everything next time.
