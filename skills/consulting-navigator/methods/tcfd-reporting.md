# TCFD Reporting
## Purpose
Organize climate-related financial risk and opportunity disclosure in a TCFD-style package covering governance, strategic implications, risk process, and measurement. It is not merely an emissions inventory or a general sustainability strategy.
## Use
- Producing climate-related financial disclosures aligned to TCFD pillars
- Linking climate risks and opportunities to governance, strategy, risk management, and metrics
- Responding to investor, lender, or regulator expectations for climate financial transparency
## Avoid
- You only need a GHG inventory without financial-risk disclosure structure—use carbon-footprint-assessment
- You need the broader multi-year ESG agenda beyond climate disclosure—use esg-strategy
- Climate strategy choices are undefined and maturity is unknown—use sustainability-maturity-assessment and esg-strategy first
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Board and management climate governance practices; climate-related risks, opportunities, and strategy implications; risk management processes covering climate exposures; metrics and targets including GHG and climate-resilience indicators; scenario analysis inputs for material climate pathways.
## Procedure
1. **Set reporting boundary and materiality.** Identify entities, assets, value-chain exposures, intended users, reporting period, and the climate issues that could affect enterprise value. Align the boundary with financial reporting where practical and explain any difference.
2. **Evidence governance.** Document board oversight, committee mandates, management accountability, escalation, and the frequency of climate decisions. Test whether the stated governance actually receives climate information and acts on it.
3. **Translate climate pathways into business effects.** Select relevant physical and transition scenarios, time horizons, and assumptions. Trace hazards, policy, market, technology, or reputation effects to revenue, cost, asset life, capital expenditure, financing, and resilience choices.
4. **Integrate risk management.** Map climate identification, assessment, prioritization, and monitoring into the enterprise risk process. Show thresholds, controls, owners, and how a climate exposure changes investment or operating decisions.
5. **Assemble metrics and disclosure.** Reconcile emissions, targets, and resilience indicators to source systems; explain calculation boundaries and performance. Draft the four-pillar narrative, cross-check internal consistency, obtain owner sign-off, and retain an audit trail.
## Output Contract
A TCFD-aligned package arranged in four lenses: oversight, business strategy, risk controls, and measures with targets. It includes material climate exposures, scenario assumptions and financial implications, accountability, metric definitions, boundaries, and evidence owners. The package must show how climate matters to financial decisions; it is not a carbon worksheet.
## Evidence
Board minutes, risk registers, financial models, emissions records, and approved targets are evidence. Scenario pathways, price trajectories, hazard intensities, and asset-response assumptions must be named, dated, and sensitivity-tested. Do not present a climate narrative as financially relevant without showing the link to a business mechanism. Keep calculation methodologies and organizational boundaries stable across periods or explain each change and its effect on comparability.
## Checks
- All four pillars have specific, internally consistent disclosures.
- Governance statements identify actual decision rights, cadence, and information flows.
- Material scenario effects connect to relevant financial-planning categories.
- Metrics have definitions, boundaries, units, source owners, and prior-period comparability notes.
- The risk-management section describes integration with existing processes, not a parallel climate spreadsheet.
## Failure Modes
- Reporting emissions totals without explaining climate risk governance or financial exposure.
- Selecting dramatic scenarios but never translating them into asset, revenue, or capital effects.
- Describing board oversight when climate matters only reach the board after annual reporting closes.
- Mixing Scope 1 and 2 methodology changes into performance claims without a restatement.
- Treating a target announcement as evidence of a credible transition plan.
