# Strategic Sourcing
## Purpose
Plan and execute a structured category strategy and sourcing process that maximizes value from the supply base. It carries a category from fact base through supplier award and adoption, rather than stopping at a price comparison.
## Use
- Redesigning how a category is sourced to improve cost, risk, quality, and supplier performance.
- Running a structured sourcing event from spend analysis through award and implementation.
- Building multi-year category roadmaps with clear supplier segmentation and levers.
## Avoid
- You only need to rank supplier power and risk exposure—use kraljic-matrix first.
- Purchase price is a poor proxy and full lifecycle cost is the decision—use total-cost-of-ownership.
- The issue is post-award inventory replenishment design—use vendor-managed-inventory.
## Inputs
**Must-have:** Clean spend data by category, supplier, and business unit; specifications, demand forecasts, and current contracts; supplier market structure and capability intelligence; stakeholder requirements for cost, quality, risk, and service; negotiation constraints and award governance rules.

**Useful additions:** should-cost data, consumption forecasts by site, sustainability requirements, supplier performance history, and transition capacity.
## Procedure
1. Clean the category fact base: normalize supplier names, classify spend, separate price from volume changes, and reconcile totals to accounts payable. Identify leakage, maverick buying, and contractual exposure.
2. Define the requirement and demand outlook with users. Challenge unnecessary specification variation before inviting suppliers to bid; distinguish non-negotiable performance requirements from legacy preferences.
3. Analyze the supply market, incumbent performance, and feasible levers: aggregation, specification redesign, competitive event, supplier development, dual sourcing, or contract reset. Build a category strategy with value, risk, and implementation implications.
4. Translate the strategy into an RFx or negotiation plan. Set supplier eligibility, bid instructions, evaluation criteria, commercial terms, confidentiality controls, and decision gates before receiving proposals.
5. Evaluate proposals using a cross-functional scorecard. Validate supplier claims through references, samples, financial checks, or site review as material. Negotiate the preferred scenario and document concessions.
6. Obtain award approval, execute the contract, and manage the switch. Track supplier onboarding, demand transfer, invoice prices, compliance, service levels, and realized value against the approved baseline.
## Output Contract
Deliver a category strategy, sourcing-event plan, supplier evaluation record, award recommendation, and implementation tracker. The award recommendation states baseline, commercial and non-commercial criteria, bid comparison, risk controls, contract terms, savings calculation, approvers, and transition owner. It must be executable through first invoice and performance review.
## Evidence
Use paid spend and contract extracts as baseline evidence, not supplier list prices. Separate committed savings, forecasted avoidance, and unvalidated opportunity. Keep bidder claims conditional until verified. Document demand, mix, and foreign-exchange assumptions that affect savings; do not count a reduction twice across price and volume initiatives. Protect bid confidentiality and retain an auditable score rationale.
## Checks
- Spend taxonomy and baseline reconcile to the chosen accounting period.
- Requirements were challenged with users before supplier evaluation.
- Score weights reflect category priorities, including risk and service where material.
- Award authority is independent of the evaluator who favors a supplier.
- Value tracking has a named finance validation point after implementation.
## Failure Modes
- Running an auction before harmonizing specifications, so bids are not comparable.
- Awarding the lowest unit price while freight, defects, minimum orders, or switching costs erase the claimed value.
- Treating a signed contract as implemented while plants continue buying from incumbents.
- Announcing savings from a forecast baseline that demand declines later make impossible to validate.
