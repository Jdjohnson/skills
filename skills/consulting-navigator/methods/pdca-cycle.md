# PDCA Cycle
## Purpose
Run a closed Plan-Do-Check-Act loop that turns a hypothesized improvement into a validated standard or a documented learning. Its unit of work is a bounded experiment with an explicit decision to adopt, adjust, or abandon.
## Use
- You need a structured iterative loop to plan, test, check, and standardize an improvement
- A change should be piloted with clear success criteria before broad rollout
- Learning and adjustment matter as much as the initial solution design
## Avoid
- You need broad cultural continuous improvement ownership across many micro-changes—use kaizen
- The problem cause is still unknown—use root-cause-analysis before Plan
- You need a full statistical DMAIC project for chronic variation—use six-sigma
## Inputs
**Must-have:** A clearly framed problem or improvement hypothesis; baseline metric and operational definition of success; a small-scale test plan (who, where, duration, controls); data collection method for Check; decision rights to adopt, adjust, or abandon and update the standard.

**Nice-to-have:** A process owner, a stable comparison group, a visual test board, and frontline observations from the current condition.

**When data is thin:** Narrow the pilot until measures can be observed directly, and keep the countermeasure reversible; a broad rollout cannot substitute for a valid Check.
## Procedure
1. **Plan the hypothesis.** Describe the current condition, causal hypothesis, target, countermeasure, test boundary, and prediction. Output: experiment charter.
2. **Do the bounded trial.** Train participants, execute the countermeasure in the selected area or period, and capture deviations without silently changing the plan. Output: trial log.
3. **Check the result.** Compare actual data with baseline, target, and prediction; investigate variation, side effects, and whether the mechanism behaved as expected. Output: check sheet and findings.
4. **Act on the learning.** Standardize the countermeasure with ownership and controls if it works; otherwise revise the hypothesis or abandon it. Output: adopt-adjust-abandon decision.
5. **Start the next loop.** Record the learning, select the next gap, and set a new target condition. Output: updated improvement backlog.
## Output Contract
Create a completed cycle record: problem statement, measurable prediction, pilot boundary, data plan, actual-versus-expected result, decision, and either revised standard work or a next experiment. The record makes the evidence for standardization inspectable and prevents an unverified countermeasure from becoming policy.
## Evidence
Capture baseline and trial data using the same operational definition. Label the causal mechanism as a hypothesis until the Check results support it. Record disruptions, staffing changes, or demand shifts that contaminate comparison. If no valid baseline or control is possible, lower the claim to learning evidence and do not declare the countermeasure proven.
## Checks
- The Plan names a prediction, not merely an activity to complete.
- The pilot boundary prevents unrelated changes from obscuring the result.
- Check compares results to both the baseline and the stated target.
- The Act decision updates the work standard, training, and audit method when adoption is justified.
- A failed trial leaves a usable learning record rather than disappearing from the improvement history.
## Failure Modes
- Calling a meeting “Plan” without writing the expected metric change or causal hypothesis.
- Expanding a favorable pilot before checking whether its result survives a normal operating week.
- Standardizing a local workaround that improves one metric while damaging safety, quality, or customer service.
- Repeating the same test because the prior result and decision were never recorded.
