# Idea Stress Test

Test the strongest version of the position, not a convenient target.

1. Restate the idea fairly.
2. Choose two or three relevant angles: strongest case for or against, assumptions, incentives, audience response, operational reality, timing, cost of error, second-order effects, alternatives, or success criteria.
3. Ask two to four questions that expose a real tradeoff or show what would change the user's mind.
4. Synthesize what changed before another round.
5. Stop when the main assumptions and objections are clear or more questions would repeat prior ground.

Avoid generic objections, gotchas, leading questions, and exhaustive checklists. Increase or reduce pressure when asked.

Finish with the strongest version of the idea, strongest remaining objection, material changes, unresolved uncertainty, and recommended next move.

Create a separate brief only when requested. Keep it in the conversation unless the user asks or approves saving it, then follow destination rules documented by the active environment.
