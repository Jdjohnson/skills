# Execution Trust

Use small records to keep decisions and commitments from decaying between conversation and execution. Run only the mode the request needs.

## Configuration

Use task systems, message channels, tracking locations, owners, and review cadences supplied by the user or documented by the active environment. Without configuration, work in the conversation and produce drafts. Never invent a destination, person, date, decision, or commitment.

## Decision receipt

Create a receipt only for a settled decision, not a proposal or open question:

```text
Decision: [one clear sentence]
Owner: [person] — by [date]
Replaces: [superseded plan or nothing]
Displaces: [work that moves or nothing]
```

Ask once for material missing fields or leave them visibly unresolved. Keep the receipt short; a decision needing substantial context belongs in a separate brief.

## Commitment capture

1. Scan the authorized period or material for commitments the user or named participants actually made.
2. Show candidate commitments and ask the responsible person to confirm, revise, or reject them. A scan never creates tasks on its own.
3. For confirmed commitments, prepare one task record with a clear action, owner, real due date, and configured destination. Skip duplicates.
4. Obtain explicit approval immediately before creating or changing tasks in an external system. Agreement with the task's wording is not mutation approval unless the user clearly authorized creation.

Capture only; do not expand into backlog grooming or workload commentary unless asked.

## Avoided conversation

Surface plausible candidates from repeated carryover, stalled commitments, or avoidance language, then ask which conversation matters most. Give each chosen item one explicit disposition:

- **Dated** — a named date, with draft hold text if useful.
- **Waived** — the user's reason it can wait.
- **Parked** — a named future period, not “later.”

Encourage specificity without overriding the user's decision. Do not create a standing register, severity score, meeting, or dashboard unless requested.

## Clarity check

Before a decision or announcement moves, verify:

1. Can the decision be stated without a hedge?
2. Is there a named owner and real date?
3. What does it replace?
4. What does it displace?
5. Who is affected and does not know yet?

If the first question fails, the decision is not settled. Otherwise output the decision receipt plus a tell-first list. Draft messages as needed, but do not send them without explicit approval.

## Periodic review

For a user-specified period, summarize available evidence: decision receipts and clarity checks, commitment sweeps and captures, avoided-conversation dispositions, and any zero-activity mode. Report what happened without inventing a motivational narrative or prescribing a new process.

## Every mode

- Treat external sends, posts, invitations, task-system writes, and other connected-system mutations as separate approval points.
- If a configured tracker is append-only, never rewrite history. Record only what actually happened.
- Keep confirmed, drafted, written, sent, and completed states distinct.
- End with a brief receipt of what was produced, where anything was written, and what was skipped with the reason.
