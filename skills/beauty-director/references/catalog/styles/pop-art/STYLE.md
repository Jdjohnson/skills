# Pop Art

## Defining grammar

Pop Art turns mass-media reproduction into the subject and surface of art. Commercial products, comic fragments, celebrity poses, headlines, and familiar consumer imagery are enlarged, repeated, cropped, flattened, or deadpan. Hard contour, process color, Ben-Day or halftone texture, screen-print misregistration, and serial presentation make the method of reproduction visible. The direction should feel cool, immediate, and knowingly commercial rather than simply colorful. Use recognizable movement-level approximation with original subjects and arrangements.

## Composition and type

Favor one blunt crop, a repeated grid, or a simple serial variation. Let an ordinary object become monumental, or isolate a face until the printed dots and contour carry as much weight as identity. Composition is usually shallow and poster-like. Type can borrow condensed advertising sans, comic display, supermarket lettering, or a single short headline, but should not compete with the image. When repetition is used, maintain consistent scale and framing so color or process changes become the event.

## Color, material, imagery, and light

Use process-driven cyan, magenta, yellow, black, vivid red, cobalt, and bright paper white. Color may be deliberately unnatural and slightly misregistered. Materials include screen-print ink, cheap magazine paper, painted flat contour, commercial vinyl, and photographic transfer. Imagery should come from ordinary public-facing visual culture or a purpose-built equivalent: food, appliances, cosmetics, printed faces, price bursts, packaging, or comic gestures. Light is mostly flattened into graphic shadow and highlight; glossy product reflections should simplify rather than add luxury realism.

## Common exits

Pop Art becomes Comic Panel when sequential narrative, gutters, and balloons dominate. It becomes Supermarket Packaging when shelf hierarchy and product information serve an actual package. It becomes Sticker Bomb when many outlined adhesive objects overlap. Avoid treating any neon portrait as Pop or centering a living celebrity likeness. Painterly expression, atmospheric depth, ornate historical framing, and muted tasteful palettes weaken the movement's mass-media bluntness.

## Medium adaptation

For identity and campaigns, use one ordinary subject, one reproduction device, and one serial rule. For packaging, separate the Pop treatment from legally required information and avoid counterfeit brand signals. For web and presentations, build bold crop and repeat into image modules while keeping body content plain. Motion can cycle process colors, shift registration, or repeat an object with controlled timing. Put recognizable movement-level approximation high in image-generation direction, followed by the mass-media grammar, print process, original subject, crop, repetition, and palette.
