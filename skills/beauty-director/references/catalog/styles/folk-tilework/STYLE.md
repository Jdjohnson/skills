# Folk Tilework

## Defining grammar

Folk Tilework builds ornament or narrative from glazed ceramic units joined to architecture. Its grammar begins with the module: square painted tiles, cut mosaic pieces, relief units, border strips, or repeated star and polygon forms. Grout and mortar are visible intervals that regulate the field. Glaze pools, edge variation, firing differences, chips, and repair make the wall materially present. Select one named regional practice before choosing motifs, because Islamic geometric tile, Portuguese azulejo, Mexican talavera, and other traditions use different processes, subjects, and cultural settings.

## Composition and type

Let the unit determine scale and repetition. A pattern may extend beyond the crop, turn through a border, concentrate around an opening, or assemble into a multi-tile scene. Geometry requires exact relational order even when individual pieces vary. Figurative traditions may use registers, frames, and repeated corner units to keep a narrative coherent across joints. Typography should remain external unless inscriptions belong to the documented tradition and are accurately written. Avoid placing generic display type over a dense wall or erasing the joint structure to create a seamless digital surface.

## Color, material, imagery, and light

Use mineral and glaze-driven color: cobalt, turquoise, green, manganese, ochre, white, terracotta, or polychrome combinations appropriate to the selected practice. Surfaces can be glossy, crackled, uneven, or relief-molded; light should reveal glaze and architecture without turning the field into polished product photography. Imagery may be geometric, vegetal, calligraphic, heraldic, devotional, domestic, or narrative depending on origin. Keep cut shapes, painted brush variation, and grout width believable.

## Common exits

The style exits into Byzantine Mosaic when tesserae, gold field, monumental sacred figures, and curved church surfaces dominate. It exits into Quilting Pattern when soft pieced cloth and seam rhythm replace fired ceramic. It exits into generic surface design when a tile-like repeat has no unit, grout, glaze, or architectural placement. Mixing star geometry, Mexican florals, Portuguese scenes, and invented script into one “global” wall is a cultural collage, not a coherent style.

## Medium adaptation

For identity and packaging, adapt a border rule, modular proportion, or palette from one documented practice without copying sacred text or community-specific emblems. For web, use tile logic to organize image modules while leaving controls and body copy calm. For interiors, specify real tile size, joints, corners, cuts, and lighting rather than applying a repeating bitmap. Motion may assemble units in laying order or let glaze catch shifting light. When referencing living traditions, name the place and makers, understand whether motifs are religious or identity-bearing, and prefer commissioned or licensed contemporary work for direct cultural expression.
