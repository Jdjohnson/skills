# Astro Chart

## Defining grammar

Astro Chart maps the sky through a defined projection and celestial reference system. Stars occupy measured positions, their marks can encode apparent magnitude, and grids describe right ascension and declination or another declared coordinate frame. Constellation boundaries, figures, ecliptic, celestial equator, poles, horizon, date, and viewing location may appear when appropriate. The style is scientific cartography of the heavens, not astrology, decorative zodiac art, or a cinematic field of randomly scattered stars.

## Composition and type

Select projection and use before designing: a full-sky rectangular map, polar chart, rotating planisphere, seasonal finder, or focused constellation sheet has different geometry. Radial systems should remain centered and calibrated; rectangular maps need clearly labeled axes and controlled seam behavior. Encode star magnitude with consistent dot size or intensity. Use thin grid lines, distinct boundary lines, and labels that avoid obscuring dense star regions. A legend should explain marks, and date or latitude belongs near the title when visibility depends on them.

## Color, material, imagery, and light

Use dark blue or black with white, warm white, or magnitude-tinted stars for night-reading versions; historical sheets may use cream paper, black engraving, and restrained hand color. Keep gradients subordinate to coordinate structure. Constellation figures can appear as faint reference art, but boundaries and measured positions must remain separable from mythic imagery. Avoid bright nebula photography unless it is itself mapped data. Star size should indicate a defined variable rather than visual whim.

## Common exits

The style exits into Cartographic Sheet when terrestrial terrain, roads, water, and boundaries dominate. It exits into Scientific Illustration when one astronomical object is rendered in detail rather than mapped relationally. It exits into Vaporwave or Y2K Cyber when neon, grids, and space imagery become mood rather than measurement. A horoscope wheel, starry poster, planet diagram, or spaceship HUD is not an astro chart without projection, coordinates, catalog relationships, and legend.

## Medium adaptation

For print, test fine lines and labels against the chosen dark or light ground. For interfaces, preserve orientation, coordinate readout, zoom hierarchy, and catalog filtering while preventing label collision. For presentations, explain projection before highlighting one route or constellation. Motion can rotate a planisphere, change the visible sky by date and latitude, or move between coordinate systems, but should retain a stable frame of reference. Use catalog data and authoritative coordinates for factual charts; clearly mark invented skies for fictional settings.
