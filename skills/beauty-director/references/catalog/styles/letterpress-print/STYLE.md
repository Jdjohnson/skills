# Letterpress Print

## Relief as visual grammar

Letterpress Print begins with a raised matrix—metal or wood type, an engraved block, linoleum, or a relief plate—inked and pressed against paper. Its identity comes from that physical encounter. Type, image, ink, paper, pressure, registration, and edition limits all shape the result. Use the direction when the printed artifact should feel deliberate, tactile, and constrained by a real press, not when a digital layout merely adds a grunge texture.

## Composition under constraint

Build from type forms, locked rectangles, woodcut shapes, ornaments, and generous margins. The press bed encourages modular composition, but exact centering is not mandatory. A bold display line can anchor the page while smaller text forms a compact block; image and type can share one ink or overlap through a second pass. Let slight registration drift or uneven inking appear locally. Keep enough clean paper around these effects so they read as process evidence rather than damage.

## Type, ink, and stock

Typography is the subject. Choose faces whose physical mass suits the job: sturdy serif, grotesk, slab, or wood display type with clear counters and substantial strokes. Mix sizes with intention and avoid a random vintage-type sampler. Work with one to three inks, including deep black, oxblood, rust, forest, or indigo on warm white, cotton, or handmade stock. Ink should show crisp edge pressure with occasional bite; blind impression may add relief without color. Raking light can reveal a modest deboss, but an excessively deep “punch” is a contemporary fetish rather than a universal historical requirement.

## Imagery and light

A strong lead should show both the printed face and its tactile construction: a finished sheet angled to catch impression, or an active press bed with inked type and a nearby proof. Close views can teach paper fiber, ink density, or registration; wider views can establish the hand-operated process. Keep hands, rollers, composing sticks, furniture, and quoins secondary to the print itself. Photograph under directional light with accurate neutral color so black ink does not turn glossy or paper tooth disappear.

## Common exits

Avoid Photoshop distress, fake letterpress shadows, neon gradients, smooth offset printing, glossy coated stock, and perfect screen typography pretending to be handmade. Do not default to wedding invitations, sentimental script, or generic maker logos. A historical wanted-poster cliché is not the whole medium. Conversely, fluorescent poster printing can still be letterpress when grounded in actual type, relief, and press process; muted “heritage” color is not a requirement.

## Medium adaptation

For identity and packaging, use a compact set of robust type, limited ink, and stock whose tooth remains visible. Editorial documents can borrow lockup logic, rules, and colophon-like production notes without degrading long text. Digital interfaces should translate pressure into restrained borders, imperfect image edges, and typographic hierarchy while keeping body text clean. In presentation design, one oversized line and one relief image can carry a slide. Motion should show composition, inking, impression, lift, and reveal in a short physical sequence rather than animating synthetic scratches.
