# Modular Poster

## Defining grammar

Modular Poster is a single-surface direction built for immediate public impact. A small number of hard-edged zones carry headline, image, date, place, or call to action, and the relationships among those zones create the composition. Use it when the message must register from across a room or in a quick scroll. The grid is present, but unlike a continuous identity or editorial system, it serves one decisive poster moment.

## Composition and type

Choose the dominant module first. It may be an oversized word, a cropped image, a color block, or a simple geometric form. Build two or three supporting modules around it with clear changes in scale. Allow blocks to bleed beyond the edge and let alignment create tension rather than symmetry. Display type should be bold enough to act as shape, while secondary information stays compact and ordered. Limit the number of sizes and avoid turning every line into a separate event.

## Color, material, imagery, and light

High contrast matters more than a fixed palette. Use a strong ground, one dominant chromatic relationship, and enough tonal separation for the hierarchy to survive at distance. Flat ink, screen print, offset texture, or crisp digital color all work if surfaces remain direct. Photography should be reduced to one forceful crop and treated as a module, not as background atmosphere. Illustration can work when it has a simple silhouette. Lighting within an image should preserve the subject’s shape and contrast rather than produce cinematic mood.

## Common exits

The style becomes International Typographic Style when the poster behaves like one page in a repeatable information system. It becomes Billboard Bold when the read is reduced to one image and a few words for highway distance. It shifts into Swiss Phototypography when the main action is the fusion of giant type and photography. Common failures include too many modules, paragraph-length copy, decorative frames, weak contrast, fake paper distress, and arbitrary geometry that does not support the message.

## Medium adaptation

For print, test the design at full size and as a small thumbnail. For social posts, preserve the dominant module and rebuild the grid for each aspect ratio instead of cropping blindly. In motion, reveal modules in the same hierarchy the eye would read them, then settle into a strong final frame. For event systems, derive related posters from a shared module kit without making every layout identical. On environmental surfaces, use architecture as the edge of the composition. The direction succeeds when the message remains unmistakable after details disappear.
