# Web Brutalism

## Defining grammar

Web brutalism exposes the page as an information system. Default-like controls, visible hierarchy, direct links, plain backgrounds, hard rules, and browser-native behavior replace decorative simulation. Its best work is not careless; it is unusually explicit about what is clickable, current, or important. Use it when speed, authorship, public access, or technical candor should outrank brand polish.

## Composition

Organize around document flow, lists, indexes, tables, and strong sectional breaks. A page may be asymmetrical, but scanning order must remain unmistakable. Allow long text columns only when line length is controlled. Navigation can remain visibly persistent rather than disappearing into icons. Avoid modular marketing-card repetition, excessive centered stacks, and ornamental emptiness that presents minimalism instead of usable information.

## Type

System sans, monospaced faces, browser serifs, and underlined links are valid when roles stay consistent. Let headings announce document structure rather than merely change size. Labels may be terse and technical. Preserve real text and selection behavior. Fake terminal copy, unreadable all-caps paragraphs, novelty cursors, and random font switching substitute costume for the medium’s functional directness.

## Color and material

Begin with black, white, browser blue, or a sharply limited pair of interface colors. Solid fills and one-pixel rules are stronger than atmospheric gradients. Material is the screen itself: pixels, focus rings, scroll behavior, selection, and state changes. Resist paper textures, glass panels, inflated shadows, and skeuomorphic surfaces unless the content genuinely requires them.

## Imagery and light

Use images as records, diagrams, evidence, or blunt interruptions in the document, not as ambient decoration. Native aspect ratios and explicit captions suit the grammar. There is no simulated studio light; contrast comes from interface relationships. For generated references, request a recognizable movement-level early-web information page with original neutral content, visible links, semantic sections, and honest browser behavior.

## Common exits

The style fails when “ugly” becomes permission for broken navigation, poor contrast, or inaccessible interaction. It becomes operational utility when signage and instruction systems dominate beyond the browser. Graphic brutalism shares hard blocks and loud type, but prioritizes graphic confrontation over document behavior. Retro web nostalgia is another exit: pixel icons and obsolete chrome cannot replace a coherent information architecture.

## Medium adaptation

For product interfaces, keep forms native-feeling, states explicit, and errors close to the affected field. Editorial sites can expose archives, authorship, timestamps, and related links without decorative shells. Presentations translate the approach through plain tables, strong headings, and visible citations. Print adaptations should resemble technical documents or web printouts, not screenshots pasted onto paper. Motion is state change—loading, focus, reveal, sorting, expansion—not cinematic transitions layered over a simple document.
