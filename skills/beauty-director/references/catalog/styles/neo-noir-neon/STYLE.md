# Neo-Noir Neon

## Core grammar

Neo-Noir Neon updates noir through contemporary city light: colored practicals, reflective surfaces, surveillance-era distance, and uncertainty after dark. Neon color alone is insufficient. The scene needs human-scale tension, deep shadow, and a narrative question. Use this direction for nocturnal stories, music imagery, mobility campaigns, or interfaces that borrow from cinematic night without becoming a glowing software dashboard.

## Composition and hierarchy

Favor street-level viewpoints, compressed alleys, windshield layers, and figures caught between light sources. Build depth with alternating bands of black, reflected color, and distant signs. A small person or vehicle can anchor scale while remaining partly unreadable. Clean symmetrical skylines feel touristic; crooked sightlines, wet foregrounds, and occluded corners produce more useful tension. Keep generous dark intervals so chroma has something to illuminate.

## Type and graphic language

Type should be contemporary and spare: compact sans caps, narrow labels, or a single precise title block. Let color spill and scene depth do the expressive work. Avoid synthetic glow around every letter, cyberpunk display alphabets, and dense HUD furniture. Those moves collapse photography into Neon Night UI or Y2K Cyber.

## Color, material, imagery, and light

Magenta and cyan are familiar anchors, but electric red, sodium amber, acid green, and violet can work when they originate in believable practical light. Wet asphalt, glass, painted metal, and vehicle surfaces carry reflections. Keep skin tones plausible under mixed sources. Blacks should retain small amounts of texture rather than becoming empty digital cutouts. Rain, steam, haze, and flare are useful only when they reveal light paths or depth.

## Use across media

In motion, let colored sources enter and leave the frame as the subject moves. Photography should privilege environment over posed nightlife portraits. For web, decks, and editorial layouts, use one cinematic image with disciplined dark framing and limited accent color. Packaging can borrow reflective gradients and night photography, but it should not simulate trademarked signs or recognizable branded districts.

## Common exits

Bright entertainment districts without danger read as tourism. Smooth magenta-cyan gradients without real space become interface styling. Excess fog erases the city and moves toward Fog Atmosphere. Classic monochrome crime framing belongs to Film Noir. Avoid gratuitous foreign-script signs used as futuristic texture; place and language should be contextual rather than exotic decoration.

## Direction recipe

Shoot or construct a quiet street after rain. Hold the camera near pedestrian height, place the subject beyond the brightest reflection, and preserve several deep-black planes. Use two differently colored practical sources, one reflective foreground, and a withheld destination. The result should feel like a story already underway.
