# Field Guide

## Defining grammar

Field Guide is a portable identification system, not simply a book about nature. It helps a reader distinguish similar organisms, objects, or phenomena under practical conditions. Build each page around diagnostic comparison: observed specimens, field marks, measurements, habitat, range, season, behavior, and concise notes. Illustrations or photographs should normalize angle and scale enough to compare while preserving the features that separate one entry from another. The visual standard is usefulness at the moment of recognition.

## Composition and type

Organize entries into repeated modules with a stable location for common name, scientific name, image, scale, description, range, and status. Place similar species near one another and use arrows, brackets, silhouettes, inset details, or small keys to point at diagnostic structures. A spread may lead with one family overview and then descend into species rows. Typography should be compact, durable, and highly legible; use weight and size for hierarchy rather than decorative display faces. Keep captions attached to their images and make page numbers, tabs, or color bands support rapid lookup.

## Color, material, imagery, and light

Use accurate restrained color that preserves relevant distinctions in plumage, leaf, shell, fruit, mineral, cloud, track, or other subject matter. Neutral ground and even illumination make comparison easier. Line drawings can isolate structure; photographs can show texture and habitat; range maps can encode distribution with one or two clear fills. Paper may be matte, weather-tolerant, and slightly warm, but avoid heavy vintage distress. Every image should answer an identification question rather than decorate the page.

## Common exits

The style exits into Botanical Plate or Anatomical Plate when one specimen and its detailed internal or diagnostic parts dominate. It exits into Specimen Label when the compact institutional record attached to a collected object becomes primary. It exits into Academic Journal when argument, citation, and continuous research prose replace field lookup. A nature coffee-table spread, wildlife poster, or gallery of unrelated photographs is not a field guide without comparative identification behavior.

## Medium adaptation

For apps, preserve the repeated record, comparison view, filters, and diagnostic callouts while adding search and location responsibly. For presentations, build one comparison problem per slide and align the specimens consistently. For packaging or identity, borrow tab systems, compact labels, and measured illustration without implying scientific authority the content does not possess. Motion can move from family to species, reveal a diagnostic difference, or update range by season. Test at actual handheld size: names, scale, and the decisive visual distinction must remain readable without zoom.
