# News Broadsheet

## Defining grammar

News Broadsheet organizes many current facts under time pressure while making editorial priority visible. Its grammar includes an authoritative masthead, multi-deck headlines, dense columns, captioned documentary imagery, modular story zones, and paper that records the speed of printing. Use it when the visual should communicate public consequence and newsroom hierarchy, not merely old-paper nostalgia.

## Composition and type

Build the page from lead story outward. The masthead establishes identity; the main headline, deck, byline, photograph, and caption create the first reading path. Secondary stories should have clear but subordinate entry points. Narrow columns permit efficient scanning, while rules and gutters separate material without turning the page into a spreadsheet. Use a hardworking serif for text and decisive headline faces with enough range for urgency. Alignment may appear busy, but it must remain controlled.

## Color, material, imagery, and light

Black ink on warm newsprint is the core material signal. One spot color or a restrained color photograph can mark priority, but a glossy full-spectrum palette shifts the tone toward a magazine. Halftone texture should arise from reproduction rather than a decorative filter. Photographs need documentary relevance, readable action, and captions that identify what is shown. Favor available or direct light that preserves event truth; cinematic relighting undermines the news contract. Maps and small information graphics can support a story when sourced and labeled.

## Common exits

The node becomes Literary Journal when slow prose and generous margins replace urgency. It becomes Magazine Feature when one narrative package receives luxurious pacing, and Photo Essay when image sequence carries the argument with little text. Common failures include showing only an archival title page, omitting captions, using fake headlines as texture, building an unreadable wall of columns, or confusing any newspaper scan with a strong broadsheet system. Density must reveal importance rather than conceal it.

## Medium adaptation

For print, test the full front page and an interior news page at real size. For websites, translate the hierarchy into lead, live, local, analysis, and secondary modules without reproducing narrow columns literally. Mobile layouts should maintain story priority and clear provenance as modules stack. In motion or broadcast graphics, use headline, timestamp, source, and image with similar discipline. Social cards should identify the publication and story plainly instead of manufacturing sensational clickbait. Across media, corrections, updates, and time state are part of the visual system.
