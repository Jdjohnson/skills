# Literary Journal

## Defining grammar

A Literary Journal is a reading-first periodical for essays, fiction, criticism, and poetry. Its visual authority comes from restraint: careful type, generous margins, modest navigation, and pacing that lets long-form work accumulate meaning. Use it when the publication should feel edited and durable without becoming scholarly apparatus or commercial magazine spectacle. Covers may be expressive, but the interior system is the true node.

## Composition and type

Begin with prose measure, leading, and page proportion. Choose one text face that remains comfortable over many pages, then add a restrained companion for titles, author lines, folios, and running heads. Flush-left ragged-right settings suit contemporary work; justified text can work when spacing and hyphenation are excellent. Give openings more air than continuations, but avoid oversized title treatments that consume the reading experience. Section changes should be discoverable through interval, small rules, color, or concise labels.

## Color, material, imagery, and light

Warm white, black, muted ink, and one quiet accent support the genre. Paper texture may be visible, especially in small-press work, but distress should never become nostalgia theater. Photography and illustration are selective: one image can open an issue, mark a portfolio, or create a pause, while constant decorative imagery pulls attention from the writing. Favor observational, archival, or art-led visuals with captions and clear editorial purpose. Lighting in photographed material should feel natural and contemplative rather than campaign-polished.

## Common exits

The node becomes Magazine Feature when pull quotes, dramatic openers, and commercial pacing dominate. It becomes Academic Journal when abstracts, citations, figures, and formal apparatus organize the page, and Culture Review when interviews, reviews, art sections, and a stronger publication brand create a broader editorial mix. Common failures include judging the style from covers alone, using contents pages as the primary reference, treating white space as emptiness, or making every article visually identical.

## Medium adaptation

For print, test real essays, short pieces, poems, footnotes, and contributor notes before locking the grid. For web, preserve readable measure, section rhythm, and issue identity while adding search and responsive navigation. For newsletters, carry the hierarchy and tone without pretending an email is a printed spread. Social excerpts should present a complete thought and a simple issue marker rather than turning the journal into quote graphics. Across media, typography should support sustained attention and the editorial voice should remain stronger than decoration.
