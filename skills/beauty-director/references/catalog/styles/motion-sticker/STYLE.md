# Motion Sticker

## Core grammar

Motion Sticker gives a sticker-like character or object kinetic life. Bold contour, squash and stretch, anticipation, motion arcs, repeated poses, speed accents, and cutout bounce must make movement visible even in a still reference. Use it for reactions, social animation, onboarding, youth campaigns, event graphics, and micro-interactions that need immediate expressive energy.

## Composition and hierarchy

Design the movement path before the decorative details. A three-pose strip can show anticipation, contact, and release; a single frame can use a strong arc, echo pose, or trailing mark. Keep the main silhouette readable throughout deformation. Preserve volume as the form squashes or stretches. Leave directional space in front of the action so the motion does not collide with the frame.

## Type and graphic language

Short exclamations, impact words, and compact labels may join the action if they share the outline and timing. Type should react to motion without becoming illegible. Avoid filling the frame with comic sound effects when one arc or pose sequence would communicate better. The style is sticker-like, but it should not copy a messaging platform’s proprietary pack.

## Color, material, imagery, and light

Use flat bold color, a consistent outer contour, and limited shading so deformation remains obvious. White or light offset can preserve the sticker silhouette. Small shadows may imply a lifted cutout, but deep rendered space is unnecessary. Motion lines should follow the actual arc and diminish coherently; arbitrary radial streaks create noise rather than force.

## Use across media

Animated formats should set timing, spacing, anticipation, overshoot, and settle deliberately. Static packages need sprite strips or multiple poses because one neutral icon cannot prove motion. Social and web uses should respect reduced-motion settings and offer a still state that preserves meaning. Physical stickers can show sequential poses across a short strip.

## Common exits

A static outlined icon belongs to a sticker family, not Motion Sticker. A full backing sheet becomes Sticker Sheet System. Photographic blur lacks the drawn deformation grammar. Constant looping without anticipation or settle feels mechanical, and excessive squash can destroy character identity.

## Direction recipe

Choose one simple action and thumbnail its line of motion. Draw anticipation, impact, and recovery poses, keeping volume consistent while shape changes. Add one motion arc and only the speed marks needed to clarify direction. Test the sequence at small scale, then animate with a clear settle rather than endless jitter.
