# Kids Picture Book

## Core grammar

Kids Picture Book is a sequence of illustrated spreads built around character, story beats, image-text pacing, and anticipation at the page turn. A friendly standalone drawing is not enough. Use this direction for child-centered publishing, learning materials, family campaigns, narrative presentations, and interfaces that benefit from clear emotional progression.

## Composition and hierarchy

Design the spread, not just the page. Account for the gutter, reading direction, and the relationship between left-page setup and right-page consequence. Keep one primary action legible from a distance, then reward closer looking with a recurring object or environmental clue. Child-eye-level viewpoints and generous shapes often improve accessibility. Preserve a calm text zone rather than placing copy over every available surface.

## Type and graphic language

Large friendly type, open counters, short line lengths, and clear contrast support emerging readers. Hand lettering can add voice if consistency and legibility survive. Distinguish narrator, dialogue, and sound effects through scale or placement instead of many unrelated fonts. The title should feel part of the illustrated world without becoming retail package branding.

## Color, material, imagery, and light

Warm characters, limited but expressive palettes, and visible material texture can create intimacy. Watercolor, gouache, colored pencil, cut paper, or clean digital paint all work when the method remains coherent across spreads. Light should clarify time, mood, and focus without cinematic over-modeling. Use color changes across the sequence to signal emotional development.

## Use across media

Print requires trim, bleed, safe text, gutter, and page-turn testing. Motion adaptation should retain illustrated staging and reveal rather than add constant camera moves. Presentation or web stories can map each spread to a screen, but scrolling must preserve beat boundaries. Educational uses should keep decorative detail subordinate to the intended concept.

## Common exits

A single mascot pose is character design, not a picture book. A dense cover with badges becomes Board Game Box or toy packaging. Adult magazine pacing, small type, and ironic visual ambiguity work against the child-centered premise. Avoid borrowing culturally specific clothing or settings as generic whimsy without grounded context.

## Direction recipe

Write one sentence describing the beat, then thumbnail a two-page spread around it. Place the character action first, reserve a readable text shape, and introduce one clue near the outer right edge. Test the spread at child-held scale and ask whether the next page feels necessary.
