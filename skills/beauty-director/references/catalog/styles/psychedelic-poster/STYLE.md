# Psychedelic Poster

## Defining grammar

Psychedelic Poster is the countercultural concert-poster language that emerged prominently in 1960s San Francisco and related scenes. Warped hand lettering, saturated complementary color, optical vibration, dense illustration, and figure-ground ambiguity ask the viewer to linger and decipher. Art Nouveau curves may be recycled into more unstable forms; faces, flowers, waves, flames, and ornamental fields merge with the words. The style should feel immersive and slightly resistant to immediate reading, while still functioning as an event poster.

## Composition and type

Treat lettering as image. Words may swell, bend, mirror, or fill negative space, but the event information needs a deliberate reading path. Use a central figure or mandala-like mass, tightly framed by type and pattern, or let the entire field become one interlocked surface. Horror vacui is appropriate, yet maintain internal rhythm through repeated curves and nested shapes. Avoid dropping an ordinary font onto a colorful background. Supporting venue and date copy can be clearer than the primary title.

## Color, material, imagery, and light

Use vibrating complements—red/green, orange/blue, magenta/cyan—plus black, cream, or fluorescent accents. Adjacent colors may be equal in value so edges shimmer. Screen-print or offset texture, drawn contour, overprint, and slight registration shifts reinforce the poster medium. Imagery may include original musicians, symbolic figures, botanical forms, eyes, hands, cosmic shapes, or urban counterculture cues without copying a known poster. Light is graphic and internally colored rather than photographic.

## Common exits

The style becomes Art Nouveau when lettering and ornament settle into elegant fin-de-siècle clarity. It becomes Vaporwave when digital gradients, grids, and 1980s–90s nostalgia take over. It becomes Carnival Poster when the hierarchy is loud but straightforward. Avoid generic tie-dye, rainbow gradients, drug clichés, copied band marks, or direct imitation of a living poster artist. Illegibility must be intentional and bounded; critical event details should still be recoverable.

## Medium adaptation

For posters and campaigns, build one custom-lettered title and one dense image field, then extract simpler supporting assets. For packaging, use optical color and warped forms while keeping regulatory information separate. For web, reserve the immersive field for hero moments and provide accessible text outside the image. Motion can pulse color relationships, slowly warp lettering, or reveal nested imagery without causing unsafe flicker. Image generation should specify an original fictional event, hand-drawn distorted type, 1960s print process, vibrating complements, dense integration, and no existing band or poster composition.
