# Magazine Feature

Magazine feature is the visual language of a substantial reported story designed to earn attention before it asks for sustained reading. It belongs to profiles, criticism, service journalism, travel stories, and narrative packages that need more theatre than a literary journal but more continuity than a campaign. Its signature is an unmistakable opener followed by disciplined continuation pages: the first spread announces a subject and point of view, while later pages settle into a readable cadence.

## Defining grammar

Build the opener around one dominant photograph, a decisive headline, a short deck, byline, and a clear entry into the article. Scale contrast should be bold without becoming poster design. A useful feature sequence alternates expansive image moments with denser reading moments, using pull quotes, sidebars, captions, and folios to vary pace. Every device must serve the story; decorative editorial furniture with no narrative job makes the result feel synthetic.

## Composition and type

Use a stable multi-column grid, then break it deliberately at the opener or a major section turn. Body text should remain continuous enough for long reading. Display typography can be expressive, but the deck, byline, captions, and running elements need a coherent hierarchy that survives beyond one spread. Pull quotes are secondary invitations, not competing headlines. On small screens, preserve the sequence of headline, deck, byline, lead image, and opening paragraphs instead of compressing everything into a generic banner.

## Color, material, imagery, and light

Let the subject determine the photographic treatment. Portraits may be staged, environmental, or candid, but they should communicate character rather than merely supply visual texture. Use a restrained base palette and one editorial accent that can organize section markers or rules. Paper grain, halftone, or rich coated color may appear when they support the publication's voice. Lighting should look art-directed enough to hold a spread while retaining believable skin, objects, and locations.

## Common exits

The style fails when the opener has no continuation system, when every page competes at maximum volume, or when stock imagery carries a story it cannot substantiate. It also fails when long copy is treated like a decorative block, captions are omitted, or pull quotes repeat the headline's job. Fashion editorial is more image-sovereign and less accountable to prose; news broadsheet is denser and more urgent; literary journal is quieter and less promotional.

## Medium adaptation

For websites, translate spreads into paced scroll chapters with clear reading width and intentional image breaks. For presentations, show two or three connected spreads so the system is visible, not just a cover. For social crops, retain the feature's headline and photographic point of view without pretending the crop is the full story. For motion, use restrained sequencing, slow reframing, and typographic reveals that echo page turns. The hero should show a complete feature opener alongside one continuation spread so narrative hierarchy, image-text pacing, and the publication system are all legible at once.
