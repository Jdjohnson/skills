# Carnival Poster

## Defining grammar

Carnival Poster is a loud public promise built from wood-type scale, saturated circus inks, hyperbolic illustration, attraction hierarchy, and showman copy. It announces acts, dates, spectacle, and rarity with little hesitation. A central performer, animal, ride, or stunt carries the pitch while banners, bursts, and secondary scenes stack around it. The density should feel intentional and sale-driven, not like random vintage clutter.

## Composition and type

Lead with the attraction name or the single most spectacular image. Use a strong top banner, central scene, and lower information block, or a radial arrangement that still preserves event, date, and place. Display type may be slab, tuscan, shadowed, arched, condensed, or outlined, but each effect needs a role. Supporting copy should form short, emphatic units. The hierarchy may be crowded, yet a viewer should know the show before reading every claim.

## Color, material, imagery, and light

Use red, yellow, blue, green, black, and paper cream in bold printed fields. Allow halftone, coarse lithographic texture, overprint, and age, but keep the main figure and headline crisp. Imagery may include acrobats, riders, animals, rides, sideshow facades, banners, or impossible feats when historically and ethically appropriate. Graphic light is often painted into the illustration through sharp highlights and theatrical rays rather than photographed realistically.

## Common exits

The style becomes Belle Époque Lithograph when one elegant figure, integrated hand-lettering, and flatter urban color dominate. It becomes Billboard Bold when everything reduces to one image and a few words. It becomes Victorian Ephemera when multiple small cards replace the single event poster. Avoid using demeaning historic sideshow language, racial caricature, animal cruelty, or disability as spectacle. Generic distressed type on red paper is not enough; the poster must visibly organize an entertainment pitch.

## Medium adaptation

For event campaigns, create one master poster hierarchy that can collapse into tickets, banners, and social crops without losing show, date, or place. For packaging, use attraction bands and wood-type energy around an original product story. For web and presentations, let the hero carry density and simplify interior pages. Motion can reveal acts in sequence, swing banners into place, or flash attraction bursts with restraint. Image generation should specify an original event, act hierarchy, print process, legible invented copy, and ethical contemporary subject matter rather than copying a historic circus brand.
