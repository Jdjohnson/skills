# Engineering Blueprint

## Defining grammar

Engineering Blueprint is a reproducible working drawing whose lines, dimensions, symbols, and notes tell someone how a structure, machine, or component is built. Historical cyanotype turns the original dark line into white line on a blue ground; the visual style should retain that negative-print logic without confusing it with neon wireframes. Plans, elevations, sections, details, schedules, material notes, scales, and title blocks form a coordinated sheet set. Technical information must be plausible, internally related, and legible.

## Composition and type

Lay out the sheet from general to specific. A principal plan or elevation receives the largest area; sections and details align nearby; dimension strings sit outside the object; notes and schedules occupy controlled columns. Keep consistent line weights for cut, outline, hidden, center, dimension, and hatch information. The title block records sheet title, project, scale, date, number, and revision. Technical lettering should be compact and neutral. Do not scatter invented equations or labels merely to make the page appear complex, and do not crop away the sheet identity in the lead image.

## Color, material, imagery, and light

Use deep Prussian or cyanotype blue with white or pale blue lines, plus slight exposure variation, fold, edge wear, or annotation only when appropriate to a real historical sheet. Current negative-print interpretations may be clean, but they should preserve the hierarchy of line weight and measured notation. Orthographic views dominate; restrained axonometric or exploded details may assist explanation. Avoid glossy gradients, luminous bloom, black cyber backgrounds, and decorative grid overlays that turn construction information into science-fiction interface art.

## Common exits

The style exits into Circuit Schematic when electrical function and standardized component symbols replace physical dimensions. It exits into Lab Notebook when dated experiments, calculations, crossings-out, and evolving thought replace finalized working information. It exits into Architectural Monograph or design presentation when rendered atmosphere and narrative outweigh buildable documentation. Any blue background with fine white lines is not a blueprint without scale, dimensions, coordinated views, and sheet order.

## Medium adaptation

For editorial or identity, use a small coherent drawing excerpt together with authentic scale, dimension, and title behavior. For presentations, reveal plan, section, and detail progressively but keep their relative positions understandable. For web, offer full-sheet overview and zoom rather than forcing tiny technical copy into a card. Motion can move from whole assembly to section cut to detail or show a cyanotype exposure developing. Validate any real engineering content with qualified practitioners; when the drawing is illustrative, clearly avoid implying construction approval or professional certification.
