# Arts and Crafts

## The grammar

Arts and Crafts is an integrated design system, not a rustic filter. Its character comes from the agreement between object, surface, ornament, and method: timber reads as timber, joinery helps explain construction, plant forms become ordered repeat patterns, and useful things receive careful workmanship. The movement can appear in a room, chair, book, textile, tile, package, or identity, but the work should always reveal an intelligence of making. One floral wallpaper crop is not enough; combine pattern with structure, material, or print craft so the broader system is unmistakable.

## Composition and pattern

Build layouts from sturdy modules rather than a glassy modernist grid. Botanical repeats may be dense, but they need clear rhythm, interlocking positive and negative shapes, and a visible repeat logic. Give headings, panels, borders, and images the proportion of joined parts: each section should feel fitted rather than floated. Asymmetry is welcome when the masses balance. Avoid a centered wreath, isolated sprig, or ornamental frame that reduces the direction to heritage stationery.

## Type, color, and material

Choose a warm humanist serif, a robust old-style face, or a restrained slab with substantial strokes. Type can carry a slightly compact private-press cadence, but should remain readable and avoid faux-medieval novelty. Let paper tooth, wood grain, woven fiber, hammered metal, block-print variation, or glazed tile supply texture. Color should feel mineral, vegetable, and grounded: leaf green, madder or plum, ochre, indigo, parchment, and oak. Use color in purposeful roles rather than as an indiscriminate sepia wash.

## Imagery and light

The best lead image shows more than decoration: an interior where furniture, textile, and surface pattern belong together; a well-joined oak object beside a printed page; or a broad applied-design view that makes craft and domestic use legible. Photograph materials with gentle side light that catches carving, weave, and joinery without turning everything into dramatic luxury. For generated imagery, emphasize integrated botanical pattern, honest wood construction, hand-process variation, and a complete usable setting.

## Common exits

The style fails when it becomes cottagecore, generic botanical watercolor, dark Victorian clutter, fantasy medievalism, or mass-market faux distress. Chrome, slick acrylic, artificial woodgrain, and perfect vector vines erase the material argument. Do not make ornament more important than the object carrying it, and do not confuse Arts and Crafts with Art Nouveau's whiplash line or Shaker austerity.

## Adapting the direction

For identity and packaging, use a limited repeat, substantial typography, tactile stock, and one construction detail instead of covering every surface. On websites, translate joinery into clear section relationships and use texture sparingly behind accessible type. In presentations and documents, let botanical dividers, warm paper grounds, and block-like hierarchy create continuity. For interiors and products, prioritize genuine materials, visible joints, useful proportions, and coordinated applied pattern. In motion, reveal the repeat being built, printed, woven, or assembled rather than animating leaves as decorative confetti.
