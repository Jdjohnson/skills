# Illuminated Manuscript

## Defining grammar

Illuminated Manuscript is a page system in which writing, decorated initials, border ornament, miniature images, and precious material are integrated. Parchment or paper remains visibly structural: columns, ruling, margins, and text blocks organize the page before ornament enters. Gold leaf, tempera color, historiated initials, marginal figures, and framed miniatures create emphasis according to the book's function. There is no single universal manuscript look. Armenian, Ethiopian, Islamic, Jewish, Insular, Byzantine, and Western European traditions differ in script, page architecture, imagery, theology, and ornament; use one documented tradition at a time.

## Composition and type

Begin with a credible page grid: one or two text columns, measured margins, line spacing, and a clear reading order. Use an enlarged initial or miniature as a structural interruption, not as a floating decoration. Borders may be dense, but they should respond to the page's edges and text. Typography must not be a fantasy-medieval mashup. When actual historical scripts are inappropriate or unreadable, pair a restrained contemporary serif with an ornamental initial inspired by the chosen tradition. Never invent sacred text, pseudo-calligraphy, or decorative glyphs that could be mistaken for a real language.

## Color, material, imagery, and light

Use parchment cream, mineral reds and blues, carbon black, verdigris or green earth, and metallic gold or silver according to evidence. Let pigment sit as opaque color rather than modern gradient. Gold should catch angled light and change with the viewing position; it is material, not yellow fill. Imagery may include narrative miniatures, devotional figures, flora, fauna, diagrams, or marginalia only when grounded in the chosen book culture. Keep surface wear, ruling, binding edge, and pigment variation subtle and credible.

## Common exits

The style fails when it becomes a generic fantasy game interface, mixes scripts and religious symbols, or treats sacred imagery as decorative texture. It exits into Byzantine Mosaic when tesserae, monumental placement, and architectural gold ground replace the portable page. It exits into Victorian Ephemera when chromolithographic scraps and advertising typography replace manuscript structure. Avoid random dragons, faux Latin, glowing runes, and excessive jewel effects. Cultural and religious content must be named, sourced, and used respectfully.

## Medium adaptation

For editorial and presentation work, borrow the page hierarchy, initial, border, and material accents while keeping modern text accessible. For identity and packaging, use one documented ornamental system and avoid sacred iconography unless the context warrants it. For web, treat illumination as a section-opening device rather than coating every control. For motion, reveal layers as a scribe or illuminator might build them—ruling, script, pigment, then gold. Image generation prompts should name the manuscript tradition, approximate date, page function, materials, and script treatment, and should not blend unrelated traditions.
