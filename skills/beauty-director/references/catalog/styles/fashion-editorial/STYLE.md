# Fashion Editorial

## Defining grammar

Fashion Editorial builds an image-led story through styling, casting, photography, sequence, and page composition. It does more than document clothes: it creates a point of view around silhouette, gesture, setting, identity, or cultural mood. Use it when a publication or campaign needs controlled visual tension and deliberate art direction. The node is photographic and contemporary; historical costume plates belong to another tradition.

## Composition and type

Plan the story as a sequence of looks rather than isolated portraits. Vary full figure, close detail, environmental frame, and unexpected crop while holding a coherent visual argument. Controlled asymmetry lets the figure press against an edge, cross the gutter, or face into open space. Type remains sparse and confident: a short title, credits, captions, and occasional display gesture. It should not crowd the clothes or imitate a retail catalog. Use the spread, not the single page, as the compositional unit.

## Color, material, imagery, and light

Palette begins with the garments, skin, location, and production concept. It may be monochrome, saturated, muted, or metallic, but color grading must unite the series. Paper and screen finish should support the photographic treatment; excessive gloss is not automatically luxurious. Light can be hard and graphic, soft and atmospheric, or direct and documentary when the choice is consistent with the story. Preserve fabric texture, silhouette, and body detail. Retouching should not erase material reality or turn people into plastic surfaces.

## Common exits

The style becomes a Couture Lookbook when product sequence and collection completeness outrank narrative tension. It becomes Magazine Feature when reporting and text hierarchy carry equal weight, and a fashion plate when illustration records a garment apart from an art-directed photographic world. Frequent failures include generic lifestyle imagery, unrelated looks, gratuitous sensuality, excessive props, illegible type over faces, and using luxury signals without an editorial idea. Styling alone does not create a story.

## Medium adaptation

For print, edit contact sheets against the full page sequence and test gutter placement before final crops. For responsive web, design alternate portrait and landscape crops while preserving order and credits. In motion, extend the same casting, light, and styling concept through gesture and pacing rather than assembling behind-the-scenes fragments. For social formats, publish a deliberate mini-sequence instead of one disconnected hero. In presentations, show references for silhouette, light, location, and layout separately so the production team can execute the shared direction.
