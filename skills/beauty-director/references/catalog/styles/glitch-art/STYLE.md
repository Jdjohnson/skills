# Glitch Art

## Defining grammar

Glitch Art makes the behavior of a failing or deliberately misread signal visible. Compression blocks, channel offsets, scan-line displacement, tearing, duplicated frames, datamoshing, dropped data, and broken color become the composition. The result should suggest a specific media process, even when it is simulated. A random distressed overlay or a clean picture split into red and cyan copies captures only one superficial cue.

Start from an image, video, waveform, or display system whose information can visibly rupture. Use a recognizable movement-level approximation grounded in digital artifact behavior, then build an original subject and failure pattern. Preserve enough source information for the viewer to register what has been interrupted.

## Composition and type

Let the error reorganize hierarchy. A horizontal tear can cut through a focal face, a compression field can erase a quiet region, or delayed frames can pull movement into repeated bands. Use one dominant failure mechanism and one secondary artifact; applying every effect produces decorative noise. Irregular alignment is useful, but retain a visual anchor so the piece does not become undifferentiated texture.

Type can fragment with the same process as the image, yet essential words should remain recoverable. Favor sturdy sans or monospace forms that survive displacement. Offset channels, missing rows, and duplicated glyph fragments are more credible than novelty distortion fonts. Keep ordinary text outside the corrupted zone when comprehension matters.

## Color, material, imagery, and light

RGB primaries, electric cyan, hot red, saturated blue, and harsh white often emerge naturally from channel separation. Pair them with black, video gray, or remnants of the source palette. Do not add neon simply because the subject is digital; color should follow the chosen error model.

The material can be raster data, analog video, CRT scan behavior, corrupted JPEG structure, or encoded motion. Imagery with a clear silhouette or face gives the interruption consequence, while abstract geometry makes the process easier to inspect. Light is usually emitted or electronically clipped. Physical monitor stacks can supply context when their screens visibly fail.

## Common exits

The style becomes Vaporwave when pastel nostalgia and smooth drift replace rupture. It becomes Y2K Cyber when chrome optimism and glossy products dominate. It becomes generic cyberpunk when the image is merely dark, neon, and futuristic. Avoid scratch textures, torn paper, film burns, and halftone unless the chosen failure genuinely crosses media. A polished app screen with one offset headline is an accent, not a full Glitch Art direction.

## Medium adaptation

For still images, preserve crisp artifact edges and export without compression settings that accidentally soften or overwrite the intended damage. Motion can use datamoshing, frame persistence, sync loss, and short bursts of tearing; include calm intervals so each break reads. Websites should confine active distortion to bounded media and respect reduced-motion preferences. Identity systems need an intact master mark plus controlled corrupted variants, never a logo that is illegible in every context. Print can translate scan bands and channel offsets through separations, overprint, or misregistration. Presentations should use the effect for moments of interruption rather than all body content. Audio-reactive installations can link visible faults to signal events, but photos of equipment alone do not establish the visual language.
