# Dada Collage

## Defining grammar

Dada collage assembles found print, photography, typography, mechanical fragments, and ordinary matter through discontinuity, chance, satire, and conceptual collision. The result questions stable meaning rather than simply creating a lively mood board. Use it for cultural work, critique, absurdist editorial, or campaigns that benefit from deliberate incompatibility. Historical recognition comes from the operation, not copied artifacts.

## Composition

Combine fragments with dramatic changes of scale, impossible adjacency, rotated axes, torn silhouettes, and areas of surprising emptiness. A pasted face may meet a machine part or bureaucratic stamp, but one tension should hold the field together. Allow chance to generate options, then edit decisively. Avoid uniformly filling the page; relentless density removes the shock produced by an unexpected relationship.

## Type

Mix newspaper columns, display fragments, captions, numerals, and mechanical lettering as physical pieces. Text can interrupt grammar, expose source hierarchy, or become an image. Preserve enough legibility for the collision to register. Do not rely on contemporary cutout-font presets, random word salad, or borrowed manifestos. Original language and neutral archival fragments keep the reference instructive without imitating a particular protected composition.

## Color and material

Newsprint, aged white, ink black, photographic gray, and one or two printed colors provide the base. Introduce paper edges, paste shadows, fold wear, stamp ink, and halftone screens according to the fragment’s source. Material variety matters more than simulated dirt. Avoid seamless digital blending, luxurious finish, or a uniform sepia wash that collapses distinct pieces into nostalgia.

## Imagery and light

Use anonymous portraits, machines, diagrams, commodity packaging without marks, maps, hands, eyes, and civic documents as rearrangeable matter. Flat scanner light preserves their objecthood; cast shadows may reveal actual layers. Generated work should specify a recognizable movement-level Dada photomontage made from original invented ephemera, irrational but purposeful juxtaposition, and clearly separate paper sources.

## Common exits

It becomes punk zine when direct subcultural publishing and urgent calls to action lead. It becomes ordinary editorial collage when fragments merely illustrate one theme, or surrealism when a seamless dream image replaces visible assembly. Generic retro scrapbook treatment is another exit. Maintain conceptual abrasion, heterogeneous sources, and an element of play that destabilizes the first obvious reading.

## Medium adaptation

Posters can concentrate on one impossible composite and a small set of typographic intrusions. Editorial sequences may let an object recur in changing relationships. Websites should use layered fragments without blocking navigation or degrading text selection. Packaging can distribute a composition across faces so assembly occurs in handling. Motion may reveal cuts, substitutions, and scale jumps like live montage, but should not smooth the pieces into cinematic realism or default parallax.
