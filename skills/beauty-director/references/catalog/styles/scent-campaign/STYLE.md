# Scent Campaign

## Defining grammar

Scent campaign design translates an invisible sensory proposition into object, atmosphere, material association, and ritual. The bottle remains a key piece of evidence, while color, condensation, smoke, botanical matter, fabric, or landscape suggest notes and emotional temperature. Use it when desire and memory must lead, but keep enough product truth for the work to remain a campaign rather than an abstract film still.

## Composition

Build around one flacon, cap, gesture, or sensorial event. The object may be centered ceremonially or offset within a large atmospheric field. Use layers and reflections to imply depth while keeping the silhouette recognizable. Avoid scattering many ingredients like a recipe chart, hiding the product completely, or using a generic centered pack shot with decorative vapor added as an afterthought.

## Type

Typography is usually sparse: name, concentration, note family, and a short evocative line. A refined serif, precise sans, or distinctive custom wordmark can work if it does not imitate a known fragrance house. Ensure regulatory and product information remains legible in practical applications. Avoid excessive letter spacing, ornamental scripts, and vague poetry that could describe any luxury object.

## Color and material

Glass, lacquer, metal, liquid, stone, petal, wood, resin, and fabric create the sensory vocabulary. Palettes may be translucent, nocturnal, mineral, botanical, or sun-warmed, but each should correspond to the scent concept. Control refraction and edge highlights. Avoid indiscriminate gold, synthetic glitter, rainbow flares, and impossible liquid behavior that makes the vessel feel digitally hollow.

## Imagery and light

Use macro bottle studies, hands applying fragrance, original still lifes, and environments that express temperature or memory. Backlight reveals glass and liquid; broad side light describes label and cap; hard beams can create a ritual moment. Generated references should request recognizable movement-level fragrance advertising with an original unbranded flacon, physically plausible materials, a specific sensory atmosphere, and a legible relationship between vessel and notes.

## Common exits

The style becomes jewelry vitrine when close inspection and precious setting dominate, silk editorial when fabric and fashion pacing displace the fragrance object, or quiet luxury when sensory atmosphere is reduced to general restraint. It fails when ingredients are literal decoration or when mist substitutes for a concept. The viewer should sense a distinct character and still know what object carries it.

## Medium adaptation

Campaign systems need a lead image, product truth view, ingredient or atmosphere details, and practical retail crops. Ecommerce pages should balance desire with size, concentration, notes, and packaging information. Presentations can sequence concept, vessel, material palette, and touchpoints. Physical sampling should align paper and scent experience. Motion may use evaporation, light travel, fabric response, or a measured pour; generic slow-motion particles quickly become interchangeable luxury shorthand.
