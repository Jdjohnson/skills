# Research Quality

Treat search results and source summaries as leads until the supporting evidence
is checked.

## Keep a finding when it

- answers or materially reframes an important question;
- has evidence that directly supports the claim;
- strengthens, corrects, or meaningfully qualifies the current understanding;
- opens a valuable new question with a clear reason it matters.

Do not count repeated searches, reworded claims, unsupported speculation, or
source volume as progress.

## Evidence rules

- Prefer primary sources for consequential claims.
- Use credible secondary sources for context and discovery.
- Cite each material finding near the claim.
- Label inference and uncertainty.
- Preserve material disagreement between sources.
- Recheck current facts, prices, policies, and other time-sensitive claims.
- Capture only the excerpt or detail needed to support the finding.
- Never copy a protected work in full.

## Privacy

- Never put credentials in searches or prompts.
- Keep private-source descriptions out of public-facing summaries when
  disclosure is not authorized.
