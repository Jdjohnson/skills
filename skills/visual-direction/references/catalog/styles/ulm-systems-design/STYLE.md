# Ulm Systems Design

## Defining grammar

Ulm Systems Design treats a product, identity, or information problem as a family of related decisions. Use it when the work should communicate rational construction, serial compatibility, technical clarity, and social usefulness. The visual result is restrained because every dimension, component, symbol, and material has a role. A single beautiful object is insufficient; the node becomes visible through relationships among multiple objects or information units.

## Composition and type

Organize information on a strict grid with repeated views, aligned dimensions, and consistent numbering. Show families, variants, exploded relationships, or common components. Orthographic and diagrammatic views are more appropriate than dramatic perspective. Typography should be neutral, compact, and technical without becoming military or futuristic. Labels, numbers, and rules need a repeatable hierarchy. Use white space to separate levels of the system and keep annotations close to the elements they describe.

## Color, material, imagery, and light

Favor white, gray, black, muted industrial materials, and one functional signal color. Material choices should reveal production logic: molded plastic, sheet metal, ceramic, wood, or paper shown honestly. Photography works when objects are evenly lit and comparable in scale. Diagrams may use fine lines, silhouettes, and cutaways, but decoration and dramatic rendering are distractions. Light should expose edge, joint, proportion, and surface rather than create luxury atmosphere. A neutral background supports comparison across the family.

## Common exits

The node becomes Bauhaus Workshop when exploratory form and material exercises take priority over a resolved system. It shifts into Corporate Modernism when identity consistency across communications is the main problem, or into Operational Dashboard when live metrics and screen controls dominate. Common failures include showing only the Ulm building, treating any gray product as Ulm, using a one-off concept object, adding ornamental technical marks, or borrowing famous identity work without explaining the underlying relationships.

## Medium adaptation

For products, document shared components, manufacturing constraints, variants, and interfaces. For identity systems, show how marks, signs, documents, and objects operate as one coordinated environment. In editorial work, use comparative plates, sequences, and concise annotations. For digital tools, translate the method into a component library with explicit states and rules, while avoiding generic rounded-card styling. In presentations, lead with the system map before individual outcomes. Motion can assemble components, compare variants, or expose dependencies. Every adaptation should make the organizing logic more legible than any isolated artifact.
