# Isometric Play

## Core grammar

Isometric Play builds miniature worlds on a consistent parallel projection. Equal-axis structure, readable stacking, modular rooms or streets, cutaway visibility, and toy-like scale let complex space remain diagrammatic and cheerful. Use it for service maps, educational worlds, game-like explainers, campus or city illustrations, and systems that benefit from spatial overview without realistic perspective.

## Composition and hierarchy

Set the projection before drawing objects and keep all major edges faithful to it. Organize the world into a few clear modules with paths between them. Height and overlap should reveal relationships rather than hide important parts. A hero building or room can anchor the center, while smaller units extend the system. Leave enough ground plane around the world to preserve its miniature-object read.

## Type and graphic language

Labels may sit on the ground plane, attach to callouts, or occupy separate flat panels. Use a simple sans and avoid forcing long text onto angled surfaces. Icons should share the projection only when they depict spatial objects; utility controls can remain front-facing. Do not let game-interface furniture overwhelm the illustrative map.

## Color, material, imagery, and light

Bright controlled palettes help distinguish modules. Use one consistent light direction so every wall, roof, and object shares shadow logic. Materials should remain simplified: matte blocks, limited texture, and small highlights. Avoid photoreal surface noise, depth-of-field blur, and dramatic atmosphere, which conflict with the inspectable system view.

## Use across media

Illustration, data storytelling, onboarding, and web explainers are strong fits. Motion can assemble modules, lift floors, or trace paths while preserving the camera. Presentation graphics should crop the world without changing projection. Product and environment diagrams can use exploded isometric layers, but labels and accessibility states need a separate readable plane.

## Common exits

Converging lines create ordinary perspective. A pile of colorful primitives becomes Geometric Toy if no complete spatial world exists. Flat repeated blocks become Pattern Playground. Highly realistic architecture with atmospheric sky reads as visualization rather than playful system illustration.

## Direction recipe

Establish three equal axes and a fixed object scale. Construct one central module, add three related spaces, and connect them with a readable route. Apply one light direction and one palette hierarchy, then audit every edge for projection drift. The viewer should understand both the whole world and the role of each module.
