# Anatomical Plate

## Defining grammar

Anatomical Plate turns bodily structure into an ordered teaching image. It may show a whole figure, organ, system, cross-section, dissection layer, or comparative animal anatomy, but every visible element must preserve coherent orientation and relationship. Precise contour, selective color, leader lines, numbers, captions, and inset details clarify what lies beside, within, behind, or beneath something else. The goal is didactic understanding, not shock, beauty advertising, or decorative morbidity.

## Composition and type

Center the principal body or organ and reserve generous surrounding space for labels. Use bilateral symmetry when it helps the reader compare sides, then break it purposefully for cutaways or layered views. Leader lines should leave the figure cleanly, avoid crossing, and terminate at exact structures. Number systems need a stable sequence and a nearby legend; direct labels need enough contrast and room to remain readable. Insets can enlarge joints, vessels, tissues, or stages. Typography should be clinical and quiet, with italics or weight used consistently for taxonomic and hierarchical distinctions.

## Color, material, imagery, and light

Use restrained natural color or a clear conventional code: arterial red, venous blue, bone cream, muscle red-brown, nerve yellow, or monochrome line with selected accents. Fine engraving, lithographic color, pen line, or modern vector illustration can all work if they preserve structure. Keep the ground neutral and illumination even. Three-dimensional modeling should avoid dramatic reflections and cinematic shadows that hide connections. Texture can distinguish tissue, but gore, fluids, and torn surfaces should appear only when medically relevant and never as spectacle.

## Common exits

The style exits into Botanical Plate when a whole plant and diagnostic botanical parts replace body systems. It exits into Field Guide when multiple species and field identification become primary. It exits into Classic Infographic when quantities, process, or public explanation outweigh anatomical fidelity. A skeleton poster, medical stock photograph, tattoo flash sheet, or surreal body collage is not an anatomical plate without coherent structural explanation.

## Medium adaptation

For education and editorial work, decide the learning objective first, then reveal only the layers needed to answer it. For interfaces, allow controlled toggling among systems while preserving position and label alignment. For presentations, use one body system or section per frame and enlarge labels for distance viewing. Motion can peel layers, rotate a model slowly, or trace circulation, but should never make the viewer lose anatomical orientation. Verify medical content with qualified sources and reviewers; visual polish cannot compensate for invented anatomy or ambiguous leader lines.
