# Narrative Woodcut

## Defining grammar

Narrative Woodcut tells through the constraints of relief printing. The artist cuts away what will remain paper and leaves raised lines and shapes to receive ink. This reversal encourages blunt contour, white cuts inside black areas, compressed detail, and silhouettes that survive imperfect transfer. Use those qualities to stage action, character, setting, and consequence in one forceful scene or a serial group. The style can range from early book illustration to popular tale prints, but the carved block and printed impression must remain legible.

## Composition and type

Build the image from black-white mass before adding small lines. Figures can overlap architecture, foliage, weather, and props in a shallow dramatic field. Diagonals, repeated gestures, and alternating dark zones should guide the eye through the event. A serial work may repeat a frame size or title strip while allowing each scene a different internal balance. Because woodcut and movable type are both relief processes, text can share the page naturally, but keep the type historically or materially plausible. Avoid delicate copperplate effects and digital outlines of identical weight.

## Color, material, imagery, and light

Use black ink on cream or toned paper as the default, with one or two added colors only when they remain subordinate to the relief structure. Let knife, gouge, grain, inking, pressure, and paper absorbency produce variation. Light is encoded as unprinted paper cut from darkness rather than rendered by smooth gradient. Imagery favors decisive action, ritual, labor, landscape, satire, legend, and moral or historical episodes. Keep marks economical enough that the print reads at page scale.

## Common exits

The style exits into Ukiyo-e when registered polychrome blocks, atmospheric gradation, patterned surfaces, and Edo composition dominate. It exits into Punk Zine when photocopy, collage, marker, and improvised type replace carved relief. It exits into Engraving when fine incised lines and tonal crosshatch become primary. A woodgrain overlay, linocut-style icon set, or distressed vector portrait is not Narrative Woodcut unless the scene and mark logic behave as a relief print.

## Medium adaptation

For editorial illustration, design in black and white first, carve or simulate believable relief decisions, and test at the final printed size. For packaging, use one narrative emblem or scene with restrained type rather than covering every panel in rough marks. For web and presentation, place woodcut images on calm paper-like fields while maintaining modern navigation and readable copy. Motion can reveal the negative cutting process, roll ink across the raised surface, or sequence several prints like pages. Generated imagery should reproduce movement-level relief grammar and original subject matter, not trace a protected contemporary print.
