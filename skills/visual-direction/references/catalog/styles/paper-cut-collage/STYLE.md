# Paper-Cut Collage

## Core grammar

Paper-Cut Collage constructs scenes and shapes from physically legible sheets. Scissor cuts, torn fibers, colored-paper overlap, shallow cast shadows, and handmade irregularity distinguish it from vector illustration or a montage of photographic clippings. Use it for editorial illustration, children’s storytelling, cultural programming, craft campaigns, and tactile motion graphics.

## Composition and hierarchy

Build from broad ground shapes toward smaller foreground details. Overlap should explain which pieces sit above or below, while slight misalignment keeps the assembly human. Combine cut and torn edges intentionally: clean cuts clarify geometry; tears soften landscape, fur, cloud, or organic forms. Keep depth shallow enough to read as paper rather than a miniature set.

## Type and graphic language

Type may be cut from paper, printed on a separate layer, or kept clean outside the collage. If letters are hand-cut, preserve readable counters and a consistent baseline. Avoid mixing many novelty alphabets or using ransom-note clipping unless the message genuinely calls for that history. One sturdy type voice usually provides enough contrast to the irregular image.

## Color, material, imagery, and light

Warm red, blue, yellow, leaf green, kraft brown, black, and cream can establish clear layers, but any coherent colored-paper palette works. Vary fiber, coating, and weight subtly. Raking soft light should reveal edges and a few millimeters of shadow. Digital simulation must include credible thickness, contact, and edge variation; perfectly smooth Bezier contours will read as flat vector art.

## Use across media

Illustration and print benefit from photographing the assembled original at high resolution. Motion can move individual pieces with stop-frame restraint, preserving their physical pivot and overlap. Web and presentation uses should retain enough scale for the edge texture. Packaging may translate the layers into emboss, die cut, or stacked stock, provided production remains structurally plausible.

## Common exits

Historical engraved figures compiled on one sheet are print montage, not colored-paper construction. Photo cutouts dominate toward collage editorial. Thick dimensional foam becomes a diorama, while pattern-only repeated pieces belong to Pattern Playground. Do not erase every irregularity during cleanup.

## Direction recipe

Limit the scene to six colored stocks and three depth levels. Cut the major silhouettes, tear one organic family, overlap pieces with visible contact, and photograph under a broad raking source. Inspect edges at full size and confirm the scene still reads clearly at card scale.
