# Comic Panel

## Defining grammar

Comic Panel turns time into spatial sequence. Ink contour, panel size, gutters, captions, balloons, sound effects, viewpoint changes, and controlled reveal work together so the reader understands what happens and when. A style reference must show at least a short sequence; one isolated superhero image is illustration, not panel grammar. The strongest pages vary shot scale and panel rhythm while preserving characters, setting, and reading order.

## Composition and type

Establish a clear directional flow through regular grids, inset panels, broken frames, or deliberate full-width beats. Larger panels slow time or establish place; narrow repeated panels accelerate action; silent panels create pause. Keep balloon tails tied to speakers and place balloons in reading order before drawing detail around them. Lettering should be compact, consistent, and highly legible. Captions, dialogue, and sound effects need different voices without becoming typographic chaos. Gutters are active timing devices, not leftover space.

## Color, material, imagery, and light

Black ink contour and value structure should work before color. Use flat limited color, process-dot texture, spot accents, or full digital color according to the chosen comic tradition. Preserve paper, ink, or print evidence when referencing historical pages. Imagery can range widely, but character continuity, readable action, and environmental cues must survive every panel. Light should support staging and emotional change rather than vary randomly from frame to frame.

## Common exits

The style becomes Pop Art when a comic fragment is enlarged, repeated, or treated as an isolated mass-media object. It becomes Kids Picture Book when page-turn illustration and integrated prose replace panel sequence. It becomes Storyboard when rough functional shot planning replaces finished lettering and graphic rhythm. Avoid random speech balloons, generated gibberish, inconsistent characters, duplicated panels, and action that crosses the established screen direction without purpose. Do not default to copyrighted superheroes or living artists' recognizable styles.

## Medium adaptation

For editorial and social, build three to six panels around one readable beat. For presentations, use panel size to control reveal and keep one idea per frame. For web, preserve reading order responsively; vertical scroll comics can replace page grids but still need pacing. For motion, animate panel transitions, balloon appearance, or limited internal action without turning the work into full cinema. Image generation should specify original characters, panel count, reading direction, shot progression, balloon placement, and consistent costume and setting.
