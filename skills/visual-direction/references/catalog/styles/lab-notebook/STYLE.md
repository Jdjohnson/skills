# Lab Notebook

## Defining grammar

Lab Notebook records scientific work as it happens. A convincing page connects date, question, materials, setup, measurement, observation, calculation, correction, and next step. The value lies in traceable evolution: failed trials remain visible, crossed-out values are replaced rather than erased, apparatus sketches clarify configuration, and later notes may refer back to earlier entries. This is not a generic distressed journal or a page covered in unrelated equations. Every mark should belong to one experiment or research sequence.

## Composition and type

Use chronological pages, stable margins, page numbers, headings, and enough open space for additions. Apparatus drawings can occupy the center with measurements and labels gathered nearby; tables may align repeated trials; calculations can descend in a readable chain. Handwriting may vary with speed and tool, but key values and labels must remain decipherable. Tape-ins, graphs, specimen sketches, and instrument outputs are useful when integrated into the record. Avoid polished magazine layouts and fake spontaneity arranged as decorative collage.

## Color, material, imagery, and light

Use ruled, gridded, or plain bound paper with graphite, black or blue ink, red correction, and occasional colored pencil or pasted instrument output. Wear should follow handling: softened corners, smudges near calculations, tape yellowing, or a stain at the bench, not uniform antique distress. Photograph or render under even archival light so handwriting, pressure, erasure, and paper texture remain clear. Imagery is functional—apparatus, waveform, specimen, circuit, graph, or geometry—and should relate directly to notes and values.

## Common exits

The style exits into Engineering Blueprint when finalized dimensions, coordinated orthographic views, and title blocks replace evolving observation. It exits into Field Guide when portable identification and comparative species records dominate. It exits into Academic Journal when methods and conclusions are formally edited for publication. A planner, grocery ledger, artist sketchbook, or mood-board notebook does not qualify without an experiment-specific chain of evidence.

## Medium adaptation

For editorial work, reproduce a full page before enlarging a compelling sketch or correction so context is not lost. For interfaces, model chronological entries, attachments, version history, and immutable timestamps rather than imitating paper stains. For presentations, reveal question, setup, trial, and conclusion sequentially while retaining the original record. Motion can trace a revision or animate measured change, but should not clean up uncertainty. If fabricating an illustrative notebook, keep the scientific scenario internally consistent and label it as a reconstruction rather than presenting invented notes as historical evidence.
