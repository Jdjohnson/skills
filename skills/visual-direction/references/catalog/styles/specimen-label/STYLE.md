# Specimen Label

## Defining grammar

Specimen Label is the compact physical record that keeps a collected object connected to identity, provenance, and institutional custody. It may be pinned beneath an insect, tied to an object, placed in a tray, stored with a herbarium sheet, or sealed near a jar. Catalog number, taxonomic or object name, locality, collector, date, field number, type status, and preparation method form a dense hierarchy. The style is not visitor-facing museum interpretation; it is collection infrastructure at object scale.

## Composition and type

Prioritize the identifier that links label and database, then arrange scientific name, place, collector, date, and secondary codes in a predictable order. Multiple small labels may divide original field data, later determination, accession, barcode, and type status rather than forcing everything onto one card. Use compact serif, sans, typewriter, handwriting, stamp, or thermal print according to period and institution. Alignment should conserve space while preserving line-by-line scanning. Keep enough margin for pin, hole, fold, or string and never let decoration compete with the record.

## Color, material, imagery, and light

Materials include acid-free card, handwritten field slip, printed paper, tied tag, label inside fluid, archival ink, stamp, pencil, barcode, and small color-coded status mark. Cream, white, pale gray, and aged paper dominate; red or other color may signal type or warning according to a documented system. Photograph under even light so tiny type and attachment remain visible. Wear, old determinations, and replacement labels can show the history of curation, but do not add arbitrary coffee stains or fictional institutional marks.

## Common exits

The style exits into Field Guide when comparison, illustration, habitat, and reader identification dominate. It exits into Museum Wall Label when title, interpretation, and visitor reading distance become primary. It exits into Lab Notebook when chronological experiments and working observations replace specimen identity. A library card, vintage luggage tag, apothecary label, or minimalist product hangtag may share compact typography but lacks the catalog-to-object relationship.

## Medium adaptation

For editorial or identity, borrow the tight identifier hierarchy, ruled spacing, and small status marks while avoiding fake accession claims. For interfaces, map each physical field to structured data and retain links among current name, historical determinations, collection event, rights, and unique identifier. For presentations, enlarge the label without redesigning its hierarchy and show the specimen beside it. Motion can connect field number to catalog record or reveal successive determinations. Protect sensitive locality data where collection policy requires it, and never invent provenance for a real cultural or natural-history object.
