# Art Nouveau

## Defining grammar

Art Nouveau joins structure and decoration through continuous organic line. Its signature is the whiplash curve: an elastic S-shape that can become hair, stem, ironwork, border, lettering, or furniture profile. Botanical observation supplies growth logic, but the result is designed rather than naturalistic. Image, type, frame, object, and architecture should feel conceived together. The style can be floral and sensual or sharply linear and geometric depending on region, yet it always resists pasted-on historical ornament in favor of a unified new form.

## Composition and type

Let a dominant curve organize the whole field. Figures and text can nest inside botanical arcs, while borders grow into image structure instead of forming a separate rectangle. Use asymmetry, elongated vertical formats, cropped silhouettes, and areas of flat pattern against calmer ground. Lettering should be integrated: custom display forms may echo stems or hair, but counters and word shapes must remain readable. Pair expressive headings with a simple serif or humanist sans for body text. Do not scatter unrelated flourishes around a conventional centered layout.

## Color, material, imagery, and light

Useful palettes include muted olive, ochre, cream, dusty rose, peacock blue, black, and warm metallic accents. Some regional variants support brighter jewel color, but avoid turning every surface neon. Materials include lithographic ink, stained glass, wrought iron, carved wood, enamel, tile, and patterned textile. Imagery favors elongated figures, flowers, insects, vines, hair, water, and crafted architectural detail. Light is usually even enough to preserve line and flat color; in three-dimensional work, raking light may reveal iron, glass, or carving without producing Baroque darkness.

## Common exits

The style exits into Belle Époque Lithograph when public entertainment, color printing, and poster-scale messaging dominate. It exits into Arts and Crafts when material honesty and repeating handcraft pattern replace flowing integrated line. It exits into Rococo when shell scrolls and salon ornament replace botanical growth. Avoid generic floral borders, symmetrical wedding stationery, “fairy” imagery, or modern boho leaves with no structural curve. Deco sunbursts, stepped geometry, and polished chrome are a different movement.

## Medium adaptation

For posters and identity, design the headline and image as one silhouette. For packaging, wrap a continuous line across panels and reserve ornament for the product story rather than nutrition or legal copy. For web and presentations, use the curve to guide section flow, illustration, or framing while keeping interaction controls plain. For interiors, integrate ironwork, glazing, furniture, and surface pattern rather than buying unrelated floral objects. Motion should trace, unfurl, or grow along the line with controlled organic timing. Always decide which regional Art Nouveau vocabulary is being used before combining motifs.
