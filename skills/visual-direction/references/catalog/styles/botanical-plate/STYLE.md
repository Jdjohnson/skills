# Botanical Plate

## Defining grammar

Botanical Plate is a scientific visual record that shows a plant completely enough to support description and identification. Present the whole habit together with diagnostic parts that may be hidden, tiny, seasonal, or impossible to see simultaneously in a photograph. Leaves, roots, stems, flowers, fruit, seed, attachment, and dissected structures can be enlarged or sectioned while remaining related to the main specimen. Accuracy, comparison, and selective emphasis matter more than picturesque arrangement.

## Composition and type

Place the whole plant or representative branch as the principal figure, then arrange details around it in a logical sequence. Use small letters or numbers to connect parts to concise captions. Include scale bars or magnification where size matters. Allow generous white space so delicate contours and fine internal structures remain distinct. Scientific and common names should be typeset quietly and consistently, with taxonomic conventions respected. Avoid floral-bouquet balance, symmetrical wedding-stationery arrangements, and text placed over the specimen.

## Color, material, imagery, and light

Fine pen line, graphite, stipple, watercolor, hand-colored engraving, or precise digital drawing can all serve the style. Use natural color selectively to distinguish structures and maturation rather than heighten romance. Even illumination and neutral ground are essential. Tiny spikelets, venation, hairs, stamens, seed capsules, and cross-sections may be enlarged far beyond life size if clearly related and scaled. Preserve the discipline of observation; loose washes, dramatic shadow, and atmospheric garden settings obscure the diagnostic purpose.

## Common exits

The style exits into Botanical Watercolor when painterly mood, isolated blossoms, and expressive wash outweigh scientific completeness. It exits into Field Guide when several species, habitat, range, and quick identification dominate. It exits into Specimen Label when collection metadata rather than visual morphology becomes primary. A pressed leaf collage, floral repeat, herb-shop illustration, or attractive plant portrait is not a botanical plate without whole-part analysis and scientific structure.

## Medium adaptation

For editorial and packaging, retain the main specimen, a few meaningful details, and a clear labeling system instead of using the plant as border filler. For interfaces, permit zoom into diagnostic parts while keeping the whole specimen visible as orientation. For presentations, reveal the whole first and then isolate enlarged structures. Motion can grow from root to flower or move between seasonal parts, but should not invent biology. Work with botanists or authoritative records when accuracy matters, and separate real species documentation from fictional botanical world-building.
