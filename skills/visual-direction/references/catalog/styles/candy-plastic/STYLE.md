# Candy Plastic

## Core grammar

Candy Plastic pairs molded industrial form with edible-looking color and approachable softness. Rounded edges, translucent or frosted polymers, glossy surfaces, ribbed detail, and broad specular roll create playful material depth. Use it for product concepts, cosmetics, accessories, packaging, 3D illustration, and youth-facing campaigns where the material itself should feel delightful.

## Composition and hierarchy

Treat objects as a small coordinated family. Vary height, silhouette, and translucency while keeping scale believable. Overlap can reveal color mixing through clear material, and a close crop can emphasize ribbing or wall thickness. Preserve space around the hero so highlights and cast shadows remain readable. Avoid weightless blobs; even softened forms need molded seams, bases, or functional cues.

## Type and graphic language

Rounded sans, soft geometric lettering, and compact labels can echo the objects without becoming bubble-letter caricature. Type should usually remain flat and crisp beside the rendered material. Avoid glassmorphism panels, chrome Y2K lettering, and candy-brand trade dress. A small technical label can counterbalance sweetness when the product needs credibility.

## Color, material, imagery, and light

Candy pink, mint, baby blue, lilac, tangerine, and milky white are useful in limited combinations. Distinguish transparent, translucent, frosted, and opaque surfaces through thickness, refraction, and highlight behavior. Use a large soft key with controlled edge lights so the object glows without losing geometry. Fine mold seams, ribs, and soft feet can establish manufacture; scratches, heavy grain, and raw fibers contradict the polished material.

## Use across media

Product 3D and studio photography lead. Motion can turn objects slowly or pass light through translucent walls. Packaging should use material windows or renderings honestly rather than imply a polymer the physical item lacks. Web and social layouts can borrow color and rounded shape, but should not turn every container into a frosted floating panel.

## Common exits

Wooden modular blocks belong to Geometric Toy. Flat pastel illustration lacks molded depth. Visible aggregate, shredded polymer, and sustainability storytelling create a different recycled-plastic language. Overly sharp chrome and neon move toward Y2K Cyber, while soft paper edges become Paper-Cut Collage.

## Direction recipe

Design three functional objects from one rounded geometry family. Assign one translucent, one frosted, and one glossy opaque material, then light them with a broad key and narrow rim. Check that wall thickness, ribs, contact, and specular movement all describe believable molding before adding playful color.
