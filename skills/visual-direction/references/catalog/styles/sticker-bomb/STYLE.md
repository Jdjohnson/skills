# Sticker Bomb

## Defining grammar

Sticker Bomb is a dense surface collage made by overlapping many discrete adhesive graphics over time. Die-cut outlines, varied scales, torn corners, lifted edges, abrasion, fading, repeated tags, and almost no exposed ground create the identity. The surface should feel portable, public, and accumulated—on a case, board, pole, wall, vehicle panel, or object—rather than arranged as a pristine sticker sheet. Individual stickers may be simple, but their collisions create a second composition.

## Composition and type

Work from large anchors to medium fillers to small gap-closing marks. Rotate and overlap pieces so no single grid controls the field, while preserving a few readable focal graphics. Let edges disappear under later layers. Type is embedded inside stickers: tags, condensed names, slogans, icons, and hand lettering can coexist, but avoid adding a separate clean headline over the whole field. Density should vary slightly so the eye can move through the collage instead of meeting uniform noise.

## Color, material, imagery, and light

Use mixed high-chroma color, black-and-white marks, fluorescent accents, weathered paper, glossy vinyl, matte postal labels, and visible adhesive wear. White die-cut borders help individual pieces remain legible. Imagery may include original characters, tags, symbols, local messages, small illustrations, and event remnants. Light should reveal the applied surface, edge lift, scuffs, and layer thickness without turning stickers into floating digital cards. A raking side light can make accumulation physical.

## Common exits

The style becomes Sticker Sheet System when coordinated motifs sit neatly on one backing sheet. It becomes Dada Collage when torn printed fragments and absurd photomontage replace adhesive objects. It becomes Punk Zine when page reproduction, staples, and paste-up dominate. Avoid copying recognizable commercial marks, street artists' signatures, or protected characters. Do not imply vandalism or apply real stickers to property without permission. A random pile of isolated clip-art icons on a clean background is not a bomb.

## Medium adaptation

For packaging and product surfaces, build an original sticker library and apply it to a plausible object with real overlaps. For campaigns, use one dense field as a hero and keep supporting information outside it. For web, treat the collage as imagery rather than placing interactive controls at random angles. Motion can add pieces sequentially, reveal older layers, or lift one edge, but should preserve physical stacking. Image generation should specify an original sticker vocabulary, legal surface, accumulated layering, wear, die-cut borders, and near-total ground coverage.
