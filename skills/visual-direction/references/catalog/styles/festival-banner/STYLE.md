# Festival Banner

## Defining grammar

Festival Banner uses repeated suspended paper or cloth to transform shared space for a named ceremony, procession, holiday, or gathering. The unit may be hand-cut papel picado, sewn flag, appliquéd banner, carried standard, or another locally specific form. Its visual force comes from multiplication across air: color spans streets, marks a route, frames a plaza, or moves above a crowd. Start with the particular festival and construction method. Generic triangular bunting does not carry the same social or cultural meaning.

## Composition and type

Arrange units in strands, ranks, clusters, or processional sequences. Repetition establishes the large rhythm, while variation in motif, color, wear, and wind prevents a mechanical pattern. Use perspective along the line of suspension to show distance, or look upward so cutwork appears against sky and architecture. Lettering may identify an event, date, saint, group, or sponsor when accurate to the source; do not fill banners with invented language. Digital layouts can borrow alternating panels and airy intervals without pretending the screen itself is a ceremonial street.

## Color, material, imagery, and light

Use tissue paper, amate-derived paper, cotton, satin, felt, embroidery, cord, wood poles, or other documented materials. Color can be saturated and serial—pink, orange, blue, yellow, green, purple, white—but should follow the selected celebration. Backlight reveals cut paper as lace; side light shows folds and sewn layers; wind changes overlap and shadow. Motifs may be floral, geometric, commemorative, devotional, ancestral, political, or playful according to context. Avoid weatherproof plastic gloss unless the documented contemporary practice actually uses it.

## Common exits

The style exits into Folk Embroidery when a garment or textile surface and its stitch language dominate. It exits into Sticker Sheet System when small isolated icons are arranged for selection rather than suspended in space. It exits into event branding when a logo system and standardized sponsor graphics become primary. Papel picado used as a generic fiesta border, Tibetan prayer flags reduced to color strips, or sacred procession standards mixed into party décor are not respectful Festival Banner direction.

## Medium adaptation

For editorial or exhibition work, show the banners in public space and explain maker, material, event, and function. For identity, adapt suspension rhythm, alternating color, or cut-positive and cut-negative relationships without lifting sacred or community-specific imagery. For web, use light repeated separators and open sky-like space while keeping navigation stable. Motion should respond to airflow, slack, and changing overlap rather than bounce like interface stickers. For a living festival, work with cultural participants, commission makers where possible, preserve accurate language, and keep memorial or devotional symbols tied to their actual use.
