# Vaporwave

## Defining grammar

Vaporwave is an internet-born remix of 1980s and 1990s consumer memory. Pastel sunset gradients, obsolete computing fragments, mall interiors, classical sculpture, Japanese commercial references, low-fi video artifacts, and slowed retail atmosphere are combined into an unreal digital collage. Its mood is nostalgic and ambivalent: familiar promises of leisure, technology, and abundance return as softened, displaced images.

Use a recognizable movement-level approximation while creating original subjects, typography, arrangement, and image material. The style is stronger when the viewer can identify several eras or sources colliding inside one coherent dream, not when a single pink-and-blue gradient carries the entire direction.

## Composition and type

Favor layered, often symmetrical compositions with an obvious horizon, framed object, statue-like figure, corridor, grid, or floating interface fragment. Large empty regions can create suspended time. Repetition, cropping, and scale mismatch should feel intentional. A receding checker grid may organize depth, but it is only one possible device and should not become compulsory.

Typography can combine extended sans, early desktop system type, monospaced labels, or carefully contextualized Japanese text. Keep the main title sparse and let spacing contribute to the detached mood. Do not use unfamiliar language as meaningless decoration; verify wording and context when language is visible.

## Color, material, imagery, and light

Build around dusty pink, aqua, lavender, peach, blue-violet, and pale yellow, with a dark neutral available for legibility. Gradients should resemble sunset, screen bloom, fluorescent retail light, or aging video rather than contemporary app branding. Chrome, marble, glass block, plastic plants, glossy tile, and low-resolution screens create the material mix.

Imagery may include invented mall architecture, original classical-form sculpture, generic consumer objects, palms, clouds, and early-computing windows. Light is soft, diffuse, and frequently artificial. VHS tracking, compression, or scan texture can age the scene, but harsh rupture belongs to Glitch Art.

## Common exits

Vaporwave becomes Y2K Cyber when translucent gadgets, bubble type, and optimistic chrome take control. It becomes Neon Night UI when a usable dark interface replaces collage. It becomes generic synthwave when a hard neon sunset, sports car, and cinematic speed dominate. Avoid random Japanese characters, copied album art, recognizable commercial interiors, and classical sculpture used as a lone shortcut. Pastel color without consumer-memory evidence is not enough.

## Medium adaptation

For social and image work, use a strong central silhouette and broad color fields that survive small screens. Motion should favor slow zoom, drift, looping environmental effects, and occasional analog disturbance. Websites can use atmospheric transitions around a conventional readable content layer; task interfaces should not inherit the collage wholesale. Presentations need a restrained master with collage concentrated in covers and section breaks. Identity systems should keep a plain primary mark and reserve the richer treatment for campaigns, events, or releases. Packaging can use gradients, iridescent stock, soft-focus image panels, and low-fi labels while maintaining regulatory clarity. Environmental installations benefit from projected loops, reflective surfaces, and ambient retail light. Whenever found material is considered, keep the historical research anchor separate from the final distributable image decision.
