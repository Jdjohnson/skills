# Graphic Brutalism

## Defining grammar

Graphic brutalism uses blunt type, hard-edged blocks, exposed layout seams, abrasive contrast, and deliberately unresolved joins to confront the viewer. It is a visual grammar, not an anti-brand strategy or a promise of rebellion. Use it when communication needs force, candor, and friction while still operating as a coherent designed system. Awkwardness must be authored.

## Composition

Set oversized elements against narrow columns, let rules collide with type, crop images severely, and expose abrupt changes in scale. One dominant block should establish the page’s pressure. Misregistration or ragged alignment can appear, but recurring anchors keep the system recognizable. Avoid a tidy card grid, balanced lifestyle collage, or random displacement that has no relationship to content hierarchy.

## Type

Choose heavy grotesks, condensed display faces, plain system fonts, or sharp serifs with visibly different duties. Headlines may run to the edge, stack tightly, or occupy most of the field. Body copy remains readable and structurally separate. Do not use novelty “brutalist” fonts as the entire concept. The force comes from scale, interval, collision, and sequence more than eccentric letter shapes.

## Color and material

Favor black and white with one unsoftened signal color, or a small group of solid clashing fields. Use flat ink, coarse halftone, overprint, bare paper, and visible cut edges sparingly. Avoid ambient gradients, translucent glass, and premium metallic embellishment. Roughness should clarify a production stance; applying identical noise to every layer simply makes the work look filtered.

## Imagery and light

Images function as evidence, confrontation, or raw material: direct portraits, architectural fragments, documents, objects, and tightly cropped gestures. Hard flash, flat scan, and high-contrast reproduction are appropriate. Decorative stock imagery is not. A generated reference should describe recognizable movement-level confrontational graphic design with original subjects, blunt typographic blocks, exposed seams, and disciplined abrasive contrast.

## Common exits

The style becomes punk zine when DIY publishing materials and subcultural voice dominate, web brutalism when browser behavior and document structure lead, and Dada collage when chance juxtaposition replaces systematic pressure. It fails when offensiveness is treated as a visual shortcut or when poor legibility is defended as authenticity. The result should feel uncompromising, not unfinished through neglect.

## Medium adaptation

Identity systems need a repeatable law for crops, rule weights, scale jumps, and color collisions. Websites can use blunt boundaries and exposed states while retaining semantic markup and accessible focus. Presentations should alternate dense evidence with singular, oversized claims. Packaging works through direct printing, oversized labeling, and structural folds. Motion may slam, cut, occlude, or snap between scales, but timing should remain intelligible rather than becoming continuous glitch or visual punishment.
