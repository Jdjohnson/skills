# Swiss Phototypography

## Defining grammar

This direction makes photography and typography equal structural material. The image is not an illustration placed beside copy, and the type is not a caption added afterward. Both are locked to a strict grid through crop, scale, overlap, and alignment. Use it for posters, covers, campaigns, and feature openers that need photographic immediacy with modernist discipline.

## Composition and type

Select a photograph with a strong edge, gesture, horizon, or subject that can align to the layout. Crop firmly; a partial face, building, object, or landscape can create more structure than a polite complete view. Introduce one oversized sans letterform or short word that shares the image field, then support it with smaller flush-left information. Type may mask the photograph, reverse from it, or extend beyond the edge, but its placement must follow the grid. Avoid casual text-over-photo treatment and unrelated floating captions.

## Color, material, imagery, and light

Black and white photography with one vivid accent is a reliable starting point, not a rule. Color images work when their dominant shapes and tones cooperate with the type. Keep surfaces matte or cleanly printed; effects such as glow, bevel, and torn collage weaken the precision. Choose objective, documentary, architectural, or product imagery with enough tonal separation to support letterforms. Directional light, hard crop boundaries, and clear silhouettes help. If the photograph is visually diffuse, the type will look pasted on rather than integrated.

## Common exits

The node exits into International Typographic Style when image and type return to separate grid cells and the broader information system becomes primary. It becomes Neo-Grotesk Editorial when reading pace and captions matter more than large-scale fusion. It becomes a generic magazine cover when display type merely sits over a hero photograph. Avoid freeform montage, multiple unrelated images, ornamental fonts, low-contrast text, fake foreign-language fragments, and cropping that destroys the subject without creating a stronger form.

## Medium adaptation

For posters and covers, establish the photo crop and dominant type together at the first sketch. For responsive web work, define alternate crops and type scales rather than allowing automatic wrapping to break the relationship. In motion, let the image and letterforms reveal or mask one another along grid lines. For presentations, use the grammar selectively on section openers and keep content slides calmer. In brand campaigns, vary subject and crop while holding alignment, typographic proportion, and color behavior stable. The medium can change, but photograph and type must continue to behave as one composition.
